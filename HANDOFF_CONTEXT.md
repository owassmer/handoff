## Product agreement

Handoff takes responsibility from learning that a tenancy is ending through two independently progressing outcomes: the unit is ready for its next occupant, and the departing tenancy's financial relationship is resolved. The account includes rent, utilities, damage, credits and deposits. Responsibility includes investigation, inspections, provider engagement, repairs, funding, communications, disputes, refunds, recovery and an accepted decision to stop pursuing a balance. Sending a matter to another provider does not itself finish the job. Marketing and leasing remain separate.

Design around the work and authority to act. Existing staffing arrangements are facts to understand, not required application roles. Handoff may perform work that currently justifies a position. Users have permissions; owner, contracting party, provider and payee relationships retain their actual business meaning.

One coordinating agent investigates, reasons, recommends and carries accepted intent into execution. The operator accepts or changes the coherent work plan and budget before repair commitments, and the account recommendation appropriate to the financial decision at hand. Acceptance binds the intended result, scope, budget and explicit requirements. Handoff manages delivery details within that mandate and brings a reasoned revision when a material change requires a new decision. Do not convert ordinary implementation choices into more operator work.

Combine consistent logic with adaptable reasoning. Code handles exact arithmetic and determinate rules. The agent interprets intent, weighs evidence, applies standards and works through unfamiliar circumstances. Legal informatics helps express and interpret goals as well as determine obligations. Private instructions coexist with other parties' rights. Use focused semantic judgments where they improve the work; a new framework is not an outcome.

Legal research is agent-led investigation supported by Jev and code. The agent reasons and orchestrates along an instructed flow, retrieves context, follows dependencies, and synthesizes findings. Jev supplies rapid, high-volume atomic semantic judgments with explicitly sufficient inputs and a defined use for each answer. Code manages structured I/O, source identity, validation, dispatch, and exact computation. Atomic questions may require substantial context. Calibrate those tasks within the actual flow, measuring retrieval and context assembly separately from model judgment and final operating decisions.

Represent recognizable business things and meaningful distinctions. A unit, tenancy and occupant have different identities. A job, invoice, liability, concession and settled payment mean different things. Physical work, money, authority and information can change independently. Genuine obligations describe something owed or required in the world; every software task need not become an obligation.

Make the operator experience a usable place to understand, decide and follow work. Use structured amounts and comparisons, relevant reasons, actual documents and correspondence, and coherent direct editing and conversation. Use plain language throughout the product and implementation. Design rationale and technical verification belong in internal work. One Demo indicator is sufficient context for the demonstration environment.

The operator buys two connected benefits: automation of the full physical and financial flows, and better decisions that protect the operator's money and reduce exposure to disputes and litigation. The legal and decision engine must shape investigation, repair scope, commitments, account treatment and settlement throughout the work. Avoid unnecessary work and vendor charges, preserve legitimate recovery, meet obligations, and resolve disagreements on a defensible and economically sensible basis. A refund, deduction, settlement or decision to pursue a balance is a case-dependent result, not a predetermined product goal.

Measure useful completion, decision quality, remaining human coordination and avoidable expense and exposure. Distinguish demonstrated improvements from estimated savings or risk reduction. Founder value also depends on customer payment, delivery cost, repeatability, distribution and the resulting revenue, funding and exit possibilities. Research recommendations, modeled economics and proposed company shapes remain proposals until chosen. A successful demonstration does not establish customer economics.

## October 3, 2026 — workflow agreements (Owen, with the coordinator)

These supersede earlier repository text where they differ, including statements that Foundry is the iteration environment.

- Foundation: design from the agreement and how the work actually runs, not from the existing code. That code was the means to a proof of concept. Build fresh off Foundry now, on the stack Handoff will keep. The Foundry demonstration stays untouched as a fallback.
- Demonstration: staged at key moments with live decisions. Each moment is a saved copy of the whole case: Handoff's records, every outside system's records and the clock. In the meeting the operator opens a fresh copy, decides live, and watches Handoff carry it forward.
- Account posture: maximize lawful recovery. Keep every defensible charge, concede only on new evidence, and negotiate only after the tenant disputes. "Defensible" means a legal basis, the evidence the law requires (photos, invoice or hours and rate), an amount no more than restores move-in condition after ordinary wear, and timely delivery. Flat fees are out. Each kept charge shows its exposure for the operator's information. Three refinements apply:
  - one company-wide charge schedule (useful lives, rates, holdover, chargeable lease fees), set once and applied to every unit;
  - net recovery, not gross, governs whether to pursue, hand off or write off a balance beyond the deposit;
  - once a dispute arises, a settlement weighs the likely small-claims result, cost and statutory exposure.
- Acceptances: the operator sets the charge schedule once as a standing instruction. For each move-out the operator reviews the pre-move-out list of problems quickly, because it is handed over at the inspection. The operator then accepts the account before it is sent. Increases, corrections, settlement offers and balance decisions come back for acceptance. A final invoice at or below the estimate completes under the same acceptance.
- Turn and account: each problem in the move-out record links to the work that fixes it and to a responsibility finding (tenant damage, ordinary wear, or present at move-in). The charge is the reasonable cost times the tenant's share under the schedule. Owner upgrades never reach the tenant. The account goes out by day 21, using an estimate where an invoice is missing, and is completed within 14 days of the invoice. Handoff schedules evidence to land in time, never delays the unit, and never picks a costlier repair because the tenant pays.
- Turn labor: mixed. On-site maintenance does paint, cleaning and minor repairs as work orders with hours and rate; vendors do carpet and larger work.
- Tenant side: a thin page reached by a secure link, sent in Handoff's emails under the operator's name. Email stays the channel of record. The page shows status and dates and the account line by line with evidence. It collects the refund method and forwarding address in writing, lets the tenant question a line or pay a balance, and confirms closure. There are no tenant accounts.
- Departing tenant in the demonstration: a simulated tenant with a hidden brief acts through the same emails and page a real tenant would use. Connor takes the tenant's role live at one staged moment.
- Disputes: Handoff defends charges on its own with each line's evidence and rule. It brings a decision to the operator only when new evidence weakens a charge (a correction) or the stakes rise (hold or settle, with the economics). Legal duties such as documentation requested within 14 days are carried out without a decision. There is no pre-set settlement range at first. Dispute outcomes by charge type can later inform an explicit standing instruction.
- Outside systems: the property-management ledger stays the books of record. Handoff's account is the working account; it posts accepted results to the ledger and reconciles what the ledger shows. The demonstration imitates each outside system as a separate system behind an interface shaped like the real one: ledger, payments, utility biller, in-house maintenance, and a collector for the balance-owed variation. Each has its own records and a hidden scenario on the business clock, and Handoff never sees the scenarios. The real systems replace them later at the same interfaces.
- Coverage: the main path includes a final utility bill that arrives late. The first variation is a balance owed beyond the deposit, handed to a collector, with corrections that must reach the collector. A returned refund and several adult tenants are engine tests, not demonstration moments.
- Agent platform (Owen, October 3): LangGraph is the agent layer. Start with the open-source library in Handoff's own service, with its state in Handoff's Postgres; the hosted Agent Server and LangSmith can be added later if needed. Each wake runs as a fresh step on the case's thread. Long waits for operator decisions and deadlines are stored as Handoff's own data rather than kept as runs paused inside the framework, so code can change between wakes. Every side effect that moves money or posts to a ledger carries an idempotency key. The evaluations behind this choice are in `research/platform/`.
- Language (Owen, October 3): TypeScript throughout. The LangGraph service, the operator app and the tenant page share one language and the same types. The legal research pipeline stays in Python and exports rule files that the service loads. Runtime calls to Jev go through a TypeScript client, or a small Python sidecar if one is needed.
- Judgment layer (Owen, October 3): semantic questions in the legal trees go through a provider-neutral judgment layer, which picks a provider per question by measurement. Each question names its inputs, decision policy and log. Questions over photos go to OpenAI's Decisions API, announced September 29, 2026 and multimodal but in limited preview, once Handoff has access. Text questions go where they measure best, which is Jev today. Each question's provider is chosen on labelled sets for accuracy, calibration and cost, with a frontier vision model as the baseline. The evidence analyst's neutral observations stay as the human-readable record.
- Unbilled final utilities (Owen, October 3): submetered water follows its own statute. The submeter is read within five days, or the previous month's amount is billed, and the charge is deducted with the final bill attached (Civ 1954.207). Any other utility not billed by day 21 stays off the statement. It is billed separately when it arrives and handled as a balance under the net-recovery rule.
- Account-core legal readings (Owen, October 3, on the A2 tree review in `research/legal-engine/jurisdictions/CA/account_core/trees/REVIEW.md`). These are working law for the trees and the agent, open to revision if new authority turns up:
  - Documents and photographs under 1950.5(h)(2) are a duty attached to each repair or cleaning deduction, not a condition of it. An honest miss leaves the charge as a provable claim, recoverable by setoff or a later claim (*Granberry*), not as money already kept. A bad-faith miss forfeits the deposit and risks up to twice the deposit in damages. Handoff never sends a repair or cleaning charge without its documents, and cures any gap the moment it is found. Each kept line shows whether it is fully proven, defensible but unperfected, or at risk.
  - Ordinary wear: a condition is chargeable only if both its cause (an accident, misuse or neglect) and its extent go beyond normal use, and it is not accumulated wear. Age counts only through the schedule's useful-life share.
  - Water: the five-day read runs from the day possession returns. A partial final month is prorated by days from the previous month's bill.
  - Several adult tenants without an all-tenant agreement get one check payable to all (C8 exposure on branch A). Whether a paper-lease clause is enough agreement to email the statement stays contested (C14); the tenant page collects a separate agreement.
  - The 1945 renewal presumption is rebuttable. Handoff never accepts a payment as rent for days after the lease ends while the tenant is still in possession; those days are holdover.
  - The conservative smaller readings stand: vendor and machine carpet cleaning are professional cleaning; restoration lines count toward the $125 threshold; tenancies begun before 2003 are outside the cleaning tree; a lease debt's existence rests on the lease.
  - Bad faith is a question of intent. Honest mistakes and carelessness are not bad faith; a pattern is, such as routinely omitting documents, charging flat fees, or keeping money after learning a charge was wrong. Handoff's record of what it knew and when is the operator's evidence of good faith.
- Holdover (Owen, October 3): the demonstration lease has a holdover clause making days after the lease end rent at the contract daily rate, with no premium. The company charge schedule sets the daily rate as monthly rent divided by the days in that month. The six days are $547.00, deducted from the deposit as unpaid rent under 1950.5(b)(1), which keeps them out of C6.
