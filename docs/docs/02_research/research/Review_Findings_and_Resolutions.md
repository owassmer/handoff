# Review findings and resolutions

Reviewed against the 15 September 2026 research cutoff. This is a record of substantive challenges and resulting revisions, not a customer study or a transcript of private reasoning.

| Challenge raised | Change made | Question still requiring field evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Selecting only vacant-home examples in week one would assume the proposed winner. | The first week now compares turns, occupied maintenance, takeovers and monthly financial operations. It includes a competent operator with few apparent coordination problems and permits specialist opportunities when access warrants them. | Which recurring loss is both material and within a new provider's control? |
| Faster readiness may produce no additional collected rent. | The main playbook and research show the zero-revenue downside and the break-even number of additional collected days. The Field Guide distinguishes readiness, availability, lease start and actual collection. | Did the service change the path to paid occupancy, and what would have happened otherwise? |
| Draft narrative and workbook used different illustrative prices and delivery effort. | All final turn examples use the same baseline: $100 per turn, no monthly base charge, 35 provider minutes and $2 communications/model cost per turn, plus monthly support and amortized setup. | What price will the buyer pay, and what does actual delivery cost? |
| Buyer value was once mislabeled as provider contribution in an automation formula. | Provider contribution is service revenue less delivery and remediation costs. Buyer surplus is captured improvement less price and remaining customer cost. | Who receives each benefit and who pays each expense? |
| Accountability without operational authority could become an empty promise. | The playbook distinguishes controllable commitments from owner, trade, access and leasing dependencies. Pilot scope requires practical scheduling, escalation and approved spending rights. | Is enough avoidable delay inside the service's actual authority? |
| Competition was treated more harshly in alternative lanes than in turns. | Every finalist is assessed against an incumbent or service alternative, payer capture, delivery cost and route to the next customer. Vendoroo is explicitly recognized as a close managed-maintenance comparator. | Can this service outperform buying, configuring or properly using an existing option? |
| Broad coverage could be mistaken for equally deep evidence everywhere. | The documents explicitly distinguish a broad operating model from residential-led quantitative analysis. | Does commercial, association or another specialist work offer better access and economics? |
| A founder-fit judgment could prematurely dismiss finance operations. | Suitability is conditional on specialist capability, permissions and a repeatable sponsor; it is not treated as an established personal limitation. | Which work can the founder reach, understand and deliver reliably? |
| Proprietary learning was presented as a possible moat before enough observations existed. | It remains a conditional future advantage. Immediate tests concern reliability, total effort, setup burden and payment. | Do repeated cases actually improve decisions or costs across customers? |

## Final arithmetic reconciliation

For 1,000 units, 35% annual turnover and all turns eligible, the illustration has 29.1667 service events per month. With three additional collected rent days per event at $2,000 monthly rent, a 30-day convention and $22.50 of realized manager labor value per event:

- Integrated buyer benefit before price: **$6,489.58/month**.
- Service price: **$2,916.67/month**.
- Integrated buyer net value: **$3,572.92/month**.
- Fee-manager net value at an 8% marginal management fee, without ownership economics: **−$1,793.75/month**.
- Provider contribution after modeled customer delivery costs: **$1,779.51/month**, before sales, central engineering, general overhead and taxes.
- Integrated buyer net value with no additional collected rent: **−$2,260.42/month**.
- Integrated buyer break-even: **1.1625 additional collected rent days per turn**, given all other assumptions.

These are consistent scenario calculations, not evidence of achievable rent recovery or willingness to pay. The neutral recurring-maintenance example in the economic appendix represents a different event population and is separately labeled.

## Recommendation after challenge

Vacant-unit readiness remains a concrete lead experiment. Public evidence supports its plausibility and a fair comparison method; it does not establish that it is the best available company. The first paid responsibility, buyer and eventual business form should follow observed losses, obtainable authority, delivery economics and a buyer's commitment.
