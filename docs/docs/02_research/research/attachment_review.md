## The McKinsey exhibits change the interpretation

Page 3 allocates modeled US construction-and-real-estate work hours to people (46%), agents (41%) and robots (13%) under a late scenario of expert estimates. These are technical-potential estimates over combined sectors, not observed deployment, employment loss or a property-manager time study. Page 8 places property managers in a people-centric occupational group; the group's percentages cannot be assigned to property managers individually. Neither figure supports pricing, staffing reductions or an addressable-market estimate for Handoff.

Page 4 explicitly allows some judgment work to become controlled, repeatable work as capability and governance improve. The paper is therefore less categorical than the claim that AI can do steps but cannot do thoughts. Its approval-click example still needs independent outcome checks: frequent approval can reflect rubber-stamping as well as reliability.

The sensor-and-smart-lock opening depends on installed hardware, correct location, available staff, lawful entry, reliable sensing and safe actuation. It is an illustrative future workflow. Useful decisions can be supported without sensors; the proposed business should not silently inherit a hardware installation project.

The maintenance time-saving (>30%), renewal (3-7%) and homebuilder response-time (>90%) statements on pages 7 and 9 describe consulting experience without enough methodology to estimate expected effect. The renewal statement does not establish whether the change is relative percent or percentage points. Keep these as hypotheses to test, not forecast inputs.

## Specific corrections to the strategy review

| Claim in the review | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The CBS experience is representative | No representative sample is supplied | Treat it as a useful case, not market incidence |
| 3,200+ professionals in Buildium research | The research program covers managers, owners and renters; the combined count is not the denominator for every statistic | Do not compute confidence intervals or extrapolate firm counts from that number |
| Only 8% automated means the remaining work is structurally unautomatable | Adoption measurement does not identify capability, economics, permission or task volume | Reopen both decision automation and process redesign |
| AppFolio Plus includes a generally available read/write API | Current public pricing describes the public API as read-only; partner integrations have separate access | Test each required read and write operation before promising execution |
| EliseAI has a universal $25,000 minimum | No vendor-confirmed universal minimum established | Exclude from competitive pricing and market sizing |
| A deposit case is worth $40-75 because penalties are high | Penalty exposure is not willingness to pay or expected loss | Price against measured workload and avoidable harm, then test actual purchase |
| Routine verification will take 2-5 minutes and deliver 55-65% margins | No timed operating sample supplied | Use editable assumptions and count all review, support, exceptions and rework |
| One case per unit annually | Neither household changes nor deposit events occur once per unit by definition | Derive event frequency from the relevant lifecycle; allow missing/unknown frequency |
| Operating under a manager's license is sufficient | Contractual delegation does not itself satisfy licensing or employee-specific exemptions | Determine the actual legal role for each action and jurisdiction |
| Virginia contractor involvement creates an extra 15 days | Section 55.1-1226(E) also requires damages exceeding the deposit and timely written notice | Do not encode a generic contractor extension |
| Colon v. Martin supports New York deposit remedies | The cited 35 N.Y.3d 75 case concerns municipal pre-action examinations under General Municipal Law section 50-h | Remove the case citation; refer to applicable deposit legislation directly |
| Owning a management firm is the natural next step | Outside the user's established partnership scope and unnecessary to test the company | Compare owner-operators as customers; do not prescribe buying their businesses |
| First $10k/$100k at stated dates is realistic | No pipeline, buyer commitments or procurement evidence supplied | Replace revenue predictions with concrete activities and observed gates |

## What past projects contribute

The useful transfer from CPA is to put quantitative comparisons and repeatable calculations behind consequential choices, and evaluate them against historical cases without pretending hindsight was available. The useful transfer from CORDON is to distinguish a requirement, the facts that make it applicable, and whether those facts are actually established. The useful transfer from the Palantir work is to connect a choice to the action that changes operations. Integration experience contributes practical awareness of identities, permissions, stale records and write failures.

Those lessons do not require a generalized ontology, a legal compiler, a new operating system, a novel name for ordinary records, or a document generated for every completed task. The operator should see a clear recommendation, the information needed to trust it, and the next action. A receipt, work order, invoice or brief activity history is enough when it answers the actual question.

No detailed source material on the SBA lending project was supplied or recovered, so no specific technical claims about it are used. The user's warning about recurring jargon is itself sufficient reason to keep the design plain.
