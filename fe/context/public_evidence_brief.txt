 What the evidence shows
 Reuse the mechanism. Re-derive the legal answer for the property, tenancy, agreement and event date.

 1 / Cost allocation is a decision
 The FTC complaint describes turn costs entering a resident ledger before a second review, with review allegedly
 skipped in some regions. The entered order addresses the named defendant's charging practices. Demo
 implication: owner work, proposed tenant charges and approved posting must remain distinct. [S01] [S02]

 2 / A case can fail between systems
 Pioneer's blank form explicitly distinguishes condition reporting from a maintenance request. Henderson separately
 instructs residents to notify Accounting to stop auto-drafts. Demo implication: one recorded event must not silently
 stand for another. [S29] [S25]

 3 / Invoices and statements evolve
 Silver v. Rudeen recounts a preliminary estimate followed by later invoices and a revised balance. Goodeill
 examines whether the manager diligently pursued the accounting. Demo implication: distinguish waiting, follow-up,
 interim work and final completion. [S04] [S05]

 4 / A good system preserves valid deductions
 In Pulley v. Milberger, the appellate court affirmed retention of a $3,700 deposit and an additional $2,000 landlord
 award on the supporting record. Evaluation implication: correct charges must succeed; automatic refunds or
 universal escalation are not success. [S06]

 5 / Geography changes the output
 NC has conditional interim/final accounting. Washington's current rule differs from the historical 14-day context of
 the cases above. Colorado's 2026 changes address returned refunds; California's current text addresses photos
 and multi-adult refund recipients. Implementation implication: same case structure, jurisdiction-specific decisions.
 [S09] [S10] [S11] [S13]
 Two adjacent examples are retained for evaluation, not initial market expansion: the NCREC McMillan disciplinary account tests custody
 and authority; Pennsylvania's Home365 announcement tests actual completion despite software adoption. [S07] [S08]
 Evidence status matters: allegations are not findings; consent terms are not nationwide law; Silver decided dismissal/limitations, not every
 underlying charge. Case notes and source locators are provided in the notebook.

 Bring a worked example
 These are constructed demonstrations informed by public evidence—not actual prospect cases or completed agent
 tests.

 D01 / Green: allocate before charging
 Assume a $2,000 deposit, a supported $250 tenant-caused repair and $800 of ordinary owner repainting. Show the
 proposed $250 deduction / $1,750 refund, owner approval and separate accounting actions. Never route the full
 turn invoice into the resident ledger. [S18] [S14]

 D02 / Red Door or Henderson: act before the final invoice
 In a qualifying NC case at day 28, the extent of the repair claim is still unknown. Show the interim accounting due
 within 30 days, the assigned missing information, and the final-accounting requirement within 60. Do not invent a
 charge or use 60 as the ordinary deadline. [S09]

 D03 / Pioneer: connect conditions and work
 A move-in form records a cabinet problem; no service record is supplied; the move-out quote includes cabinet work.
 Show what was already documented, what may be new, and the specific question needed for allocation. Do not
 fabricate a maintenance request or repaired condition. [S29]
