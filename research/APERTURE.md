## 1. Purpose (intent §2, agreed)

Handoff takes responsibility for a move-out and turnover from the moment an ending tenancy is known. It has two
outcomes, and they progress independently:
1. The unit is ready for its next occupant.
2. The departing tenancy's financial relationship is resolved: settled, refunded, recovered, or closed by an
   accepted disposition.

Handoff is not deposit closeout, not leasing or marketing, and not a replacement PMS. The account covers rent,
utilities, damage, credits and deposits, not the deposit alone (blueprint §4).

## 2. Customer and the value they get

Who: third-party managers of scattered single-family and small multifamily rentals, dependent on vendors and email,
with no on-site staff. Corroborated by the internal record and four external lanes (ADJUDICATION.md, row 4;
public evidence brief p.1). An owner-funded budget matters, because a fee manager captures little of the rent gain
(Research_Synthesis §7).

What they get:
- One accountable party that finishes the job. Their software is installed but incomplete, and the leftover work
  sits at the boundaries between systems, parties and authority (strategy review, findings 1 and 3).
- A settlement that is correct under the unit's own law on its own dates, and on time. In New York a missed 14-day
  statement forfeits the right to keep anything; Virginia allows 45 days.
- Each charge backed by evidence the turn already produced: quotes, invoices, photos, the move-in record.
- Cost kept separate from responsibility. A repainting invoice is a cost, not proof the tenant is liable (intent §3;
  evidence brief lesson 1). Correct deductions are kept, too (evidence brief lesson 4).
- Relief measured per move-out in minutes, days and dollars. The target is 30% less recurring handling at
  comparable quality (0-to-1 plan §05).

For owners and departing tenants, the value is an account that is explainable and fair, with fewer disputes.

## 3. Value for us

- Shape: an accountable service first, converting to software as automation is proven. In the simulation, P9 and P4
  lead on revenue and funding (a paying customer within 12 months: 88-89%; seed: 51-52%). The simulation is
  calibrated on generic base rates, so these are rankings, not forecasts (sim/REPORT.md).
- An outcome is priced higher than a tool ($250-450 per turn against $100-250). It is also harder to bundle away
  (8-22% commoditization risk a year against 15-35%).
- The legal engine is what makes that responsibility safe to take. It is also what turns the service into software,
  because judgment-heavy work becomes automatable only once its rules are compiled and its standards broken down
  (legal-engine/SYNTHESIS.md §3a). Each rule change is recurring value (§3b).
- Founder fit: Owen has already built and had accepted a staged legal compilation (CORDON) and a typed judgment layer
  (Jev).

## 4. Direction (adjudicated 2026-09-28; order, not yet a recorded Owen decision)

1. Gate: one paying operator and measured numbers.
2. Depth for the same buyer: move-in baseline, pre-move-out inspection, the turn, a settlement grounded in the
   turn's evidence, and a licensed hand-off for balances owed. Lead with settlement in strict-deadline states.
3. A second operator in the same segment, changing one dimension.
4. Owner-funded projects (capex, casualty) on the same engine.
Not now: occupied maintenance, leasing, screening, owned collections, owned payment rails, or a horizontal legal
engine.

## 5. Where the legal work sits

- Stage A is the "maintained, dated source material" that the intent (§3) and blueprint (§4, §7) require for
  outcome 2. It serves blueprint C.3-C.5 and grounds the agent. It is not a product by itself and not a general
  legal language (blueprint §7; adjudication addendum §7).
- Division of labor:
  - Code decides what the law fixes: clocks, amounts, forfeiture.
  - The agent reasons about standards and evidence.
  - The operator accepts or changes the account (intent §5).
- The legal meaning is resolved in full, with nothing left open (Owen, 2026-09-28). An outcome that depends on facts
  is stated as a condition. Applying a standard to one case is the agent's reasoning, shown when it affects a
  decision (intent §7).
- Aperture rule: a provision enters when it changes the manager's decision in the settlement chain for a unit in a
  chosen jurisdiction. Jurisdictions follow the first prospect's footprint: every state Holland Partner Group
  operates in (California, Washington, Colorado, Oregon, Arizona), each with its county and city layers, plus federal
  overlays (Owen, 2026-09-30; see "First contact"). Each is built through the jurisdiction-pipeline skill.
  Superseded: the 2026-09-28 set (New York State with the NYC layer, Virginia, federal; California out). New York,
  NYC and Virginia stay compiled and are parked.
- Segment fit (NYC, parked): in NYC, most units in the research segment are market-rate and governed by GOL 7-108. Rent
  stabilization reaches buildings of six or more units (RSL 26-504) and units under tax-benefit programs, which
  larger managers mostly run. Both regimes are compiled. Market-rate leads review and the first test cases;
  rent-stabilized and rent-controlled units follow (Owen, 2026-09-28, (a)).
- Gate before any build into the account: the three-way test runs after Owen accepts Stage A. It compares the
  existing process, a capable model with the same records, and that model with the compiled law, on reconstructed
  cases (adjudication addendum §6; 0-to-1 plan §05). If the model alone does as well at acceptable cost, simplify.

## 6. What would change this

- Walkthroughs: 5-8 scattered-site managers in New York and Virginia, each reconstructing their last 10 move-outs.
  Whichever half they pay for decides the lead in step 2 (ADJUDICATION.md, "cheapest test").
- If the first operator funds maintenance and not settlement, the case for maintenance comes back.
- Founder facts: capital and full-time status. In the simulation, part-time work until the first round roughly
  halves every outcome.
