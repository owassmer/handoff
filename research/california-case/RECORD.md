## (b) Case procedural status

*Brooks v. Greystar Real Estate Partners, LLC*, S.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-01729-LL-VET. The docket was last updated October 2, 2026. Seigel remains a named plaintiff. There is no class certification, settlement or judgment.

- **Sep. 18, 2023:** Filed. The complaint named Greystar and several hundred owner entities. No Breakwater owner was named in any version of the complaint. The owner entities were later dismissed (ECF 30, 48). — official record
- **Jul. 19, 2024:** The court compelled McGill to arbitration (ECF 44). On Mar. 31, 2025 it denied certification of that ruling for interlocutory appeal (ECF 55).
- **Sep. 26, 2024:** Ruling on the First Amended Complaint ([ECF 48](sources/brooks-greystar-ecf048-order-fac.txt)). The §1950.5 and UCL claims survive. A "flat cleaning fee" is a set fee charged "without regard to the condition of the unit", and that theory survives. Owners are needed only for relief that would extinguish their balances. The Civ. Code §3345 penalties were struck. — court finding
- **Aug. 7, 2025:** Ruling on the Second Amended Complaint (ECF 56). Owners are not necessary parties, and deposits are "owned by tenants … and held by landlords". The utility-charge "Unauthorized Deduction Class" was struck without leave, because §1950.5(b) permits deposit use "for any purpose". — court finding
- **Aug. 15, 2025:** TAC filed (ECF 58); it is the operative pleading. **Sep. 11, 2025:** Answer filed (ECF 63).
- **Nov. 18, 2025:** Early neutral evaluation; the case did not settle (ECF 66).
- **Dec. 17, 2025:** The Ninth Circuit denied the Rule 23(f) petition, No. 25-5332 ([ECF 68](sources/brooks-greystar-ecf068-ninth-cir-order.txt)).
- **February–September 2026:** Discovery. A protective order was entered (ECF 74), and plaintiffs were ordered to produce deposit documents (ECF 75). A sampling protocol for tenant files led to further disputes (ECF 86, 111–114).
- **Mar. 25, 2026:** Amended schedule ([ECF 83](sources/brooks-greystar-ecf083-scheduling-order.txt)). Class-certification discovery closes Oct. 5, 2026, and the class-certification motion is due **Nov. 20, 2026**. On Sep. 14, 2026, ECF 115 "Setting Briefing Schedule and Vacating Case Deadlines" was entered; its text was not retrieved and may change these dates.
- **Pending motions (PACER only, contents not retrieved):**
  - ECF 90: plaintiffs' motion for leave to file a Fourth Amended Complaint (Jul. 7, 2026; fully briefed).
  - ECF 91: Greystar California's motion for partial summary judgment (Jul. 27, 2026), with exhibits A–K and a statement of facts. It was opposed in ECF 102/107 and replied to in ECF 110.
  - ECF 119: motion to compel (Oct. 2, 2026), filed with sealed lodgings.

## (c) Property, ownership and financing

- **Owner.** CMFA Special Finance Agency VII. It is a joint powers authority formed July 1, 2021 by the California Municipal Finance Authority (CMFA) and the City of Huntington Beach, and a "public entity" (Gov. Code §6507).
  - The deed transfer from Breakwater Apartment Homes, LLC is dated **Aug. 19, 2021** (APN 142-181-05). — official record ([DIR 2023-008](sources/dir-public-works-2023-008-breakwater.txt), citing the county recorder; [FY2024 audited statements](sources/cmfa-sfa7-financial-statements-fy2024.txt), Note 1)

- **Bonds, Aug. 19, 2021.** Trust Indenture dated Aug. 1, 2021; trustee Wilmington Trust. — official record
  - Senior 2021A-1: $138.75M (CUSIP 12574UAA2, due 2056)
  - Junior 2021A-2: $78.135M (12574UAC8, due 2047)
  - Subordinate 2021B: $5M (12574UAB0), issued to Catalyst

## (d) Restrictions, tenant-facing obligations and consequences of ownership

**Restrictions** — official record ([staff report](sources/cmfa-2021-07-23-staff-reports.txt); [FY2022](sources/emma-breakwater-annual-report-fy2022.txt) and [FY2023](sources/emma-breakwater-annual-report-fy2023.txt) annual reports):
- A Regulatory Agreement exists, but its text was not retrieved. Per the staff report, it sets at least 33% of units at ≤80% AMI, 33% at ≤100% AMI and the rest at ≤120% AMI. Rents are capped at 30% of the tier's income; the general JPA summaries say 35%. The restrictions last as long as bonds are outstanding.
- Existing tenants were not displaced. Over-income tenants may stay "indefinitely but at market rents", which implies the tiers apply on new leasing.
- Missing the tiers is a default under the Regulatory Agreement; its only remedy is specific performance. It can lead to termination of Catalyst's agreement and, at the Agency's discretion, replacement of the property manager.
- 2021 limits: for a three-bedroom at 80% AMI, the maximum rent was $2,690 and the planned Agency rent $2,625.
- Program units at 12/31/2022: 113 / 47 / 32 at 80 / 100 / 120% AMI, with 208 "other" (vacant, unknown or unqualified). At 12/31/2023: 115 / 46 / 32 / 207, labelled "(TCAC)", i.e. measured against the state tax credit committee's income limits.
- Not found in any retrieved source: tenant-facing program rules on income certification and recertification, rent increases, notices, or deposits.
- Investor-facing duty: each quarterly report must include the rent roll, concessions, a security-deposit audit and advance rents. These schedules record every deposit's disposition. The Q1-2023 and Q2-2023 reports would show Seigel's, but they were not indexed (see (f)).

**Questions for the legal agent** (flagged here, not resolved):
1. **Public-entity landlord.** The landlord is a JPA public entity (DIR; audited statements). Open questions:
   - Whether the Government Claims Act applies to a deposit claim against the Agency (Gov. Code §§905, 911.2, 945.4).
   - Whether Gov. Code §818 limits §1950.5(l) bad-faith damages.
   - How small-claims procedure works against a public entity.
   - Whether the manager is separately liable as agent. ECF 56 cites *Leaser v. Prime Ascot* on manager restitution liability.
2. **Prevailing wage.** DIR (Feb. 26, 2025) held the Breakwater exterior repaint ($1,031,200 contract) a public work. Its reasoning: property revenue and bond proceeds alike are the Agency's public funds. Open questions:
   - Whether contracted turn work (the closet rebuild, painting, carpet, cleaning) is a public work or maintenance under Lab. Code §§1720(a)(1) and 1771, with the $1,000 threshold, contractor registration and project registration.
   - How that affects the "reasonable" cost that may be charged to a tenant.
3. **Rent-cap and just-cause exemptions.** Do the restricted-housing exemptions (Civ. Code §1947.12(d)(1), §1946.2(e)(9)) apply unit by unit? Unit 308 was in the 80% tier; other units are "unqualified".
4. **AB 1850 (2022).** Its minimum standards for JPA middle-income acquisitions may not reach a 2021 acquisition. This is unverified.
5. **Other public-entity rules.** Unclaimed refunds (the Dec. 2024 rent roll carries an "Unclaimed Property" liability of $18,902), public-records access, and collection of balances owed to a public owner.

## (e) Practice allegations (Greystar California)

- **Allegations** (TAC ¶¶470–486). Greystar denies all of them (Answer ¶¶471–486).
  - Centralized deposit policy, with "standardized charges" used instead of actual cost.
  - Charges for normal wear and tear, and for work that was not done.
  - A "Summary of Move Out Charges" that gives no hours, rates or vendor invoices.
  - Standard "cleaning fees" charged regardless of the unit's condition.
  - Late refunds or none; bills and collection notices sent once the deposit runs out.
- **Other plaintiffs' examples** (allegations):
  - $235 and $240 "cleaning fee"
  - $20 "Final Utility Admin Fee & Move Out Fee"
  - work-order lines such as "Damages – Sub-task – 6057172 – Floor – Carpet – Replace" $215.58, "Walls Paint – Full" $174.17, "Cleaning Based on Full Unit – Full" $120
  - "Paint @ $39/hr 5 hours LABOR" $195
- **Admissions** (Answer ¶¶32, 35, 36, 470).
  - Greystar California acts as agent for third-party owners.
  - It oversees deposit administration.
  - It handles leasing, rent, repairs, move-outs and holding deposits "pursuant to … the respective property management agreements".
  - It denies that handling is uniform across properties. Greystar Real Estate Partners neither owns nor manages any property.
- **Greystar's stated practice** (allegation; affirmative defenses 1, 5, 8, 26, 33):
  - Itemized statement and refund within 21 days.
  - Hours and rate shown for in-house work; vendor invoices supplied.
  - Good-faith estimates, with invoices sent later.
  - Statements mailed to the forwarding address.
  - Some residents received an "initial move-out package containing purchase orders only".
  - Charges are "generally paid to third parties".
  - Balances belong to the owners.
