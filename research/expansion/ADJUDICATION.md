Test of the adjudicator's prior
- Prior (this morning): "same workflow with a second operator, then occupied maintenance."
- First half holds (unanimous sequencing rule). Second half fails: no external lane supports it, and the internal
  support is one inherited sentence. Withdrawn.

Adjudicated answer
1. Gate: expansion waits for one paying operator and measured numbers (minutes, days, dollars recovered per move-out).
2. First expansion is depth, not breadth: own the tenancy boundary for the same buyer. Move-in baseline, pre-move-out
   inspection, the turn, settlement grounded in the turn's quotes, invoices and photos, and a licensed hand-off for
   balances owed. Lead the story with settlement in strict-deadline states (NY, VA, CA, CO); the turn is its evidence.
3. Customer axis: second operator in the same segment, changing one dimension.
4. Breadth: owner-funded episodic projects (capex, casualty) for the same buyer, on the same engine.
5. Not now: occupied routine maintenance, leasing, screening, owned collections and payment rails, new verticals.

Strongest case against
- Settlement is the most contested piece in 2026 (Obligo inside every major PMS; TurnOps at $19/report; My AI Front Desk
  claims notice-to-ledger). Per-event dollars are small, recovery touches debt-collection law, and managers may not pay
  for transparency that exposes their markups. Maintenance, which this answer drops, is where event volume is: if the
  first operator funds maintenance and not settlement, the internal case reasserts.

Cheapest test that separates the options
- 5-8 operator walkthroughs (NY and Virginia, scattered-site managers) reconstructing their last 10 move-outs each:
  time and dollars in the turn vs the settlement, who approved what, what they would pay per close-out, and whether
  owner-funded projects run through the same people. The answer to "which half do they pay for" decides step 2's lead.

What the lanes could not answer (founder facts)
- Capital, full-time status, operating experience, licensure and goals. These decide company shape (service, software,
  engine, operator) and whether an exit is the target. No lane found them in any artifact.
