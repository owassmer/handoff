Handoff expansion research: adjudication protocol
Written 2026-09-28, before any lane reported. Not shown to any lane.

Question (Owen): once the manager-side move-out property work and the tenant-account settlement are
built, how should the company expand? Framing: first principles, founder-centric, wide aperture.

Why the lanes are separated
- L0 (internal) reads our own artifacts: Handoff Docs repo, the Handoff Context media set and the two
  notepads. It reports what we already believe, why, and how well it is evidenced. It does no web research.
- L1-L5 (external) research the market from a shared neutral brief. They never see our prior hypotheses,
  rankings, economics or recommendations, so they cannot inherit them.
- Our artifacts already carry conclusions (a lead lane, a candidate ranking, economics, kill criteria).
  A lane that reads them first anchors on them and tends to confirm them. Separation makes agreement
  informative: when a lane that never saw our reasoning reaches the same place, that is corroboration,
  not an echo. Disagreement is also informative, in two different ways (below).

Disclosure of the adjudicator's prior
- Earlier today Ferro recommended "same workflow with a second operator, then occupied maintenance."
  That recommendation drew on the internal artifacts. The adjudication tests it; it does not get a pass
  because it came first.

Method
1. Normalize after the fact. Map each lane's options into one taxonomy built from what the lanes produced,
   not from the internal research's candidate list.
2. Convergence matrix. For each option, record which lanes reached it and on what evidence.
   - External lanes converge with each other, independently: market signal.
   - Internal and external agree: corroborated; weight rises.
   - Internal only: either private knowledge (built capability, founder facts, partner access the web
     cannot see) or anchoring (inherited, thinly evidenced, stale). Classify each case by checking the
     internal evidence behind it.
   - External only: a blind spot in our prior reasoning, or a lane without the founder/product facts
     that rule it out. Check it against the founder facts and the built capability.
3. Evidence weighting. Measured over asserted; primary over secondary; 2024-2026 over older; operator
   behavior (what they pay for, churn from, refuse) over vendor marketing and analyst claims. Sentiment
   stays labeled as sentiment. A number without a source does not enter the adjudication.
4. Founder-fit filter. Apply the documented founder facts only; name every conclusion that depends on an
   unknown founder fact (capital, full-time status, network, prior operating roles).
5. Build-reuse check. Score each option against what the built system actually reuses (L0 inventory),
   not against the idea of reuse.
6. Falsification. For the top options, state the cheapest test with real operators that tells them apart.
7. Output to Owen: the landscape, the convergence matrix, a recommendation with its evidence and the
   strongest case against it, and one multiple-choice question.

Rules
- No lane's rhetoric counts as evidence. A lane's confidence is an input, not a vote.
- Do not average. Explain each disagreement.
- Keep Owen's standing decisions (intent notepad: marketing and leasing separate; operator judgment at
  accept/change; minimality) as constraints on the recommendation, and flag any lane finding that
  challenges one of them rather than silently dropping it.
