## 2. The purpose and the complete job

The product is Handoff—not Deposit Closeout. Handoff takes responsibility for a move-out and property turnover. It starts when the business learns that a tenancy is ending, works out what is needed, obtains information, prepares recommendations, gets the necessary decisions, coordinates physical delivery and resolves the departing tenancy’s remaining financial relationship.
- Outcome one: the property is ready for its next occupant.
- Outcome two: the departing tenancy’s financial relationship is resolved.
- These outcomes progress independently. Financial duties may need action before repairs finish; a property can be ready while recovery or a dispute continues.
- Responsibility includes arranging inspections, engaging providers, coordinating repairs and following delivery through completion—not merely obtaining paperwork from someone else.
- Responsibility also includes questions, disputes, recovery, settlement or an accepted decision to stop pursuing a balance. Giving a matter to a collector or professional adviser does not, by itself, finish Handoff’s job.
- Marketing and leasing remain separate. Handoff is not automatically a replacement for every function of a property-management business or every system that business uses.
The desired value is useful work completed with less remaining human coordination and better decisions—not more generated text, more recorded provenance, a larger ontology, or a high approval rate. A technically completed demonstration does not by itself establish customer demand, legal sufficiency, commercial viability or measured economic benefit.
## 3. The central thesis: consistent logic and adaptable reasoning

The user emphasized combining consistent logic through the known world with adaptability to unexpected circumstances and variance. Legal informatics is central to that synthesis. It supplies shared concepts, relationships, obligations, standards and ways to express and interpret intentions. It is not merely a collection of prohibitions or a compliance checklist.
Real operating situations cannot be exhaustively enumerated in advance. Agreements are incomplete, evidence can conflict, standards require interpretation, and circumstances change. A coherent world model and reliable calculations should make reasoning consistent without forcing the agent into a fixed recipe for every case.
- Use explicit, tested logic for exact arithmetic, identity, balance constraints and genuinely determinate rules. A missing amount is not zero; requesting a payment is not proof that it settled.
- Use substantive agent reasoning for context, evidence, responsibility, reasonable treatment, competing interpretations and practical courses of action. The agent must do the thinking before it brings a recommendation—not merely label something ‘judgment required’.
- Rules and standards coexist. A standard can be decomposed and evaluated without pretending that it has one unique arithmetic answer.
- The agent must understand the purpose of an instruction as well as its literal wording. New evidence or an unfamiliar circumstance should lead to investigation or a reasoned adjustment, not automatically to a generic blocked state.
- Do not recreate the rejected model in which a fixed process handles ‘routine’ work and all unfamiliar situations are handed to a human. Adaptability is part of Handoff’s capability, not an exception to it.
- Ground applicable requirements in the actual agreement, relevant place and time, and maintained sources. Model memory and a user’s preference are not a maintained legal library.
- Private instructions and commercial goals exist alongside the rights and obligations of other parties. A useful recommendation is not simply the largest deduction or recovery that benefits one side.
A useful example: a repainting invoice supplies cost information. It does not establish resident liability. The agent must consider the agreement, condition history, ordinary wear, relevant requirements and evidence. It may then recommend a treatment or concession. A later payment is another event. These distinctions support judgment; they are not invitations to expose a separate technical form for each distinction.
## 4. The actual world must remain legible

The ontology must represent recognizable things and relationships in the operator’s world. Plain labels are necessary but insufficient: renaming a confused abstraction does not make it sound. Some technical records may be necessary internally, but they must not displace the business model or become the operator’s workflow.
- A property is not a tenancy. An occupant is not the whole contractual arrangement. A person’s departure does not necessarily end the tenancy.
- A job describes work undertaken. An invoice describes what a supplier requests. An obligation describes what a party owes or must do, for whom, on what basis and when.
- Genuine obligations are first-class business objects and connect intended outcomes with work and financial performance. Do not turn every program check, information request or software task into an obligation.
- Physical activity, money, authority and information are different flows. One can change without all the others changing at the same time.
- Source reports, factual observations, interpretations, recommendations, user decisions and performed results must remain distinguishable. An asserted claim is not automatically an agreed or adjudicated obligation.
- Separate cost from responsibility, a supported claim from a chosen concession, held money from money owed, commitments from available funds, and a submitted operation from its actual result.
- Independent business identities deserve independent records. Bounded owned details may stay with their parent. Avoid both a giant authoritative case JSON blob and an object for every field or sentence.
- Shared records are authoritative parts of the world, not decorative projections of a hidden engine snapshot. Operational history preserves accepted facts and decisions without copying all history into every update.
## 5. The agent–operator working agreement

The agent owns moving the Handoff to completion. It obtains information directly from relevant people and systems, asks focused questions, follows up, develops recommendations and carries accepted intent forward. The operator should not become its message relay or manually enact its internal steps.
- The agent prepares a complete recommended work plan and budget. The authorized operator accepts or changes it before repair commitments are made.
- The agent prepares the complete account recommendation appropriate to the decision needed. The operator accepts or changes it before that treatment is carried forward. ‘Complete’ does not require pretending that all eventual facts are already final or waiting past a real obligation’s deadline.
- The user explicitly located judgment in this acceptance/change. Do not add a separate routine-versus-exception review system around it.
- If new material evidence warrants a different account, the agent develops a revised recommendation with its reasons and returns for the appropriate judgment. It does not silently alter the agreed outcome.
- Accepting a work plan binds the intended result, scope, budget and any explicitly fixed requirements. Handoff manages remaining delivery choices. An explicit choice such as using a named provider is honored; ordinary scheduling or equivalent delivery adjustments need not become new approval requests.
- Recommend practical resolutions using what is owed, the evidence, recovery prospects, expense, delay and relevant instructions. The agent can recommend a justified compromise or write-off rather than make the operator invent the strategy. Later fine-tuning should improve this capability.
- When disagreement arises, explain the specific factual or interpretive issue, incorporate relevant context and offer a coherent recommendation. Do not use an ‘exception’ label instead of reasoning or treat the agent as an approver of the operator’s judgment.
## 6. People, permissions and organizational flexibility

The user’s point was not to make an accountant role optional. It was to avoid encoding a company’s staffing structure as a prerequisite for doing work. Handoff may perform the work that originally justified a human position. Requiring that position anyway would preserve the very handoff the product should remove.
- Model the work and authority to act, not a predetermined division of human labor. ‘The account needs reconciliation’ does not imply ‘an accountant must reconcile it.’
- People are users with configurable permissions. Broad User/Admin access presets are appropriate; they are not professions or an organization chart.
- One user can make several kinds of decisions. Several users can divide or share decisions without redesigning the underlying process.
- Do not replace a few rigid job roles with dozens of permission-based pseudo-jobs. Introduce a permission boundary for a concrete access or authority need.
- Authorized users normally share visibility within their company workspace, with configurable restrictions where needed. Assignments direct attention; they do not automatically hide other work.
- Real relationships still matter: who owns the property, who is party to an agreement, who is owed money and who undertook the work. Those are business facts, not application-user roles.
- Identity and permission are enforced on the server, independently of UI filtering. Preserve source protections and record the actual actor. The agent cannot fabricate a user’s acceptance or grant itself authority.
## 7. Interaction and language

The new operator surface is work-centered: active Handoffs, current progress, prepared recommendations and decisions. Contextual conversation supports that work. It is neither a transcript-only product nor a catalogue of backend Actions.
- Support both focused direct edits and conversation. Both update one coherent proposal. The operator should not have to repair dependent details manually.
- Display important reasons, consequences and relevant source material where they help a decision. Make original material accessible without putting identifiers, hashes, clocks and technical validation details in the normal view.
- Plain, real-operator-world language is a project-wide requirement: object and resource names, properties, descriptions, buttons, headings, errors, generated recommendations and new code/API/module names—not just screen copy.
- Excessive caveating and unessential over-provenance were identified as project-wide failures. One small Demo indicator establishes the environment. Do not repeat test, simulated, fixture, implementation-stage or non-effect disclaimers throughout ordinary work.
- Meaningful uncertainty remains part of the work. Avoid blanket qualifications; explain the actual unresolved issue when it affects a decision or next action.
- Implementation A–D describe how we build. They must never become product modes, record titles, operator stages or user-facing vocabulary.
- Do not reuse the old four-tab/27-command interface as a product specification. The earlier charge-review improvement established some clarity, not approval of that overall architecture.

## 8. Minimality and the five clarified engineering commitments

Minimality and essence-only are design constraints, not a later cleanup pass. Establish the necessary behavior, then use the smallest adequate mechanism. A real correctness requirement does not automatically justify a framework, service, object family or multi-stage workflow.
- 1. Compute computable consequences through checked logic without a compulsory AI round-trip. Broader interpretation still receives reasoning. Do not build a separate simple/complex edit classifier or competing proposal paths.
- 2. Bind acceptance to the content reviewed and preserve accepted/issued material. Retain unaccepted revisions only when there is a concrete use, such as a real communication or comparison. Do not archive every draft or create a redundant versioning subsystem by default.
- 3. Existing work and payments can be recognized in the same business records. A Handoff instruction reference is necessary when Handoff initiated the operation, not universally. Do not invent requests or retroactive approvals, a separate historical-work engine or a parallel ledger.
- 4. Responsiveness is an experience to measure. Acknowledge work promptly and show useful progress. Start with one shared durable coordinator; add a faster wake-up mechanism only if observed delay warrants it—not a second interactive agent or another service in advance.
- 5. Prove the authorization, stale-result and duplicate-effect properties actually needed. Match recovery to the business consequence. An unanswered information request need not inherit a bank payment’s uncertainty procedure; unaffected work should continue. A busy flag is not a distributed guarantee, but neither does that justify a universal execution framework.
## 9. Learning, continuity and changes in the world

A business-judgment change is case-specific unless explicitly given continuing scope. Previous cases may inform new recommendations by analogy, but repeated concessions do not silently become company rules. Explicit standing instructions have an issuing authority, scope and relevant effective context. Correcting a genuinely shared fact, such as an address, is a different operation.
- Keep the case-fact change loop distinct from changes in applicable rules, agreements and guidance. The agent must adapt to both.
- Changing or withdrawing a plan does not erase work already commissioned or a payment possibly sent. Preserve the actual remaining commitments and resolve them.
- Preserve useful operational history: accepted facts, decisions, versions that matter, commitments, attempts and results. This is different from maintaining an obsolete implementation or a comparison archive.
- Model output, passing tests, a published tag and an operator’s approval are different kinds of evidence. Do not use one as a substitute for the others.
- The first model must genuinely reason and use tools. Fine-tuning comes later; it should not be used as a reason to postpone agency until after a large rigid backend has been built.
## 10. Implementation direction already chosen

- Replace the deposit-centric domain/ontology, application contracts and product surface. There is no backward-compatibility requirement for the current demo.
- Reuse independently useful calculation, transaction/recovery and authenticated-connection mechanisms in the existing TypeScript v2 backend. It remains the sole business backend. Do not recreate a deleted backend, a second rules engine or an implementation-history archive.
- The first milestone is one complete working foundation with a real coordinating agent, meaningful case information, operator judgment and test implementations of external interfaces. It is not another backend-only project or a scripted agent demonstration.
- Handoff owns the operational record for its job. Vendor-independent adapters obtain information and post results to other systems. Neither a particular PMS’s schema nor a full PMS replacement is assumed.
- Begin with one coordinating agent per Handoff, using specialized tools and bounded helpers where useful. Do not start with a digital departmental hierarchy of independent agents.
- Email and document exchange are the first channels; the model remains capable of supporting messaging and phone later.
- Retire obsolete objects, Actions, screens and test records after replacement verification. Preserve original sources, useful mechanisms, accepted business history and normal version control; do not maintain a parallel legacy product.
- A scoped Main promotion can be appropriate when required and approved. Main code/schema publication, Main case data and live external activation are separate decisions. Do not invent a blanket ban on Main promotion or silently combine these decisions.
## 11. What the demonstration is—and is not

The user supplied a completed real-case framework, some of it reconstructed or derived, to give Handoff meaningful material to operate on. It is not the basis of the product design, and neither is the demonstration. Preserve the known context faithfully; supplement public-source acquisition gaps coherently rather than turn them into artificial missing-information blockers.
- Separate source-acquisition gaps from situations where the business really lacks information. The latter can be useful operating situations for Handoff to investigate; the former need not dominate the demonstration.
- Keep source originals intact. Record preparation choices internally. Do not relabel archival imagery as different actual evidence or claim a reconstructed event is verified history.
- Historical facts, dates and applicable rules must remain coherent. Judicial material is relevant when it applies in the scenario; it is not a predetermined answer to supply to every earlier reasoning turn.
- Do not add production logic keyed to one case, person, company, document ID, charge label or expected outcome. Demonstrate variation through the same model and tools.
- A realistic scenario does not replace evaluations of changed facts, alternative agreements, different outcomes, recovery or permissions.

## 13. Working with the user and maintaining alignment

- Do the reasoning work. When a consequential choice is genuinely unresolved, explain the alternatives and a recommendation, then ask one multiple-choice question at a time with room for comments. Do not ask the user to invent the model from a blank prompt. Use a batch when the user specifically requests one.
- Do not keep asking the user to reconfirm implications already established. Engineering basics belong to the builder. Bring back material changes in architecture, authority, scope or added machinery with evidence and the smallest alternative.
- Make the business purpose of a requested operator check clear. Do not turn the operator into a tester of internal commands or use successful technical checks as proof of usability.
- Maintain the plan and todo posture. Mark implemented, published, independently verified and accepted states accurately. A saved plan edit or completed todo is not user approval of its content.
- Explicitly preserve the user’s emphasis in every subagent brief. Require plain language and minimality in the first output, rather than relying on repeated cleanup after integration.
- Keep scope authorization explicit. A successful intermediate milestone does not authorize the next phase, live external activity or unrelated cleanup. Preserve concurrent user work and source protections.
- Never copy credentials into documents, logs or prompts. Do not bypass denied access through private endpoints or unreviewed permission broadening.
