--- Response (2025-09-05):
On July 30, 2025, the complainant, [name removed], notified our office via voicemail and email that the garage door had closed on his vehicle while he was exiting the parking garage. Upon receiving this report, I immediately reviewed the surveillance footage. The video showed that the vehicle had fully exited the garage and cleared the safety sensor but then stopped with the back end of the car still under the door. Once the sensor was no longer engaged, the garage door proceeded to close, resulting in contact with top/trunk area of his vehicle.
I promptly communicated these findings to [name removed], provided photos of the sensor and its alignment, and explained how a recurrence could be avoided by remaining within the sensors range until the vehicle has completely exited the garage.
On July 31, 2025, we exchanged further emails. While I provided clarification, ****** maintained his belief that the garage door had malfunctioned. He requested a copy of the surveillance footage, which I confirmed would be available the following week. I also requested that he provide photographs of the vehicle damage along with a professional repair estimate to document the extent of the damages.
On August 5, 2025, I supplied a copy of the recorded footage, confirmed that an incident report had been submitted, and informed him that the matter was under review by our insurance carrier. In addition, I confirmed that a qualified vendor specializing in garage door operations had inspected the system and verified that it was functioning properly with no issues identified. After receiving this information, ****** disputed the findings and expressed frustration with the conclusion.
On August 25, 2025, ****** sent multiple emails to various company contacts, reiterating his position that the garage door malfunctioned and expressing concern regarding the status of the claim. I have consistently explained that while I cannot expedite the carriers review process, he is welcome to contact me at any time for updates. I also provided him with the direct contact information of the claim specialist managing the case.
On August 29, 2025, the Claims Specialist corresponded with the complainant via email and sent a denial letter which cited that we would not be liable to pay for the scratches on his vehicle because the garage did not malfunction, and the complainant did not fully exit the garage to clear the garage door. 

We were unable to upload the garage camera footage even when compressed in a zip file but included a still from a video supplied by the resident showing the vehicle damage, email correspondence between the resident and property manager and email correspondence between the Claims Specialist and the resident.
