COMPLAINT TEXT (2026-08-12):
Living at The Villas at ********* has meant getting used to something no resident should have to get used to: the building's fire alarm sounding twice within about a week, both times for reasons that were never explained, and both times followed by silence. Not an evacuation notice, not an all-clear, not so much as a single email. People simply drifted back inside after ten minutes because staying outside indefinitely wasn't sustainable, not because anyone told us it was safe.I brought this to the senior property manager directly and asked a simple question: is anyone looking into why this keeps happening? The answer I got was that nothing would be investigated and that fire alarms can trigger for any reason, full stop. For a building with families, elderly residents, people with disabilities, and pets, that's not an acceptable place to leave things. Safety shouldn't hinge on luck.I escalated to Holland Residential's corporate office in hopes that someone above property level would take this more seriously than a shrug. If you're considering this community, know that when something actually goes wrong, don't expect answers, and don't expect anyone to volunteer them unless you push hard for it yourself.

Hi ********,

Thank you for reaching out and for taking the time to provide the additional context regarding your concerns. I understand how unsettling it can be to experience multiple fire alarm activations, particularly when there is limited information available afterward regarding what occurred. I also appreciate you sharing your experience with the communication you received from our onsite team.

I have reviewed the email correspondence and want to first provide some clarification regarding our process when a building fire alarm is activated. The fire alarm system is monitored by a third-party monitoring company, and when an alarm is activated,the monitoring company receives the signal, and the *************** is dispatched to respond and assess the situation. Our onsite team does not determine whether an active fire response is necessary or when a building is safe to re-enter during an active incident; that direction ultimately comes from the responding ***************.

That said, I also recognize that there is a difference between the technical response to an alarm and the experience of our residents during and following that event. Even when our team has limited information regarding what triggered an alarm, we can still do our best to communicate what we do know and provide reassurance regarding the process that was followed.

After reviewing your correspondence, I will be working with our team on a more thoughtful and consistent approach to communication following situations such as this. In particular, when an incident significantly impacts residents, I would like our team to communicate as promptly as reasonably possibleeven if the update is simply to acknowledge the event, confirm that the appropriate emergency response occurred, and let residents know that we will share additional information if it becomes available.

I also understand that the tone of communication matters, especially when someone is reaching out because they are concerned about their safety, their pets, or their ability to evacuate their home. While our team may not always have the answers a resident is looking for, we can still make sure those concerns are acknowledged and communicated with care. 

With respect to the cause of the recent alarms, the information available to the onsite team has been limited, particularly for the most recent activation, which occurred outside of normal business hours. If additional information is provided by the ***************, monitoring company, or another appropriate party that requires action by the property or should be communicated to residents, our team will respond accordingly.

While we unfortunately cannot guarantee that a fire alarm will never activate again, we can continue to evaluate how we communicate during and after these situations so residents feel better informed about what is occurring and what they should expect.

Thank you again for bringing your concerns to my attention. Feedback like this gives us an opportunity to review not only our operational response, but also how we communicate with and support our residents during situations that can understandably be stressful.
