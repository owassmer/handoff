# Part 7: the edition differences that matter to readiness work

Saved-source reassessment, October 2, 2026. No further retrieval. This compares actual text; it does **not** establish the disputed emergency continuation date.

**The current-status difference matters principally because it changes whether older-building remodeling is exempt and whether ordinary two-inch lumber is an acceptable open-eave underside material.** Roof repair's 50-percent threshold and Class A requirement are already in the underlying text; they are not a new reason to make the August package current.

## Base versus emergency/readoption

| Provision | Actual difference | Handoff decision affected |
|---|---|---|
| **101.3.1 and former 101.3.1.1 — geographical application** | Base application described new buildings after specified permit dates, including all SRA zones, locally designated **Very High** zones and locally designated WUI areas. Emergency 101.3.1 instead expressly includes “Lands in Local Responsibility Area (LRA) identified by the State Fire Marshal as **High or Very High** Fire Hazard Severity Zone” and land locally designated High/Very High, retaining separately designated WUI areas. R pp.1–3; E p.1-15. | Whether a property in an LRA **High**, rather than Very High, zone enters the code's express applicability path. This section still says “New buildings”; existing-work consequences must be composed with 503.1/507 and other applicable authorities, not inferred from geography alone. |
| **101.3.1 exception 5 and 503.1 exception 2 — older-building work** | Base contains: “**Additions to and remodels of buildings originally constructed prior to July 1, 2008.**” Emergency deletes this exception in both places. The operative 503.1 lead sentence remains: “Buildings and structures hereafter constructed, **modified** or relocated into or within wildland-urban interface areas shall meet the construction requirements in accordance with Chapter 5.” R pp.2–4; E pp.1-15, 5-3. | A readiness job that modifies/remodels an older building can no longer use this particular age exemption if the emergency version controls. That changes specifications and permissible materials for, for example, exterior remodeling associated with necessary repair. This is not a finding that every small repair requires whole-building retrofit; classification of the actual work and other limitations still matter. |

**Readoption makes no substantive change to the emergency version.** The May 6 recording, saved as `aIGHCPOB_WI-transcript.txt`, 39:09–39:17, describes readoption of what was previously approved and says “There's been no changes made to that.” Its April 7 Statement of Progress, p.3 items2/4, expressly describes deletion of trigger-date exceptions and the 503.1 exception as previously adopted. The published E text confirms their removal. The older initial filed-IET capture's omission of 503.1 is not a basis to silently reconstruct a different published emergency edition.

## What August changes—and what it does not

1. **507.1 roof repair/replacement trigger is substantially unchanged.** A sub-item4-16/P281 preserves: “50 percent or more replaced in a 12-month period” and “All portions of a roof covering applied during an addition, alteration or repair to an existing structure” must have Class A classification. The duplicated `5050` in plain extraction is struck-out `50` plus inserted `50`. August replaces “meet at least a … fire classification” with “be classified as … when tested in accordance with ASTM E108 or UL 790.” Thus a small repaired roof portion already has the Class A requirement; the fifty-percent trigger already routes larger replacement to 504.2/504.2.1. The emergency package does not amend 507.1. Its continuity may affect applicability gates, but does not create a new numerical roof threshold.

2. **101.5 is newly adopted, not merely newly printed.** CAM green1-4 expressly says “Adopt section 101.5 which was not adopted before.” A sub-item1-4/P53–55 allows additions/alterations “without requiring the existing building or structure to comply with all of the requirements of this code,” provided the addition/alteration meets new-building requirements, except provisions specifically applicable to existing conditions; work cannot make the building unsafe or violate the code. This matters to avoiding unnecessary whole-building upgrades while correctly hardening altered work. The provision's appearance in E's printed model text does not establish prior state adoption.

3. **Roof installation choices change even though 507.1's trigger does not.** A sub-item4-4 replaces 504.2.1's roof-void alternatives with roof-deck, eave, and ridge/hip requirements. New roof-deck alternatives include “A roof assembly that, when tested **without the roof covering material**, will pass the ASTM E108 or UL 790 **Class C** fire test”; the new text also permits listed cap-sheet/mineral-wool approaches and retains a treated-sheathing exception. Eave openings must use fireblocking/noncombustible birdstops/eave closures; hips/ridges may be mortared **or otherwise protected with noncombustible material** (A P159–174). This changes installation/product choices for replacement roofing routed by 507.1. It does **not** lower the complete roof's Class A requirement to Class C. A4-3 also adds ASTM D4869 Type IV underlayment to the slate-roof testing exception.

4. **Exterior-wall and vent product choices change.** A4-8/504.5 removes the standalone wall-assembly menu entry “one layer of 5/8-inch … Type X gypsum sheathing applied behind the exterior wall covering or cladding” and reorganizes the surviving construction/covering compliance paths (A P194–222). One cannot infer that every gypsum-containing assembly is prohibited: tested/rated routes remain. A4-12/504.10.1 newly requires ASTM E2886 flame-intrusion testing “in both the horizontal and vertical orientations” (A P249–253). A4-13 adds a 144-square-inch maximum for each off-ridge field-vent opening and reframes which vents use that alternative (A P256–262). These changes can affect replacement siding, assemblies and roof/attic vent purchasing.

5. **August is not just certification of identical emergency text.** A1-3 deletes the emergency's separate locally designated WUI-area item and most application exceptions; it retains the emergency removal of the old permit-date triggers and does not supply a new historical permit-date threshold in their place. This is text scope/timing, not an effective-date finding; A1-5 changes maintenance language to the edition under which structures were constructed/required to be installed, with separate landscape/defensible-space requirements. A4-7 carries forward the emergency open-eave deletion without an additional substantive change. The complete August FET therefore cannot substitute for emergency text merely because it is described as a certifying package.

## Narrow consequence for status research

The unresolved fact is material: which applicability/older-building exemption and open-eave material menu govern a covered readiness job at the relevant permit date. The August future package and its additional repair specifications are already separately identifiable. Do not make its future publication or the unchanged 507.1 threshold a proxy for emergency continuity. Conversely, do not remove this question because a particular demonstration property lacks these facts. There is a direct, reusable existing-building repair decision that differs between the candidates.
