                             Next steps include distributing a draft scope and deliverables document to
                             subgroup participants via email, with a follow-up meeting if clarification is
                             needed.
                        ii. Marco Mack outlined the core philosophy of the subgroup: rather than
                             prescribing mandatory retrofit requirements, the effort is focused on
                             developing voluntary best practices with a tiered structure that enables
                             property owners to understand levels of protection, pursue incremental
                             improvements, and potentially receive credit for exceeding minimum
                             standards. The framework envisions three tiers: below code minimum, at

                             slope), developing guidance on yard mitigation that complements
                             defensible space, referencing existing home hardening standards
                             (including IBHS essential and enhanced levels), and framing wall-to-roof
                             assemblies as an integrated system. The subgroup is also considering a
                             retrofit maintenance component.

   III.   NEW BUSINESS
          A. Sub-Groups
               1. Exterior Sprinklers
                     i. Kevin Scott (KH Scott & Associates LLC) reported that the newly formed
                          exterior sprinklers subgroup held its first meeting the morning of
                          September 2nd. Two potential paths were identified: (1) voluntary home
                          hardening adoption by individual property owners, and (2) incorporating
                          exterior sprinklers as a required additional level of protection in very high
                          fire hazard severity zones.
                     ii. The subgroup reached unanimous agreement that exterior sprinklers are
                          not a replacement for ignition-resistant or fire-resistant construction; they
                          are an additional layer of protection. Their primary function is to address
                          ember accumulation risk, particularly combustible items such as patio
                          furniture, umbrellas, and other materials placed within the defensible
                          space zone that may not be regulated by code. Exterior sprinklers are not
                          intended to provide protection against a direct flame front.
                     iii. On water supply, Kevin Scott explained that exterior systems can operate
                          at approximately 10 GPM, are zone-cycled rather than continuous, and
                          are designed to pre-wet surfaces to reduce ignition probability. He noted
                          that the system draw is comparable to domestic usage and would not run
                          all zones simultaneously.
                     iv. Kevin Scott also raised an independent but related concept: requiring an
                          excess flow valve on domestic water supply lines for all structures in WUI
                          areas, whether or not sprinklers are installed. The rationale is that when
                          structures burn and supply lines are damaged, uncontrolled water loss
                          can deplete community water systems. This was identified as an issue
                          worthy of separate consideration.
                     v. Larry Williams (Ventura County) raised the water supply concern more
                          broadly, asking whether system design accounts for limited rural water
                          supplies and community system impacts. Kevin Scott confirmed this was
                          a central discussion item, noting the cycling approach and low flow design
                          as mitigating factors.
                     vi. Marco Mack noted informal experience in Santa Cruz with coordination
                          between water districts and emergency responders to shut off meters
                          during wildfire events, as well as experience with low-flow drip mist
                          systems at eaves that provided effective pre-wetting with minimal water
                          consumption. Marco Mack indicated he would follow up with Kevin Scott
                          directly.
                     vii. The subgroup meets again in two weeks. Kevin Scott's contact
                          information was shared in the chat for those wishing to participate.

          B.   Tiered Approach
                 1. The tiered approach was raised as a continuation of discussion from the prior
                    meeting, where it was suggested that each subgroup consider tiered
                    requirements as part of their work.
                 2. Greg Andersen (Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety) summarized
                    the current state of discussion: if OSFM is seeking a tiered approach aligned
                    with the International WUI Code structure, the clear principle is that the lowest
                    tier must not represent a downgrade from what California currently requires.
                    Additional tiers would add more restrictive requirements where appropriate. Greg
                    Andersen cautioned that this is a large undertaking that likely extends beyond
                    the current cycle and called for a deliberate plan if it is to be pursued.
                 3. Marco Mack noted that this concept aligns with efforts at the ICC's FCAC level
                    and described a tiered framework concept he is developing around different fire
                    exposure types and proximity to property lines—including the idea that less than
                    three feet to a property line should involve entirely non-ignitable exterior
                    construction, and that additional specifications could address water-resistive
                    barriers in close-proximity scenarios.
                 4. Kevin Reinertson provided historical context, noting that a tiered approach for
                    the high and very high fire hazard severity zones was contemplated when
                    Chapter 7A was originally created over 20 years ago, but was never
                    implemented due to policy changes. He expressed strong support for moving
                    forward, characterizing the current California code as the appropriate floor for
                    moderate zones, with escalating requirements for high and very high. Kevin
                    Reinertson also identified conflagration/high-density scenarios as a fourth
                    potential tier element.
                 5. Kevin Scott (KH Scott & Associates LLC) emphasized that whatever
                    requirements are established for "very high" in the SRA should be consistent
                    with "very high" in the LRA—the fire hazard level, not the jurisdiction type, should
                    drive the requirement.
                 6. Marcelo Hirschler (GBH International) acknowledged support for the tiered
                    approach conceptually but urged the group to pursue changes in coordination
                    with the IWUI rather than in isolation, noting that California's historically different
                    approach creates ongoing challenges. He pointed out that the IWUI public
                    comment period (November 23 through January 12) presents a timely
                    opportunity to submit proposals. Greg Andersen responded that both efforts can
                    be pursued simultaneously, developing California code language and proposing
                    to ICC are not mutually exclusive. Kevin Reinertson agreed, adding that
                    progress in California could serve as a catalyst for improving IWUI Class 2 and
                    Class 3 requirements at the national level.
