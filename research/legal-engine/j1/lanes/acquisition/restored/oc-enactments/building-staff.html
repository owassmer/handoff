SUMMARY:

 

Adoption of the proposed Ordinances, "2025 Building Standards, Plumbing Code, Mechanical Code, Electrical Code and Fire Code" and Resolutions amending Title 7 (Building Regulations) and Title 3 (Fire Protection and Explosives) of the County of Orange Codified Ordinances will meet state mandates and support construction of safe private and public structures and facilities.

 

 

BACKGROUND INFORMATION:

 

The State Health and Safety Code (HSC), Section 17958 (Attachment V) mandates that the California Building Standards Commission (CBSC) adopt and publish the California Code of Regulations (CCR), Title 24, also referred to as the California Building Standard Codes, every three years. The section of the HSC also authorizes local governments to adopt more restrictive amendments to the California Construction Codes (CCC) based on local climatic, topographical and geological conditions. Title 24 of the CCC includes the Building Code; Residential Code; Energy Code; Green Building Standards Code (GBSC); Plumbing, Mechanical, Electrical, existing Building Code, Wildland-Urban Interface Code and Fire Codes.

 

The County of Orange (County) is required by State of California (State) law to adopt the latest edition of these codes once adopted by the State. The updated codes, along with County-specific amendments, will become effective January 1, 2026, as mandated by state law, and will apply to all unincorporated areas of Orange County and County-owned facilities.

 

The 2025 Edition of the CCR, Title 24, has been adopted by the CBSC. It incorporates both state-specific codes and Model Codes as developed and published by non-profit organizations such as the International Code Council (ICC), International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO) and National Fire Protection Association (NFPA).

The Board of Supervisors (Board) adopted the 2016, 2019 and 2022 editions of these CCCs, along with County-specific amendments, on November 22, 2016; November 25, 2019; and December 6, 2022, respectively.

 

The 2025 CCC adoption proposes to carry forward existing County amendments, due to local climatic, topographical and geological conditions. Findings are required to justify the code modifications. Justifications for these amendments are provided in the attached resolutions, which include the required findings.

 

Upon approval, the proposed ordinances would amend Title 7 - Building Regulations and Title 3 - Fire Protection and Explosives of the County's Codified Ordinances. While closely aligned with the State code, the proposed ordinances include existing administrative amendments that support local building regulation management, such as permit issuance and appeal processes, requirements for underground utilities, permit expiration/renewal, special inspection provisions and violation/penalty enforcement. Local fire protection provisions are based on recommendations from the Orange County Fire Authority.

 

The proposed ordinances would also maintain the existing County amendments:

 

a.

Seismic repair/reconstruction standards for buildings damaged after a major earthquake,

b.

Specific design criteria related to wind speed, seismic design category, flood hazard zones, etc.,

c.

Steel reinforcement requirements in residential footings to improve earthquake resistance,

d.

Requirements for drainage devices at retaining walls to reduce water pressure,

e.

Clarification of "Sustainability" definitions in the Green Building Standards Code, and

f.

Clarification of water conservation inspection procedures for automatic outdoor irrigation systems, as required in the GBSC.

 

The proposed amendments would provide enhanced safety and protection for residents in the unincorporated areas of Orange County. They are also consistent with recommendations endorsed by the ICC Chapter of the County’s Code Uniformity Group and have been reviewed by several local stakeholder groups (support letters included as Attachments W-Y). Most incorporated cities in Orange County are adopting similar local amendments.

 

Compliance with CEQA: The proposed project is statutorily exempt from review under CEQA, as a ministerial action pursuant to Public Resources Code Section 21080(b)(1) and Section 15268(c) of the CEQA Guidelines.
