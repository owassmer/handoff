## Publication authority and closure decision

[CRC 1.6(9)](https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/one/rule1_6) defines a local rule by general application to court or courtroom practice, including orders, policies, forms and standards. The source boundary therefore does not depend on whether the court calls something a rule or a miscellaneous order. [CRC 10.1030](https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/ten/rule10_1030) requires adopted appellate local rules to be published through the Reporter of Decisions and supplies a minimum 45-day interval before effectiveness. [CRC 8.72(a)](https://courts.ca.gov/cms/rules/index/eight/rule8_72) separately requires public electronic and print filing requirements. These authorities support using the current published local rules, IOPP, procedural pages and their named supplements as the general-practice inventory.

The archive is mixed: the recovered appointment order is internal administration; the recovered criminal writ-service order is generally procedural but outside this aperture; the rehearing rescission is a relevant procedural source. It would be false to infer that all clerk-held orders are internal, or that a nonpublic order can never matter. Case-specific directions belong to the actual case record, and newly identified general requirements trigger source updates. No actual additional missing civil requirement is established by the clerk-request sentence. There is no need to invent an executable all-orders unit or hold J1 open for a hypothetical archive sweep.
