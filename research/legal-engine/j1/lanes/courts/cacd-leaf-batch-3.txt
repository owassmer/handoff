L6@P0: I. NOTICE OF MAGISTRATE JUDGE’S AVAILABILITY
L7@P0: 1. Under the Court’s rules, habeas cases are ordinarily assigned to a district judge and referred to a
L8@P0: magistrate judge. For any final ruling in the case, the assigned magistrate judge prepares a
L9@P0: “Report and Recommendation” for the district judge to review. After reviewing the “Report and
L10@P0: Recommendation,” the district judge will make a final ruling.
L11@P0: 2. A magistrate judge is available under 28 U.S.C. § 636 (c) to conduct all proceedings in this case,
L12@P0: including a final ruling on your claims. However, a magistrate judge can handle all matters in the
L13@P0: case if all parties voluntarily consent.
L14@P0: 3. The Court encourages parties to consent to magistrate judge jurisdiction. Consent allows the case
L15@P0: to proceed before a single judicial officer and avoids the delay associated with the preparation
L16@P0: and review of a “Report and Recommendation.”
L17@P0: 4. However, consent is entirely voluntarily and you are free to withhold consent to magistrate judge
L18@P0: jurisdiction. There is no penalty for declining consent.
L19@P0: 5. If both parties consent to have a magistrate judge decide the case, the Court will determine
L20@P0: whether to assign the case to a magistrate judge for all purposes. If the case is so assigned, the
L21@P0: case will proceed only before the magistrate judge. Either party may appeal the magistrate
L22@P0: judge’s rulings directly to the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals, as if a district judge had decided
L23@P0: the matter.
L24@P0: 6. If both parties do not consent to have a magistrate judge decide the case, the case will be
L25@P0: assigned to a district court judge and referred to a magistrate judge.
