L179: ## Are You a Landlord?
L180: 
L181: You can set up tenant sub-meters that feed into your master meter.
L182: 
L183: 
L184: 
L185: ## Are You a Tenant?
L186: 
L187: The electric meter for your unit feeds into a master meter controlled by your landlord.
L188: ## How Are Rates for Tenants Calculated?
L189: 
L190: We charge different rates depending on the type of unit you live in. If you live in an apartment building, a mobile home, an RV park, or any other multi-family dwelling, your electric bill is probably set up on your landlord's “master meter/sub-meter” billing plan.
L191: ## What Are the Tenant’s Rights?
L192: 
L193: Your electric rates should be the same as they would be if you were billed directly. As a California sub-metered resident, you have special utility rights:
L194:   * You should receive an itemized bill of electricity charges at the same time your landlord receives the bill.
L195:   * You should be provided a percentage, based on your electricity use, of any rebates applied to the owner’s account during the billing period.
L196:   * Your rate schedule should be posted by the landlord where everyone can review it, and the schedule should be made available for copying.
L197:   * Your sub-meter should be maintained by your landlord.
L198: ##

L200: ## Metering Explained
L201: 
L202: 
L203: 
L204: ### What Is a Master Meter?
L205: 
L206: In a multi-family dwelling like an apartment building or a mobile home park, the landlord will receive a “master meter” electric bill. The landlord uses this master bill to charge tenants for each unit’s electricity use. Rates billed to tenants by the landlord must be the same as the rates billed to the landlord. These rates must be posted in a place where tenants can read them.
L207: ### What Is a Sub-Meter?
L208: 
L209: A “sub-meter” measures the electricity use of a single unit in a multi-family dwelling. These meter readings are added together to make one main “master meter” reading. Your landlord can use the sub-meter readings to bill you for your unit’s electricity use, but must use the rates you would receive if you were billed separately.

L213: ## Rates Vary by Property Type
L214: ## Apartments & MultiFamily Homes
L215: 
L216: cite121†Image L217: 
L218: If you live in an apartment building or a multi-family house with a master meter and sub-meters, your electricity charges are billed on the DMS-1 rate schedule. If the building was constructed or converted for multi-family use after July 1, 1982, each unit is metered separately and is not eligible for DMS-1 rates.
L219: ## Mobile Homes & Manufactured Housing Communities
L220: 
L221: cite121†Image L222: 
L223: If you live in a mobile home or manufactured housing community that has a master meter and sub-meters, your billing rates are determined by the DMS-2 rate schedule. Any construction started after January 1, 1997 is not eligible for this rate unless the housing community qualified for the rate before January 1, 1997.
L224: ## Residential RV Parks
L225: 
L226: cite121†Image L227: 
L228: If you live in a residential RV park, your electricity is billed using the DMS-3 rate schedule. Your RV park qualifies for this rate only if it is sub-metered. RV units must be used as residences and be reserved for pre-paid month-to-month tenants.

L174: Advice Letters
L175: 
L176: this element added for skip to main content issue fixes
L177: An Advice Letter is a document submitted to the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) requesting review of SCE’s request to change its tariffs in a manner previously authorized by statute or CPUC order, to conform the tariffs to the requirements of a statute or CPUC order, to request CPUC authorization to deviate from its tariffs, or to propose a new product or service.
L178: SCE may seek a rate increase by means of an advice letter only if use of an advice letter for this purpose is authorized by statute or CPUC order.
L179: The advice letter process provides a quick and simplified review of the types of utility requests that are expected neither to be controversial nor to raise important policy questions.
L180: 
L181: Electric, gas, water, and telecommunication advice letters submitted to the CPUC are categorized below based on their current status.

L15: ## We’re not just SoCalGas — we’re your neighbors. The safety of our customers, employees, and communities is fundamental to what we do.
L16: We work around the clock to provide safe, reliable service. Our employees regularly perform essential work, such as maintenance, inspections, repairs, and service visits, to help keep your natural gas service running safely. Depending on the type of work being performed, some visits are scheduled, while others may occur without advance notice. To complete this work, SoCalGas employees require safe access to customer property, including clear pathways to natural gas meters and pipelines.

L76: cite12†Learn More L77: ## Does SoCalGas Have a Right to Be on My Property?
L78: 
L79: 
L80: 
L81: cite13†Image: Woman working on Gas Meter L82: To provide safe, reliable service, our employees may require access to natural gas meters, equipment, and pipelines on customer property. While SoCalGas strives to provide advance notice of work that may require access, some work may be performed at any time without prior notice, in accordance with Tariff Rules 13, 21, and 25, as approved by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC).
L83: Rule 13 states, “All meters, regulators, service pipe, equipment, fixtures, etc., installed by the Utility on the customer's premises for the purpose of delivering gas to the customer, with the exception of pipe installed by the Utility beyond the meter, shall continue to be the property of the Utility and may be repaired or replaced by the Utility at any time." Rule 21 states that SoCalGas "shall at all times have the right to enter and leave Applicant's Premises for any purpose connected with the furnishing of gas service." And Rule 25 states that SoCalGas has "the right of ingress to and egress from the customer's premises at all reasonable hours for any purpose reasonably connected with the furnishing of gas." Learn more about Tariff Rules 13, 21, and 25.

L85: ## Awareness Helps Keep Us Safer — Together
L86: ###
L87:  Utility Scams
L88: 
L89: There has been an increase in cite15†utility scams , with impostors posing as utility workers to gain access to customers’ homes. Some may wear hard hats or reflective vests to appear legitimate and may threaten utility shutoffs unless immediate payment is made. Customers should stay alert to potential scams and question anyone claiming to represent SoCalGas. If someone arrives at your property claiming to be from SoCalGas, consider the following:
L90:   * SoCalGas occasionally performs routine maintenance and other work that may require access to customer property, including safety inspections. This work may occur without advance notice. All SoCalGas employees are required to carry an official SoCalGas photo identification badge, which you can request to see at any time. cite16†Most also wear branded uniforms with a SoCalGas logo and drive company vehicles. L91:   * A SoCalGas employee typically visits a customer’s home in response to a specific service request. In some cases, you may receive a text, email, call, or mailed notice informing you about work in your area. If you are not expecting a visit and someone asks to enter your home, call us to confirm. To verify anyone claiming to represent SoCalGas, ask for an identification badge or call us at 1-800-427-2200.
L92: ###  Dog Safety
L93: 
L94: We love dogs as much as you do, but even friendly dogs can become protective. Please secure your dog whenever a SoCalGas employee is working on or around your property and follow cite17†safety guidelines to help keep our employees safe.
