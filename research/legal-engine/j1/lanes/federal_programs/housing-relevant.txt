L56@P1: IV. Issues with Emergency Call Systems
L57@P1: Older emergency call systems are typically manual “pull-cord” technology, that, when
L58@P1: pulled by a resident, alert a monitored call-center, front-desk, or emergency response
L59@P1: service. Advances made in wireless and electronic technology now offer a broader set of
L60@P1: call-system options. In a 2014 memorandum,8the Office of Multifamily Housing (MFH)
L61@P1: described how the functional requirements of an older emergency call system could be
L62@P1: met using a variety of more-modern options, including electronic and wireless emergency
L63@P1: alert systems.
L64@P1: MFH is aware that Owners may lack the resources to repair or replace inoperable pullcord systems. In addition to providing a false sense of security to residents, these defunct
L65@P1: systems may be cited during HUD inspections if the system is blocked, does not function
L66@P1: properly, or if a pull cord is higher than six inches off the floor.
L67@P1: 9 Even properties with
L68@P1: fully functioning pull-cord systems or other modernized technologies may have trouble
L69@P1: obtaining commercial insurance, as some insurers consider the systems to pose liability
L70@P1: risks.
L71@P1: 10 Overall, these circumstances can pose significant financial and operational
L72@P1: challenges to Owners of housing for the elderly.
