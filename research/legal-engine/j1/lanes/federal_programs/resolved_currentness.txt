L19@P0: I. PURPOSE
L20@P0: This notice revises, updates, and finalizes the administrative procedures of National
L21@P0: Standards for the Physical Inspection of Real Estate (NSPIRE) for the Housing Choice
L22@P0: Voucher (HCV) and Project-Based Voucher (PBV) Programs, and, by use of Housing
L23@P0: Quality Standards (HQS) for their inspections, the Section 8 Moderate Rehabilitation
L24@P0: (Mod Rehab)1 Programs (collectively known hereafter as “voucher programs”).
L25@P0: This notice applies to all public housing agencies (PHAs) administering voucher
L26@P0: programs, including Moving to Work (MTW) PHAs.
L27@P0: II. BACKGROUND
L28@P0: On May 11, 2023, HUD published the Economic Growth Regulatory Relief and
L29@P0: Consumer Protection Act: Implementation of National Standards for the Physical
L30@P0: Inspection of Real Estate (NSPIRE) final rule (“NSPIRE final rule”) in the Federal
L31@P0: Register (88 FR 30442). NSPIRE establishes a new approach to defining and assessing
L32@P0: housing quality, strengthens HUD’s physical condition standards, and improves HUD
L33@P0: oversight by aligning and consolidating the inspection regulations across multiple
L34@P0: programs used to evaluate “HUD housing,” as defined in 24 CFR 5.701(c).
L35@P0: Implementation of the NSPIRE final rule ensures that residents of HUD housing live in
L36@P0: safe, habitable dwellings. It also ensures the items and components located both inside
L37@P0-1: 1 24 CFR part 8822
L38@P1: and outside and within the units of HUD housing are functionally adequate, operable, and
L39@P1: free of health and safety hazards. The rule also states that HUD will publish NSPIRE
L40@P1: standards through a Federal Register notice at least once every 3 years to respond to the
L41@P1: public and assisted housing portfolio’s evolving needs and to research findings.
L42@P1: During the first phases of implementation of the NSPIRE final rule, HUD commenced
L43@P1: inspections for public housing and multifamily housing properties in July and October
L44@P1: 2023. More information can be found on HUD’s NSPIRE Official Notices and Proposed
L45@P1: Rules webpage.
L46@P1: With the NSPIRE final rule, NSPIRE for voucher programs became effective October 1,
L47@P1: 2023, and redefined HQS for these programs. On September 28, 2023, HUD announced
L48@P1: the first extension of the compliance date to October 1, 2024.2 On July 5, 2024, HUD
L49@P1: extended the compliance date to October 1, 2025.3 On September 30, 2025, HUD further
L50@P1: extended the compliance date to February 1, 2027.4 This latest extension will provide
L51@P1: PHAs with additional time to train their staff, communicate with landlords and access
L52@P1: additional technical assistance from HUD to support their transition to NSPIRE. HUD
L53@P1: issued the first administrative procedures notice for the voucher programs with Notice
L54@P1: PIH 2023-28, later revised by Notice PIH 2024-26. This notice supersedes both PIH
L55@P1: notices.

L31@P0: summary of compliance requirements.

L40@P1: it would not enforce full PHA compliance by January 1, 2025. Instead, HUD enforced

L43@P1: basis of what PHAs could successfully implement and report to HUD via the Inventory
L44@P1: Management System/Public and Indian Housing (PIH) Information Center (IMS/PIC).
L45@P1: HUD indicated that it would issue further guidance on the deadline to comply with the
L46@P1: outstanding provisions of sections 102 and 104 of HOTMA.

L2: cite1†California Department of Housing and Community Development L3: # Grants & Funding
L4: 
L5: By administering programs that provide grants and loans (from both state and federal housing programs), HCD creates rental and homeownership opportunities for Californians from all walks of life, including veterans, seniors, young families starting out, people with disabilities, farmworkers, and individuals and families who are experiencing homelessness.
L6: Over the last three decades, HCD has provided more than $3 billion of funding for the development of affordable housing and associated infrastructure, but HCD’s role does not end once the awards are made. Through long-term monitoring, HCD ensures the developments continue to provide safe and affordable homes, and that the homes remain well-maintained and financially sound.
