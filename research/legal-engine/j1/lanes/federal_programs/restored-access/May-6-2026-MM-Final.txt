Agenda Item 6. Review and discuss the 45-day state agency proposals for the
2025 Intervening Code Adoption Cycle.

          a. Procedures and general discussion

Executive Director Stoyan Bumbalov provided an overview of the procedure and
clarified that the Commission will not take any vote or action. He also specified that the
public could provide comments and ask questions, but that the comments/questions
discussed at this meeting would not be included in the 45-day rulemaking record.

Commissioner Comments:
Commissioner Klausbruckner asked if there could be a discussion of AB 130 before
discussing the rulemaking proposals.
Commissioner Ramirez asked if each item can be discussed regarding AB 130.
Executive Director Stoyan Bumbalov stated that simple questions could be answered
during the meeting, but for more complicated questions legal council may need
additional time to prepare a response for the commission.
Commissioner Klausbruckner asked if the 6-year moratorium in AB 130 was specifically
related to cost of construction.
Chair Nick Maduros responded there are specific exceptions allowed; changes are
precluded during the moratorium unless they meet one of the exceptions.
Executive Director Stoyan Bumbalov clarified that the 6-year moratorium is for
residential construction, but the restrictions for the intervening code adoption cycle have
no sunset date and they apply to everything, not only residential construction.
Public Comments:
Enrique Huerta, Senior Director of Policy and Legislation with Climate Resolve, asked if
the building moratorium during the intervening code adoption cycle would conduct an
intersectional analysis of how extreme heat, wind, and drought affect building conditions
in the WUI.
Executive Director Stoyan Bumbalov summarized the effect of AB 130 and clarified that
no changes to Title 24 could be proposed except those that meet the exceptions
provided in the bill.

          k. Division of the State Architect – Access Compliance (DSA-AC) –
              ACCESS
             • DSA-AC California Building Code, Part 2, Chapters 2, 11B, and 35

Eric Driever, Principal Architect with DSA, presented the access proposal. He noted
comments received during the CAC meeting led to significant modifications to the 45-
day submittal. This is in alignment with the California Health and Safety Code Section
18942.


Commissioner Comments:
Commissioner Klausbruckner asked if DSA had reviewed the letter submitted by
CALBO with their comments and objections. She also asked DSA to clarify why their
proposal is not considered a substantive change, and why there is a confusion between
CALBO's perspective and DSA's perspective.
Eric Driever explained that some of the changes are substantive, but they comply with
the AB 130 exception for changes which conform with the changes to federal standards.


                                                                            10 | P a g e
He added that some of the 45-day changes may address some of the concerns raised
by CALBO.

Chair Nick Maduros asked how DSA will address the recommendations for disapproval
and/or further study at the CAC ACCESS meeting.

Eric Driever stated that DSA appreciated the recommendations but disagreed and made
changes based on the California Health and Safety Code Section 18942.

Commissioner Haskin asked why some of the proposals that were disapproved or sent
for further study by the CAC ACCESS Committee were carried forward by DSA in
disagreement with the CAC recommendations.

Eric Driever responded that CAC comments were considered and, in some cases,
followed. However, DSA believed the revised proposal was in compliance with AB 130.

Commissioner Rambin commented and stated her appreciation for DSA’s efforts.

Public Comments:

Sue Moe, Architect, CASp with ACCESS Compliance Consulting stated that she was on
the CALBO ACCESS committee and had reviewed the documents. She noted that
CALBO’s comment letter expressed concern that the proposed changes did not include
all the scoping requirements pertaining to the number of units which are included in
HUD 504, and had different replacement cost requirements for determining alterations
than those under the HUD 504 regulations.
Dara Schur, representing Disability Rights California (DRC), supported the proposal and
followed up on the written comments. She stated that the proposal complies with the
law, expressed disagreement with CALBO’s criticism, and strongly disagreed that the
changes are not compliant with AB 130 restrictions.

Deputy Executive Director Kevin Day summarized a written commented received from
Danile Miller expressing concerns regarding AB 130 compliance, DSA’s authority, and
the CAC ACCESS Committee’s’ recommendations.
Commissioner Klausbruckner asked for clarification on why federal standards needed to
be incorporated into the code, given that federal laws already apply.
Chair Nick Maduros stated that it may not be mandatory for all federal laws to be
incorporated into the building standards.

Eric Driever stated that there is California Statue mandating compliance with minimum
federal law. DSA is now seeking to comply with the 2014 HUD deeming notice and has
been out of compliance with this notice until now.



                                                                            11 | P a g e
Kendra Muller commented that California regulations are generally more restrictive than
federal law.

Commissioner Klausbruckner asked if federal law needed to be duplicated in state
regulations if it already applies.
Eric Driever responded that incorporating the federal law into the building code make
the requirements of the law enforceable by DSA and local building departments.
Sue Moe questioned whether it is feasible to incorporate all federal standards into state
regulations, given the number of different federal agencies that exist and provide
funding to state entities.
Chair Nick Maduros asked whether the concern raised regarding the proposed scoping
requirements was valid and whether other states similarly adopt federal requirements
into their state laws.

Eric Driever stated that some proposals did need to be revised to better interpret federal
law.
Michelle Davis, Supervising Architect with DSA, indicated that other states do not
incorporate the federal standards into state regulations. She stated that the proposed
standards include the requirements in the HUD Deeming Notice and were not the
requirements included in UFAS.
