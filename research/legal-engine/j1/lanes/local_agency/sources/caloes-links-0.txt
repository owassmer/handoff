L0@P0: EXECUTIVE DEPARTMENT
L1@P0: STATE OF CALIFORNIA
L2@P0: PROCLAMATION OF A STATE OF EMERGENCY
L3@P0: WHEREAS in the past several years, California has experienced increasingly
L4@P0: severe and destructive storms, including atmospheric river events that have
L5@P0: caused widespread flooding, mud and debris flows, landslides, high winds,
L6@P0: power outages, road and highway closures, evacuation orders, damage to
L7@P0: homes and critical infrastructure, and disruption of essential services; and
L8@P0: WHEREAS climate change is intensifying these weather and hydrologic
L9@P0: extremes in California, contributing to increasingly severe swings between
L10@P0: drought and flooding, and heightening the risks that extreme storms pose to
L11@P0: communities, infrastructure, natural resources, and the economy; and
L12@P0: WHEREAS California is entering the 2026–2027 wet season amid the
L13@P0: ongoing development of a strong and likely historic El Niño event in the Pacific
L14@P0: Ocean; and
L15@P0: WHEREAS the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration’s
L16@P0: National Weather Service Climate Prediction Center, in its August 13, 2026,
L17@P0: diagnostic report, found that the El Niño event is strengthening, with a greater
L18@P0: than 90 percent chance of a very strong event during Northern Hemisphere fall
L19@P0: and winter of 2026–2027; and
L20@P0: WHEREAS the Climate Prediction Center projected a 69 percent chance
L21@P0: that the event will reach historic strength during the October through December
L22@P0: 2026 season, defined as a three-month Relative Oceanic Niño Index, and
L23@P0: exceeding the strength of all prior El Niño events in the historical record
L24@P0: beginning in 1950; and
L25@P0: WHEREAS the National Weather Service Climate Prediction Center has
L26@P0: found that atmospheric conditions over the tropical Pacific are consistent with a
L27@P0: strengthening El Niño, including changes in equatorial winds and an eastward
L28@P0: shift in enhanced convection and rainfall toward the central and eastern
L29@P0: Pacific, making it more likely that at least some parts of California will experience
L30@P0: stronger storms in coming months; and
L31@P0: WHEREAS elevated baseline coastal water levels, combined with high
L32@P0: tides, large waves, high surf, and coastal storms, can increase risks of coastal
L33@P0: flooding, beach erosion, bluff erosion, transportation disruption, and damage to
L34@P0: public and private infrastructure; and
L35@P0: WHEREAS California has already begun to experience effects attributed to
L36@P0: this El Niño event, including unusual precipitation and significant coastal
L37@P0: flooding; and
L38@P0: WHEREAS historical experience demonstrates that very strong El Niño
L39@P0: events do not produce uniform statewide outcomes, but can be associated
L40@P0: with exceptionally wet and destructive winter conditions in California; and
L41@P0: WHEREAS California’s recent experience with atmospheric rivers, debris
L42@P0: flows following wildfires, flash flooding, coastal erosion, inundation, and damage
L43@P0: to transportation and flood-control infrastructure demonstrates the importance
L44@P0: of procuring and positioning resources, clearing waterways, and taking
L45@P0: additional actions to prepare before severe storms arrive; and
L46@P0: WHEREAS areas affected by recent wildfires are particularly susceptible to
L47@P0: flash flooding, mud and debris flows, erosion, sediment transport, and road
L48@P0-1: closures during high-intensity rainfall; andWHEREAS in light of that experience, local governments, local water
L49@P1: agencies, and local flood control authorities have requested action now to
L50@P1: enable them to move quickly to begin preparations for preventing and
L51@P1: responding to the impacts of potential flooding; and
L52@P1: WHEREAS low-income communities, farmworker families, rural residents,
L53@P1: seniors, persons with disabilities, residents with limited transportation access,
L54@P1: people experiencing homelessness, and communities in flood-prone, coastal, or
L55@P1: post-fire areas may face disproportionate risks; and
L56@P1: WHEREAS the demonstrated potential for a very strong or historic-strength
L57@P1: El Niño event to cause elevated coastal water levels and high-impact storms in
L58@P1: California warrants early, coordinated, and targeted preparedness actions to
L59@P1: protect Californians, critical infrastructure, agricultural lands, natural resources,
L60@P1: and the State’s economy; and
L61@P1: WHEREAS under the provisions of Government Code section 8558(b), I find
L62@P1: that conditions of extreme peril to the safety of persons and property exist due
L63@P1: to the developing El Niño event; and
L64@P1: WHEREAS under the provisions of Government Code section 8558(b), I find
L65@P1: that the developing El Niño event is likely to cause conditions that, by reason of
L66@P1: their magnitude, are likely to be beyond the control of the services, personnel,
L67@P1: equipment, and facilities of any single local government and require the
L68@P1: combined forces of a mutual aid region or regions to appropriately respond;
L69@P1: and
L70@P1: WHEREAS under the provisions of Government Code section 8625(c), I find
L71@P1: that local authority is inadequate to cope with the magnitude of the extreme
L72@P1: peril posed by the developing El Niño event; and
L73@P1: WHEREAS under the provisions of Government Code section 8571, I find
L74@P1: that strict compliance with various statutes and regulations specified in this
L75@P1: Proclamation would prevent, hinder, or delay the mitigation of the effects of the
L76@P1: developing El Niño event.
L77@P1: NOW, THEREFORE, I, GAVIN NEWSOM, Governor of the State of California,
L78@P1: in accordance with the authority vested in me by the State Constitution and
L79@P1: statutes, including the California Emergency Services Act, and in particular,
L80@P1: Government Code section 8625, HEREBY PROCLAIM A STATE OF EMERGENCY to
L81@P1: exist statewide due to the developing 2026–2027 El Niño event.
L82@P1: IT IS HEREBY ORDERED THAT:
L83@P1: 1. All agencies of the state government are to utilize and employ state
L84@P1: personnel, equipment, and facilities for the performance of any and all
L85@P1: activities consistent with the direction of the Governor’s Office of
L86@P1: Emergency Services and the State Emergency Plan. The Governor’s Office
L87@P1: of Emergency Services shall coordinate statewide preparedness activities
L88@P1: for potential severe weather, flooding, coastal flooding, erosion, mud and
L89@P1: debris flows, and related impacts associated with conditions resulting from
L90@P1: the El Niño event, including atmospheric rivers and other severe storm
L91@P1: systems. The Governor’s Office of Emergency Services shall work with local
L92@P1: and tribal governments to support emergency planning, resource
L93@P1: coordination, and requests for state assistance. To protect their safety, all
L94@P1: residents are to obey the direction of emergency officials with regard to
L95@P1-2: this emergency.2. As necessary to assist local governments and for the protection of public
L96@P2: safety and the environment, state agencies shall enter into contracts to
L97@P2: arrange for the procurement of materials, goods, and services necessary
L98@P2: to quickly assist with the response to and recovery from the impacts of this
L99@P2: emergency. Applicable provisions of the Government Code and the
L100@P2: Public Contract Code, including but not limited to travel, advertising, and
L101@P2: competitive bidding requirements, are suspended to the extent necessary
L102@P2: to address the effects of this emergency.
L103@P2: 3. The California Environmental Protection Agency and the California
L104@P2: Natural Resources Agency, including boards, departments, and offices
L105@P2: within those agencies, are directed to prioritize work to help local
L106@P2: agencies prepare for anticipated impacts from the El Niño event. In
L107@P2: particular, those state agencies are directed to prioritize permitting,
L108@P2: consultation, and technical assistance to support local efforts to prevent
L109@P2: or mitigate impacts from anticipated or potential flooding, landslides, and
L110@P2: mud and debris flows, including local efforts to expedite levee and other
L111@P2: flood management infrastructure repairs, vegetation management,
L112@P2: debris removal, sediment management, bridge scour protection, and
L113@P2: channel-capacity improvements. State agencies are directed to work
L114@P2: with local agencies to issue any necessary permits or other approvals for
L115@P2: such efforts as quickly as possible, including by identifying and invoking
L116@P2: existing emergency authorities or other pathways for streamlined review.
L117@P2: On or before October 16, 2026, the Secretary for Environmental Protection
L118@P2: and the Secretary of the Natural Resources Agency shall submit a joint
L119@P2: recommendation to my office as to whether further action or emergency
L120@P2: suspensions are necessary to support and expedite this work.
L121@P2: 4. The Governor’s Office of Emergency Services shall coordinate the State’s
L122@P2: preparation for and response to the developing El Niño event, including
L123@P2: by working with the Department of Water Resources and other
L124@P2: appropriate government agencies to prepare to pre-position personnel,
L125@P2: equipment, supplies, commodities, and other resources based on forecast
L126@P2: conditions, local needs, and identified risks. In particular, this work shall
L127@P2: draw on the Department of Water Resources’ guidance regarding floodrisk-reduction activities.
