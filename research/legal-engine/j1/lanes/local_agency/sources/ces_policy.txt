L47@P3: I. Common Terms and Definitions
L48@P3: Access Point refers to the point of entry into CES for households experiencing homelessness or at risk of
L49@P3: homelessness.
L50@P3: Open Access Points will assist anyone (within their SPA/ subpopulations) with completing the CES
L51@P3: Assessment or provide a warm hand-off to an appropriate Access Point to meet the household’s
L52@P3: needs. Open Access Points will receive direct referrals from 2-1-1 Orange County United Way's
L53@P3: Virtual Front Door.
L54@P3: Closed Access Points will assist only those enrolled in their program(s) to complete the CES
L55@P3: Assessment. Households will need to reach out to your agency directly to be referred to the
L56@P3: agency’s program and determine eligibility. If the household is not eligible for the agency’s
L57@P3: program, the household will provide a warm handoff to an Open Access Point for CES enrollment.
L58@P3: Bed Reservation System Assessmentis a standardized processfor determining a household’s shelter needs
L59@P3: and interests.
L60@P3: Case Conferencing involves exchanging information between service providers participating in CES to
L61@P3: enhance service coordination, minimize duplication in services and expedite access to services when
L62@P3: needed and available. The case conferencing is aimed at ensuring that individuals and families being
L63@P3: prioritized for a housing resource per the CES policy can have their service needs adequately met and the
L64@P3: housing resource is an appropriate match.
L65@P3: Community Queue (CQ) is the list that holds all CES households awaiting to be referred, or matched, to
L66@P3: opportunities such as emergency shelter, supportive services, or housing opportunities. The CQ may be
L67@P3: referred to as the prioritized list.
L68@P3: Continuum of Care (CoC) is a regional or local planning body that coordinates housing and service funding
L69@P3: forindividuals and families experiencing homelessness. The CoC strategizesthe communityplantoorganize
L70@P3: and deliver housing and servicesto meet the specific needs of people who are experiencing homelessness
L71@P3: as they move to stable housing and maximize self- sufficiency.
L72@P3: Developer is a participating CES partner organization that constructs or renovates a housing community
L73@P3: with the intention of making units available to CES and for those experiencing homelessness.
L74@P3: Diversion in CES refers to problem-solving strategies used to reduce the length of time that people
L75@P3: experience homelessness by accessing alternatives to entering emergency shelter or living in places not
L76@P3: meant for human habitation.
L77@P3: Dynamic Prioritization is a process in which households enrolled into CES who are referred to the
L78@P3: Community Queue (CQ) to be referred to a housing opportunity. The way in which names appear on the
L79@P3: CQ is dependent on a number of factors, including but not limited to, length of verifiable homeless
L80@P3: experience, ongoing physical or mental health conditions verified by a licensed professional, verifiable ties
L81@P3: to a city, last city in which the household was in prior to beginning their current episode of homelessness,
L82@P3-4: and more.5

L84@P4: Emergency Shelter means any facility with the primary purpose of providing temporary shelter for people
L85@P4: experiencing homelessness which does not require a household to sign leases or occupancy agreements.
L86@P4: Episode of Homelessness is a period of time that an individual or family is experiencing homelessness as
L87@P4: defined in the homeless definition included in Attachment A. Episodes of homelessness are separated by
L88@P4: permanent ortemporary housing of atleastseven nights orstays in an institution of 90 days or more.
L89@P4: Family is a household that consists of at least one adult aged 18+ and has either, at least one child aged 0
L90@P4: – 17, a pregnant mother, or is in the process of reunification. This type of household is eligible for the
L91@P4: Family Coordinated Entry System program enrollment.

L93@P4: and Urban Development mandated database used to collect participant-level data on the provision of
L94@P4: housing and services to individuals and families experiencing homelessness, as well as individuals and
L95@P4: families atrisk of homelessness. HMIS isrequired to follow data standards established by the United States
L96@P4: Department of Housing and Urban Development.
L97@P4: Homeless Prevention is services and/or financial assistance to prevent people from experiencing
L98@P4: homelessness.
L99@P4: Household is a group of people that are enrolled into any of the three components of the Coordinated
L100@P4: Entry System program. A household can be defined as a Family with any number of adults aged 18+ and
L101@P4: any number of children aged 0 – 17, or pregnant mother, or a household can be defined as any number of
L102@P4: Individuals aged 18+ without children or a pregnant mother. One household will equal one Coordinated
L103@P4: Entry System program enrollment. Household may be referred to participant.
L104@P4: Housing means community-based housing without a designated length of stay and includes permanent
L105@P4: supportive housing and rapid rehousing.
L106@P4: Housing Assessment is a standardized process of determining a household’s permanent housing and
L107@P4: service needs and interests.
L108@P4: Housing First is an approach that prioritizes connecting people experiencing homelessness to housing as
L109@P4: quickly as possible to end their homelessness. Housing fist prioritizes client choice in the housing process
L110@P4: and supportive service participation.
L111@P4: Housing Provider is a participating CES partner agency that creates housing opportunities in HMIS that
L112@P4: are available for CES households through weekly match meetings. These opportunities can be rapid rehousing, permanent supportive housing, or other tenant-based vouchers.
L113@P4: Individual is an adult aged 18+. This type of household is eligible for the Individual Coordinated Entry
L114@P4: System program enrollment, regardless of the number of adults in the household.
L115@P4: Interim Housing is a type of emergency shelter that provides temporary shelter for people experiencing
L116@P4: homelessness, meet basic needs such as food, safety, and hygiene, and be supported to see and obtain
L117@P4-5: housing.6

L119@P5: Match Meeting is the virtual meeting where households are referred to supportive services or housing
L120@P5: opportunities made available by supportive service providers and housing providers. These meetings are
L121@P5: moderated by the Individual and Family CES administrative agencies, as well as the Office of Care
L122@P5: Coordination for the Transitional Aged Youth and Veteran registries. These meetings are required to take
L123@P5: place weekly, unless there are no opportunities made available for that week, or during a dark week
L124@P5: (explained in a later section).
L125@P5: Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH) is housing that includes supportive service to assist formerly
L126@P5: homeless individuals and families with a disabling condition to live independently and maintain housing
L127@P5: stability. These housing opportunities utilize two different models, entire housing developments, and
L128@P5: scattered sites where units can be in different housing developments.
L129@P5: Public Housing Authority (PHA) a participating CES agency that is a public entity, often at the local or state
L130@P5: level, authorized to develop, operate, or assist in the development of housing. These authorities manage
L131@P5: publicly owned housing and other federally subsidized housing programs. In Orange County, there are four
L132@P5: PHAs, Anaheim Housing Authority, Garden Grove Housing Authority, Santa Ana Housing Authority, and
L133@P5: Orange County Housing Authority.
L134@P5: Rapid Rehousing (RRH) is housing with time limited rental assistance and supportive services to assist
L135@P5: individuals and families experiencing homelessness, with or without a disabling condition, move as quickly
L136@P5: as possible into housing and maintain housing stability. For RRH opportunities, the maximum amount of
L137@P5: rent that a household may pay can be up to 100% of the rental amount. In general, the goal will be that
L138@P5: households pay no more than 50% of their income in rent at RRH program completion.
L139@P5: Referral is a successful match for a CES participating household to an appropriate CES service such as
L140@P5: emergency shelter, supportive services, or housing opportunity. This may also be known as a match.
L141@P5: Referral Partner is a CES participating agency that makes matches (referrals) to programs and services
L142@P5: made available by housing, emergency shelter, and supportive service providers for CES households.
L143@P5: Service Planning Area (SPA) is a regional sector within Orange County. The three geographic regions are
L144@P5: the North, Central and South Service Planning Areas designated to improve service coordination and align
L145@P5: resources among regional citiesin Orange County. Reference the Planning section for the Service Planning
L146@P5: Areas.
L147@P5: Shelter collectively refers to emergency shelter, including Interim Housing, and transitional housing. These
L148@P5: are temporary living situations provided to meet basic needs while the individual or family experiencing
L149@P5: homelessness does not have permanent housing.
L150@P5: Street Outreach is a program where an organization will perform direct service referrals for those who are
L151@P5: experiencing unsheltered homelessness.
L152@P5: Survivor is any individual or family who is fleeing, or attempting to flee, domestic violence; has no other
L153@P5: residency; and lacks the resources or support networks to obtain permanent housing. This type of
L154@P5: household is eligible to be enrolled into Coordinated Entry System for Survivors, if applicable.
L155@P5: Transitional Aged Youth (TAY) is a household where the head of the household is someone aged 18 – 24.
L156@P5-6: This can be either an adult only household or a family and can be eligible for the Individual or Family 7
L157@P6: October 2025
L158@P6: Coordinated Entry System, depending on household composition. This household will also be eligible for
L159@P6: any TAY specific housing opportunities.
L160@P6: Transitional Housing means temporary, supportive housing, where all households have signed a lease or
L161@P6: occupancy agreement, with the primary purpose of facilitating movement of individuals and families
L162@P6: experiencing homelessness into permanent housing. Transitional housing is generally provided for a
L163@P6: limited time period, from two weeks up to 24 months.
L164@P6: United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) is a cabinet-level agency that
L165@P6: oversees federal programs designed to help Americans with their housing needs. HUD seeks to increase
L166@P6: access to affordable housing.
L167@P6: Veteran an individual who has served in the United States Armed Forces (Army, Marines, Navy, Coast
L168@P6: Guard, Space Force, either in active or reservist status) and has had their service verified by the office of
L169@P6: Veteran Affairs.
L170@P6: Victim Service Provider (VSP) HUD defines a VSP to mean a private nonprofit organization whose primary
L171@P6: mission is to provide direct services to victims of domestic violence. This term includes permanent housing
L172@P6: providers- including rapid re-housing, domestic violence programs (shelters and non-residential), domestic
L173@P6: violence transitional housing programs, dual domestic violence and sexual assault programs, and related
L174@P6: advocacy and support services programs.
L175@P6: Violence Against Women Act (VAWA)1 a federal law that, in part, provides housing protections for people
L176@P6: applying for or living in units subsidized by the federal government and who have experienced domestic
L177@P6: violence, dating violence, sexual assault, or stalking, to help keep them safe and reduce their likelihood of
L178@P6: experiencing homelessness.
L179@P6: II. Introduction
L180@P6: The Coordinated Entry System (CES) standardizes the way individuals and families at risk of
L181@P6: homelessness or experiencing homelessness access are assessed for and referred to the housing and
L182@P6: services that they need for housing stability.
L183@P6: The Office of Care Coordination is the lead CES Agency for the Orange County Continuum of Care (CoC).
L184@P6: The Orange County CoC utilizes three different components that comprise CES across the CoC. These
L185@P6: three components are Individual CES (ICES), Family CES (FCES), and CES for Survivors (SCES).
L186@P6: Additionally, the Office of Care Coordination manages two additional registries: one for Veterans, and
L187@P6: one for Transitional Aged Youth (TAY). These two additional subpopulations may have specific housing
L188@P6: opportunities and supportive services that verified Veterans and TAY eligible households may qualify for.
L189@P6: More information on these three CES subpopulations and two registries can be found on the Orange
L190@P6: County CES Learning Academy.

L193@P7: III. Goal
L194@P7: 
L195@P7: The goal of the Coordinated Entry System (CES) is to effectively connect individuals and families
L196@P7: experiencing homelessness or at risk of homelessness to appropriate services and housing interventions
L197@P7: to address and end homelessness in Orange County through:
L198@P7: • Dynamic prioritization
L199@P7: • Collaborative coordination
L200@P7: • Intentional resource utilization
L201@P7: • Equitable resource distribution
L202@P7: • Regional Service Planning Area prioritization
L203@P7: CES is required to incorporate all programs funded through the Continuum of Care (CoC), the Emergency
L204@P7: Solutions Grants (ESG) and the Homeless Housing, Assistance and Prevention (HHAP) programs. Other
L205@P7: services, shelter and housing programs not receiving any of the funding listed above may voluntarily
L206@P7: participate in CES. The goal is to incorporate as many homeless service programs as possible into CES.
L207@P7: These policies and procedures cover referrals for all resources available through CES, including:
L208@P7: • All CoC, ESG, and HHAP -funded programs, including survivor dedicated resources.
L209@P7: • Federal, State, and Local funded programs required to participate in CES.
L210@P7: • Any resources that voluntarily participate in CES.

L271@P9: a housing crisis. Access Points include mobile street outreach teams, navigation centers, emergency
L272@P9: shelters and supportive services agencies.

L274@P9: • Connect eligible households with CES through a standard assessment process.

L277@P9: • Submit a new/updated Current Living Situation Assessment, at minimum, every 90 days or the
L278@P9: household will be automatically removed from being referred to any housing opportunities.
L279@P9: Maintain accurate and timely data in HMIS, as outlined in the annual CES Participating Agency Agreement
L280@P9: form, or a comparable database for survivors.

L339@P12: VI. Access

L341@P12: The Orange County CES is operated so that households seeking housing or supportive services can easily
L342@P12: access services. CES and available housing and supportive services are widely advertised throughout the
L343@P12: CoC. Marketing strategies include digital and printed media. By displaying posters, flyers, and resource
L344@P12: guides, participating agencies within each SPA will support marketing efforts and mainstream partners
L345@P12: such as libraries, schools, police stations, and community centers will be invited to support marketing
L346@P12: efforts as well. Training explaining how the Orange County CES works is available for a wide variety of
L347@P12: partners at egovoc.com/ces-training. In some cases, mainstream partners, such as U.S. Department of
L348@P12: Veteran Affairs (VA), the Orange County Health Care Agencies (HCA) and Social Services Agency (SSA)
L349@P12: Family Resource Centers, may serve as Access Points.
L350@P12: Any required or recommended training will cover CES policies and procedures including assessment

L854@P26: Chronic Homelessness Definition
L855@P26: HUD published the Defining Chronically Homeless Final Rule clarifying the definition of chronic
L856@P26: homelessness. The definition of chronically homeless is:
L857@P26: • A homeless individual with a disability as defined in section 401(9) of the McKinney-Vento
L858@P26: Assistance Act (42 U.S.C. 11360(9)), who:
L859@P26: o Lives in a place not meant for human habitation, a safe haven, or in an emergency shelter,
L860@P26: and
L861@P26: o Has been homeless and living as described for at least 12 months* or on at least 4
L862@P26: separate occasions in the last 3 years, as long as the combined occasions equal at least 12
L863@P26: months and each break in homelessness separating the occasions included at least 7
L864@P26: consecutive nights of not living as described.
L865@P26: • An individual who has been residing in an institutional care facility, including jail, substance abuse
L866@P26: or mental health treatment facility, hospital, or other similar facility, for fewer than 90 days and
L867@P26: met all of the criteria of this definition before entering that facility**; or
L868@P26: • A family with an adult head of household (or, if there is no adult in the family, a minor head of
L869@P26: household) who meets all of the criteria of this definition, including a family whose composition
L870@P26: has fluctuated while the head of household has been homeless.
L871@P26: *A “break” in homeless is considered to be 7 or more nights.
L872@P26: **An individual residing in an institutional care facility for less than 90 days does not constitute a break in
L873@P26: homelessness.
