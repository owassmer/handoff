L92@P4: Chapter 1 – Policy Overview
L93@P4: 1.1 Objective
L94@P4: The primary objective of the Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook (CCCPH) is
L95@P4: the protection of public health through the establishment of standards intended to
L96@P4: ensure a public water system’s (PWS) drinking water distribution system will not be
L97@P4: subject to the backflow of liquids, gases, or other substances. In addition, by providing
L98@P4: basic educational information on backflow prevention, the State Water Resources
L99@P4: Control Board (State Water Board) intends to build a foundation of awareness within the
L100@P4: regulated community regarding the importance of backflow protection and crossconnection control, leading to the implementation of a robust cross-connection control
L101@P4: program for PWSs.
L102@P4: 1.2 Applicability
L103@P4: The CCCPH and its standards apply to all California PWSs, as defined in California’s
L104@P4: Health and Safety Code (CHSC, section 116275 (h)). Compliance with this CCCPH is
L105@P4: mandatory for all California PWSs.
L106@P4: 1.3 Policy Development Background and Legal Authorities
L107@P4: Through the adoption of the CCCPH, the State Water Board is exercising its authority,
L108@P4: under California’s Safe Drinking Water Act0F

L110@P4: (SDWA), to establish enforceable standards
L111@P4: applicable to California’s PWSs. Failure to comply with the CCCPH may result in the
L112@P4: issuance of compliance, enforcement, or other corrective actions against a PWS.

L181@P8: Chapter 2 – Background on Backflow Protection
L182@P8: and Cross-Connection Control
L183@P8: 2.1 What is a Cross-Connection?
L184@P8: A cross-connection is an interconnection between a potable water supply and a nonpotable source via any actual or potential connection or structural arrangement between
L185@P8: a PWS and any source or distribution system containing liquid, gas, or other substances
L186@P8: not from an approved water supply. Bypass arrangements, jumper connections,
L187@P8: removable sections, improperly installed swivel or change-over devices and other
L188@P8: temporary or permanent devices through which, or because of which backflow can
L189@P8: occur are considered to be cross-connections.4F
L190@P8: 5 The CCCPH includes acceptable
L191@P8: installation criteria for swivel-ell and other types of backflow prevention assemblies
L192@P8: (BPAs) to prevent backflow.
L193@P8: Backflow is the undesired or unintended reversal of flow of water and/or other liquids,
L194@P8: gases, or other substances into a PWS’s distribution system or approved water supply.
L195@P8: The presence of a cross-connection represents a location in a distribution system
L196@P8: through which backflow of contaminants or pollutants can occur. Backflow occurs when
L197@P8: a non-potable source is at a greater pressure than the potable water distribution system.
L198@P8: Backflow can occur from either backsiphonage or backpressure. Backsiphonage
L199@P8: occurs when a non-potable source enters the drinking water supply due to negative
L200@P8: (i.e., sub-atmospheric) distribution system pressure. Backpressure occurs when the
L201@P8: pressure from a non-potable source exceeds the pressure in the potable water
L202@P8: distribution system.
L203@P8: Backsiphonage may be caused by a variety of circumstances, such as main breaks,
L204@P8: flushing, pump failure, or emergency firefighting water demand. Backpressure may
L205@P8: occur when heating, cooling, waste disposal, or industrial manufacturing systems are
L206@P8: connected to potable supplies and the pressure in the external system exceeds the
L207@P8: pressure in the distribution system. Both situations act to change the direction of water,
L208@P8: which normally flows from the distribution system to the customer, so that non-potable
L209@P8: substances from industrial, commercial, or residential premises flows back into the
L210@P8: distribution system through a cross-connection.
L211@P8: Cross-connections are not limited to industrial or commercial facilities. Submerged
L212@P8: inlets are found on many common plumbing fixtures and are sometimes necessary
L213@P8: features of the fixtures if they are to function properly. Examples of this type of design
L214@P8: are siphon-jet urinals or water closets, flushing rim slop sinks, and dental cuspidors.
L215@P8: 5 California Department of Health Services (DHS), Public Water Supply Branch. (1988). Guidance
L216@P8-9: Manual for cross connection Control Program (Green Manual). California Department of Health Services.7
L217@P9: Older bathtubs and lavatories may have supply inlets below the flood level rims, but
L218@P9: modern sanitary design has minimized or eliminated this cross-connection in new
L219@P9: fixtures. Chemical and industrial process vats sometimes have submerged inlets where
L220@P9: the water pressure is used as an aid in diffusion, dispersion and agitation of the vat
L221@P9: contents. Even though a supply pipe may be installed above a vat, backsiphonage can
L222@P9: still occur. Siphon action has been shown to raise a liquid in a pipe such as water
L223@P9: almost 34 feet. Some submerged inlets are difficult to control, including those which
L224@P9: are not apparent until a significant change in water level occurs or where a supply may
L225@P9: be conveniently extended below the liquid surface by means of a hose or auxiliary
L226@P9: piping. A submerged inlet may be created in numerous ways, and its detection may be
L227@P9: difficult.
L228@P9: Chemical and biological contaminants have caused illness and deaths during known
L229@P9: incidents of backflow, with contamination affecting several service connections, and the
L230@P9: number of incidents reported is believed to be a small percentage of the total number of
L231@P9: backflow incidents that actually occur. The public health risk from cross-connections
L232@P9: and backflow is a function of a variety of factors including cross-connection and
L233@P9: backflow occurrence and type and amount of contaminants.
L234@P9: 2.2 Purpose of a Cross-Connection Control Program
L235@P9: The purpose of a cross-connection control program is to prevent the occurrence of
L236@P9: backflow into a PWS’s distribution system in order to protect customers from
L237@P9: contamination or pollution from any on-site hazards. Properly installed and maintained
L238@P9: BPAs, devices or methods provide protection against the threat posed by many
L239@P9: conditions typically found on a user’s premise.
L240@P9: The use of approved BPAs ensures that the appropriate performance evaluation of the
L241@P9: assembly was conducted. It is important and required by the CCCPH to select and
L242@P9: properly install an approved BPA that is capable of protecting the distribution system
L243@P9: from the hazard identified. The success of a program depends on individuals that are
L244@P9: knowledgeable about cross-connection control to identify actual and potential hazards,
L245@P9: apply principles of backflow protection and prevention, and implement cross-connection
L246@P9: control policies and procedures. A successful program will have ongoing surveillance of
L247@P9: a PWS to ensure BPAs, devices or methods are working, and identify new hazards or
L248@P9: changes in the distribution system. Certified specialists are needed to properly evaluate
L249@P9: the degree of hazard that exists in the distribution system. Hazards typically identified
L250@P9: in distribution systems along with the required level of protection are specified in
L251@P9: Chapter 3 of the CCCPH.
L252@P9: 2.3 Notes on Applicability of the Cross-Connection Control
L253@P9: Policy Handbook
L254@P9: The CCCPH provides the basis for regulating the use and management of crossconnection control programs and BPAs in PWSs, and related requirements for
L255@P9-10: supporting programs and policies. Activities or uses outside of the scope of the 8
L256@P10: authority of the State Water Board to regulate PWSs are not regulated by the CCCPH,
L257@P10: including California Plumbing Code requirements and definitions not related to PWSs.
L258@P10: Recycled water cross-connection control installations and programs for the purposes of
L259@P10: protecting the recycled water supply are not regulated by the CCCPH, although a PWS
L260@P10: that uses recycled water is regulated by the CCCPH to ensure that a PWS’s drinking
L261@P10: water system has adequate backflow protection from a recycled water system.
L262@P10: Water systems that do not meet the definition of a PWS (e.g. “State Small Water
L263@P10: Systems” under CCR Title 22, Article 3) are not regulated by the CCCPH, although they
L264@P10: may need to comply with the California Plumbing Code, local health agencies, and other
L265@P10: laws or entities.

L267@P10: comply with both the California Plumbing Code and CCCPH. The California Plumbing
L268@P10: Code and the CCCPH will overlap in protection of these user premises. To ensure
L269@P10: compliance, these noncommunity water systems may need to have internal crossconnection control programs within the user premises.

L271@P10: within the premises. Compliance with the California Plumbing Code can be verified by
L272@P10: the PWS and used for compliance with the CCCPH. Compliance with the CCCPH is
L273@P10: documented through the hazard assessment and maintenance of an inventory of fieldtestable BPAs and methods. Annual field testing of BPAs is required. Where the
L274@P10: minimum backflow protection differs between the California Plumbing Code and the
L275@P10-11: CCCPH, the more protective minimum protection will be required.9
