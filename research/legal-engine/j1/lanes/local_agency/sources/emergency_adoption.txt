# Orange County Proclaims State of Local Emergency Related to Hurricane Marie

September 17, 2026

Image: Graphic from the County of Orange announcing a local emergency related to Hurricane Marie. As of Thursday, September 17 at 3 p.m., the County has proclaimed a local emergency related to Hurricane Marie

PRESS RELEASE# 002
Date: September 17, 2026
Time: 1500 hours
On September 17, 2026, the County of Orange proclaimed a local emergency in
response to the ongoing coastal impacts related to Hurricane Marie. The Director of
Emergency Services requested that the Chair of the Board of Supervisors and Chair of
Orange County's Emergency Management Council Doug Chaffee sign the proclamation
of local emergency. The local emergency proclamation was approved and will be
considered for ratification at a special meeting to be scheduled next week. Details of
the meeting will be forthcoming.
The Cities of Dana Point, Laguna Beach and San Clemente have each proclaimed local
emergencies in response to weather and tidal events caused by Hurricane Marie that
have resulted in significant damage to public infrastructure and private property within
their respective cities.
Orange County coastal cities have requested that the County of Orange proclaim a
Local Emergency in its capacity as the Operational Area (OA) and in support of the
needs of coastal communities and jurisdictions impacted by Hurricane Marie and
additional concern about additional El Nino-related weather events throughout the
upcoming winter season.
The County/QA EOC (Operational Area Emergency Operations Center) continues to be
activated to a Low Level, staffed by the OC Sherriff's Department (OCSD) Emergency
Management Division personnel.

# Orange County declares countywide emergency over Hurricane Marie damage

Josh DuBose

Updated Thu, September 17, 2026 at 11:53 PM UTC

Orange County officials proclaimed a local emergency Thursday as coastal communities continue to deal with widespread erosion, damaged homes and battered infrastructure left behind by powerful swells generated by Hurricane Marie.

The Sept. 17 proclamation comes after Dana Point, Laguna Beach and San Clemente each declared their own local emergencies in response to destructive waves, high tides and severe coastal erosion.

Officials said coastal cities requested that Orange County declare the emergency in its role as the region’s Operational Area, allowing the county to better support communities dealing with damage from Hurricane Marie and prepare for additional weather-related impacts in the months ahead.

The county’s Operational Area Emergency Operations Center remains activated at a low level and is being staffed by Orange County Sheriff’s Department Emergency Management Division personnel.

# Hurricane Marie Swell Aftermath: County Proclaims Local Emergency After Dana Point, Laguna Beach and San Clemente; Beach Road Homes Red-Tagged, Rail Reopened, El Niño Prep Underway

Orange County has proclaimed a local state of emergency for Hurricane Marie coastal damage and El Niño preparation (Sept. 17), following earlier proclamations by Dana Point, Laguna Beach, and San Clemente; the county is requesting a statewide declaration from Gov. Newsom, and state and federal funding commitments are beginning to materialize as structural repairs and beach-restoration work continue.

Mayor John Gabbard said one home had already been demolished and he expected the other nine red-tagged homes would also have to come down. The California Coastal Commission approved emergency permits for rock armoring across 17 properties; homeowners complained of eight pages of emergency-permit stipulations and a $1,600 fee, and no city, county, state or federal financial aid had been offered. Supervisor Katrina Foley said she was pressing the Army Corps of Engineers, the Coastal Commission and the Governor's office on sand replenishment. The city said no city-maintained infrastructure was damaged.

Orange County activated its Emergency Operations Center on Friday, Sept. 11, 2026. Supervisor Katrina Foley asked for a county local-emergency proclamation and said she was in contact with Rep. Mike Levin about a federal declaration, but Board Chair Doug Chaffee split from her, citing concern that a declaration could affect insurance rates; as of Sept. 15 neither the county nor the Governor had proclaimed an emergency for the Hurricane Marie swell damage. Foley said San Clemente and Newport Beach officials had asked the county to declare. San Clemente staff told Voice of OC the pier lost a pylon and 17 braces, with repairs estimated at about $300,000, and the council was expected to consider its own emergency declaration the following week; no San Clemente proclamation had been confirmed by Sept. 15. No Newport Beach or Seal Beach proclamation was found.

Newsom plus sand replenishment and shoreline protection before winter; an executive order covers debris removal, vegetation management and flood-risk reduction. San Clemente's council had asked the county for the proclamation on Sept. 15.

SOURCE →
  * DOC San Clemente emergency proclamation, Sept. 15

On Sept. 15, 2026 the San Clemente City Council unanimously proclaimed a local emergency authorizing sand placement at North Beach, berms to protect the North Beach restrooms and lifeguard headquarters, and up to $1 million in emergency spending; pier damage (one pylon, 17 braces, flooding) is estimated at about $300,000. NWS put the chance of a very strong El Niño this winter at about 90%. On Sept. 25 the OC Register reported the city will receive $1 million in state funding for the North Beach sand placement.

SOURCE →
  * DOC County proclamation mechanics; state and federal follow-ons

The county's Sept. 17, 2026 local emergency was proclaimed at 3 p.m. by Board Chair Doug Chaffee, as Emergency Management Council chair, at the request of the emergency services director, with ratification set for a special Board meeting the following week; the county acted as the Operational Area after Dana Point (Sept. 9, ratified Sept. 15), Laguna Beach and San Clemente (Sept. 15) proclaimed their own, and the Operational Area EOC stayed at a low activation staffed by OCSD Emergency Management. Supervisor Katrina Foley asked Gov. Newsom for a state proclamation the same day. Damage snapshot as of Sept. 14: Laguna Beach yellow-tagged six homes; Dana Point yellow-tagged seven and red-tagged nine to ten on Beach Road. On Sept. 23 Voice of OC reported OCFA is in line for more than $1.8 million in federal funds for a dedicated search-and-rescue task force ahead of El Niño. A Sept. 25 Strickland release says the governor has proclaimed a state of emergency for the coming El Niño season; a secondary account dates that to Sept. 21 and describes it as statewide (unverified against the governor's office).
