In an effort to prevent additional fires and discourage illegal dumping incidents, DTSC has adopted the “Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption.”

## Regulation Overview

The Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption was first adopted as a temporary emergency rulemaking on October 7, 2024. It was then readopted twice, with the first readoption taking effect on April 7, 2025, and the second on July 8, 2025. On November 17, 2025, the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption became a permanent subsection in the California Code of Regulations through the regular rulemaking process.

This exemption amends section 66261.6 of title 22 of the California Code of Regulations by adopting an existing federal recycling exemption for industrial ethyl alcohol in 40 Code of Federal Regulations, section 261.6(a)(3)(i). Promulgated in 1985, this exemption was intended to reduce redundancy with existing distillery regulations established by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms (now the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau or TTB), and it initially applied to “spent” industrial ethyl alcohol alone. 
This rulemaking exempts spent, unused, and off-specification industrial ethyl alcohol from most of the hazardous waste regulations established in the California Code of Regulations. This exemption is intended to encourage generators to bypass the current hazardous waste incinerator backlog through recycling, which will decrease the retention time of ethyl alcohol-based hand sanitizer stockpiles and reduce the risk of fires and illegal dumping. It should be noted, however, that the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption does not apply to isopropyl alcohol-based hand sanitizers, nor to household hazardous waste. The full text of this exemption, and its accompanying rulemaking documents, can be accessed from the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption’s Final Regulations webpage.

## Information on Potential Alternatives

Through our research and based on public comments, we identified ethyl methacrylate (EMA) as a potential alternative to MMA. EMA is a member of the same chemical class as MMA (acrylic monomers) and has similar physicochemical properties and hazard traits. According to information DTSC received from manufacturers, MMA can be found as a contaminant in EMA-containing nail products.

DTSC has not vetted EMA for safety, functionality, or performance, and providing this information does not constitute an endorsement by DTSC. Manufacturers that are responsible for complying with DTSC’s listing of nail products containing MMA at concentrations greater than 1,000 ppm as a Priority Product will need to consider the safety and performance of alternatives if submitting an Alternatives Analysis Report.
