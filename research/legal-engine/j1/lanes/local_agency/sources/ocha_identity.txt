L33@P2: provides an overview of the family Self-Sufficiency program and the purpose of the FSS
L34@P2: Action Plan.

L39@P3: PART I: THE FAMILY SELF-SUFFICIENCY (FSS) PROGRAM
L40@P3: AND FSS ACTION PLAN
L41@P3: 1-I.A. OVERVIEW OF THE FAMILY SELF-SUFFICIENCY PROGRAM
L42@P3: The origins of the FSS program are in two pilot projects implemented in 1986 and 1990, Project
L43@P3: Self-Sufficiency and Operation Bootstrap, respectively. These projects were set up to test selfsufficiency programs for families with housing subsidies, and both demonstrated that families
L44@P3: needed essential services in order to move toward economic self-sufficiency. These services
L45@P3: include child care, transportation, medical care, and long-term education and training.
L46@P3: In the wake of the successful demonstration of these projects, family self-sufficiency became one
L47@P3: of the initiatives under the Homeownership and Housing Opportunities for People Everywhere
L48@P3: (HOPE) program enacted in 1990, and the FSS program was subsequently created under the
L49@P3: National Affordable Housing Act the same year.
L50@P3: FSS built upon and refined both Project Self-Sufficiency and the bootstrap program. It remained
L51@P3: a voluntary program in 1991 and 1992, but became mandatory in 1993 for any new increments
L52@P3: of funding issued to PHAs. The 1993 regulations were further modified by the Quality Housing
L53@P3: and Work Responsibility Act of 1998 (QHWRA).
L54@P3: The purpose of the FSS program is to coordinate housing assistance with public and private
L55@P3: resources to enable assisted families to achieve economic self-sufficiency. The purpose and basic
L56@P3: requirements of the FSS program are further elaborated upon in Chapter 2.
L57@P3: This Family Self-Sufficiency program is administered by the Orange County Housing Authority
L58@P3: (OCHA) for the jurisdiction of the County of Orange.
L59@P3: 1-I.B. APPLICABLE REGULATIONS
L60@P3: Applicable regulations for Housing Choice Voucher FSS programs include:
L61@P3:  24 CFR Part 5: General Program Requirements
L62@P3:  24 CFR Part 8: Nondiscrimination
L63@P3:  4 CFR Part 903: Public Housing Agency Plans
L64@P3:  24 CFR Part 982: Section 8 Tenant-Based Assistance: Housing Choice Voucher Program
L65@P3:  24 CFR Part 984: Section 8 and Public Housing Family Self-Sufficiency Program
L66@P3: 1-I.C. THE FAMILY SELF-SUFFICIENCY ACTION PLAN
L67@P3: The FSS Action Plan is required by HUD. The purpose of the FSS Action Plan is to establish
L68@P3: policies for carrying out the FSS program in a manner consistent with HUD requirements and
L69@P3: local goals and objectives contained in the PHA’s Agency Plan. This FSS Action Plan is a
L70@P3: supporting document to the PHA Agency Plan, and is available for public review as required by
L71@P3: 24 CFR Part 903.
L72@P3: This FSS Action Plan is set forth to define the PHA’s local policies for operation of the program
L73@P3: in the context of federal laws and regulations. All issues related to FSS not addressed in this
L74@P3-4: document are governed by such federal regulations, HUD handbooks and guidebooks, notices, Page 3 of 47
L75@P4: and other applicable laws. The policies in this FSS Action Plan have been designed to ensure
L76@P4: compliance with the consolidated ACC and all HUD-approved applications for program funding.
L77@P4: The PHA is responsible for complying with all changes in HUD regulations pertaining to the
L78@P4: FSS program. If such changes conflict with this plan, HUD regulations will take precedence.
L79@P4: Administration of the FSS program and the functions and responsibilities of PHA staff shall be
L80@P4: in compliance with the PHA’s personnel policy and HUD’s FSS regulations, as well as all
L81@P4: Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) regulations, in addition to federal, state, and local fair housing
L82@P4-5: laws and regulations.Page 4 of 47
L83@P5: PART II. REQUIREMENTS OF THE FSS ACTION PLAN
L84@P5: 1-II.A. OVERVIEW
L85@P5: A PHA must have a HUD-approved Action Plan before implementing an FSS program,
L86@P5: regardless of whether the FSS program is a mandatory or voluntary program. Further, this action
L87@P5: plan must comply with the requirements specified for the plan in the regulations
L88@P5: [24 CFR 984.201(a)].
L89@P5: The regulatory requirements dealing specifically with the FSS Action Plan itself largely involve
L90@P5: the development, revision, and required contents of the Action Plan. This part covers those
L91@P5: requirements.
L92@P5: 1-II.B. HUD APPROACH TO POLICY DEVELOPMENT
L93@P5: In developing policy for the FSS Action Plan, PHAs need to be aware of the distinction HUD
L94@P5: makes between mandatory and discretionary policies.
L95@P5:  Mandatory policies are those driven by legislation, regulations, current handbooks, notices,
L96@P5: and legal opinions.
L97@P5:  Discretionary policies consist of those developed for areas in which the PHA has regulatory
L98@P5: discretion, or with regard to optional, nonbinding guidance including guidebooks, notices
L99@P5: that have expired, and recommendations from individual HUD staff.
L100@P5: HUD expects PHAs to develop policies and procedures that are consistent with mandatory
L101@P5: regulations and to make clear the optional policies the PHA has adopted. The PHA’s FSS Action
L102@P5: Plan is the foundation of those policies and procedures for the FSS program. HUD’s directions
L103@P5: require PHAs to make policy choices that provide guidance to staff and consistency to program
L104@P5: applicants and participants.
L105@P5: Following HUD guidance, even though it is not mandatory, provides a PHA with a “safe
L106@P5: harbor.” HUD has already determined that the recommendations and suggestions it makes are
L107@P5: consistent with mandatory policies. If a PHA adopts an alternative strategy, it must make its own
L108@P5: determination that the alternative approach is consistent with legislation, regulations, and other
L109@P5: mandatory requirements. There may be very good reasons for adopting a policy or procedure that
L110@P5-6: is different than HUD’s safe harbor, but PHAs should carefully consider those decisions.Page 5 of 47
L111@P6: 1-II.C. FSS ACTION PLAN DEVELOPMENT AND REVISION
L112@P6: Development of Action Plan [24 CFR 984.201(b) and (c)]
L113@P6: When developing an FSS Action Plan, a PHA must do so in consultation with the Chief
L114@P6: Executive Officer of the applicable unit of general local government and the Program
L115@P6: Coordinating Committee (PCC).
L116@P6: In addition, a PHA that is establishing its FSS program must submit an action plan to HUD for
L117@P6: approval within 90 days after the PHA receives notice from HUD of approval of the PHA's
L118@P6: application for funding that establishes the obligation to operate an FSS program. This deadline
L119@P6: is required unless the dates are extended by HUD for good cause.
L120@P6: For voluntary FSS programs, the PHA must submit its action plan and obtain HUD approval of
L121@P6: the plan before it can implement the FSS program. This includes a voluntary program established
L122@P6: because the PHA chose to implement an FSS program that exceeds the minimum size for a
L123@P6: mandatory program (see Section 2-II.A. for a discussion of mandatory versus voluntary
L124@P6: FSS programs).
L125@P6: Revision to the FSS Action Plan [24 CFR 984.201(c)(2)]
L126@P6: Following HUD’s initial approval of the Action Plan, no further approval of the Action Plan is
L127@P6: required unless the PHA proposes to make policy changes to the Action Plan or increase the size
L128@P6: of a voluntary program, or to revise the FSS Action Plan as needed to comply with changes in
L129@P6: HUD regulations. The PHA must submit any changes to the Action Plan to HUD for approval.
L130@P6: OCHA Policy
L131@P6: OCHA will revise the Action Plan as needed, and will submit for HUD approval, in
L132@P6: accordance with above.
L133@P6: 1-II.D. CONTENTS OF THE PLAN [24CFR 984.201(d)]
L134@P6: HUD regulations state that there are several components that must be included in the FSS Action
L135@P6: Plan. At a minimum, the Action Plan must cover the policies and procedures of the PHA for
L136@P6: operation of a local FSS program as follows:
L137@P6:  Family demographics, including a description of the number, size, characteristics, and other
L138@P6: demographics such as racial and ethnic data, in addition to the supportive service needs of the
L139@P6: families expected to participate in the program. (Chapter 1)
L140@P6:  Estimate of participating families, which means the number of families which can reasonably
L141@P6: be expected to receive supportive services under the FSS program.
L142@P6: (Chapter 2)
L143@P6:  Eligible families from any other local self-sufficiency program who are expected to agree to
L144@P6: execute an FSS contract of participation. (Chapter 2)
L145@P6:  Timetable for program implementation, including the schedule for filling FSS slots with
L146@P6-7: eligible families. (Chapter 2)Page 6 of 47
L147@P7:  A statement of the PHA’s FSS family selection procedures, including a description of how
L148@P7: the procedures ensure that families are selected without regard to race, color, religion,
L149@P7: disability, sex, familial status, or national origin. (Chapter 4)
L150@P7:  A description of the incentives that the PHA intends to offer to families to encourage
L151@P7: participation in the FSS program (an incentives plan), including the establishment of the
L152@P7: escrow account. (Chapter 4)
L153@P7:  Outreach efforts, which include a description of the PHA’s efforts to recruit eligible families,
L154@P7: the actions the PHA will take to ensure that both minority and nonminority groups are
L155@P7: informed about the FSS program, and how the PHA will make this information known.
L156@P7: (Chapter 4)
L157@P7:  A description of the FSS activities and supportive services to be provided by both public and
L158@P7: private resources to FSS families, and identification of these public and private resources.
