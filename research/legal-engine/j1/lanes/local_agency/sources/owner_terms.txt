L9@P1: THE HOUSING CHOICE VOUCHER
L10@P1: The Housing Choice Voucher (HCV) program (also known as the Section 8 program) is the federal
L11@P1: government's primary program for assisting very low-income families, the elderly, and the disabled to afford
L12@P1: decent, safe, and sanitary housing in the private market.
L13@P1: Housing assistance is provided by subsidizing a portion of the family’s rent, allowing voucher holders to
L14@P1: access affordable housing opportunities. The voucher holder is free to choose any type dwelling unit if it
L15@P1: meets certain requirements for Affordability, Rent Reasonableness and Housing Quality Standard (HQS).
L16@P1: Various dwelling types are acceptable for program participation, such as: apartments, condominiums,
L17@P1: manufactured/mobile homes, single-family homes, and may include the family's current residence. The
L18@P1: landlord retains private property rights including management, tenant selection, and maintenance. HUD
L19@P1: determines the rules and regulations for the Housing Choice Voucher Program.
L20@P1: Housing Choice Vouchers are administered locally by Public Housing Agencies (PHAs). The PHAs receive
L21@P1: federal funds from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) to administer the
L22@P1: voucher program. The Orange County Housing Authority (OCHA) administers the Housing Choice Voucher
L23@P1: Program for the County of Orange and 31 cities within Orange County. Anaheim, Garden Grove, and Santa
L24@P1: Ana operate their own programs. The OCHA currently assists over 10,000 households throughout the County
L25@P1: with the participation of over 3,600 landlords.
L26@P1: A family that is issued a housing voucher is responsible for finding a suitable housing unit of the family's
L27@P1: choice where the owner agrees to rent under the program. This unit may include the family's present
L28@P1: residence. A housing subsidy is paid to the landlord directly by the PHA on behalf of the participating family.
L29@P1: The family then pays the difference between the actual rent charged by the landlord and the amount
L30@P1: subsidized by the program.
L31@P1: The PHA determines a payment standard, which is based on the Fair Market Rent (FMR) established by HUD.
L32@P1: The payment standard is used to calculate the amount of housing assistance a family will receive. However,
L33@P1: the payment standard does not limit and does not affect the amount of rent a landlord may charge or the
L34@P1: family may pay. A family which receives a housing voucher can select a unit with a rent that is below or
L35@P1: above the payment standard. The family is required to pay a minimum of 30% of their adjusted monthly
L36@P1: income toward rent, with the OCHA paying the difference up to the payment standard. By program
L37@P1: regulation, whenever a family moves to a new unit where the rent exceeds the payment standard, the family
L38@P1-2: may not pay more than 40 percent of their adjusted monthly income for rent.3
L39@P2: BENEFITS FOR THE LANDLORD
L40@P2: By collaborating with the OCHA, property owners have access to tenants with valuable rental subsidies,
L41@P2: which are designed to protect tenants from life’s adverse events, such as job loss, with the added benefit of
L42@P2: providing greater financial security to property owners.
L43@P2: TOP FINANCIAL ADVANTAGES ENJOYED BY PROPERTY OWNERS WHO RENT TO SECTION 8 ARTICIPANTS
L44@P2: • ON-TIME, CONVENIENT PAYMENTS – The OCHA pays its portion of the rent at the beginning of the
L45@P2: month and offers the convenience of direct deposit.
L46@P2: • PROTECTION FROM TENANT FINANCIAL LOSSES – When a participant’s income decreases, the
L47@P2: housing authority’s portion increases to cover the loss of income. This provides the property owner
L48@P2: and tenant with a valuable form of insurance against tenant income losses.
L49@P2: • FREE MARKETING AND ENDLESS SUPPLY OF POTENTIAL TENANTS – The OCHA is eager to list your
L50@P2: vacancy for free. By advertising with the OCHA through their referral listings, property owners have
L51@P2: access to a large pool of tenants. Property owners should still screen all potential tenants.
L52@P2: • INCENTIVE FOR TENANT LEASE COMPLIANCE – To increase tenant compliance, the OCHA provides
L53@P2: counseling, warnings or reminders to participants who have received notices from their property
L54@P2: owners and are at risk of eviction. If it is proven that a tenant violated their lease, the tenant may be
L55@P2: terminated from the HCV program.
L56@P2: • SAFEGUARD AGAINST LOSS RENT DURING EVICTION – Unfortunately, not every tenant (Section 8 or
L57@P2: otherwise) will live up to his or her lease agreement and the property owner will be forced to evict. If
L58@P2: this occurs with an HCV tenant, the OCHA will continue to pay the Housing Authority’s share of the
L59@P2: rent through the eviction process until the date the tenant moves out or is locked out.
L60@P2: • COMPETITIVE RENTS – The OCHA pays market rents if comparable rents exist in the neighborhood
L61@P2: and the tenant’s rent portion is affordable. If a unit is not subject to local rent control rules, there are
L62@P2: no caps on rent increases for HCV tenants, if comparable rents continue to exist in the neighborhood.
L63@P2: • PREVENTION OF OVERCROWDING – Throughout Southern California, families are doubled or tripled
L64@P2: up in two and three-bedroom units in order to afford the rent. Overcrowding increases, the property
L65@P2: owner’s utility costs, places additional wear and tear on the unit, and leads to conflicts between
L66@P2: neighbors. By renting to an HCV participant, a property owner is not faced with overcrowding. The
L67@P2: OCHA restricts the number of individuals allowed in the unit according to the number of bedrooms in
L68@P2: the unit.
L69@P2: • FREE UNIT INSPECTIONS – Under the Section 8 program, the Housing Authority conducts unit
L70@P2: inspections. Usually, the repairs requested by the Housing Authority are minor and related to routine
L71@P2: maintenance. However, the inspection can reveal a problem in its early stages, such as a small roof
L72@P2-3: lead, minor plumbing issue, or termite problem, before significant damage is done.4

L86@P4: HOW TO PARTICIPATE
L87@P4: If you are approached by a prospective HCV 8 participant, follow these easy steps:
L88@P4: 1. Verify that the person has been issued a current voucher from a Housing Authority. You may request
L89@P4: that the participant show you their voucher.
L90@P4: 2. Screen the applicant using your normal screening procedures. The Housing Authority has not
L91@P4: screened the family’s behavior or suitability for tenancy.
L92@P4: 3. Following the screening, the landlord and the voucher holder completes the Request for Tenancy
L93@P4: Approval (RTA).
L94@P4: What does the Housing Authority do with the Request for Tenancy Approval?
L95@P4: 1. Check the paperwork for completeness.
L96@P4: 2. Confirm that the rent does not exceed the tenant’s maximum allowable rent.
L97@P4: 3. Conduct a Rent Reasonable test to ensure the rent charged is reasonable. The rent must not exceed
L98@P4: rents for comparable, unassisted units in the area.
L99@P4: 4. The Housing Specialist will contact you to conduct an HQS move-in inspection.
L100@P4: 5. The Housing Specialist will conduct the HQS inspection. Inspections will be conducted within seven
L101@P4: business days of the date the RTA was received (unless the unit is not vacant or is not ready for
L102@P4: inspection). The lease and contract will become effective the day after the inspection passes HQS
L103@P4: and the date agreed upon by the owner and tenant.
L104@P4: 6. If the participant and the landlord are both present at the inspection, both the rental agreement and
L105@P4: the Housing Assistances Payments Contract (HAP) may be completed at that time.
L106@P4: The Security Deposit will be paid by the tenant. The amount should not exceed what is charged in the
L107@P4: private market or what is paid by unassisted tenants.
L108@P4: Lead-Based Paint Regulations that became effective September 15, 2000 require the OCHA inspectors to
L109@P4: conduct a visual assessment for deteriorated paint in dwelling units built before 1978 and occupied by
L110@P4: families with children less than 6 years of age. The OCHA encourages owners and managers of rental
L111@P4: properties to conduct an inspection of the property prior to our inspection. If the unit meets the above
L112@P4: criteria and/or you have questions, please contact our office at 714-480-2866. For more information, you
L113@P4-5: may also call 1-800 424-LEAD for guidelines or visit www.hud.gov/offices/lead.6
L114@P5: HOUSING QUALITY STANDARDS
L115@P5: For your reference, we have listed some of the most common reasons that cause a unit to fail Housing
L116@P5: Quality Standards (HQS) inspections. You may use this list to conduct a walkthrough of your unit prior to the
L117@P5: HQS inspection. Please be advised that the Orange County Housing Authority will not be able to enter into a
L118@P5: Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) contract if the unit fails the HQS inspection. This listing is not considered
L119@P5: to be a complete list of causes for HQS failure. It is just a point of reference that you can use prior to the HQS
L120@P5: inspection.
L121@P5: • All ceilings, walls and floors must be strong, sturdy and in their permanent positions.
L122@P5: • A working smoke detector with a live battery must be installed on every level of the unit, including in
L123@P5: the basement and outside of sleeping rooms. If the dwelling unit is occupied by any hearing-impaired
L124@P5: person, smoke detectors must have an alarm system designed for hearing impaired persons as
L125@P5: specified in NFPA 74 (or successor standards).
L126@P5: • The entire unit, both inside and outside, including window frames, must be free of cracking, scaling,
L127@P5: chipping, and loose paint. This prevents exposure to possible lead-based paint hazards.
L128@P5: • Where there are four or more consecutive steps, handrails must be securely attached. This applies to
L129@P5: both the interior and exterior of the unit.
L130@P5: • This unit must be free of roaches, rodents or any other infestations.
L131@P5: • The entire unit, interior and exterior, must be free from electrical hazards. There may be no loose,
L132@P5: hanging, or exposed wires. All three-prong outlets must be wired correctly.
L133@P5: • Every room used for living must have either two working outlets or one working outlet and a
L134@P5: permanently installed light fixture.
L135@P5: • All light switches and outlets must have secured plate undamaged covers installed.
L136@P5: • All windows and doors must be secure when closed, and weather tight.
L137@P5: • All windows and doors that are accessible from the outside must have working secure locks.
L138@P5: • Electrical fixtures and wiring must not pose a fire hazard.
L139@P5: • The bathroom must have either an openable window for ventilation or an exhaust fan.
L140@P5: • The hot water tank’s pressure release valve must have a discharge line extending downward to six
L141@P5: inches from the floor.
L142@P5: • Stove vents must have the appropriate “stove vent” duct tape.
L143@P5: • The water heater must be secured by two straps or anchors.
L144@P5: • Every room used for living must have an adequate heat source. If the source is gas, it must be vented
L145@P5: to the outside. If the source is electric, it must be permanently installed and controlled by a separate
L146@P5: thermostat.
L147@P5-6: • Every bedroom must have at least one openable window for ventilation.7
L148@P6: • The unit must be free from any accumulation of garbage or debris, both inside and outside.
L149@P6: • Dryers in the unit or common areas must be free of lint.
L150@P6: • Clutter or storage of items near a heater or water heater is not acceptable.
L151@P6: • Multi-family owners must provide “refuse disposals;” facilities include trashcans with covers, garbage
L152@P6: chutes or dumpsters with lids.
L153@P6: • Keyed locks on interior doors are not acceptable.
L154@P6: • There may be no cracks on power outlet sockets & cover plates.
L155@P6: • Bathrooms = tub & sink, must have a stopper.
L156@P6: • Carbon Monoxide Alarm must be installed.

L170@P7: LANDLORD’S OBLIGATIONS
L171@P7: The role of the landlord in the HCV program is to provide decent, safe, and sanitary housing to a tenant
L172@P7: at a reasonable rent. The dwelling unit must pass the program's housing quality standards and be
L173@P7: maintained up to those standards as long as the owner receives housing assistance payments. In
L174@P7: addition, the landlord is expected to provide the services agreed to as part of the lease signed with the
L175@P7: tenant and the contract signed with the Housing Authority. Additional requirements:
L176@P7: • Comply with Fair Housing laws and equal opportunity requirements;
L177@P7: • Comply with terms of the Housing Assistance Payments Contract (HAP) - form HUD-52641;
L178@P7: • Screen families for suitability as renters;
L179@P7: • Comply with Housing Quality Standards (HQS) requirements, and Federal, State, and local laws;
L180@P7: • Notify the OCHA in writing of any program violations immediately as it occurs;
L181@P7: • Notify the OCHA in writing if the tenant violates the rental agreement and is evicted by sending
L182@P7: copies of notices served to the tenant;
L183@P7: • Provide a 60-day notice to the tenant with a copy to the OCHA of a rent increase request;
L184@P7: • Housing Assistance Payment (HAP) will continue as long as the tenant remains in the unit or until
L185@P7: a court decision is reached;
L186@P7: • Enforce the rental agreement; collect the rent due and any security deposit or charges for
L187@P7: damages to unit by the family;
L188@P7: • Refund the deposit within 21 days or provide tenant with an accounting of charges when the
L189@P7: tenant vacates;
L190@P7: • Notify the OCHA in writing of any address or telephone number change;
L191@P7-8: • Notify the OCHA in writing of changes in ownership (selling property) or agents.9
L192@P8: FAMILY OBLIGATION
L193@P8: When a family selects a housing unit, and the Housing Authority approves the unit and lease, the family
L194@P8: signs a lease with the landlord for at least one year. The tenant may be required to pay a security
L195@P8: deposit to the landlord. After the first year the landlord may initiate a new lease or allow the family to
L196@P8: remain in the unit on a month-to-month lease.
L197@P8: When the family is settled in a new home, the family is expected to comply with the lease and the
L198@P8: program requirements, pay its share of rent on time, maintain the unit in good condition and notify the
L199@P8: Housing Authority of any changes in income or family composition.
L200@P8: • Provide the OCHA with complete accurate information;
L201@P8: • Comply with the Family Obligations listed on the Voucher;
L202@P8: • Cooperate in attending all appointments scheduled by the OCHA.
L203@P8: • Locate a unit that is suitable for the family, meets HQS requirements, and meets rent
L204@P8: reasonableness criteria (as determined by the OCHA when compared to similar unassisted units
L205@P8: in the immediate vicinity);
L206@P8: • Keep the unit decent, safe and sanitary;
L207@P8: • Pay only the portion of the rent calculated by the Housing specialist;
L208@P8: • Comply with the terms of the rental agreement;
L209@P8: • Notify the OCHA of any changes in income or family composition in writing within 15 days of the
L210@P8: change;
L211@P8: • Provide OCHA and the landlord with a 30-day notice to move in writing as required;
L212@P8: • Cooperate with the landlord by informing him/her of any necessary repairs in writing;
L213@P8-9: • Notify the OCHA of any known program violation.10
