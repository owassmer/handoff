L31@P1: What Is HMIS?
L32@P1: The Homeless Management Information System (HMIS) is a web-based information system used by organizations that
L33@P1: serve homeless and at-risk individuals in Orange County in order to compile information about the persons they serve.
L34@P1: Why Gather and Maintain Data?
L35@P1: HMIS will gather and maintain unduplicated statistics on a regional level to provide a more accurate picture of our
L36@P1: region’s homeless and at-risk population. HMIS will also help us understand client needs, help organizations plan
L37@P1: appropriate resources for the clients they serve, inform public policy in an attempt to end homelessness, streamline and
L38@P1: coordinate services and intake procedures to save client’s valuable time, and so much more.
L39@P1: Written Client Consent
L40@P1: Each client must complete a Consent to Share Protected Personal Information in order for their identifying information
L41@P1: to be shared with other agencies participating in HMIS. If the client refuses to provide consent, only the agency serving
L42@P1: the client will have access to his or her information. Clients cannot be denied services for refusing to provide consent. A
L43@P1: copy of the form will be provided to the client upon request.
L44@P1: Common Questions
L45@P1: What violations are considered HMIS grievances?
L46@P1: Client should complete the HMIS Grievance Form if they feel their privacy and security regarding data stored in HMIS
L47@P1: was violated, or if their HMIS rights were violated. Examples of violations could include the client’s HMIS record being
L48@P1: shared with someone who is not authorized to access their record, or a client being denied access to review their client
L49@P1: record as outlined in the HMIS Policies and Procedures.
L50@P1: Who can access my information?
L51@P1: Only staff who work directly with clients or who have administrative responsibilities can look at, enter, or edit client
L52@P1: information. Please visit our website for a list of the Agencies Contributing Data to HMIS (ochmis.org > About HMIS >
L53@P1: Contributing Agencies). Please note that this list can change frequently and without notice; therefore the website should
L54@P1: be consulted for the most recent list.
L55@P1: Who will receive my information?
L56@P1: No client identifying information (names, dates of birth, etc.) will be released to entities not participating in HMIS without
L57@P1: your consent. Information is stored in an encrypted central database. Only organizations that are contributing data to
L58@P1: HMIS and have agreed to abide by the HMIS Policies and Procedures will have access to HMIS data.
L59@P1: Don’t I have a right to privacy?
L60@P1: Clients do have the right to privacy, and also the right to confidentiality. You are entitled to a copy of the privacy notice
L61@P1: upon request. Clients have the right to know who has modified their HMIS record. You also have the right to request
L62@P1: access to your HMIS client record. Review the HMIS Client Record Requests page (https://ochmis.org/hmis-client-recordrequests/) for more information about this process. You have the right to review this data with agency staff. You may not
L63@P1: see other clients’ records, nor may they see your information.
L64@P1: What if I don’t want to provide information?
L65@P1: Clients have the right not to answer any questions, unless entry into a program requires it. You may not be denied
L66@P1: services based on your refusal to sign a Consent to Share Protected Personal Information.
L67@P1: What if I believe my rights have been violated?
L68@P1: Clients have the right to file a grievance with the agency or with the HMIS Administrative Office at 211OC. Grievances
L69@P1: must be filed through written notice. Clients will not be retaliated against for filing a complaint.
