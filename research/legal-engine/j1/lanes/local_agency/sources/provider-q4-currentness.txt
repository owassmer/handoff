L328@P21: C. Presentation by Tom Roadcamp, Civil Engineer, Brightline West, Regarding
L329@P21: High-Speed Passenger Rail Project Update
L330@P21: CSLB Chair Galarza asked NSCB Chair, Kent Lay, to start the presentation for agenda
L331@P21: item C.
L332@P21: Chair Lay introduced the first agenda item, an update on the high-speed passenger rail
L333@P21: project, presented by Tom Roadcamp, a civil engineer with Brightline West. Roadcamp
L334@P21: brings experience from the Florida line expansion and various safety projects. Chair Lay
L335@P21: welcomed him and invited him to begin his presentation.
L336@P21: Roadcamp said Brightline West is a new high-speed rail project connecting Southern
L337@P21: California to Las Vegas, following the company’s higher-speed rail line operating
L338@P21: between Miami and Orlando. The 220-mile corridor will run from Las Vegas to Rancho
L339@P21: Cucamonga, California, using fully electric trains with zero emissions and no at-grade
L340@P21: crossings. Trains will operate at speeds up to 186 mph, completing the trip in about two
L341@P21: hours, with hourly departures in each direction and an expected annual ridership of 8.6
L342@P21: million passengers.
L343@P21: Roadcamp said the project originated with Desert Express, which completed the first
L344@P21: environmental studies in 2011. Brightline took over in 2018, optimized the design, cost,
L345@P21: and schedule, and in 2023 the project secured a federal-state partnership grant with the
L346@P21: Nevada Department of Transportation. Groundbreaking occurred in 2024, and
L347@P21: construction is now underway. The work is divided into packages, including civil
L348@P21: construction for guideways, structures, and drainage; track and systems installation;
L349@P21: and vertical construction for stations and maintenance facilities.
L350@P21: Roadcamp explained that most of the line will be a single track with passing sections
L351@P21: strategically placed to maintain hourly service. Stations will include a Las Vegas hub
L352@P21: near Interstate 15 and Las Vegas Boulevard, a full-service Victor Valley station, a
L353@P21: commuter-focused Hesperia station, and a major Southern California terminal at
L354@P21: Rancho Cucamonga, which will connect directly to Metrolink and offer large parking
L355@P21: facilities.
L356@P21: Roadcamp stated that Siemens Mobility is supplying 10 trainsets, each with seven cars
L357@P21: and seating for about 430 passengers, with the ability to double capacity during peak
L358@P21: demand. A dedicated maintenance facility in Sloan, Nevada, will handle inspections,

L360@P22: servicing, and heavy maintenance. Roadcamp finished by thanking the NSCB for
L361@P22: allowing the opportunity to provide an update on the project.

L371@P22: D. Joint Discussion with Nevada State Contractors Board (NSCB)
L372@P22: Chair Galarza began a joint discussion with NSCB, introducing its Executive Officer
L373@P22: David Behar and CSLB’s Registrar David Fogt to lead the conversation.
L374@P22: 1. Discussion Regarding CSLB and NSCB Operational and Structural
L375@P22: Comparison
L376@P22: Registrar Fogt provided updates on four items. First, California’s contractor license
L377@P22: population increased only 1 percent since June 2024, adding about 624 licenses, but
L378@P22: the fund balance remains strong with over a six-month reserve of about $50 million.
L379@P22: Second, the state expanded reciprocity agreements, previously with Arizona, Nevada,
L380@P22: and Louisiana, to now include North Carolina. Reciprocity allows waiving trade exams
L381@P22: for certain classifications, though applicants must still pass the law and business exam.
L382@P22: Third, licensing data showed around 100 out-of-state contractors sought California
L383@P22: licenses through reciprocity in the past year, while about 2,000 California licensees
L384@P22: pursued licenses in other states, mainly to expand business rather than relocate.
L385@P22: Finally, the Board recently approved trade exam waivers for out-of-state licensees who
L386@P22: have held licenses in good standing for at least five years and passed the NASCLA
L387@P22: commercial builders’ exam, which closely aligns with California’s general building exam.
L388@P22: Additionally, California can waive the trade exam for the B-2 Residential Remodeling
L389@P22: license when applicants hold equivalent licenses in other states.
L390@P22: Chief of Licensing, Carol Gagnon, expressed interest in expanding reciprocity
L391@P22: agreements with more states, noting that contractors can already work in many places,
L392@P22: even internationally, and that creating additional opportunities would benefit licensees.
L393@P22: Registrar Fogt provided several California updates. First, the minor work exemption was
L394@P22: increased from $500 to $1,000, but with restrictions including no permits can be
L395@P22: required and no workers may be hired — to prevent unsafe unlicensed work and issues
L396@P22: with workers’ compensation. Second, on restitution, CSLB’s enforcement program
L397@P22-23: 13BOARD MEETING MINUTES
L398@P23: recovered nearly $35.3 million for consumers in the last fiscal year, including over $27.8
L399@P23: million through settlements and $7.4 million from assisting with civil judgment
L400@P23: collections. Third, on complaints, unlicensed contractor complaints dropped to 19
L401@P23: percent of total cases (historically 20–25 percent), and solar complaints also declined,
L402@P23: from about 220 per month to 180. However, accessory dwelling unit (ADU) complaints
L403@P23: are rising sharply. In the past 18 months, five contractors alone generated 462
