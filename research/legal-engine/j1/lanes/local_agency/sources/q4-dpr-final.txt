In an effort to prevent additional fires and discourage illegal dumping incidents, DTSC has adopted the “Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption.”

## Regulation Overview

The Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption was first adopted as a temporary emergency rulemaking on October 7, 2024. It was then readopted twice, with the first readoption taking effect on April 7, 2025, and the second on July 8, 2025. On November 17, 2025, the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption became a permanent subsection in the California Code of Regulations through the regular rulemaking process.

This exemption amends section 66261.6 of title 22 of the California Code of Regulations by adopting an existing federal recycling exemption for industrial ethyl alcohol in 40 Code of Federal Regulations, section 261.6(a)(3)(i). Promulgated in 1985, this exemption was intended to reduce redundancy with existing distillery regulations established by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco and Firearms (now the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau or TTB), and it initially applied to “spent” industrial ethyl alcohol alone. However, U.S. EPA reinterpreted 40 Code of Federal Regulations, section 261.6(a)(3)(i) in a response letter to an ethanol recycler, then reiterated this determination in a reissued memorandum to regulators, and now unused industrial ethyl alcohol (including unused ethyl alcohol-based hand sanitizer) qualifies for this exemption.

### What does this Exemption Do?

  * The Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption applies to generators and transporters operating in California.
  * It exempts industrial ethyl alcohol from the hazardous waste regulations established in California Code of Regulations, title 22, division 4.5, and Health and Safety Code section 25153.6 when it is “legitimately recycled” at a TTB-permitted facility, per the requirements of 40 Code of Federal Regulations, section 260.43.
  * This exemption retains the existing regulations for the import and export of hazardous recyclable materials established in California Code of Regulations, title 22, division 4.5, chapter 12, article 8.

This rulemaking exempts spent, unused, and off-specification industrial ethyl alcohol from most of the hazardous waste regulations established in the California Code of Regulations. This exemption is intended to encourage generators to bypass the current hazardous waste incinerator backlog through recycling, which will decrease the retention time of ethyl alcohol-based hand sanitizer stockpiles and reduce the risk of fires and illegal dumping. It should be noted, however, that the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption does not apply to isopropyl alcohol-based hand sanitizers, nor to household hazardous waste. The full text of this exemption, and its accompanying rulemaking documents, can be accessed from the Industrial Ethyl Alcohol Exemption’s Final Regulations webpage.

# WSIP Early Funding - Removal of Dollar Amount Cap

The Water Storage Investment Program (WSIP) implements Proposition 1, Chapter 8, which provided $2.7 billion for the public benefits of water storage projects. To implement this chapter, the California Water Commission (Commission) adopted program regulations, as required in Chapter 8. Water Code section 79755(c) allows for early funding of projects to assist with work related to completion of environmental documents and permits.

On June 30 th, 2025, Commission staff sent via email and listserv, a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking to Remove the Dollar Amount Cap on Early Funding (Notice). The Notice and other related documents were also uploaded to the Commission website on June 30 th, 2025. On July 4 th, 2025, the Notice was published in the California Regulatory Notice Register.

The proposed change remove s the dollar amount cap for early funding (23 CCR § 6010 (b)(5) and (e)(1)), which could potentially contradict the percentage cap for early funding as set forth in the WSIP regulations (23 CCR § 6010(b)(4)).

## INFORMATIVE DIGEST OF PROPOSED ACTION AND POLICY STATEMENT OVERVIEW PURSUANT TO GOVERNMENT CODE SECTION 11346.5(A)(3)

*Background and Effect of the Proposed Rulemaking*  
This notice of proposed rulemaking will remove the dollar cap for early funding for the WSIP (23 CCR § 6010 (b)(5) and (e)(1)). The proposed regulations do not result in a re-examination of the merits of a WSIP project, nor do they change the total amount of funding a project may receive from the WSIP if the project meets all the statutory requirements of Water Code section 79755. 
The proposed change would address the potential conflict between the $135 million cap on early funding and the 5% of project MCED cap on early funding.

*Determination of Inconsistency and Incompatibility with Existing State Regulations Pursuant to Government Code 11346.5(a)(3)(D)*  
The proposed changes would address the potential conflict between the $135 million cap on WSIP early funding (23 CCR § 6010(b)(5) and (e)(1)) and the 5% of MCED per project cap (23 CCR § 6010(b)(4)), by removing the dollar amount cap for early funding. The proposed regulations are not inconsistent or incompatible with existing state regulations.

The proposed regulations were developed to be consistent with the following sections of California Code: Government Code sections 11340.5 and 11342.600 requiring any rule of general application to be adopted as a regulation.

The Executive Officer has determined that the proposed regulation is not inconsistent or incompatible with existing regulations. The Executive Officer has concluded that 23 CCR § 6010(b)(5) and (e)(1) are the only regulations concerning the dollar amount cap on early funding.
