# History of the California Building Standards Commission

1905 to present

The California Building Standards Commission (CBSC) is a commission within the Department of General Services and is charged in state law with the responsibility to administer the process of approving and adopting building standards for publication in the California Building Standards Code (Cal. Code Regs., Title 24). This is a brief summary of key events that led to the present form of CBSC, California laws regarding buildings, and the California Building Standards Code.

2025

After the conclusion of the 2024 Triennial Code Adoption Cycle, the 2025 edition of Title 24 was published on July 1, 2025, with an effective date of January 1, 2026. Prior to the start of the 2025 Intervening Code Adoption Cycle in December, CBSC convened the Coordinating Council and conducted several pre-cycle workshops to draft code proposals for supplements to the 2025 edition.

Several legislative bills chaptered in 2025 that may impact the future activities of CBSC and other state agencies with authority in Title 24.

AB 130 Housing (Committee on Budget, Chapter 22). Enacts a six-year moratorium on state and local building standards affecting residential units, and also enacts permanent restrictions on the type of Title 24 proposals a state agency may promulgate during an intervening code adoption cycle.

AB 253 California Residential Private Permitting Review Act: residential building permits (Ward, Chapter 487). Requires local jurisdictions to prepare and post a residential building permit fee schedule to its website, if they prescribe residential building permit fees. Authorizes an applicant for a building permit to retain a private professional provider to perform the plan check if the estimated timeframe for plan check provided by the city/county exceeds 30 business days or the city/county has not determined the completed application is compliant within 30 days.

SB 283 Energy storage systems (Laird, Chapter 407). In addition to enacting specific requirements for permitting and inspections of energy storage systems, this bill requires SFM to consider proposing building standards in the next triennial cycle to restrict the location of energy storage systems to dedicated-use noncombustible buildings or outdoor installations.

2024

The California Building Standards Commission (CBSC) appointed Stoyan Bumbalov as the new Executive Director in May 2024. Deputy Executive Director Kevin Day returned to his regular duties after serving as Acting Executive Director from May 2023 to April 2024.

During the 2024 Triennial Code Adoption Cycle for the 2025 edition of Title 24, CBSC administered the rulemaking process for 43 packages from proposing and adopting state agencies. This included six code advisory committee meetings from March to July 2024, multiple 45- and 15-day public comment periods from July to November 2024, and preparation of the final rulemaking packages for review and approval by the commission during public meetings in December 2024 and February 2025.

Several legislative bills chaptered in 2024 that may impact the future activities of CBSC and other state agencies with authority in Title 24.

AB 176 Education finance: education omnibus trailer bill (Committee on Budget, Chapter 998). Requires, until January 1, 2027, state and local fire marshals assigning occupancy and use, or change of use, classifications and issuing fire clearance approvals for day care centers, when making those classifications and conducting those inspections on or after January 1, 2023, to use certain provisions of the California Building Standards Code as they read on December 31, 2022. The bill would require the Office of the State Fire Marshal, in consultation with the State Department of Social Services, to promulgate regulations pertaining to occupancy standards for day care centers no later than January 1, 2027.

The bill also authorizes HCD to propose related building standards for consideration by CBSC. Requires a report to specified committees of the legislature by January 1, 2027, regarding the outcomes of HCD’s research and the recommendations developed.

SB 1152 State Fire Marshal: fire safety: regulations: lithium-based battery systems: telecommunications infrastructure (Limón, Chapter 781). Requires the State Fire Marshal (SFM), before the next triennial building codes adoption cycle, to propose updates to the requirements relating to lithium battery systems. The updates must address the specific environments in which communications utilities are required to deploy the lithium-based battery systems in order to meet specified requirements relating to backup electricity for telecommunications infrastructure.

2023

CBSC successfully completed the 2022 Intervening Code Adoption Cycle which resulted in supplements to the 2022 edition of Title 24. The supplements were published on January 1, 2024, and effective July 1, 2024. This cycle was extremely robust with 30 code proposals, including first-in-the-nation mandatory embodied carbon reduction regulations and new voluntary bird-friendly building standards for non-residential occupancies. There was also the early adoption of model code language allowing all-gender multi-user restrooms for specified occupancies.

Another momentous adjustment in 2023 was the return to in-person commission meetings. After two years of virtual meetings, staff produced several hybrid meetings to comply with adjusted Bagley-Keene Open Meeting Act requirements. A benefit of holding public meetings both in person and virtually is increased public participation in the code adoption process.

During the precycle phase of the 2024 Triennial Code Adoption Cycle, CBSC held three public CALGreen Electric Vehicle Workgroup meetings in coordination with other state agencies and attended by electric vehicle advocates and other stakeholders (Fall 2023).

Several recently passed laws impacted CBSC and other agencies with authority to develop regulations in Title 24. All of these new laws are within the Statutes of 2023:

AB 529 Adaptive Reuse Projects (Gabriel, Chapter 743) requires the Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD) to convene a working group regarding adaptive reuse residential projects, including identifying and recommending amendments to California building standards in Title 24.

AB 835 State Fire Marshal: building standards: single-exit, single stairway apartment houses: report (Lee, Chapter 345) requires the State Fire Marshal (SFM) to research standards for single-exit, single-stairway apartment houses with more than two dwelling units in buildings above three stories, and provide a report to specified legislative committees and to the California Building Standards Commission by January 1, 2026.


Due to the ongoing coronavirus pandemic—and in accordance with SB 189 (Chapter 48, Statutes of 2022)—2022 CBSC public meetings did not have a physical location. SB 189 allows agencies to hold virtual meetings without complying with specific Bagley-Keene Open Meeting Act requirements until July 1, 2023. The meetings were viewable through a live webcast on CBSC’s YouTube channel, and members of the public had the ability to participate and offer verbal comments using the Zoom webinar platform and/or teleconference.

Additionally, several laws impacting building standards and their development were passed by the state legislature. Some bills of interest are:

AB 209 Energy and climate change (Committee on Budget, Chapter 251) Section 8 directs CBSC to consider adoption of safety standards for refrigerants from the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-conditioning Engineers (ASHRAE) and Underwriters Laboratories (UL) currently being developed by the Office of the State Fire Marshal (SFM). If not adopted into Title 24, effective July 1, 2024, standards may not be prohibited from being adopted by state or local agencies. Section 31 directs the Department of Housing and Community Development (HCD), by January 1, 2025, to submit policy recommendations to the legislature that are designed to ensure that residential dwelling units can maintain the recommended maximum safe indoor air temperature. The recommendations are to be developed in consultation with other agencies as specified.

For more information, including, filing an emergency housing ordinance, refer to Housing and Community Development’s (HCD) information bulletin:

HCD Information Bulletin 2020-09

AB 2960 (Gipson, Chapter 148, Statutes of 2020) Shelter crises: fire & life safety standards. This bill authorizes a city with a population of more than 3,500,000 to permit the operation of an emergency housing facility year round when the facility does not comply with state building standards for local fire and life safety standards if they submit reasonable standards to the State Fire Marshal that include specified minimum requirements, including, among other things, 24-hour active fire watch, emergency evacuation signage and emergency egress lighting, among other things. The State Fire Marshal is required to review the standards within 30 days and either approve them or respond as to why they do not meet the threshold requirements. The bill authorizes permits for a period of 90 days and authorizes 90-day extensions, not to exceed 730 days of operation, and prohibits the authorization of new permits on and after January 1, 2023.

Several pieces of legislation were introduced at this time in response to the Loma Prieta earthquake. In particular, AB 204 (Cortese) increased the regulatory authority of CBSC to include, in general, existing buildings having at least one unreinforced masonry bearing wall. Specifically, the bill required CBSC to adopt and publish by reference the Appendix Chapter I of the Uniform Code for Building Conservation (UCBC) to provide standards for buildings specified in that appendix.

1990

AB 4082 (Chapter 1289, Statutes of 1990) required the California Building Standards Commission, in conjunction with all state agencies involved in the adoption of building standards and the interested public, to conduct a comprehensive review of state building standards and statutes relating to state building standards, beginning January 1, 1991 and continuing through December 31, 1992.
