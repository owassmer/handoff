DIVISION OF WATER RIGHTS
Clear Lake Watershed Co-Management Agreement
The Division of Water Rights joined State Water Resources Control Board (State Water
Board) leaders and other tribal, state, and local governments in the Clear Lake
Watershed on September 11, 2026, to sign a co-management agreement to restore the
Clear Lake watershed. The agreement aims to improve water quality, restore habitat,
strengthen native fish populations, and support healthier ecosystems for future
generations. State Water Board Executive Director, Eric Oppenheimer, signed the
agreement on behalf of the Board.
The Division of Water Rights (Division) has expanded its work in the Clear Lake
watershed since it began working on the Clear Lake Hitch emergency in 2022, moving
from emergency response to a sustained investment in science, monitoring,
enforcement, tribal capacity, and long-term watershed health. The Division has invested
over $1.3 million in direct contracts and monitoring support for a groundwater–surface
water study, facilitation and engagement, and water-rights enforcement. Together,
these investments are building the scientific foundation and partnerships needed for
collaborative management of the Clear Lake watershed.
Through the Clear Lake Hitch Emergency Information Order, the Division collected new
information on groundwater pumping and surface water diversions from roughly
1,300 properties, strengthening the basis for understanding water use and its
relationship to streamflow near hitch spawning areas. The Division is also investing in
the people, partnerships, and monitoring needed to put science to work by supporting
facilitation and collaboration, harmful algal bloom and eDNA monitoring, and providing
equipment and technical support for Tribal-led stream, groundwater, and water-quality
monitoring.
Together, these efforts are creating a shared scientific foundation for decision-making,
combining state resources and regulatory tools with tribal knowledge, monitoring, and
stewardship to better understand the watershed and identify actions that can make a
meaningful difference. This agreement represents the next step in that commitment.

Water Right Petitions
Approved City of Yreka’s time extension petition: On September 4, 2026, the
Division approved the City of Yreka’s petition to extend the development period for
water right Permit 15379 (Application 22551), which authorizes municipal, domestic,
and industrial diversions from Fall Creek and a tributary to Fall Creek in Siskiyou
County. Although the City requested an extension through 2042, the order grants an
extension only through December 31, 2036, finding that the City demonstrated due
diligence and good cause but has not made sufficient progress toward full beneficial use
to warrant an additional 20 years. No protests were received. The amended permit
requires annual reports documenting progress toward full development, conservation
efforts, growth, infrastructure improvements, and constraints on water use, and updates
conditions concerning bypass flows, measuring and monitoring, urban water
management, and cannabis cultivation. Staff determined that the extension complies
with the California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) and will not adversely affect public
trust resources.
Approved Georgetown Divide PUD Temporary Transfer: On August 25, 2026, the
Division approved Georgetown Divide Public Utility District’s (GDPUD) temporary
transfer of up to 3,250 acre-feet of water stored in Stumpy Meadows Reservoir under
Permit 12827 to Westlands Water District for agricultural use. The water will be
released from Stumpy Meadows Reservoir, conveyed through Folsom Lake and the
Delta, and rediverted at the Jones or Banks Pumping Plants, with reservoir releases
ending September 30, 2026. The Division determined that the transferred water would
otherwise remain in storage and that, as conditioned, the transfer will not injure other
legal users or unreasonably affect fish, wildlife, or other instream beneficial uses. Key
conditions require execution of a reservoir refill agreement with the Department of
Water Resources (DWR) and the Bureau of Reclamation (Reclamation), coordination
of Folsom Reservoir operations and cold-water pool protection with Reclamation,
compliance with applicable regulatory and endangered-species requirements, and

reporting of transfer deliveries and subsequent reservoir refill. Prior to approving the
temporary transfer, GDPUD informed the Division that surveying for the Foothill Yellow
Legged Frog would delay the water release window for the transfer. A condition was
added to the order to allow for a revised water release schedule with concurrence from
DWR, Reclamation, the California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW), and the
United States Forest Service (USFS).
Approved Foresthill PUD Temporary Transfer: On August 26, 2026, the Division
approved Foresthill Public Utility District’s (FPUD) temporary transfer of up to
2,000 acre-feet of water, plus up to 105 acre-feet to cover conveyance losses (total
2,105 acre-feet), stored in Sugar Pine Reservoir under Permit 15375 to Westlands
Water District for agricultural use. The water will be released from Sugar Pine Reservoir
between September 14 and October 22, 2026, then conveyed through Folsom Lake and
the Delta for rediversion at the Jones and Banks pumping plants or San Luis Reservoir.
The Division determined the transferred water would otherwise remain in storage and
that, as conditioned, the temporary change will not injure other legal users of water or
unreasonably affect fish, wildlife, or other instream beneficial uses. Key conditions
require execution of a reservoir refill agreement with DWR and Reclamation,
coordination of Folsom Reservoir operations and cold-water pool protection with
Reclamation, execution of a Warren Act contract, and reporting of transfer deliveries
and subsequent reservoir refill.

California American Water Company Monterey Cease and Desist Order – On
October 21, 2025, Monterey Peninsula Water Management District (MPWMD) filed an
application with the State Board pursuant to Water Code section 1832 to modify Orders
WR 2009-0060 and WR 2016-0016. Specifically, MPWMD requests that the State
Board issue an amended order that rescinds Condition 2, updates reporting
requirements, and includes a trigger that would allow the Division’s Deputy Director to
reinstate Condition 2, if necessary. This proceeding is currently underway;
159 comment packages were received by the August 19, 2026 due date, and six
rebuttal packages were received by the September 18, 2026 due date. A hearing date is
scheduled for November 5, 2026. Additional information on this proceeding is available
on the Division’s CalAm case website.
Hodys – On August 5, 2026, the Division issued an administrative civil liability
complaint against Jeremy Hodys and Ryan Shields for alleged water rights and
cannabis cultivation violations on properties in the Blue Rock Creek watershed in
Mendocino County. The complaint alleges unauthorized surface water diversions,
failure to file required diversion statements, violations of the Cannabis Cultivation Policy
involving water storage and recordkeeping, and water use for cannabis cultivation
without the required state license. The Division proposes $41,000 in joint and several
liability, plus $29,000 against Mr. Hodys individually and $11,500 against Shields
individually. Mr. Hodys has requested a hearing before the Administrative Hearings
Office (AHO), which has been scheduled for November 20, 2026.

Hale – On September 21, 2026, the Division issued an administrative civil liability
complaint proposing a $157,890 penalty against Douglas J. Hale for alleged violations
of the Scott River watershed groundwater curtailment order. Division staff documented
irrigation on nine days between June 18 and August 12, 2026, while curtailments were
in effect and estimated 22.63 acre-feet of water use on those days. The complaint cites
continued irrigation after a notice of violation, prior curtailment violations, and potential
harm to instream flows and senior water users. The proposed liability is subject to
Mr. Hale’s right to request a hearing, which must be submitted by October 12, 2026.

Scott River Watershed Curtailments: On June 9, 2026, Addendum 12 to
Orders WR 2024-0024-DWR and WR 2026-0005-DWR reinstated curtailments
for all surface water rights in the Scott River Watershed. On June 10, 2026,
Order WR- 2026-0008-DWR imposed curtailments and required reporting for
groundwater pumping under schedule C of the Scott River Adjudication and
overlying groundwater diversions associated with parcels listed in attachment A
of the Order. On July 2, 2026, Addendum 1 conditionally suspended curtailments

USGS Fort Jones gage. A large portion of the farming and ranching communities

Shasta River Watershed Curtailments: Diversions impact the flow of the
Shasta River, causing water levels to rise or fall based on amount of water being
diverted. Conditional curtailments remain in effect for the Shasta River watershed
under Addendum 6 to Order 2024-0006-DWR. Conditional curtailments set
additional requirements that must be met before junior water rights can divert,
including increased coordination with the State Water Board and/or the Scott
River and Shasta River Watermaster District. As of September 15, flows at the
USGS Yreka gage continue to fluctuate around the May 1 - September 15 flow
requirement of 50 cfs, with the flow requirement increasing to 75 cfs for
September 16-30, and 105 cfs for October. The Division of Water Rights will
continue to monitor flows, flow forecasts, precipitation and temperature forecasts,
and watershed conditions regularly to determine if adjustments to the
curtailments are appropriate.

Additional information on the Emergency Regulation and related flow efforts in the Scott
River and Shasta River watersheds is available on the Board’s website for these efforts.

Groundwater Regulatory Branch
Urban and Industrial Stormwater Infiltration Policy: The Division of Water Quality
plans to release the draft Water Quality Control Policy for Urban and Industrial
Stormwater Infiltration in late October 2026 for a 45-day public comment period. The
draft Policy establishes a risk-based framework for evaluating urban and industrial
infiltration projects by considering pollutant types, land use, industrial activities, and
drainage area size. It provides clear, peer-reviewed, science-based requirements to
ensure projects are designed and implemented to protect groundwater quality and
beneficial uses according to their risk level. The draft Policy also identifies conditions
where infiltration may be prohibited. During the comment period, staff will hold a public
Board workshop to present the draft Policy, answer questions, and gather feedback.
Wastewater Needs Assessment (WWNA) Advisory Group Meeting: As specified in
the State Water Board Resolution No. 2022-0019, the State Water Board authorized the
Executive Director or designee to enter into contracts to support a statewide effort to
conduct an assessment of wastewater needs to improve sanitation access to all
Californians and to help achieve the human right to water goals. State Water Board
contracted with the University of California, Los Angeles – Luskin Center for Innovation
(UCLA, primary investigator contracted), Sacramento State – Office of Water Programs,
University of California – Agriculture and Natural Resources, and University of
Massachusetts – Amherst (WWNA project team) to provide information on and
strategies to address California’s water-related sanitation system needs. The WWNA
contract lasts for four years (2023 to 2027) and is conducted in sequential 2-year
phases.
The WWNA project team formed an Advisory Group, made up of diverse organizations
across California, to advise on project development and draft analyses, inform the
WWNA team on wastewater data and information, and to provide other key areas of
expertise and perspectives. The Advisory Group began meeting in July 2024 and has
since met quarterly. The next quarterly meeting will be held on October 30, 2026, and
will provide a high-level overview of progress implementing the Phase 2 Workplan with
a focus on the WWNA Solutions & Costs (Phase 2D) and At-Risk Assessment (Phase
2D).

Water agencies agree to wastewater upgrades after Russian River spill: Under an
enforcement settlement with the North Coast Water Board, Russian River County
Sanitation District and Sonoma Water will improve the Guerneville wastewater system.

SWAMP Freshwater and Estuarine Harmful Algal Bloom Program
Labor Day Pre-holiday Assessment and News Advisories
The SWAMP Freshwater and Estuarine Harmful Algal Bloom (FHAB) Program
organizes pre-holiday assessments at high recreation sites before major holiday
weekends including Memorial Day, Fourth of July, and Labor Day to monitor for harmful
algal blooms. The FHAB Program funds most of the laboratory analyses and enlists
assistance for the monitoring from Regional Boards, tribes, counties, irrigation districts,
homeowners' associations, non-profits, and federal and state partners. The latest preholiday assessment monitored 118 sites. Monitoring results trigger public health
advisories ranging from no swimming to the highest advisory level of danger advising
the public to stay out of and off the water. News advisories were published to notify the
public of the elevated recreational advisory at 6 popular waterbodies since the day-today advisory posting is voluntary and not enforced. The monitoring results and all event
response to public reports are published to the HAB Reports Map at:

Groundwater Protection Targets:
In 2018, the State Water Board adopted Order WQ 2018-0002, which required the
development of a methodology (Groundwater Protection Formula) to develop townshiplevel estimates of current N loading from agriculture (Groundwater Protection Values)
and estimates of the N loading necessary to meet receiving water limitations
(Groundwater Protection Targets). On June 30, 2023, the Central Valley Water Board’s
Executive Officer conditionally approved Groundwater Protection Targets for
379 townships in the region.
During fall 2025 and spring 2026, Central Valley Water Board staff engaged with State
Water Board staff, third-party groups, and environmental justice groups to initiate a
scientific peer of the Groundwater Protection Formula, Values, and Targets framework.
The resulting feedback package was finalized on May 4, 2026, and will inform updates
to the Targets anticipated to be submitted in late 2026 as well as updates to an
uncertainty and validation workplan previously submitted by the third-party groups.
Management Practice Evaluation Program:
To facilitate the implementation of improved nitrogen management practices, the ILRP
General Orders contain a third-party requirement to develop and implement a
Management Practice Evaluation Program (MPEP). The intent of this program is to
comprehensively evaluate the protectiveness of various management practices in
reducing nitrate leaching across a wide range of crops. The scope of this effort was
expanded in response to Order WQ 2018-0002 to also include 1) development of cropspecific coefficients for conversion of yield to nitrogen removed and 2) determination of
acceptable ranges for multi-year ratio of nitrogen applied to nitrogen removed (A/R).
On April 3, 2026, Dr. Daniel Geisseler published updates to the report entitled Nitrogen
concentrations in harvested plant parts – Update 04/2026. This update was completed
in collaboration with the Southern San Joaquin Valley Management Practices
Evaluation Program, resulting in new or updated estimates for 11 crops. More
information can be found on Dr. Geisseler's webpage.
On June 23, 2026, the Central Valley Water Board Executive Officer issued acceptable
multi-year A/R ratio target values, referred to as Nitrogen Efficiency Targets. Staff
continues to collaborate with State Water Board and stakeholders to evaluate how
these and other metrics can be best utilized within the ILRP.

Central Valley Water Board staff hold quarterly stakeholder meetings to provide an open

Enrollment Summary:
Staff conducts significant compliance and outreach activities to ensure growers are
obtaining appropriate enrollment in the program. The use of Geographic Information
Systems (GIS) continues to be a vital tool in this effort. Staff utilizes GIS maps that
incorporate the latest Department of Water Resources land use data, current
membership lists, WDR land application sites, and past inspection data to identify
irrigated lands within the Region. Staff is also working with the Central Valley Water
Board’s Confined Animal Program to identify irrigated agricultural operations that are
covered by their Waste Discharge Requirements. There are fluctuations in irrigated
agricultural acreage due to various factors such as, land use conversions, water
allocations, retirements, land fallowing, ownership transfers, and regulatory coverage in
other programs. Current efforts are helping to provide a clearer picture of commercial
irrigated lands in the Region and the status of enrollment in the ILRP. Staff continues to
identify and follow up with potential non-filers with the goal of enrolling all commercial
irrigated lands in the ILRP. Trends in ILRP enrollment can be found in the
California Water Boards’ Annual Performance Report.
Domestic Well Monitoring:
All ILRP enrollees are required to monitor drinking water wells on enrolled parcels for
nitrate plus nitrite as nitrogen if the wells are used for human consumption.
As of August 25, 2026, approximately 14,736 wells have been sampled with 32 percent
of the wells exceeding the nitrate drinking water standard (10 mg/L nitrate as nitrogen),
triggering the user notification requirement. Staff has prioritized tracking notification
submittals and conducting follow-up, successfully achieving approximately 99 percent
compliance.
While the monitoring data are publicly available on GeoTracker, ILRP staff provides
assistance as needed to CV-SALTS Nitrate Control Program Management Zones staff
to inform their interim drinking water replacement efforts and long-term solutions.
Anonymous Field-level Reporting:
Order WQ 2018-0002 required third-party groups to develop anonymous Member IDs
and anonymous APN IDs to be permanently associated with Irrigation and Nitrogen
Management Plan (INMP) Summary Report data submitted by growers. This format
continues to provide sufficient information for program oversight and progress tracking
and has not limited staff’s ability to identify and work with individual growers to address
reporting and/or performance issues.

This Water Boards’ Cannabis Cultivation Program (Cannabis Cultivation Program or
Program) update provides a summary of significant actions and achievements
accomplished by the State Water Board’s Division of Water Quality, Division of Water
Rights, Office of Enforcement, and nine Regional Water Board staff (collectively referred
to as the Water Boards) since the April 2026 Executive Director’s Report. Additional
information on the Cannabis Cultivation Program, including the Cannabis Cultivation
Policy – Principles and Guidelines for Cannabis Cultivation (Cannabis Policy),
General Waste Discharge Requirements and Waiver of Waste Discharge Requirements
for Discharges of Waste Associated with Cannabis Cultivation Activities (Cannabis
Cultivation General Order), Cannabis Small Irrigation Use Registration (SIUR), and
enforcement efforts, is available on the Water Board’s Cannabis Cultivation Program
webpage.
WATER BOARDS’ CANNABIS CULTIVATION PROGRAM:
The Cannabis Program’s regulatory functions continue to be diminished due to
persistent non-payment of fees by an ongoing portion of regulated entities. This ongoing
revenue shortfall has constrained resources and limited the Program’s ability to
maintain a balanced approach between enforcement and compliance assistance. As a
result, while enforcement capabilities are improving, the Program faces continued
challenges in sustaining comprehensive regulatory oversight. Annual fees directly fund
regulatory staffing resources. To help address this issue, annually staff do outreach to
the delinquent fee payers notifying them of the issue and that their Cannabis General
Order enrollment will be terminated if the fees remain unpaid which also consequently
jeopardizes their Department of Cannabis Control license. Despite these efforts
hundreds of terminations occur every year.
The Water Boards set program fees annually. For FY 2027–28, staff proposed a flat
28% fee increase for enrollment in the Cannabis General Order. This increase is
necessary to support the current staffing levels of 10 regulatory positions state-wide.
Although the Program was authorized for 31 fee-funded positions, only 10 positions are
currently filled due to the ongoing shortfall in fee revenue. The fee increase is intended
to only cover the cost of those 10 filled positions.
Given both the instability in the legal cannabis market and other regulatory costs being
imposed on this transitioning industry, the Program has been hesitant to propose
adjustments to its fee structure in recent years. This has resulted in the Program’s fees
remaining relatively low with only small fee increases compared to other Water Board
agricultural programs. Stagnant fees combined with a much lower collection rate
compared to other comparable programs resulted in a need to raise fees to maintain
minimal staffing for administering this critical program.

In addition to the fee increase, staff are also working to improve enrollment accuracy to
ensure growers are in the correct General Order tier and paying fees that are most
representative of the staff time needed to regulate their specific site. Future stakeholder
meetings will be held to solicit feedback on tier structure and program funding issues.

Enrollment
The Division of Water Quality processes all applications for enrollment statewide under
the Cannabis Cultivation General Order and enrolled 129 applicants between
January 1, 2026, and July 31, 2026. This level of new enrollment has been generally
consistent for the last four consecutive years. The total number of active Cannabis
Cultivation General Order enrollments is currently around 4,000- a reduction of around
400 enrollments from last year. Additionally, we anticipate around 500 additional
terminations to be issued in December due to lack of payment over multiple years,
further reducing the revenue supporting Program regulatory staff.
Between January 1, 2026, and July 31, 2026, the Division of Water Rights issued two
SIUR enrollments, bringing the total registered SIURs to about 530.
Enforcement
The Program continues to focus on enforcement of unregulated cannabis cultivation
sites which often have egregious water quality threats and unauthorized water
diversions. Program enforcement staff accompany law enforcement from partner
agencies on search warrant inspections and document evidence of environmental harm
resulting from non-compliance with regulatory protections. Staff use this evidence to
support criminal enforcement as well as administrative enforcement to facilitate cleanup
and remediation of environmental harm, encourage enrollment in our regulatory
programs when possible, and to deter illegal cultivation and the adverse water quality
impacts associated with that activity.
This year, enforcement staff has supported 146 search warrant inspections at illegal
cultivation sites and issued 15 formal administrative enforcement actions.
The Division of Water Rights, Cannabis Enforcement Section (CES) has been
coordinating with the Department of Cannabis Control (DCC) to identify licensed
cannabis cultivators that divert surface water without an appropriative water right - such
as a Cannabis Small Irrigation Use Registration (SIUR) - and/or failed to report their
diversion and use of water. CES identified 147 priority properties that appeared to be
diverting water for cultivation without a water right.
To identify these properties, CES relied on multiple information sources, including,
Statements of Water Diversion and Use that reported cannabis irrigation; Department of
Fish and Wildlife’s Lake and Stream Alteration Agreements indicating diversion or
reservoirs used for cultivation; DCC licensing data; SIUR revocations; complaints; CES
58

field inspection data; partner agency Notices of Violation; former water rights filings
claiming fully contained springs; and aerial images.
DCC issued Notice to Comply (NTC) letters to all 147 licensed cultivators during August
2025 and required each licensee to contact CES to identify and confirm their water
source(s). CES completed 74 site investigations in May 2026 for those that responded
to the NTC letter. CES identified approximately 40 of the 74 investigated sites as
requiring further infield follow-up. CES has conducted seven joint field inspections with
DCC in 2026 of the approximate 40 that required additional follow-up. Approximately
33 of the 74 CES investigated sites came into compliance after contact with CES. DCC
and CES continue to coordinate outreach and potential DCC enforcement on the
73 licensees who did not respond to the NTC, including one licensee that acknowledged
diverting water for cannabis cultivation but refused to obtain a water right.
Outreach
Remote outreach events continue to be popular with both regulatory agencies and
stakeholders. Local in-person meetings are also occurring, and attendance at those
events is on the rise. A couple of noteworthy examples include North Coast Water

Humboldt Cannabis & Environmental Stewardship Symposium and multiple Central
Valley Water Board outreach events addressing various topics including how to enroll,
understanding the Cannabis Policy requirements, site development, road design, and
indoor cultivation. Table 3 lists the significant public outreach events hosted or attended
