L48: # At the top of the Golden Gate Bridge, Governor Newsom signs his final bill — investing in California’s next generation of innovation
L49: What you need to know: Standing at the top of the Golden Gate bridge, Governor Newsom signed the last bill of the 2025-26 regular legislative session — putting a $7.5 billion research bond before voters to invest in the scientists, universities and breakthroughs that will shape California’s future and advancing California’s status as the capital of innovation, research and technology.
L50: SAN FRANCISCO – For generations, California has been the birthplace of new ideas and new industries – from the Gold Rush era, the creation of the internet, to becoming the global epicenter of the artificial intelligence industry.
L51: Building on that legacy, Governor Gavin Newsom today announced his final bill signing of his last regular legislative session by investing in the people and institutions that will drive California’s success for generations to come.
L52: Standing at the top of the Golden Gate Bridge, the Governor signed Senate Bill 895 by Senator Scott Wiener (D-San Francisco), which puts a $7.5 billion science and health research bond before voters in March 2028, supporting research at California universities and institutions in medicine, public health, climate, wildfire prevention, agriculture and other fields.
L53: Once considered impossible to build, the Golden Gate Bridge has become an enduring symbol of California ingenuity and ambition.
L54: 
L55: California doesn’t settle for what’s possible — we shape what comes next. Great companies start small, big ideas start with a risk, and every generation of innovators has helped build this state into the fourth-largest economy in the world. We owe those dreamers and doers the opportunity to compete, succeed and thrive — because the future will always happen here first.
L56: Governor Gavin Newsom
L57: 
L58: The bill signing marks a symbolic moment, as this bill is the last of 1,160 bills acted on during this legislation session and 7,803 bills acted on by Governor Newsom since the start of his administration since 2019. cite26†View the b-roll from the bill signing here†govca.box.com .
L59: ## Building the next generation of California breakthroughs
L60: 
L61: SB 895 will make a major investment in the people and institutions behind California’s innovation economy. The legislation places a $7.5 billion research bond before voters in March 2028 and creates the California Foundation for Science and Health Research to support scientific research and development through grants, loans and contracts.
L62: “With this investment, California is imagining a better future for millions of families,” said Senator Wiener. “Scientific advancements are curing and preventing diseases, improving the lives of people living with chronic conditions, protecting our communities from wildfires, lowering the cost of food, and powering our economy. We cannot allow the federal government to throw away that hope for a better future.
L63: SB 895 is a bold step to protect science, improve lives, and continue California’s global leadership on innovation for many years to come.”
L64: “Washington has played political games with life-saving research, canceling studies on cancer, Alzheimer’s disease, and other diseases, and putting scientists out of work. With Governor Newsom’s signature on SB 895, California is saying, ‘Enough!,’” said UAW International President Shawn Fain. “SB 895 puts the power in the hands of voters to invest in science, protect the truth, and keep discovery moving so that breakthroughs serve working-class people and our communities, not just those at the top.”
L65: “This is a major achievement, made possible by an extraordinary coalition of people who understand the importance of investing in scientific research,” said UC President James B. Milliken. “The University of California’s research enterprise improves lives, creates opportunity, and drives innovation and economic growth across our state and the entire nation. We are grateful to Gov. Newsom, Sen. Wiener, members of the legislature, and our partners at UAW for their leadership and collaboration on this issue.
L66: I also want to thank the thousands of UC faculty, staff, students, researchers, alumni, and supporters who made their voices heard and helped us achieve this important milestone.”
L67: Funding would support research in biomedical science, public health, disease prevention and behavioral health, as well as work addressing some of California’s biggest challenges, including climate change, wildfire prevention, extreme weather, ocean science, agriculture and water.  Research proposals would undergo scientific peer review based on their merit and potential benefits to Californians, public health, the environment and natural resources.
L68: The measure would also invest in California’s world-class public universities, including $1 billion for related infrastructure projects at the University of California and the California State University.
L69: ## Investing in the engine behind California’s innovation economy
L70: 
L71: As the world’s fourth largest economy, California continues to lead the nation where it matters most — creating new businesses, attracting venture capital, driving technological innovation, expanding advanced manufacturing, growing high-tech industries, and producing more than any other state.
