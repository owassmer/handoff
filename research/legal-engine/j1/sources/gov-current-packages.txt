L48: # California’s nation-leading AI framework just got stronger, Governor Newsom signs more first-in-the-nation worker protections and more
L49: #### Signs executive order declaring the obvious
L50: 
L51: What you need to know:  Governor Newsom signs new, first-in-the-nation protections for California workers, creating stronger safeguards for AI involvement in healthcare and building stronger trust and transparency for people using AI – all while signing another executive order to actually define artificial intelligence.
L52: SACRAMENTO – Advancing on his work to create a strong AI safety framework, Governor Gavin Newsom is continuing to cement California’s AI safety framework by signing new laws to protect workers and consumers – and signing an cite26†executive order permanently declaring Artificial Intelligence to be called “Artificial Intelligence” in California.
L53: Super intelligence is clearly not coming from the White House – that’s why California continues to lead. AI should expand opportunity – not come at the expense of workers and families. As this technology reshapes the workplace, California is putting people at the center, ensuring we all have a voice in the decisions shaping their future.
L54: 
L55: Governor Gavin Newsom
L56: ## Protecting workers
L57: 
L58: Building on the Governor’s nation-leading cite27†executive order to prepare workers and businesses for potential AI disruption, new first-in-the-nation legislation signed will protect people in the workplace from the impacts of artificial intelligence, automated technology, and AI-powered workplace surveillance.
L59: 
L60: ✅ Ensuring real people review automated employment actions by prohibiting employers from only relying on AI when making a disciplinary action or termination decision.
L61: ✅ Providing more transparency by requiring employers to provide if a mass layoff, relocation, or termination is caused by an AI system.
L62: 
L63: ✅ Protecting employee privacy by banning the use of workplace surveillance tools by employers in a bathroom located in the workplace.
L64: 
L65: ✅ Requiring California public higher education institutions to create training for students, faculty, and staff on AI training and procurement.
L66: ✅ Keeping lawyers responsible for practicing law by prohibiting them from fully handing over core legal work, such as drafting briefs or providing legal judgment, to AI.
L67: “Workers across California have demanded that our state lead the way in regulating AI in our workplaces. And today, we see that begin to happen,” said the California Federation of Labor Unions, AFL-CIO President Lorena Gonzalez speaking on SB 947, SB 951, AB 1331, and AB 1883. “By signing these four first-in-the-nation bills, Governor Newsom has shown the country how we can begin to put guardrails on AI at work to ensure human oversight over this technology, limit surveillance, and put workers first.
L68: We must continue to push for regulations to ensure we control this no technology, not be controlled by it.”
L69: Governor Gavin Newsom signs legislation to strengthen worker protections with legislators, labor leaders, and workers.
L70: 
L71: cite28†A b-roll package of the Governor signing this legislation can be downloaded here.†govca.app.box.com L72: ## Empowering doctors to make the final call
L73: 
L74: Californians should be able to trust that the doctors and healthcare professionals caring for them can use their training, experience, and judgment even when AI is part of the process. These new protections help keep people, not algorithms, at the center of healthcare decisions.
L75: 
L76: ✅ Ensuring doctors and licensed providers can use their own professional judgment when AI or other clinical decision tools are used in patient care.
L77: ✅ Requiring developers of clinical decision tools to take reasonable steps to reduce known or predictable bias in how these systems are used.
L78: ## Know what’s real. Know what’s AI.
L79: 
L80: These new protections strengthen transparency and help prevent deception.
L81: 
L82: ✅ Prohibiting the deletion of a digital watermark and allowing access to metadata to make it easier to determine whether it was AI- or user-generated.
L83: 
L84: ✅ Strengthening the California AI Transparency Act to address evolving technology and ensure Californians can identify AI-generated material.
L85: ✅ Making clear that public comments at local government meetings must come from real people, not AI bots.
L86: ## Preventing exploitation
L87: 
L88: California is taking on fraudsters and putting in safeguards to protect people’s image from being exploited online.
L89: 
L90: ✅ Updating California’s civil and criminal laws to protect likeness rights and prevent false impersonation, keeping pace with the challenges posed by AI deepfakes.
L91: 
L92: ✅ Going after fraudsters who try to steal, defraud, or impersonate Californians, including by creating explicit content without their consent, using an AI deepfake.
L93: ## Keeping Californians safe
L94: 
L95: California is acting now to keep powerful biological tools out of the wrong hands.
L96: 
L97: ✅ Requiring gene synthesis companies to follow safety guidelines, verifying who their customers are, and check what genetic material they are sending out for research on diseases like Polio and Ebola.

L112: ## California’s nation-leading framework
L113: Today’s announcement comes on the heels of the Governor’s signature on a bill creating first-in-the-nation safeguards and accountability measures. Earlier this month, the Governor signed into law Senate Bill 813 (McNerney),  establishing a framework for independent verification organizations that can assess AI systems and models for safety and risk.
L114: Governor Newsom also signed AB 1405 (Bauer-Kahan), creating a state registry for AI auditors that assess AI systems and models for compliance with state law and establishing standards for their independence, transparency, and integrity.
L115: Shortly after, the Governor issued an cite31†executive order to dramatically accelerate the implementation of the new third-party oversight of safety and security risks in AI systems, as well as independent audits. The executive order also convened a group of world-leading experts to provide, within two months, a guide for California to reinforce and strengthen its AI safety and security laws.
L116: Proposals under consideration include requiring independent third parties to write safety plans for frontier AI companies, as well as requiring companies to develop an emergency shutoff, or “kill switch,” for frontier models.
L117: This builds on the Governor’s nation-leading work, which has been underway for for years. Beginning with his cite32†first-in-nation 2023 executive order†us.list-manage.com on generative AI, Governor Newsom has pursued executive action, legislation passed in partnership with the Legislature, regulation, procurement, and public-private partnerships to address nearly every major area of AI policy:
L118: ✅ Frontier AI safety: In 2025, Governor Newsom signed SB 53, the cite33†Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act , the nation’s first AI safety law. It requires frontier AI developers to publicly disclose safety frameworks, report specified critical safety incidents to the state, and protect whistleblowers who report serious risks.
L119: ✅ Independent AI oversight: In 2026, Governor Newsom signed SB 813, making California the cite34†first state in the nation to establish a framework for certifying independent verification organizations with sufficient expertise and demonstrated independence from AI companies to objectively assess AI systems and models for safety and risk. He also signed AB 1405, establishing a state registry for AI auditors and standards for their independence, transparency and integrity.
L120: ✅ Child safety standards for AI companions: This year, Governor Newsom signed the bipartisan Adam’s Law, cite35†requiring safeguards around companion chatbots used by children, including suicide and self-harm crisis protocols, parental controls and notifications when safety settings are disabled. It is the first law in the country requiring independent child-safety audits and comprehensive risk assessments prior to release.
L121: California also enacted laws to ban toys containing AI companion chatbots for five years; prohibit use of student personal information to train AI models; and expand protections against AI-generated child sexual abuse material.
L122: ✅ Social media and children: Governor Newsom signed the strongest-in-nation legislation prohibiting social-media companies from providing users under 16 with cite35†addictive features including autoplay and algorithmic feeds based on user history and profiles.
L123: California has enacted nation-leading requirements for responsible AI and social media use for children, including cite36†warning labels for young users, required cite36†age verification signals , guidance to protect kids from after-school cyberbullying, and prohibiting online platforms from knowingly providing an addictive feed to a minor without parental consent.
L124: ✅ Deep fakes and synthetic content: In nation-leading action, cite37†California banned and cracked down on sexually explicit deepfakes, required AI watermarking, addressed deceptive AI-generated election content and required transparency around AI training data.
L125: ✅ Protecting privacy and civil liberties: Governor Newsom signed the Delete Act, cite38†a first-in-the-nation privacy law allowing Californians to direct registered data brokers to delete their personal information. California subsequently launched DROP, which enables people to submit a single deletion request to registered data brokers. In cite39†March 2026 , Governor Newsom also issued an executive order strengthening privacy and civil-liberties protections for state AI procurement.
L126: CalPrivacy has separately adopted protections involving automated decision-making technology.
L127: ✅ Workers and AI disruption: Governor Newsom signed a cite27†first-in-the-nation executive order addressing AI’s economic and labor impacts , including expanded tracking of AI’s economic effects and work to adapt job-training and public-benefit programs. California has also launched an AI Unemployment Tracker and solicited statewide public input on AI’s economic impacts through Engaged California.
L128: ✅ Cybersecurity: Governor Newsom launched a cite40†first-in-the-nation AI Cyber Defense Program to use AI to identify vulnerabilities , harden networks, and support incident response. California is also establishing an AI Cybersecurity Officer in each agency and working with frontier AI companies and critical-infrastructure partners on response protocols and tabletop exercises.
L129: ✅ AI-enabled fraud: Governor Newsom launched a cite41†Tech Fraud Task Force to combat AI-enabled fraud . California has also acted against AI-generated robocall scams and addressed algorithmic pricing through state antitrust law.

L48: # Governor Newsom signs commonsense legislation to make your life easier
L49: 
L50: What you need to know: Not all bills are tied to a specific policy issue — some are just about making life a little easier. Governor Newsom just signed new legislation to help Californians in their day-to-day lives — helping them connect to the customer service they need, avoid annoyingly loud commercials, and allow for virtual meetings with teachers when life prevents in-person connection.
L51: SACRAMENTO – Today, Governor Newsom signed commonsense bills aimed at making everyday life easier for Californians. The new laws crack down on blaring podcast and streaming commercials, make it easier to reach a real person for customer service, eliminate unnecessary fees for paid family and medical leave paperwork, speed up heat pump, water heater, and HVAC installations, and make it easier for parents to stay involved in their kids’ education.
L52: A little common sense goes a long way. Fewer loud commercials. Less time running in circles with customer service chatbots, fewer headaches — helping people doesn’t have to be complicated.
L53: 
L54: Governor Gavin Newsom
L55: ## The Governor is signing common sense bills to make life easier by:
L56: ✅ Helping customers connect with a human, not a bot (and know the difference!), requiring large businesses to make a good-faith effort to connect a customer to a human within 15 minutes after a request for human customer service is made, or schedule an appointment with the customer.
L57: The law also requires large companies to disclose when connecting with customer service if the representative is a chatbot and not a human, and also requires certain large businesses to list contact information on their websites so customers know how to reach customer service.
L58: ✅Turning down the volume on excessively loud commercials, building on cite26†legislation the Governor signed last year focused on television advertisements, by requiring that podcast and music streaming commercials can’t be louder than the content itself.
L59: ✅ Helping parents more easily connect with their child’s teacher, by giving parents the ability to request a parent-teacher conference online or over the phone if they are unable to attend in person. Building on Governor Newsom’s work to make it easier for parents to be engaged in their child’s education.
L60: ✅ Keeping more money in your pockets by eliminating junk fees for completing medical documents. Now in California, physicians can’t charge unnecessary fees to complete forms required for paid family or medical leave.
L61: 
L62: ✅ Allowing teenagers to get a library card without parental accompaniment — if you can drive a car, you should also be able to get a library card. Now 16- and 17-year-olds don’t need to bring a parent with them to get a public library card.
L63: ✅ Eliminating red tape to install residential heat pumps. When a homeowner wants to make a commonsense upgrade to their home, they shouldn’t have to worry about navigating a Byzantine permitting process. Also, creating an automated and streamlined permitting process for standard heat pump water heaters and HVAC installations, reducing the time and energy needed to make these critical upgrades.

L48: # Governor Newsom signs historic law to stop big business from shutting out competition, cuts red tape to speed up business permitting
L49: 
L50: What you need to know: The Golden State is home to over 7.6 million small businesses, the most in America, and today Governor Newsom is signing legislation to encourage fair competition and expand opportunities for entrepreneurs and workers.
L51: SACRAMENTO – Governor Gavin Newsom announced he has signed new legislation to level the playing field for California’s small businesses, cut red tape, strengthen competition, and give entrepreneurs more tools to open businesses, grow, and succeed.
L52: The new laws will crack down on monopolies, help turn vacant storefronts into thriving pop-up businesses, speed up commercial permitting, and protect businesses from abusive financial practices. Governor Newsom is making California’s economy work better for consumers, workers, and entrepreneurs, not just the biggest and best-connected.
L53: California didn’t become the fourth-largest economy in the world by chance. We got here because of the innovators, risk-takers, and entrepreneurs who started small and helped build this state from the ground up.
L54: Today we’re leveling the playing field for small businesses by making it easier to open and expand businesses, cutting red tape, and protecting competition, We’re taking on predatory practices that drive up costs and shut entrepreneurs out — making sure California’s economy works for everyone, not just the biggest and best-connected.
L55: Governor Gavin Newsom
L56: “California should be a place where our small businesses, workers, and entrepreneurs get a fair shot, and where consumers aren’t held hostage by companies that have crushed their competition and can now raise prices unchecked. I celebrate successful businesses, big and small. But when a corporation stops competing by offering the best product or service and instead uses its power to crush competitors, government has a responsibility to step in,” said Assembly Majority Leader Aguiar-Curry (D-Winters).
L57: “Thank you to Governor Newsom for signing the COMPETE Act, and to everyone who helped make it happen. California now has stronger tools to protect our small businesses, workers, and consumers and to make sure our markets work for everyone.”
L58: Here’s how new legislation signed by Governor Newsom will help power Golden State small businesses.
L59: 
L60: ✅ Making a major update to California’s antitrust and consumer protection laws. Clamping down on anti-competitive behavior, encouraging competition, and expanding opportunities for entrepreneurs and workers — including by creating new authority for the Attorney General and district attorneys to combat monopolistic behavior while also protecting small businesses.
L61: ✅Requiring cities to create a new permit that allows pop-up businesses to operate in vacant stores for up to 120 days, helping to drive up foot traffic and expand opportunities for small businesses.
L62: ✅Streamlining qualifying small business tenant-improvement permits by requiring local governments to provide applicants with a plan-check timeline and, when review is excessively delayed, allowing specified small businesses to use approved third-party plan checkers to review building plans and help them get permits and open their doors faster.
L63: ✅Helping retailers open and operate faster by allowing businesses undertaking certain tenant improvements to hire qualified architects or engineers to review and certify that building plans comply with life-safety, health, and building code requirements.
L64: 
L65: ✅Giving Californians more flexibility and convenience by allowing restaurants and bars to continue offering cocktails to-go.
L66: 
L67: ✅Protecting small businesses from abusive financial practices by unscrupulous financing companies.
L68: ✅Ensuring access to small business technical assistance by supporting providers who offer free or low-cost one-on-one, confidential consulting and training. This will help small businesses and entrepreneurs start, grow, expand, and create jobs in California.
