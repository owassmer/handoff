L48: # Governor Newsom signs legislation to accelerate wildfire prevention projects, strengthen fire response, and harden communities against future disasters
L49: 
L50: The new law builds on Governor Newsom’s successful executive order and proclamation effort that have fast-tracked more than 500 wildfire projects while cutting red tape for prescribed burns
L51: 
L52: cite26†Image L53: What you need to know: Governor Newsom today signed legislation to make law his successful emergency actions that have fast-tracked and simplified more than 500 critical wildfire-prevention projects and expanded prescribed and cultural burning and training. The legislative package also works to lower the up-front cost of making homes more fire-resistant and supports new tools to prevent and fight wildfires.
L54: SAN FRANCISCO – Governor Newsom, joined by CAL FIRE firefighters, today signed legislation to protect Californians from wildfire by speeding up fire mitigation projects, making it easier to protect homes and communities, and investing in new innovative tools to prevent and fight fires. The laws build on the Governor and Legislature’s unprecedented wildfire preparedness investments and come as the state has fast-tracked more than 500 priority prevention projects covering nearly 130,000 acres.
L55: From day one, we’ve worked to transform how California prepares for and responds to wildfires. In partnership with the Legislature, we’ve made historic investments in prevention, resilience, technology, and the emergency responders on the front lines — building a stronger system designed for the climate reality we face.
L56: These new laws carry that work forward by speeding up critical prevention projects, helping Californians protect their homes and communities, expanding proven prevention tools, and giving firefighters every possible advantage. The goal has always been bigger than responding to the next fire; it’s about leaving California safer, stronger, and better prepared for the generations that follow.
L57: 
L58: Governor Gavin Newsom
L59: cite27†A b-roll package of the Governor signing this legislation can be downloaded here.†govca.app.box.com L60: 
L61: cite28†Image L62: The new legislation creates a more durable, ongoing process to achieve the faster project reviews demonstrated through the Governor’s temporary wildfire streamlining measures, helping move critical wildfire mitigation work from planning to implementation more efficiently while maintaining environmental safeguards. With these new laws, California will have more tools to protect communities, homes, infrastructure, ecosystems, and landscapes. The legislative package will:
L63: 🌲 Speed up wildfire mitigation projects to protect communities and landscapes to avoid catastrophic wildfires by enacting into law Governor Newsom’s executive proclamation, which has already cite29†fast-tracked more than 500 priority wildfire prevention projects†wildfiretaskforce.org , including over 310,000 activity acres of treatments across nearly 130,000 footprint acres across California.
L64: Projects impacted by the new law will move critical fuel reduction, forest restoration, and community protection projects from planning to implementation faster, saving a year or more of review and red tape.
L65: 🔥 Expand tools to safely deploy prescribed and cultural burning, including increasing and cutting red tape for State-Certified Prescribed Fire Burn Bosses. New legislation will extend burn bosses’ certification from one year to three years, require the State Fire Marshal to explore ways to expand the pool of burn boss instructors, and codify cite30†Governor Newsom’s 2025 executive order to expand tools to safely deploy beneficial fire projects.
L66: 💪 Establish the Fire Innovation Unit to serve as CAL FIRE’s innovation arm for wildfire technology research, coordination, procurement, and deployment for technologies that improve community risk reduction and ecosystem and landscape-scale resilience.
L67: 
L68: ✅ Provide homeowners and communities with tools to improve community and home hardening, including establishing a low-interest loan program to incentivize home hardening and defensible space activities for homes and businesses, reducing up-front costs.
L69: 🤝 Promote local coordination by establishing the California Wildfire Preparedness Program to support county-level coordination of wildfire mitigation, prevention, preparedness, and recovery. The program will develop guidance and standards for local wildfire-risk modeling and analysis, helping communities prioritize mitigation investments.
L70: ⚡ Improve electric infrastructure safety by requiring the California Public Utilities Commission to update its rules to require the removal of permanently abandoned transmission lines, as determined by the Commission. It also requires utilities to account for active, inactive, and abandoned transmission infrastructure in their wildfire-mitigation plans.
L71: 💧 Strengthen water-system preparedness for urban retail water suppliers serving high- and very-high-fire-hazard-severity zones. Suppliers will incorporate wildfire preparedness and response into disaster-preparedness and emergency-response plans, including actions to mitigate impacts, prepare critical infrastructure, respond during a wildfire, and recover afterward.
L72: California continues to make historic investments in wildfire resilience, including forest health, prescribed fire, community hardening, home retrofits, emergency preparedness, and new technology to detect and respond to fires faster.
L73: Earlier this month, the Governor, in partnership with the Legislature, allocated $25 million from the climate bond for a statewide community wildfire mitigation program and $117 million in Greenhouse Gas Reduction Funds to support projects that prevent wildfires, increase community resilience, make our forests healthier, and advance home and community hardening.

L103: ## Streamlined permitting making an impact on-the-ground
L104: 
L105: Governor Newsom’s executive order, which has now been codified as law, has helped move critical fuel reduction, forest restoration, and community protection projects from planning to implementation faster, saving a year or more of review and red tape for more complicated projects. Partners across the state have been able to complete work more efficiently while maintaining strong environmental protections. Notable projects include:
L106:   * Protecting communities impacted by the 2025 Los Angeles firestorms: 600+ acre fuels reduction collaborative project protecting communities in the Los Angeles area near the Palisades footprint, led by the Mountains Recreation and Conservation Authority.
L107: 
L108:   * Expanding prescribed and cultural burning on the North Coast: 450-acre Prosper Ridge Community Wildfire Resilience Project that is incorporating cultural burning and prescribed fire for community protection in Humboldt County.
L109:   * Restoring large landscapes and protecting rural Northern California communities: Nearly 3,000-acre Scott Valley/Callahan Fuels Reduction Project to restore ecosystem health and protect vulnerable rural communities in Siskiyou County.
L110: 
L111:   * Assisting senior and disabled residents protect their homes in the Sierra Nevada: 302-acre project to assist senior and disabled WUI residents in Tuolumne County with removing vegetation and creating defensible space
L112:   * Restoring wildfire impacted private lands on the Central Coast: 520-acre Calforests supported disaster recovery project of private lands in Santa Cruz County impacted by the 2020 CZU Lightning Complex Fires
L113: 
L114:   * Reducing wildfire risk through prescribed grazing in Southern California: 240-acre project in San Diego County to protect the Sycuan Reservation from wildfire by reducing fire hazard with the use of 300 grazing goats.
L115:   * Protecting the Heritage Ranch community in San Luis Obispo County: 485-acre project to construct an 8-mile strategic fuel break, improve roadside clearance, and create defensible space to protect the 3,800-resident Heritage Ranch community, a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone with a history of large wildfires.
L116:   * Reducing wildfire risk near Arnold in Calaveras County: 2,171-acre strategic fuel break to protect the community of Arnold, using mechanical mastication, manual hand cutting, and piling and chipping to reduce fuel loads and prepare the landscape for prescribed fire.
L117:   * Reducing fire risk in San Elijo in San Diego County: 10-acre project to enhance community defensible space by removing hazardous and dead non-native trees and invasive vegetation, including eucalyptus, acacia, palms, and black mustard.
L118:   * Strengthening wildfire protection in the Hiouchi-Smith River Area of Del Norte County: 1,677-acre project to improve existing emergency containment lines created during the Chetco Bar and Smith River Complex fires and establish a more permanent shaded fuel break along strategic ridgetops.
L119: ## Building on nation-leading prevention funding and wildfire readiness
L120: 
L121: As part of the state’s ongoing investment in wildfire resilience and emergency response, CAL FIRE has significantly expanded its workforce over the past five years by adding an average of 1,800 full-time and 600 seasonal positions annually. Over the next four years and beyond, CAL FIRE will continue to hire additional firefighters, natural resource professionals, and support personnel to meet the state’s growing demands.
L122: Governor Newsom has invested millions of dollars to protect communities from wildfire, with $135 million available for new and ongoing prevention projects and $72 million going out the door to projects across the state. This is part of over $5 billion that the Newsom administration, in collaboration with the legislature, has invested in wildfire and forest resilience since 2019.
L123: This builds on consecutive years of intensive and focused work by California to confront the severe ongoing risk of catastrophic wildfires. Bold moves to streamline state-level regulatory processes build on long-term efforts already underway in California to increase wildfire response and forest management in the face of a hotter, drier climate.

L48: # As Trump targets public lands, Governor Newsom expands free state parks access, announces record conservation progress, and signs legislation protecting California’s coast and wildlife
L49: 
L50: Governor signs conservation legislative package as California adds 1 million conserved acres to world-leading 30×30 effort
L51: 
L52: cite26†Image L53: What you need to know: As the Trump administration attempts to open a portion of Yosemite National Park to private development, weakens protections for public lands and wildlife, and threatens California’s coast with expanded offshore oil and gas drilling, Governor Gavin Newsom today signed a conservation package protecting California’s coast, wildlife and outdoor access, while announcing the state’s largest annual increase in conserved lands to date.
L54: SACRAMENTO – As the Trump administration attempts to open a portion of Yosemite National Park to private development, weakens protections for public lands and wildlife, and threatens California’s coast with expanded offshore oil and gas drilling, Governor Gavin Newsom today signed a conservation package protecting California’s coast, wildlife and outdoor access, while announcing the state’s largest annual increase in conserved lands to date.
L55: Today, at the cite27†California Natural Resources Agency’s 30×30 Partnership Summit†youtube.com , Governor Newsom also announced the release of a cite28†new 30×30 Progress Report spotlighting California’s historic progress toward conserving 30% of its lands and coastal waters by 2030.
L56: While Donald Trump looks for ways to hand public lands to private developers and roll back the safeguards that protect our parks, wildlife, and coastal communities, California is doing the opposite. We are conserving more land than ever before, protecting our iconic coastline from new offshore oil and gas drilling, revitalizing wildlife habitat, and ensuring every Californian can experience the natural treasures that make the Golden State extraordinary.
L57: 
L58: Governor Gavin Newsom
L59: cite29†A b-roll package of the Governor signing this legislation can be downloaded here.†govca.app.box.com L60: Governor Newsom first launched California’s cite30†30×30 effort through executive order in 2020 , making California one of the first governments in the world to commit to conserving 30% of its lands and coastal waters by the end of the decade.
L61: The cite31†Legislature later codified this commitment under SB 337, which the Governor signed in 2023.†wildfiretaskforce.org The report shows California is on track to meet the 30×30 goal, building on partnerships with California Native American tribes, local communities, landowners, and conservation organizations across the state.
L62: “We’ve made tremendous progress, but this isn’t simply about reaching a number on a chart,” said California Secretary for Natural Resources Wade Crowfoot. “We’re protecting the places Californians love and depend on — our rivers, forests, coast, and open spaces — and ensuring they remain healthy for generations to come.”
L63: Under Governor Newsom, California has counted more than 3.5 million acres of conserved land and coastal waters since 2022 — an area larger than Rhode Island and Delaware combined — putting the state past the halfway mark and on track to meet the 30×30 land goal. More than 1 million acres of land were added to the 30×30 database in the past year alone, bringing the state to 27.1% of lands conserved, and 21.9% of coastal waters conserved.
L64: ## California stands up to Trump to protect its coasts, wildlife, and public lands
L65: Under Governor Newsom’s leadership and in partnership with the Legislature, California has expanded protections for sacred tribal lands, restored critical habitat at the Salton Sea, earned international recognition for marine conservation, and broadened access to parks and the outdoors.
L66: Today’s legislation builds on that progress by protecting California’s coast from expanded offshore oil drilling, strengthening wildlife coexistence and habitat connectivity, expanding access to parks and outdoor spaces, and helping prepare the next generation to address climate change. Together, these bills will:
L67: ✅ Protect California’s coast from expanded offshore oil drilling: Responds to federal efforts to expand offshore oil and gas leasing off California’s coast by prohibiting the use of existing state infrastructure or leases for new federal offshore oil and gas leases.
L68: The measure requires the State Lands Commission to consider additional factors before approving an offshore lease renewal, amendment or modification, and eliminates its authority to issue an offshore oil-and-gas lease in a current or future coastal sanctuary.
L69: ✅ Support ranchers and wildlife through coexistence: Establishes the California Wildlife Coexistence Act to reduce, minimize and mitigate conflicts between people and wildlife. The measure supports proactive wildlife-conflict response, tracking, depredation management, animal-welfare and wildlife-health efforts, while continuing compensation for verified wolf depredation losses when livestock producers employ nonlethal deterrence practices.
L70: ✅ Make wildlife connectivity a routine part of highway planning: Builds on California’s wildlife-connectivity work by requiring Caltrans to publish an inventory of wildlife connectivity assets in a supplemental list to the Transportation Asset Management Plan and continue to include wildlife connectivity as an objective in the State Highway System Management Plan.   These improvements will help animals move safely between habitats, reduce wildlife-vehicle collisions and protect drivers and passengers.
L71: ✅ Expand equitable access to the outdoors: Codifies the Governor’s Outdoors for All initiative at the California Natural Resources Agency. Outdoors for All advances efforts to ensure all Californians can recreate, enjoy and help protect the Golden State’s cultural, historic, and natural resources. It builds on more than $1 billion in investments led by Governor Newsom and First Partner Jennifer Siebel Newsom to expand parks and nature access, prioritizing underserved communities.
L72: ✅ Make the State Parks Library Pass permanent: Makes the cite32†California State Parks Library Pass†www.parks.ca.gov program permanent, paired with cite33†ongoing budget funding . Through the program, Californians can check out free state parks passes at more than 1,100 public library branches statewide. A total of 33,000 passes have been issued, helping libraries reach new audiences through programs that connect communities with state parks.
L73: ✅ Recognize student achievement in climate learning: Establishes the voluntary State Seal of Climate Literacy, allowing schools to create pathways for students to demonstrate mastery in climate-literacy disciplines.
L74: 
L75: The legislation reflects a shared commitment by the Governor and the Legislature to protect California’s natural treasures, support communities, and ensure Californians can enjoy the outdoors. Key authors of today’s legislation helped turn California’s values into action:
L76: Assemblymember Gregg Hart (D-Santa Barbara), author of AB 1448 and AB 1804: “California has made it clear that our coast is not open for expanded oﬀshore drilling. As the Trump Administration continues its reckless push to expand fossil fuel production in federal waters, AB 1448 ensures that existing infrastructure in state waters cannot be used to facilitate those efforts.
L77: AB 1804 helps ensure that all Californians can enjoy and take part in the stewardship of California’s world-renowned natural resources by visiting state parks.”
L78: Senator Lena Gonzalez (D-Long Beach), author of SB 1268: “Thank you to Governor Newsom for signing SB 1268 and making outdoor equity a lasting priority for California. Our 30×30 goals are about more than protecting land—they’re about making sure every Californian has the opportunity to access and enjoy our natural spaces. SB 1268 helps make equitable access a lasting part of California’s conservation efforts.”
L79: Senator Catherine Blakespear (D-Encinitas), author of SB 1135: “I thank the Governor for signing SB 1135, which meaningfully addresses human and wildlife conflict by using thoughtful, proactive strategies that are non-lethal. We have now re-established a wildlife coexistence program in California, so we can better protect communities, people and animals that call our state home.”
L80: Senator Dave Cortese (D-Silicon Valley), author of SB 1250: “Too many Californians have paid the price when wildlife and vehicles collide resulting in lives lost, families devastated, and animals killed. We have the knowledge and technology to prevent these tragedies, and we cannot afford to keep treating them as inevitable. SB 1250 puts prevention first.
L81: It means building safer roads, protecting wildlife corridors, and making sure California’s transportation system works for both people and the natural environment.”

L53: What you need to know: Governor Newsom is protecting communities, strengthening public safety, and pushing back against Donald Trump’s totalitarian tactics, signing a package of bills to expand oversight of federal immigration enforcement in California.
L54: SACRAMENTO – Governor Gavin Newsom today signed a package of legislation to protect California communities and strengthen civil rights and public safety by addressing immigration detention, enforcement transparency, civil rights, state facilities, public records, family services and oversight of civil detention facilities in California.
L55: The legislation reflects California’s work to use its authority to protect Californians and support oversight of federal immigration enforcement activities occurring within the state.
L56: Trump has put his political interests above the health, safety and livelihood of American families. California is taking action to strengthen transparency, accountability, and oversight around immigration enforcement in our state. This is about stepping up where the federal government has failed our communities. We will continue protecting our people, upholding the rule of law, and making clear that if the federal government operates in California we will hold them accountable.
L57: 
L58: Governor Gavin Newsom
L59: The legislation builds on California’s longstanding efforts to support immigrant communities and reflects the state’s response to an intensified federal immigration enforcement environment, including federal operations in Southern California and the federalization of California National Guard personnel in June.
L60: California cannot dictate federal immigration policy. But where federal immigration enforcement operates within California, the state can establish and enforce state-law requirements governing state property, state resources, detention facilities, public records, law-enforcement practices and access to state-administered programs.
L61:

L63: ## Establish stronger standards for law-enforcement practices
L64: 
L65: California has an interest in establishing clear standards for law-enforcement practices and equipment used within the state, including when those practices intersect with immigration enforcement.
L66: 
L67: ✅ Ban on shock gloves, setting limits on uses of wearable electroshock devices by law enforcement.
L68: 
L69: ✅ Restrictions on uses of flash-bang grenades and explosive breaching charges, including in immigration-enforcement activities.
L70: ✅ Prohibits ICE officers who have committed serious misconduct on the job from becoming peace officers or public employees in California.
