L571: 
L572: Any change to the federal rules must be designed to promote simplicity in procedure, fairness in administration, the just determination of litigation, and the elimination of unjustifiable expense and delay.
L573: An amendment to a federal rule generally takes about three years. As described in more detail at cite189†Overview for the Bench, Bar and Public , a proposed rule change is usually considered by an advisory committee and published for comment as part of a document called a Preliminary Draft during the first year of the process.
L574: It is considered by the Committee on Rules of Practice and Procedure (the “Standing Committee”) and the Judicial Conference in the second year, and by the Supreme Court and Congress in the third year.
L575: Below are the proposed amendments organized by the year they are projected to go into effect with links to the relevant Congressional, Supreme Court, Judicial Conference, Standing Committee, and Preliminary Draft materials, as such materials become available. Although the rules are projected to go into effect on the dates listed, they can be delayed for various reasons or withdrawn entirely.
L576:  The status of rule and form changes being considered by advisory committees can be found in their most recent cite163†agenda books or reports .
