L30@P1: BACKGROUND AND RULEMAKING SUMMARY
L31@P1: California continues to experience increasingly destructive wildfire seasons driven by
L32@P1: extreme weather and a changing climate, accumulated fuels, and expanding
L33@P1: development within fire-prone landscapes. While no single action can eliminate wildfire
L34@P1: risk, reducing structure ignitions remains one of the most effective ways to protect lives,
L35@P1: homes, and communities.
L36@P1: The proposed Zone 0 regulations represent one component of California's broader
L37@P1: wildfire resilience strategy. By reducing combustible materials within the first five feet
L38@P1: surrounding structures—where wind-driven embers most commonly accumulate and
L39@P1: ignite homes—the regulations seek to reduce structure-to-structure fire spread while
L40@P1: complementing existing defensible space requirements.
L41@P1: These regulations were developed through one of the Board's most extensive public
L42@P1: rulemaking efforts in recent years, balancing statutory direction, wildfire science,
L43@P1: practical implementation considerations, and extensive public input.
L44@P1: 2025 Legislative Direction and Executive Action
L45@P1: Following the devastating Los Angeles wildfires in January 2025, state leaders
L46@P1: accelerated efforts to strengthen community wildfire resilience and reduce residential
L47@P1: structure loss.
L48@P1: Assembly Bill 3074 (2020), Senate Bill 504 (2024), Assembly Bill 1455 (2025), and Governor
L49@P1: Newsom's Executive Order N-18-25 directed the Board to develop regulations
L50@P1: establishing defensible space standards within the first five feet surrounding structures—
L51@P1: commonly referred to as Zone 0—for specified fire-prone areas of California.
L52@P1: Collectively, these actions established both the policy direction and implementation
L53@P1: timeline for the Board's rulemaking responsibilities while recognizing Zone 0, the “emberresistant” zone, as an important element of California's broader wildfire resilience
L54@P1: strategy.
L55@P1: Extensive Public Engagement and Rule Development
L56@P1: Since initiation of the rulemaking process, the Board has emphasized an open,
L57@P1: transparent, and iterative public process designed to ensure that the proposed
L58@P1: regulations are informed by both the best available fire science and the practical
L59@P1-2: realities faced by California homeowners and communities.

L33@P0: resources, and guidance to make changes effectively and affordably while improving
L34@P0: fire safety for neighborhoods and communities. This happens through a phased-in
L35@P0: timeline for different activities in different zones within Zone 0.
L36@P0: More specifically, the rule will require all property owners in State Responsibility Areas
L37@P0-1: (SRAs) and Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zones in the Local Responsibility Area (LRA) to Board of Forestry and Fire Protection Draft Zone 0 Regulations / August 2026 2
L38@P1: reduce combustible materials within the first 5 feet of homes and other structures. This
L39@P1: draft seeks to balance fire mitigation with other property considerations, while allowing
L40@P1: plants and trees in parts of Zone 0.
L41@P1: Implementation is also designed to take place in two phases over five years, with
L42@P1: education, outreach, and progress towards compliance serving as the longer-term

L48@P1: TIMING AND IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH
L49@P1: The proposed rule language for the Board’s consideration recommends an
L50@P1: implementation approach that prioritizes education and outreach. While Zone 0
L51@P1: requirements will apply to new construction upon adoption of the regulation, existing
L52@P1: homes and structures can implement actions in phases over time within different areas
L53@P1: of Zone 0:
L54@P1: ● PHASE 1 (Implemented within the first 3 years): Includes removing combustible items such
L55@P1: as firewood, dead leaves and branches, mulch, and wood chips within 5 feet of the
L56@P1: structure. Removing dead and dying plants, trimming trees according to regulations, and
L57@P1: implementing or adjusting for allowable vegetation during this time period. To ensure
L58@P1: alignment with current state law, combustible debris on roofs and rain gutters, dead tree
L59@P1: branches, and branches within 10 feet of chimneys would be required to be removed
L60@P1: upon the effective date of the regulations.
L61@P1: ● PHASE 2 (Implemented within 5 years): Local jurisdictions will assign a timeline, within 5
L62@P1: years of the effective date of the regulations, for the creation of an under-eave safety
L63@P1: zone for vegetation other than trees, replacing combustible gates, and making any
L64@P1: necessary adjustments to sheds and fencing.
L65@P1: Phase 2 focuses on the area closest to the building, referred to as a non-combustible
L66@P1: “safety zone.” This phase is designed to protect the most vulnerable parts of a
L67@P1-2: structure—such as the base of the wall, windows, decks, and eaves—while still allowing Board of Forestry and Fire Protection Draft Zone 0 Regulations / August 2026 3
L68@P2: for some flexibility based on the attributes of the home or structure. The separation of
L69@P2: fuels from these vulnerable elements is reflected in the proposed draft.
L70@P2: The two areas—both the area closest to the structure under eaves, and the area comprising the
L71@P2: remainder of Zone 0—are assigned based on the following (see also Figure 1 and accompanying
L72@P2: images):
L73@P2: -A low-combustibility zone extending out from the safety zone, or the edge of an eave drip line, to
L74@P2: the remaining portion of the five feet of Zone 0 (completed during Phase 1).
L75@P2: -A non-combustible immediate “safety zone” located directly next to the home and under eaves.
L76@P2: This zone width flexes with the width of the eave. If the structure has 12-inch eaves, the safety zone
L77@P2: is 12 inches in width. If the structure has 36-inch eaves, the safety zone is 36 inches in width
L78@P2: (completed during Phase 2).
L79@P2: This proposed approach also accounts for variation in building design and aims to
L80@P2: achieve fire safety while providing flexibility that is easy to interpret. At the end of this
L81@P2: document are diagrams and visualizations to aid interpretation.

L83@P2: existing homes over a phased-in period from the effective date of the regulations
