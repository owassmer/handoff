## Finding and required correction

**R1 — consequential remedies interpretation needed before closure.** The original federal memo inventories §1692k but excludes all California remedy/cure synthesis. That leaves an avoidable gap in the promised incorporated-remedies investigation. Add a bounded response-to-violation branch with these actual authorities, not a damages calculator:

- [Afewerki v. Anaya Law Group](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2017/08/18/15-56510.pdf), 868 F.3d 771, 778–779 (9th Cir. 2017), holds §1788.30(d) survives incorporation. On its record the timely correction defeated the Rosenthal claim while the federal claim survived. This is a federal prediction of California law, not California Supreme Court authority.
- Published [Timlick v. National Enterprise Systems](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a154235.html), 35 Cal.App.5th 674, 681–690 (2019), supplies subsequent California appellate agreement. It does not permit extinguishing a putative class merely by curing its representative's claim. Footnote 5 reserves whether particular misrepresentations can be cured; stopping a practice is not automatically undoing its harm. The actual violation, discovery/notice dates, notice of violation, complete correction, resulting harm and affected people matter.
- [Gonzales v. Arrow Financial Services](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/09/23/10-55379.pdf), 660 F.3d 1055, 1064–1069 (9th Cir. 2011), permits cumulative state/federal statutory recovery within its holding; footnote 16 reserves the aggregate-over-federal-cap issue. The contrary double-recovery language in the final pages belongs to the dissent.
- [Jerman v. Carlisle](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/559/573/), 559 U.S. 573 (2010), excludes mistaken interpretation of FDCPA requirements from the federal bona-fide-error defense. Official slip retrieval failed; the linked mirror reproduces the opinion. [Rotkiske v. Klemm](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/19pdf/18-328_pm02.pdf), 589 U.S. 8 (2019), applies the violation-occurrence language of federal §1692k(d), without a general discovery rule; reserved equitable issues and California limitations must remain distinct.
- Currentness search found the published September 8, 2025 [Kashanian opinion](https://infobytes.orrick.com/wp-content/uploads/Opinion-9.8.pdf), pp.4–8: no actual damage does not eliminate California statutory standing/remedies. The source is the judicial opinion hosted by a third party. Do not automatically carry federal Article III injury requirements into state court.

Author and parent have been notified and are adding this bounded synthesis. Recheck is recorded below when available.
