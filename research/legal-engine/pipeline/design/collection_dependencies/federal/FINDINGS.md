## A/B exception decision

1. A requires an actual officer/employee of the creditor collecting for that creditor **in the creditor's name**. Capture employment/office, creditor identity, communication sender and name used. The statutory exception also covers that person's principal. An outside provider is not an employee merely because authorized to collect.
2. B requires collector and other person related through common ownership/corporate control, collection **only** for those related/affiliated persons, and collector's principal business **not** debt collection. Capture corporate relationships, customer universe and actual business. Common ownership alone fails the test.
3. When A or B (or its principal) is established, the California incorporation excludes e(11) disclosure and all g; it does **not** remove b/c/d/remaining e/f/h/i/j or identity-theft obligations.
4. Other federal exclusions, especially a(6)(F)(iii) obtaining servicing before default, do not qualify for this narrow California exception. A California-covered independent management company could therefore owe historical g even when excluded from independent federal FDCPA coverage.
5. Unproved relationship or exception is a missing case fact, not a negative answer. Obtain the management/employment agreement, corporate ownership, service-start/default chronology, actual collection practices and proposed letter before selecting the branch.

## Current federal law is a separate overlay

Compare saved 2000 and2024 sources. Substantive later changes within recovered sections are: a(1) FTC becomes CFPB Bureau (2010); g(b) adds collection-during-validation/anti-overshadowing sentences and g(d)/(e) add formal-pleading and specified noncollection-notice exclusions (2006); k(e) substitutes Bureau for Commission (2010). Typographic/editorial intrasection-reference changes are not new duties. Do not import these later words into the California frozen text. Conversely, the absence of later statutory wording does not prove preexisting case law allowed overshadowing.

Independent current FDCPA coverage needs a household-purpose transaction debt under a(5) and actual collector analysis under a(6): principal-purpose collection business OR regularly collecting debts for another; creditor using misleading third-party name included; security-interest enforcement has its limited f(6) treatment; apply each A–F exclusion to its actual conditions. Ownership of a debt does not by itself decide principal-purpose status, and obtaining a debt before default is a condition of a particular exclusion, not a universal debt definition. Parent research resolves tenancy authorities.

Current [RegF1](RegF-1.txt) and [RegF2](RegF-2.txt) establish independent coverage. If covered, apply current duties from saved §§1006.6/.14/.18/.22/.26/.30/.34/.38/.42/.100, including communication/harassment, time-barred litigation, reporting prerequisites, detailed validation content, disputes, delivery and retention. This report locates and preserves these provisions; it does not claim a full RegulationF implementation. §§1006.34(c)(2)(iii)/(3)(iv) have additional consumer-financial-product scope, so do not indiscriminately put every item on a residential account form. California-only coverage does not itself activate all RegulationF duties. Where both layers apply, one layer's permitted alternative is not automatically sufficient for the other.

**Dispute distinction:** historical g(a)(3) does not require a dispute be written to contest assumed validity; g(a)(4)/(5) and(b) require writing for specified verification/original-creditor rights and collection suspension. Ninth Circuit *Riggs v.Prober & Raphael*,681F.3d1097(2012), saved official PDF pp.6539–6545, applies *Camacho*'s oral-dispute distinction. Do not tell an operator an oral dispute is legally meaningless. Independently, e(8) reporting of dispute and California identity theft can change actions.

**Pleadings:** historical e(11) expressly excepts formal pleadings, but historical g lacks current g(d). This does not establish a universally settled affirmative rule that every pleading triggers California g. *Velazquez v.Arrow Financial Services*,S.D.Cal.No.08cv1915, Doc21(2009), official PDF pp6–7, directly explains the frozen reference and conflicting out-of-circuit interpretation, then dismisses the state claim without prejudice after dismissing federal claims; it does not settle the California merits. Searches for later California controlling treatment did not locate one. Handoff should not rely on the post2006 federal exception to omit a California notice; send an otherwise accurate timely notice when applicable or obtain case-specific litigation analysis before relying on a pleading-only exemption. This is an explicit legal-interpretation limitation with a concrete action path, not a missing source treated as resolved.

## Identity theft: §1788.18(d) and historical f(1)

Full current [1788.18](CA-1788.18.txt) supplies its own debtor definition (i), so do not restrict identity-theft review solely by the general natural-person debtor definition. This report's consumer workflow does not purport to decide all commercial applications.

- On receipt of the complete a/b package, cease collection until d review completed: signed FTC identity-theft report **or** police report (cannot demand police in addition to FTC), plus qualifying written statement. The certified-statement route's listed items apply to the extent relevant, with available-information qualifiers; do not demand every item universally. Oral claim -> tell debtor claim must be written. Incomplete written claim -> if not ceasing, send written missing-items/certification notice or FTC form, as c specifies.
- Within10business days after complete receipt, initiate review of all debtor information and available file/creditor information; if adverse information was furnished, notify the reporting agency account is disputed. The text does **not** impose10business days to complete the review.
- Send determination notice no later than10business days after conclusion. Recommence only after good-faith finding that information does not establish nonresponsibility **and** written notice of determination and basis before any further collection. A10day notice allowance does not permit earlier collection without notice.
- f(1)'s agreement-or-law authorization rule governs the determination. A ledger bearing the person's name does not resolve theft. Inspect underlying agreement, authorization/identity evidence, conflicting residence/signature records, credits and every fee/interest basis; no model score supplies authorization. The adverse evidence can require removing charges or ending collection, not merely sending a canned denial.
- If ceasing and not recommencing: within10business days of determination notify furnishing agency to delete adverse information(g)(1) and notify creditor collection ended for identity theft(g)(2). Both paragraphs expressly impose the10business-day deadline. No presumption of validity/liability arises from stopping or restarting(e). f/h cross-references concern other civil identity-theft rights and document access, not a substitute for d's review.

Code can compute deadlines after event/receipt/calendar facts are established; agent investigates contradictions and supported responsibility; Jev only assists focused semantic tasks with sufficient evidence, not statutory applicability certification. This goal establishes deadline triggers/units, not an unreviewed business-day calendar implementation.

## Adversarial operating cases for independent review

- Creditor employee collecting in creditor name: e(11)/g exception, still no invented cleaning/collection charge under f(1).
- Independent manager acquired servicing before default: possible federal F(iii) exclusion; California A/B exception not proved, so no automatic g exemption.
- Affiliate collects for unrelated client too: B's only-related-persons condition fails even if common ownership exists.
- Collector receives oral dispute day20: record dispute; do not falsely require writing for g(a)(3); distinguish b suspension and applicable other duties.
- Complete theft package, no determination by day10: collection remains stopped; initiate-review deadline must be met, but no fictitious statutory completion deadline.
- Determination completed day12; notice planned day20: no restart before written determination/basis despite notice still within allowed10business days.
- Operator generates landlord letter implying law firm participation that is absent: j branch and misleading-representation review.
- Present federal formal-pleading exemption asserted for California notice: no automatic carryover; preserve identified interpretation limitation.

Remaining factual work belongs to the actual case: coverage, A/B relationships, communication/receipt chronology, dispute content, reporting, charge basis, identity evidence and actual actions. Remaining broader scope is full litigation valuation/procedure beyond the [bounded remedies and cure synthesis](REMEDIES.md), RegulationF execution/calendar compilation, local tenancy rules, and litigation-specific pleading interpretation. None is silently counted as a production-ready California collection engine.
