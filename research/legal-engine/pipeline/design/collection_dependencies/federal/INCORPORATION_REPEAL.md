# Related historical-reference issue: CIV1788.66 and repealed CCP1179.02

Asked during Goal3 dependency work. This is a reasoned application of controlling interpretive law, not a discovered holding adjudicating §1788.66 itself.

**Do not automatically turn off CIV1788.66 because current CCP1179.02 is blank/repealed.** Current official §1788.66 remains published and prohibits sale/assignment of qualifying unpaid COVID rental debt; it references1179.02 for the debt definition, and identifies its own March1,2020–September30,2021 debt period and historical2020/2021 income facts. It contains no sunset sentence.

Primary judicial texts saved: *Palermo v.Stockton Theatres*,32Cal2d53,58–60(1948), in `Palermo-1948.html/.txt`; *People v.Rojas*,15Cal5th561(2023), slip pp9–13, in `Rojas-2023.html/.txt` (California Supreme Court opinions hosted by Stanford's court-opinion archive). Palermo states specific incorporation normally retains the referenced provision despite later amendment/repeal absent clearly contrary intent. Rojas reaffirms that rule but emphasizes the numbered reference is not dispositive: legislative intent controls; citing *InreJovanB.*,6Cal4th801,816fn10, no modern application disregards other evidence of intent. Rojas treated a particular code-definition reference as dynamic in its statutory context. Thus neither an automatic-freeze parser nor a broken-link/sunset parser establishes meaning.

Here the actual same-enactment context supports continued use of the historical debt definition. Parent's official chaptered `assigned/sources/AB832_chaptered.txt` §§2,3,19 deliberately:

- §2 gives CIV1788.65's general sale/assignment prohibition an express October1,2021 repeal;
- §3 sets out CIV1788.66's narrower, historically conditioned prohibition **without** that sunset;
- §19 supplies CCP1179.07's October1,2025 repeal of **that chapter**, not CIV1788.66.

It would collapse this deliberate distinction to carry the CCP procedural chapter's repeal over to the separate Civil Code restriction. The historical rent period and assistance/income conditions describe existing debt, not eligibility for an ongoing eviction-protection program. Parent should use the recovered contemporaneous1179.02 definitions to identify that historical debt, preserving their exact dates/wording, and independently establish §1788.66's assistance/income conditions. Neither the end of pandemic eviction protections nor the closure of an assistance program proves the debt sale permissible.

Interpretive conclusion: the best-supported operating branch retains §1788.66's sale/assignment restriction for qualifying historical debt afterOctober1,2025. Label this as synthesis of current text, same-enactment structure and SupremeCourt interpretive principles. No direct published §1788.66 post-sunset appellate holding was located in this research. If an operator seeks a transaction relying specifically on an assertion that repeal erased the restriction, do not authorize on that theory without contrary controlling authority or case-specific legal resolution. This is not an assertion that every reference to a repealed statute survives.
