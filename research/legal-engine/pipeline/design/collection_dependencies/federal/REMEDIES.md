# Bounded remedies and correction branch

Sources: full current official [1788.30](CIV1788.30.txt), [1788.32](CIV1788.32.txt), [1788.33](CIV1788.33.txt); saved official [Afewerki](Afewerki-2017.pdf), [Gonzales](Gonzales-2011.pdf), [Jerman](Jerman-2010.pdf), [Rotkiske](Rotkiske-2019.pdf); published California opinion [Timlick](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2019/a154235.html), retained web capture. ## Correcting an identified violation

CIV1788.30(d) remains available despite1788.17 incorporation. Its actual conditions are: a violation **able to be cured**; within15days after discovering it **or** receiving written notice of it, notify the debtor of the violation **and** make whatever adjustments/corrections are necessary to cure it with respect to the debtor. This is15days, not15business days. Retain both discovery and written-receipt events, notification evidence and actual completed corrections. Do not restart the window on later written notice after known earlier discovery. Do not count a plan, hidden ledger edit, mere cessation or apology as completed cure. If the harm cannot be undone, do not assume correction removes liability. The statute's nonintentional-violation defense in(e) is separate from(d) and requires the collector to prove its prerequisites by a preponderance of evidence.

*Afewerki v.Anaya Law Group*,868F3d771,778–779(9thCir2017), official slippp13–16: erroneous principal/interest allegations were corrected with notice within15days of discovery on an uncontradicted record; Rosenthal judgment affirmed under(d), while FDCPA judgment was vacated and remanded. Thus **California cure does not itself extinguish independent federal exposure**. The court distinguished imported remedies from retained defenses. This was not a holding that every financial misstatement is curable.

Published *Timlick v.National Enterprise Systems*,35CalApp5th674(2019), slippp6–9 agrees cure survives1788.17 and allows cure of its particular type-size violation. Footnotes5–6 expressly qualify curability where other harms cannot be undone. The court reversed dismissal of the entire putative class despite individual cure: do not infer that correcting one account resolves other affected accounts/class exposure. Footnote2 treats the contention that notice to counsel was insufficient as forfeited; it does not decide a general alternative-delivery rule. Correct notice through the legally proper recipient/channel after representation restrictions are considered.

Operating action: halt the offending practice, investigate whether other accounts are affected, preserve original and corrected communications/amounts, promptly complete supported corrections and debtor notice, then assess each state/federal claim separately. Do not present a15day grace period to violate the law prospectively.

## Remedies coexist; individual-only text is not the final answer

CIV1788.30(a) supplies actual damages; (b) adds$100–$1,000 court-selected penalty for willful/knowing violations; (c) costs and prevailing-debtor reasonable fees, with creditor fees dependent on court finding lack of good faith; (f) states a one-year occurrence period. However1788.17 imports1692k remedies notwithstanding contradictory limitations: class remedies and statutory damages without the same knowing-violation requirement are available. Do not read1788.30's individual-only language as eliminating class exposure. *Afewerki*pp15 and *Timlick*pp10–14 explain these relationships.

CIV1788.32 makes remedies cumulative. *Gonzales v.Arrow Financial Services*,660F3d1055,1064–1069(9thCir2011), majority officialpp13–20, permits cumulative FDCPA/Rosenthal statutory damages. Its total award was below the federal class ceiling. **Footnote16** reserves whether combined class statutory damages above$500,000 or1%net worth would be partly preempted; do not convert its holding into unlimited stacking. Footnote15 explains the below-cap case. The dissent begins officialp21 and opposes cumulative recovery; that is not the holding. These statutory damages differ from duplicating compensation for the same injury; this report does not authorize double actual-loss recovery or calculate a settlement demand. Absence of documented actual loss does not by itself remove available statutory remedies.

CIV1788.33 makes waiver of the title's provisions void/unenforceable. Do not insert a prospective lease/workflow opt-out or treat an operator instruction as waiver of debtor protections. This text alone does not resolve every later negotiated settlement/release question; those require their actual terms and relevant law.

## Two federal safeguards against false reassurance

*Jerman v.Carlisle*,559US573(2010), majority opening at577 and discussion/conclusion:1692k(c)'s bona-fide-error defense does not cover mistaken interpretation of the FDCPA's legal requirements. A legal model's confident error or an agent's good intention is not this defense. Maintain actual preventive procedures and investigate clerical/factual error conditions separately. The Court reserved questions about interpretation of other law and precise clerical/factual boundaries; do not expand its holding into a universal statement about every state-law error or import its scope mechanically into1788.30(e).

*Rotkiske v.Klemm*,589US8(2019), majority slippp4–7: absent an applicable equitable doctrine,1692k(d)'s one-year period runs from the violation, not discovery. The Court did not decide the merits of the unpreserved fraud-specific/equitable arguments. Keep violation, discovery, service and concealment events separately. Do not auto-close potential exposure one year after discovery or claim all late claims barred regardless of equity. This federal claim-period rule is distinct from the15day California cure trigger and does not determine the limitation period on the underlying tenant debt.

## Rechecked identity-theft deadline

An initial reviewer objection to the1788.18(g)(2) creditor deadline was withdrawn after direct rereading. Current saved official text expressly gives10business days after determination for **both** adverse-report deletion notice(g)(1) and creditor termination notice(g)(2). FINDINGS now states both expressly. There is no legal change to report from that withdrawn objection.

## Adversarial checks

- Timely correction and notice satisfying state cure: retain independent FDCPA assessment; do not mark all exposure resolved.
- Correction on day10 but omitted debtor notice: d conditions incomplete.
- Earlier discovery followed by later demand: retain earlier timestamp; no artificial reset.
- Harassment/injury cannot be undone merely by stopping: curability unproved.
- One account corrected but same form sent to hundreds: individual cure does not close class issue.
- Legal interpretation error despite detailed model reasoning: Jerman prevents a blanket1692k(c) defense.
- Federal complaint filed within one year of discovery but beyond violation: Rotkiske occurrence rule applies unless actual equitable doctrine changes result.
