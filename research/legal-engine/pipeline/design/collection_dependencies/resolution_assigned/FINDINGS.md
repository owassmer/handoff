# Resolved assigned-debt interpretations

October 1, 2026. Supersedes the precaution-only treatment of these two legal questions in review_assigned/REVIEW.md. Earlier artifacts preserved. These are reasoned statutory conclusions; no court is represented as having decided a question it did not decide. Confidence and case-fact uncertainty remain distinct from the legal answer.

## 1. Assignment before90days followed by aging

**Conclusion: ordinary aging alone does not bring a debt assigned before it was90days past due within CIV1788.14.5. The relevant assignment must be of delinquent debt as the section defines it. A later genuine assignment after the threshold can trigger the section.** This is the stronger construction of the text and enacted structure, not an automatic safety restriction or an exemption inferred merely from an unsuccessful case search.

Reasons:

1. Subsections(a),(b),(c),(e)identify the affected collector by a completed transfer of a defined object—delinquent debt. Substituting(g)'s definition yields an assignment of covered, nonmortgage debt at least90days past due and not charged off. The transfer-date clauses(h)/(i)likewise describe delinquent debt sold or assigned after specified dates. Read together these create a qualifying transfer cohort; they do not say every previously assigned account becomes a new qualifying assignment on day90.
2. Subsection(e)requires a notice in the collector's first written communication and measures the language obligation from initial oral contact. A transfer-cohort reading makes these obligations prospective from the qualifying placement. An aging-only reading either makes an already-sent first letter retroactively deficient or requires rewriting 'first' to mean first after newly qualifying aging. The statute states neither adjustment.
3. Newly retrieved **Assembly Banking and Finance analyses340720/340772**, June28,2021, p4, describe applying these requirements to instances when the collector is assigned a debt that is delinquent. Page6 records the deliberate30→90day threshold change to narrow scope and align delinquent-debt industry practices, alongside the prospective transfer-date amendment. FinalSenateFloor343329 pp2–5 confirms the enacted narrowing. This is legislative support for the transfer-cohort reading; it is not a direct committee discussion of a day60/day100 hypothetical. All nine availableSB531 analyses captured under resolution_assigned; retrievalIDs/hashes logged.
4. General purpose—accurate assigned-account collection—does not erase the Legislature's deliberate threshold. Regular servicing and early-stage placements still face other applicable Rosenthal/federal restrictions. Thus excluding a prethreshold placement from this particular additional record regime is not an absurd exemption from all collection law.

**Competing construction rejected:** (g)'s present tense 'is past due' could describe debt status at the time of a later request, and the statute lacks the words 'at the time of assignment.' That makes the alternative linguistically possible when(g)is isolated. But(g)is a definition inserted into completed-transfer predicates and date-specific scope clauses. The first-contact duties and narrowing amendment give the transfer-cohort reading greater explanatory consistency. We are not adding a separate servicing exemption or borrowing1692a(6)(F)(iii); the result follows from this statute's own defined assignment.

Operational facts and outcome:

- Identify the actual account/receivable assignment or collection placement, its effective date, the component's contractual due date and any cure/modification, charge-off status, mortgage status, and applicable consumer/commercial coverage. Ordinary management engagement is not conclusive evidence of the later account's actual placement date.
- Assignment atday60 and unchanged authority atday100: do not impose14.5's additional notice/30dayresponse/hold solely because the account aged. Other collection duties still apply; information may be supplied for business reasons but that policy is not this legal conclusion.
- New assignment atday100 to another collector—or a genuine later distinct assignment to the same entity—can qualify. An internal label change, file referral or invented reset is not automatically a legal assignment. Examine the instrument and actual authority.
- Assignment atday90qualifies if all other predicates and event dates are met; day89does not. A debtor's later request has no independent initial-notice30daywindow under14.5.
- A later bookkeeping charge-off by the original creditor, while the same qualified assignee retains its collection authority, does not remove the account from the qualifying transfer cohort. The assignee must answer a NEW written request under14.5 even if received after that charge-off, as well as complete pending requests and respect existing holds. The same definition cannot coherently be fixed at assignment for age but re-tested later only for charge-off to extinguish the acquired records protection. The contrary current-status reading of(g)has the same structural weakness discussed above. An assignment FIRST made after charge-off does not qualify under14.5; a later purchase/transfer is separately classified under50/52 and(f), rather than preserving an old actor's status for a new participant. Prior violations are not erased by any later event.

## 2. Late written requests under1788.52(c)

**Conclusion:52(c)incorporates1692g's30dayrequest window, except where the state title supplies different requirements. A genuinely late request, after the relevant valid-notice window has expired, does not itself activate52(c)'s15dayproduction/cessation mechanism.** A request need not use federal terminology or expressly cite the statute; the state law expressly covers written requests for information regarding or proof of the debt.

Reasons:

1. The third sentence directs consistency with1692g's validation requirements; that section repeatedly conditions mandatory written verification/original-creditor requests on the30dayperiod. Reading the reference as only reiterating the writing requirement already stated in52(c)'s first sentence deprives it of much of its function.
2. The opening exception ('except as provided otherwise in this title') gives effect to actual state differences: broader requested records, an affirmative response mechanism,15calendar-dayreceipt-based period, free provision, and agreed electronic channel. Nothing in the title expressly creates an unlimited postnotice request period or rejects1692g's window.
3. The52(d)(1)mandatory records notice describes what can be requested; it does not say 'at any time' or amend(c)'s qualification. Read with the federal validation notice it is complementary. Continued possession/access obligations in(a)/(b)serve their own purpose and do not make every later request trigger(c).
4. *Mahon v.Credit Bureau of Placer County*,171F3d1197,1201–02(9thCir1999), adjudicates1692g directly: a request made almost9months after receipt did not trigger verification. *Hernandez v.Williams,Zinman&Parham*,829F3d1068(9thCir2016), slippp22–23, confirms those rights operate in a30daywindow while requiring renewed notice by each successive collector. These give substantive meaning to the incorporated federal rule. Neither purports to hold on52(c).
5. All sevenSB233analyses and seven capturedSB890predecessor analyses summarize the state's added protection without an explicit contrary timing statement. Their broad descriptions cannot override the enacted cross-reference. SB890was not enacted; it is context only. The priorAragon screeningorder is not relied upon to settle this question.

**Competing construction rejected:** the independent state right and unconditional wording of the records notice are argued to permit indefinite requests. The opening sentence's general request language must be read with the express qualification later in the same subsection. An unlimited-request reading should not make that qualification ineffective. A statutory duty to possess/access evidence continues after the request window; its existence is not an express override of the request deadline.

## 3. Timing and fact branches needed to apply that conclusion

The early-request conclusion is a state-title interpretation, not a claim that all federalcourts imposeg(b)holds before initialcontact. The general first sentence ALONE would not explain treating the beginning and end of the federal period differently. The structural distinction does: California establishes an affirmative records-production mechanism against the actual qualifying buyer upon receipt of a debtor request; federalg(b)instead suspends a collector's activity unless it verifies. InCalifornia, buyer/debtor status and the express production trigger exist before the buyer initiates contact. The borrowed notice opportunity determines when that otherwise available special response right expires, while its creation is already supplied byCalifornia's independent trigger. Requiring prior buyer contact would add a condition to that affirmative state duty and let withholding notice prevent it from arising; refusing an established postnotice late request gives the incorporation clause effect without that consequence. This reasoned harmonization supports the asymmetry; it is not a claim that no competing interpretation exists. **Contrary authority examined:** *Lane v.Fein,Such&Crane*,767FSupp2d382(E.D.N.Y.2011), officialGovInfo courtorder, printedpp8–11 (especially10), rejected an unsolicited preinitialcommunication federalg(b)moratorium. Its reasoning depends on federalg(b)'s specified window and litigation context; it does not interpret California52(c)'s independently expressed affirmative debtor-request trigger and state-title override. Do not misrepresent this districtdecision as bindingCalifornia authority. The state response/hold arises from52(c)'s own15daymechanism, not a borrowed immediatefederalhold.

For a timely qualifying federalwritten dispute/originalcreditorrequest, the applicable federalg(b)hold begins on receipt and lasts until its required verification/information has been supplied in the required way. The state15daydeadline does not create15days of grace from that independenthold. A52recordsrequest with broader subject matter does not automatically match every federalg(b)predicate; classify both triggers from its actual contents.

## 4. Deemed compliance and continuing evidence duties

1788.14.5(f)deems a qualifying debt buyer complying with52 in compliance with14.5. Where52(c)'s response duty is not triggered because the request is genuinely late, compliance means satisfying all52obligations that do apply, including continuing(a)possession/(b)access and notice requirements—not voluntarily fulfilling an inapplicable(c)request. The provision does not create a fallback30dayright for that buyer that nullifies52's incorporated request conditions. Many charged-off buyer accounts are outside14.5's uncharged-offdefinition in any event; preserve predicates rather than inventing overlap. An ordinary assignee who is not a50debt buyer cannot invoke(f)to import52's30dayrequest window.

A late request therefore permits no unsupported written statement:52(a)/(b)still require the enumerated information and qualifying document access **before any written collection statement**. It does not authorize fees or establish balance validity. Known errors, disputedreporting, identitytheft, representation, ceasecommunication, resolveddebts and litigationdocumentation have independent effects. The engine should evaluate those concrete duties rather than attach a universal 'late therefore collect' outcome.
