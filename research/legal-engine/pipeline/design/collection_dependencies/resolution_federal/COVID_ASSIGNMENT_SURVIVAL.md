# Resolved interpretation: §1788.66 survives the CCP chapter repeal

Research resolution, October 1, 2026. This replaces the earlier suggestion in `federal/INCORPORATION_REPEAL.md` that an actual assignment decision must await a fact-identical appellate ruling. The earlier research remains preserved. This conclusion is our application of controlling California interpretive law to the actual enactments, not a claim that a court has specifically decided this precise statute's post-sunset operation.

## Conclusion

**Civil Code §1788.66 continues to prohibit sale or assignment of qualifying unpaid COVID-19 rental debt after October 1, 2025.** Code of Civil Procedure §1179.07's repeal of its chapter does not repeal the separately codified restriction or erase the incorporated historical debt definition. Handoff must continue to apply that definition and §1788.66's own historical eligibility/income conditions to a proposed sale or assignment. An operator cannot escape the prohibition by waiting until the procedural chapter or rental-assistance funding program ends.

This does not forgive the debt, impose a blanket ban on direct collection, or make all historical tenant balances unassignable. It restricts the specified transfer of the specified debt. The actual unpaid obligations, period, assistance qualification and income facts determine the result. The parent assigned-debt analysis establishes the full assistance conditions and whether the proposed arrangement transfers a claim; do not relabel a prohibited assignment as a service agreement.

## Controlling rule

*Palermo v. Stockton Theatres, Inc.*, 32 Cal.2d 53, 58–60 (1948), holds that specific incorporation ordinarily retains the referenced text as it stood and is unaffected by later amendment or repeal absent contrary legislative intent. A general reference to a system of law can instead track later changes.

*People v. Rojas*, 15 Cal.5th 561 (2023), official opinion text saved in `../federal/Rojas-2023.html/.txt`, slip pp. 9–13, cautions that naming a particular code section does not mechanically settle that distinction. Applying *In re Jovan B.*, 6 Cal.4th 801, 816–819 (1993), intent controls. Rojas's reference to the definition of criminal street gang was dynamic because the enactment sought the general contemporaneous definition, while other portions expressly froze dates. These decisions require analysis of §1788.66's content and enactment, not a code-number heuristic.

## Exact enactment and purpose

The current official §1788.66 is saved in `../federal/CIV1788.66.html/.txt`. It prohibits a person from selling or assigning unpaid COVID-19 rental debt defined in CCP §1179.02, for March 1, 2020 through September 30, 2021, where the specified historical federal-assistance qualification and household-income conditions are met. It contains no termination date. Its amendment history identifies AB 832, Stats. 2021, ch. 27, §3, effective June 28, 2021.

The complete official chaptered AB 832 is saved in `../assigned/sources/AB832_chaptered.html/.txt`. Read the provisions together:

| Enactment | Exact role |
|---|---|
| §2, Civil Code §1788.65 | General prohibition on selling/assigning the period's unpaid COVID rental debt; expressly repealed October 1, 2021. |
| §3, Civil Code §1788.66 | Narrower prohibition conditioned on historical assistance qualification and household income; no sunset. |
| §14, CCP §1179.02 | Supplies the historical definition: unpaid rent or other unpaid financial obligation of a tenant under the tenancy becoming due during the specified period, and associated defined terms. Read the full captured definition for actual classification; it is not limited to base rent merely because the short label says rental debt. |
| §19, CCP §1179.07 | Repeals **this chapter** October 1, 2025. It does not refer to Civil Code §1788.66. |

The Legislative Counsel's Digest paragraph (2) is especially direct: AB 832 would extend the debt period through September 30, 2021 and **repeal only the general prohibition** on October 1, 2021. Paragraph (8) separately describes extending the COVID Tenant Relief Act's procedural operation through October 1, 2025. The digest is evidence of the enacted design, not a substitute for statutory text; here it agrees with the express, contrasting sunset clauses.

Section 1788.66 uses historical dates twice: a closed debt period and 2020/2021 income. Its counterfactual 'would have qualified' assistance language describes an historical condition; it does not require a program to remain open on the transfer date. This is a lasting restriction on a finite class of debts, not an ongoing instruction to apply whatever future eviction program happens to exist. Retaining the definition gives every phrase work to do; deleting it would silently nullify an otherwise unrepealed statute.

## Strongest contrary argument and answer

**Argument:** Rojas says a reference to a specific definition can be dynamic. Section 1788.66 did not expressly say 'as of June 28, 2021.' Because the definition was part of a temporary pandemic chapter, its repeal should remove the class of covered debt and end the transfer restriction.

**Answer:** The absence of an express freezing phrase is not dispositive under Rojas in either direction. Legislative intent resolves it. Here the same act deliberately supplies a sunset to the broad neighboring transfer restriction and the separate procedural chapter while omitting it from the narrower transfer restriction, and the digest says only the general restriction is repealed. Unlike Rojas's ongoing general criminal definition, this statute identifies a closed historical debt period and historical income/assistance qualification. There is no future replacement definition or changed ongoing category to track. Treating chapter repeal as deletion would destroy that intentionally preserved restriction without express repeal and erase the distinction between broad temporary and narrow lasting restrictions. The historical incorporation reading harmonizes all of the enacted provisions and the reference canon; the expiration reading does not.

**Argument:** Once public rental-assistance funding expires, nobody can qualify, so the condition becomes false.

**Answer:** 'Would have qualified' is deliberately different from 'currently qualifies' or 'currently receives.' It calls for historical qualification under the identified enactments; nonapplication, program closure or absent current funds cannot alone answer it. The actual federal predicates and historical facts still must be established. This reading does not invent entitlement to new funding; it classifies a debt transfer.

## Operating application

1. Identify the proposed transfer, transfer date and precise unpaid amounts; separate qualifying historical obligations from later obligations. Mixed accounts must not be treated as all eligible or all prohibited merely because they share an account number.
2. Use the recovered historical CCP §1179.02 definition and the named federal assistance enactments. Preserve the due date, obligation source, tenancy and tenant facts, historical household-income calculation and assistance predicates. A current blank statute page is a retrieval/temporal warning, not evidence the answer is no.
3. If §1788.66 conditions are established, reject the sale/assignment and pursue permissible direct resolution or another arrangement that does not transfer the prohibited claim, subject to its own legal conditions. Do not merely flag the issue while executing the transfer.
4. If conditions are genuinely not met, this section does not prohibit that transfer; still apply debt-buyer, licensing, documentation, limitations and other applicable restrictions. If necessary case evidence is missing, investigate those facts rather than treating historical-law meaning as unsettled.
5. Record the legal conclusion as 'restriction survives; historical definition retained.' A later change in statute or controlling interpretation can change the rule; closure of the old procedure alone does not.

## Scope and review checks

- Qualifying tenant debt sold in 2026: prohibition still applies.
- Current rent due in 2026: outside the specified historical debt period; no prohibition under this section just because the same tenant has older debt.
- Assistance program closed, but historical conditions satisfied: closure does not remove transfer prohibition.
- Historical qualification not established: missing factual evidence, not a contrary legal holding.
- Broad §1788.65 expired in 2021: do not revive it for all tenants; apply only the surviving narrower §1788.66 conditions.
- CCP eviction-procedure chapter expired in 2025: do not extend those procedural protections merely because the incorporated definition remains usable for this separate restriction.
