# Resolved interpretation: California historical validation notice after first pleading

2026-10-01. Supersedes the treatment of this question as an unresolved action dependency in `federal/FINDINGS.md`; preserves that earlier artifact. This is a reasoned California-law conclusion from controlling enactment, controlling interpretive principles, and persuasive applications of identical historical text. It does not invent a California appellate holding specifically deciding this question.

## Conclusion and action

**A summons/complaint or other court document served on the consumer that conveys information about collecting the consumer debt is a qualifying communication under California's incorporated January1,2001 version of1692g. If it is that covered collector's first communication concerning the debt, it triggers the five-day written validation-notice obligation, unless the required information is already included or the debt has been paid.** Apply the actual1788.17 A/B-person/principal exception first; it exempts qualifying actors from g entirely. Current federal1692g(d)'s exclusion of formal pleadings does not remove this California duty.

Handoff should arrange the applicable validation notice before service, include it where procedurally permitted, or send it within five days after the triggering service. Where sending with/after pleadings, clearly distinguish the statutory thirty-day dispute right from the court's actual response deadline; the notice does not extend that deadline. A timely written dispute/request triggers the historicalg(b) collection suspension until the prescribed verification/judgment/original-creditor information is delivered as required. The exact delivery and calendar implementations are resolved in the accompanying timing/RegF research. No arbitrary thirty-day prelitigation waiting period is created: the statute is a dispute window, not a universal grace period.

Do not label this action merely 'safer until counsel decides.' It follows the interpretation selected below. A court can disagree, but uncertainty about the existence of a fact-identical appellate holding is not a reason to avoid interpreting the law.

## Controlling law and interpretive structure

1. Current CIV1788.17 specifically imports1692b–j and1692k remedies as readJanuary1,2001. AB1669,Stats2000ch688§1, officialvolume printedp4532, saved `AB1669volume.pdf/.txt`, expressly inserts that date and preserves onlye(11)/g exceptions for1692a(6)(A)/(B)persons/principals. The present official section retains the date despite its2024amendment. The relevant source bodies are `../federal/2000-1692a.txt`, `2000-1692e.txt`, `2000-1692g.txt` and current `CA-1788.17.txt`.
2. Historicalg(a) applies to the initial communication with a consumer in connection with collection of any debt. It contains no litigation or pleading exclusion. The statutory meaning of communication in historicala(2) is information regarding debt conveyed directly/indirectly through any medium. Reading that meaning to construe the imported duty does not replace California's own collector-coverage definition with the federal definition.
3. In the very same historical statute,e(11) **expressly** excludes formal pleadings from its disclosure requirement. The deliberate express exception there, absent fromg, defeats an implied blanket pleading exclusion ing. A validation-notice duty is distinct from mini-Miranda disclosure. The separate process-server exclusion does not transform the collector's authored pleading into a noncommunication or exempt the litigating collector's own conduct.
4. *Heintz v.Jenkins*,514US291,294–299(1995), officialsavedPDF, holds FDCPA applies to lawyers regularly engaged in consumer-debt collection even when through litigation. It rejects a broad judicial litigation exemption notwithstanding possible friction with court proceedings, and rejects inconsistent1988FTCstaff introductory language. Heintz did **not** decide this exactg question; it supplies a binding rejection of the underlying 'legal actions are outside debt collection' premise.
5. California's *People v.Rojas*,15Cal5th561(2023), slippp9–13, applying *Palermo* and *InreJovanB*, makes intent determinative when reference wording is ambiguous. Here the expressJan1,2001date removes that ambiguity. This is not a generic reference to federal law as it changes. Applying2006g(d) as current operative text would erase the Legislature's express temporal limitation.

## Contemporary agency interpretation, not a later model inference

The FTC **Commission** opinion to BasilMezines/AmericanCollectorsAssociation datedMarch31,2000 is saved from the SupremeCourt's cited-URL archive at `FTC2000.pdf` (the14page archive includes unrelated preceding material; relevantletter isPDFpages10–14, printedletterpp1–5). OCR is supplementary; printedpp3–4/PDF12–13 were visually checked against the scan.

On the second issue, the Commission expressly acknowledges the old staff commentary excluding legal action, then appliesHeintz: a court document served on a consumer conveying debt information is a communication. If the attorney had no prior communication, it is initial and requires the validation notice in the document or withinfive days. Printedp4 allows separate delivery before/after service where state procedure forbids additional notice text in court papers. Printedpp1–2 also distinguishes the thirty-day dispute period from a collection moratorium absent the qualifying dispute, subject to nonovershadowing.

This source predates California'sJan1,2001freeze, directly addresses the operating question, and demonstrates that the 'pleadings are excluded' administrative premise had already been expressly rejected by the Commission. It is persuasive agency interpretation, not binding state adjudication or a newly imported regulation. The conclusion independently follows the statutory text andHeintz even without treating the opinion as controlling.

## Judicial applications and strongest contrary case

*Thomas v.Law Firm of Simpson&Cybak*,392F3d914,917–920(7thCir2004)(enbanc), full officialopinion `Thomas.pdf`, holds service of debt-collection summons/complaint an initial communication under the old text. Its majority, officialpp5–11, rests on broad text, actuale(11)exception,Heintz, avoiding notice evasion by suing first, and workable separate delivery. It discusses court deadlines/representation rather than pretending litigation disappears. The originalJanuary2004panel was vacated; the savedDecember20enbancopinion is the authority used. A dissent disagrees; do not treat it as the holding.

*Vega v.McKay*,351F3d1334,1337(11thCir2003), fullofficialopinion `Vega.pdf`, supplies the strongest contrary appellate holding. Its actual facts involved rental debt for weekend trailer space and a summons/complaint/FDCPA-notice package. It concludes litigation is not initial communication, emphasizing the purpose of curbing abusive collection rather than legal actions and deferring to oldFTCstaff commentary. We reject that interpretation for California's historicalg because: (a) the statutory text draws no such medium exception; (b)Heintz denies a general litigation exclusion; (c)e(11)demonstrates Congress used specific pleading exceptions; and(d)Vega's administrative foundation omitted the contraryMarch2000Commission opinion. Vega's existence is genuine adverse authority, but it is neither controlling in California nor as persuasive on the incorporated text. This is reasoned selection, not majority voting between circuits.

The SecondCircuit's *Goldman v.Cohen*,445F3d152(2006), agrees withThomas and specifically identifiesVega's supersededFTCfoundation. Fullwebjudicialtext was inspected; directmirrordownload403 is recorded. The conclusion does not depend on that additional agreement becauseThomas and the primaryFTCsource provide the same reasoning firsthand.

*Velazquez v.ArrowFinancialServices*,S.D.Cal.No08cv1915Doc21(2009), saved `../federal/Velazquez-2009.pdf`, pp6–7, recognizes the frozen date, describes the split, and suggests2006was more likely clarification; it does not adjudicate the California merits, dismissing the state claim without prejudice after the federal claim. Its observation that no controlling authority was cited does not supply a California exemption. Nor does the possibility of clarifying legislative intent overcome explicit statutory language,Heintz and the prefreezeCommission interpretation. We therefore resolve the question instead of adoptingVelazquez's absence-of-binding-case discussion as a stop condition.

## Why2006does not change this result

Congress addedg(d),g(e), andg(b)'s additional sentences inPubL109-351§802(October13,2006), savedofficial `PL109351.pdf`. Whatever characterization is given to federal legislative intent, that later enactment changed the enacted words. It governs independently applicable current federalg, whose formal-pleading exception is explicit. It is not a California amendment. California may protect consumers beyond federal minimums: current1692n, savedofficialHTML/text, preserves stronger state debt-collection protections. There is no impossibility: a collector can give the California notice while federal law does not require one. A federal exception from a notice mandate is not a federal prohibition on giving an accurate state-required notice.

Treat judicial construction of unchanged historical text separately from later enactment. Later opinionsThomas/Goldman can persuasively explain the old text; the2006new statutory exception cannot be smuggled into it. Freezing statutory wording does not require the researcher to ignore later reasoned interpretation of that wording.

## Recipient-specific resolution after independent challenge

This additional branch is material: the consumer and the consumer's lawyer are distinct recipients. Treating every litigation communication as consumer-directed would overstate the notice and suspension duties.

**Counsel-only first communication:** A pleading or letter directed solely to the consumer's retained attorney, with no communication to the consumer and no threat to contact the consumer, does not itself trigger historical g(a)'s notice clock. That is our reasoned application of the unchanged phrase “with a consumer” and the Ninth Circuit's recipient distinction in *Guerrero v. RJM Acquisitions*, 499 F.3d 926, 935–939 (2007). Guerrero directly decided g(b) and e liability for a response faxed solely to counsel before verification, not the exact initial-pleading g(a) question; this application must not be misrepresented as its holding. Its reasoning nevertheless distinguishes counsel from the protected consumer across these communication provisions and directly answers the relevant statutory phrase. Retain recipient evidence rather than assuming the attorney is always the consumer for this trigger.

**Service on the consumer, including a represented consumer:** The affirmative pleading conclusion above applies when the collector causes the debt-collection complaint to be served on the consumer. *Donohue v. Quick Collect*, 592 F.3d 1027, 1031–1032 (9th Cir. 2010), official saved `review-Donohue-2010.pdf`, printed pp.1006–1008, holds such personal complaint service is a communication under e and f and expressly distinguishes Guerrero's counsel-only exception. Donohue did not decide g's frozen California notice issue; its recipient and communication holdings support, rather than replace, the historical g reasoning above. Representation alone does not turn actual consumer service into counsel-only communication. Determine lawful service and notice delivery under the applicable representation/procedure rules; do not bypass the attorney merely to give a notice and do not erase an already triggered duty because counsel has appeared.

**Counsel-only negotiation during a g(b) dispute suspension:** Guerrero holds that the responsive communication solely to counsel was not prohibited collection under g(b), and explains why attorney-to-collector settlement discussion may continue. Handoff may investigate and negotiate resolution through retained counsel within that branch rather than indiscriminately freezing all useful work. Conditions matter: it is actually directed only to counsel, not copied or directed to the consumer, and not accompanied by a threat to contact the consumer. A debtor who happens to be an attorney is still the debtor, not a qualifying intermediary (Guerrero footnote4). This branch does not remove the verification requirement before resuming otherwise prohibited consumer collection or clear duties already triggered by earlier consumer communications.

**Strongest contrary argument:** Heintz itself arose from a letter sent to counsel, and the Fourth Circuit's *Sayyed* treats attorney-directed communications differently. Guerrero expressly addresses that argument: Heintz decided whether litigating lawyers are collectors, not whether recipient identity changes liability. Donohue subsequently reaffirms the recipient distinction. We follow the Ninth Circuit's express treatment of the same federal provisions and apply its reasoning to California's unchanged incorporated text; the contrary interpretation is not silently ignored. This does not assert federal circuit interpretation binds California courts as state-law precedent, or that these cases confer immunity from independent California prohibitions, procedural sanctions or other law.

**Boundary:** Do not expand Guerrero into permission for every lawsuit, execution, garnishment, default request or third-party collection action merely because counsel is involved. Those acts are not the counsel-only response/negotiation holding. Likewise, mere service on counsel should not be coded as consumer service without the actual service and recipient facts. Keep communication to counsel, service on the consumer, and coercive collection acts separate.
