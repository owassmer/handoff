# Identity-theft dependencies: resolved operating rules

October 1, 2026. This follows the express dependencies in CIV 1788.18(f)/(h) and the concurrent federal duties affecting the same records, collection and transfer actions. It supplements the already established 1788.18 review/hold branch; it does not replace its distinct trigger with a federal form requirement.

## Conclusions

1. The California identity-theft civil remedy applies independently of the Rosenthal consumer-credit predicate. A present claimant asserting a transaction procured by unauthorized identifying-information use must investigate the actual evidence. A complete 1788.18 submission can also satisfy 1798.93 notice requirements when it meets their actual contents and timing; it is not automatic satisfaction of every damages or penalty element.
2. Records duties are separate and can overlap. PEN 530.8 covers its listed unauthorized applications/accounts and requires qualifying records free within ten business days after the request and required report/identification. Federal 1681g(e) covers the broader commercial transaction for consideration and requires controlled application/transaction records within thirty days after the qualifying request, subject to identity verification. Meeting one deadline does not excuse the other.
3. California's 1788.18(h) authorizes the collector holding PEN 530.8 records to provide them. It does not rewrite the Penal Code's covered application types or independently turn every collector into the entity that received the application.
4. Federal identity-theft notification can restrict reporting and transfer before, or independently of, a complete California review package. Track each actual trigger. Restart under 1788.18 alone does not clear a federal furnishing block or transfer prohibition.
5. Correcting collection against the wrong person does not waive a supported claim against the perpetrator or another legally responsible person. Preserve legitimate recovery against the right party and remove unsupported charges against the victim.

## California civil remedy and responsibility

Read current CIV 1798.92–97 in full. Section 1798.92 defines unauthorized acquisition and requires the victim not to have used or possessed the obtained benefit; FTC report and police report are alternatives. This is not a rule that merely denying a signature defeats liability. Investigate identity, authorization, actual occupancy/service receipt and each person's legal obligation. Conversely, a lease bearing a name is not proof that the named person authorized or received the transaction.

Section 1798.93 supplies declaratory/injunctive relief, dismissal of the affected claim on a successful cross-complaint, and conditional monetary remedies. A victim proves identity theft by preponderance. For actual damages/fees under (c)(5), establish written notice that identity theft might exist. Its FTC-report provision operates upon the claimant's written request and specifies a valid signed report completed at least thirty days before the action or within the cross-complaint; the alternative police/DMV-report route has its stated filing timing. Do not impose a claimant-requested document as an unconditional prerequisite or demand a police/DMV report in addition to the FTC report. For the civil penalty up to $30,000, (c)(6) separately requires clear-and-convincing proof of written notice at the claimant's designated credit-reporting-complaints address explaining the basis, at least thirty days before the action or within the cross-complaint; failure to investigate diligently; and continued pursuit after facts later held to establish entitlement to judgment. The statute's alternative cross-complaint route matters: do not impose a universal thirty-day prefiling wait on every form of relief. A thirty-day notice element is not a permission to ignore known theft for thirty days.

*Satey v. JPMorgan Chase*, 521 F.3d 1087, 1092–93 (9th Cir. 2008), official saved opinion pp.7–11, construes claimant as a present interest or asserted claim at suit filing, excluding the former owner after sale on that record. It does not eliminate other laws' liability for earlier conduct, nor authorize selling identity-theft debt. Pausing contact while retaining the claim is not necessarily relinquishing it. The current statutory claimant wording is the same; use current 1798.92(d)'s amended report alternatives rather than the older opinion's police-only quotation.

Sections 1798.94–95 address joinder and continuing jurisdiction; 1798.96 provides a four-year discovery/diligence period, distinct from Rosenthal/FDCPA violation periods. Its printed reference to 1798.82 is retained as printed, not silently repaired or made an execution dependency. Section 1798.97 makes remedies cumulative, preserves claims against perpetrators/benefit recipients, and excludes a transaction subject to 1747.10. The latter addresses unauthorized credit-card use/cardholder liability: distinguish a card issuer's unauthorized-use claim from the landlord's underlying tenancy claim. A card used to pay rent does not automatically classify every separate tenancy obligation as a cardholder-liability transaction.

## Which records, which recipient and which clock

PEN 530.8(a) lists credit applications/accounts, public utilities and specified other services; residential lease applications are not listed merely because they are applications. Apply the actual statutory category (for example, a credit account or public utility account), not the word 'account' on a ledger. The request must include an FTC report or police report and identifying information of the categories used; the victim can ask what categories were used. Required paper, telephone and electronic application/authorization and transaction records are free. Additional law-enforcement disclosure authorization may be requested under (c); it must identify agency, records, period and revocation right. Subsection (b)'s writing requirement applies to the specified utility entities. Nonproduction has the court-compulsion and civil remedies in (d), including the $100-per-day penalty; this is a separate ten-business-day clock from 1788.18's review initiation.

Federal 1681g(e) is broader than credit. A business that entered the alleged fraudulent rental/service transaction for consideration is within its transaction language, even if advance rent is not California consumer credit. The covered entity supplies records within its control, including records maintained for it, without charge. Eligible recipients are the victim, a federal/state/local law-enforcement agency or officer specified by the victim, and an investigating law-enforcement agency authorized by the victim. The request is written and mailed to its designated address, if any; requested identifying transaction details need be supplied only if known or readily obtainable. Verification under (e)(2) is dispensable when the entity elects to rely on an already high degree of confidence in the requester's identity. Otherwise the entity chooses a permitted identity method: government ID, identifying information of the type used by the unauthorized person, or identifying information normally required for new applications/transactions. For proof of the claim it may elect to require a police report plus a completed Bureau identity-theft affidavit or another affidavit it accepts. These federal options must not be imposed to defeat the independent California FTC-report alternative. Good-faith refusal under (e)(5) is limited to information not required by the subsection, inadequate confidence in identity after reviewing the verification, a material misrepresentation relevant to the request, or requested Internet navigation/similar visit data. Record the actual ground; mere lack of a signed original lease is not an additional refusal category. Under (e)(9), financial-privacy provisions in the specified GLBA subtitle cannot defeat victim disclosure, while other applicable disclosure prohibitions remain effective. Do not fabricate nonexistent records: (e)(8) creates no new general retention requirement, while (e)(10)'s unavailable-record defense requires a reasonably diligent search and the prescribed litigation showing. Other retention duties still apply. Section (e)(6) excludes ordinary 1681n/o private-liability treatment except as specified; do not invent automatic consumer statutory damages for every late 609(e) response.

A federal optional verification request cannot cancel the independently satisfied California FTC-report alternative or its collection hold. Each statute's submission and response requirements stay separate. Satisfy both applicable duties using one complete records delivery where possible.

## Reporting and transfer dependencies

The saved official FTC March 2026 FCRA compilation supplies the full current statutory bodies, cross-checked to the saved 2024 GovInfo 1681g text. House direct retrieval returned maintenance content, explicitly recorded as such.

- 1681m(g): an FDCPA-defined collector acting for the described creditor/report user, notified that collection information may be fraudulent or identity theft, must notify that third party and, on consumer request, provide information otherwise available under applicable dispute law. This does not require waiting for California's complete package or completed review.
- 1681s-2(a)(6)(B): a furnisher receiving the qualifying identity-theft report at its specified address may not furnish the identified information unless it subsequently knows, or the consumer informs it, that the information is correct. A merely disputed-report label is not sufficient when this separate prohibition applies.
- 1681c-2(b) and 1681s-2(a)(6)(A): preserve CRA block notifications and reasonable procedures against refurnishing blocked information. The CRA's four-business-day consumer-block duty is the CRA's duty, not a replacement landlord response deadline.
- 1681m(f): receipt of the specified 1681c-2 notification prohibits sale, transfer for consideration or placement for collection of that debt, with the statute's repurchase, securitization/collateral and whole-business transaction exceptions. An ordinary new collection placement is not an exception. Satey's claimant holding supplies no permission to evade this prohibition.

A qualifying federal report is determined under 12 CFR 1022.3(i): specific allegations to the extent the consumer can provide, valid official report with false-report penalties, and only reasonable supplementary requests. The initial request for more information must be made within fifteen days after the later of receiving the report or the consumer's request for the particular service. Supplemental requests and the final acceptance determination must occur within a further fifteen days after the initial information request. If additional information arrives on day eleven or later of that second period, the furnisher/CRA has five days to make its final determination on acceptance. These are report-acceptance clocks, not extensions of California's independent review duties. Do not make every supporting item mandatory or postpone restrictions through repeated arbitrary document requests.

## Adversarial decisions

- Advance-rent account, alleged identity theft, no California credit predicate: still apply independent claimant/records/reporting duties according to their predicates.
- Complete California FTC package without police report: commence statutory hold/review; federal verification options do not permit refusing the state package.
- Utility application records request plus California collection dispute: separately satisfy the records delivery and review-initiation duties; neither substitutes for the other.
- CRA theft-block notification plus planned collection sale: remove the debt from ordinary sale/placement; a retained claim or collection restart does not clear this restriction.
- Named person never authorized or occupied, but another person received the unit: investigate responsibility of the actual actor; do not keep pursuing the named person merely to preserve recovery.
- Named person actually authorized the transaction and received its benefit: apply the evidence to each statutory predicate; a false identity-theft label does not itself establish nonresponsibility.
