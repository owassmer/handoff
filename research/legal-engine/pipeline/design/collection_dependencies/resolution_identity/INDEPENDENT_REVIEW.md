## Conclusion

**Pass after the corrections identified below.** The reviewed findings resolve the assigned identity-theft dependencies with supported statutory interpretations and operationally distinct factual predicates. They do not rely on the absence of a case as an answer. This conclusion covers this identity-theft supplement, not the five other reopened Goal 3 interpretations or all California reporting law.

The conclusions are supported by text and statutory structure: 1798.92 describes goods, services and property independently of Rosenthal credit; 530.8 has its own enumerated applications/accounts and report/identity trigger; 1681g(e) expressly extends to commercial transactions for consideration and controlled records; and the reporting/transfer provisions have separately specified triggers and effects. A California collection restart is consequently not an override of a federal furnishing or transfer restriction.

## Substantive checks and opposing arguments

### Claimant, notice and responsibility

Satey, 521 F.3d 1087, 1092–93, saved official opinion pp.7–11, supports a present claim/interest at filing and excludes that record's former owner. It does not establish that pausing calls extinguishes a retained claim or preempt other remedies. The findings use the current statutory FTC alternative rather than copying Satey's historical police-only definition.

Independently searched later treatment and the contrary proposition that ceasing active collection necessarily ends claimant status. The official Ninth Circuit memorandum in **Kim v. BMW Financial Services, No.15-56801 (July 31, 2017), pp.4–5**, rejects the need for active collection persisting after suit where the derogatory tradeline remained. This is nonprecedential and decided in plain-error review; it supports the findings' limited distinction, not a new blanket rule that any historical report forever makes a party a claimant. [Official opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2017/07/31/15-56801.pdf).

Also located district-court applications to former owners/servicers, including Romero v. Monterey, 19cv1781 (S.D.Cal. Jan.27,2021), and Lara v. Experian, 20cv2449 (S.D.Cal.2021). Those are not elevated above the statutory text or published Satey holding. Their existence does not change the resolution: record current ownership/assertion, not merely contact frequency. No universal police-report or thirty-day prerequisite is imported from older summaries. The finding that merely possessing another person's name on a lease does not establish authorization follows the statutory unauthorized-use and benefit-receipt predicates; actual authorization and actual benefit remain evidence questions.

The 1747.10 exclusion is accurately retained. The underlying tenancy obligation and unauthorized cardholder liability are distinct claims; payment by card alone does not convert all lease obligations into a transaction subject to the cardholder statute. This is a reasoned claim-specific interpretation of 1798.97(a) and 1747.10, not presented as a judicial holding. Preservation of claims against perpetrators/benefit recipients follows 1798.97(b), while victim-specific nonliability follows 1798.93.

### Records

530.8(a)'s ten-business-day obligation runs after receipt of the request, qualifying report and identifying information. Its enumerated services include office/desk rental, not a generic independent residential-lease category; the credit/account branch must be established from the actual transaction. The separate federal commercial-transaction language supplies residential rental coverage without forcing a state consumer-credit conclusion. 1788.18(h) authorizes a collector possessing the qualifying records to provide them; it does not silently rewrite 530.8's obligor/category predicates.

The current record correctly preserves the special writing requirement for entities subject to PUC2891, the optional signed/dated law-enforcement disclosure authorization, free records and independent civil remedies. A 530.8 records response is not the same action as starting the 1788.18 review. Federal control includes records maintained for the entity by others; good-faith refusal is not a licence to refuse every disputed or incomplete lease file. The thirty-day federal duty is subject to its own verification provisions, not the state ten-day clock. Ordinary 1681n/o private remedies are excluded by (e)(6) except as provided by 1681s, as stated.

### Concurrent restrictions

- 1681m(g) applies to its FDCPA-defined third-party collector configuration on notification that information may be fraudulent, without the complete California packet prerequisite. Creditor notification and consumer-requested dispute information follow their actual predicates.
- 1681s-2(a)(6)(B) directly prohibits furnishing on its qualifying consumer report/address trigger until the specified correctness condition. A disputed flag does not satisfy that prohibition.
- A CRA's 1681c-2 notice activates the separate 1681s-2(a)(6)(A) reasonable-procedure obligation against refurnishing blocked information. The CRA's own block deadline is not relabeled a landlord deadline. The statute also assigns the CRA the power to decline/rescind under (c); a collector's California determination is not that CRA action.
- 1681m(f) expressly restricts sale, transfer for consideration and placement after the specified notification, subject to its enumerated repurchase, financing and business-transfer exceptions. Satey's claimant-at-filing construction supplies no defence to a forbidden transfer. No automatic release of this restriction upon California review completion is stated or supported.

Checked adversarial outcomes: advance rent without Rosenthal credit still reaches independently applicable identity statutes; a qualifying state FTC-only package cannot be rejected solely for a federal police-report election; concurrent utility records/review clocks remain separate; a CRA block bars ordinary new collection placement; and the actual perpetrator's responsibility is not erased by correcting the victim's account. Each depends on specified facts and authority, rather than an optional protective service policy.
