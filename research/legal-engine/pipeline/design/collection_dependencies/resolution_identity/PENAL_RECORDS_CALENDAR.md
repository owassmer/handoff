# Penal Code 530.8 records calendar

October 1, 2026. Reasoned construction of the records-production statute, distinguished from a reported holding on this exact deadline.

## Rule and computation

For Penal Code 530.8(a), count ten business days beginning after receipt of the request and the required report and identifying information. Business days here are Monday through Friday, excluding full California statutory holidays and applicable observed holidays. A custodian's chosen office schedule does not redefine the statutory period. The separate ten **court** days in subdivision (d)(1) govern the hearing after the enforcement petition is both served and filed; they do not define the initial records-production period. The categories-of-identifying-information duty is express, but the ten-day records clock must not be made conditional on extra information the statute does not require.

For a complete request received Thursday October 1, 2026, and no special proclamation, the tenth business day is Friday October 16: October 2, 5–9, 13–16. October 12 is a state holiday. This differs from the October 14 result for a ten-business-day Civil Code period that includes intermediate Saturdays. Exclude the triggering day and apply the generally applicable statutory terminal-extension rule in CCP 12a if an exceptional last-day holiday intervenes; the ordinary business-day exclusion already avoids weekends and full state holidays. See the independently reviewed [calendar analysis](../resolution_review/DEPENDENCY_AUDIT.md), including Parsons and its statutory/private-actor application, for that separate final-day rule. A partial Good Friday holiday does not remove the whole day or extend an end-of-day response deadline.

## Why this construction applies

[Penal Code 7(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=7.) directs contextual and approved usage, except for terms with a peculiar legal meaning. Neither it nor [530.8](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PEN&sectionNum=530.8.) supplies a special business-day definition. Penal Code 10 and 11 concern forfeiture and military/contempt powers, not computation; similarly numbered Civil Code provisions cannot be silently transplanted.

[Purifoy v. Howell](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2010/a123856/) (2010) 183 Cal.App.4th 166 recognizes both Saturday-inclusive and weekday usages in California statutes, then resolves an undefined term from its particular scheme. Its discussion of ordinary usage supports weekdays excluding public holidays. Its actual animal-holding ruling is not a holding about 530.8. The relevant analogy is its interpretive method: there is no single peculiar legal definition that displaces context everywhere.

Section 530.8 provides a uniform period for businesses across multiple industries to locate and furnish records, with a separate court-calendar period for judicial enforcement. This is an ordinary commercial records-response period. Applying the ordinary weekday meaning gives a uniform response interval without allowing each business to choose its own workweek, and preserves the legislature's distinction between business production and court administration. Nothing in the section incorporates Civil Code 9 or ties production days to the counterparty's weekend opening hours. The statute's protective purpose supports prompt, definite production; it does not itself authorize selecting whichever possible count is shorter.

## Strongest contrary construction and answer

Civil Code 9 describes nonholidays as business days “for all purposes,” and its banking proviso addresses banks specifically. On that reading ordinary Saturdays would count, with bank-specific optional holidays potentially excluded. The answer is statutory scope and context, not disregard of the phrase: section 9 refers to Civil Code 7, whose holidays are expressly within the meaning of that code. Purifoy expressly considered that definition and other codes' express incorporations of it, yet did not treat it as an automatic definition for every code. Section 530.8 has no such incorporation. Unlike the Civil Code collection periods addressed in the main audit, this Penal Code period therefore receives the contextual, ordinary construction prescribed by Penal Code 7(c).

The same answer covers an actual bank custodian: its presence in 530.8's list does not selectively import Civil Code 9's optional banking holidays into the Penal Code's uniform response rule. An institution's elective closure is not a new exclusion from the ten-day statutory count. The ten-court-day hearing period likewise does not import every judicial holiday into the business-production count. Full state holidays, applicable observation, and any legally operative special proclamation must be supplied for the actual year; a generic federal or bank calendar is insufficient.

This resolves the legal counting rule. Whether a particular request was complete and received on a particular date remains a factual predicate, not an unresolved legal interpretation.
