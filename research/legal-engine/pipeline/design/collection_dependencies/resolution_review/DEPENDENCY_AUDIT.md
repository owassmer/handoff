Independent research, 2026-10-01. This supersedes the earlier review's acceptance of deferring dependencies merely because they lay outside the three memo headings. The consumer is Handoff's decision whether, when, how, and for what amount the relevant actor can communicate, seek records, pause, resume, assign, or collect a tenancy obligation. This report does not certify a completed software implementation or decide unknown account facts.

## 1. California calendars are three different operations

**Apply the Civil Code business-day definition to the Civil Code collection provisions.** CIV 7 and 9 make Sundays and statutory holidays holidays and the other days business days. Ordinary Saturdays therefore count inside the five-business-day translation period in 1788.14.5(e)(2) and the ten-business-day identity-theft periods in 1788.18. The special bank-transaction rule in 7.1 is not a general Saturday exclusion for collection notices. Do not import a bank, federal, court-clerk, or company opening-hours calendar.

**“Working days” in 1788.52(e) and 1812.700(c) means Monday through Friday excluding applicable state holidays.** This is a reasoned construction of different statutory language, not a claim that CIV 9 defines working days or that a collection-specific appellate case has decided it. The ordinary modern meaning and the deliberate distinction from “business days” support it. *Puni Pa'u v Department of Forestry* (WCAB significant panel decision, 2019), pp. 10–14, directly distinguishes CIV 9 business days from working days and uses this construction for a different statute. Its weight is persuasive administrative reasoning, not binding Court of Appeal precedent. *Purifoy v Howell*, 183 Cal.App.4th 166 (2010), demonstrates that statutory context can override a generic business-day reading; its animal-holding statute's specific weekend context is absent here. A collector cannot redefine working days by electing to close its own office.

**Count first, then extend a statutory last day when necessary.** CIV 10 excludes the triggering day. CIV 10–11 preserve the following-business-day rule for a holiday; CCP 12a extends a specified statutory period ending on Saturday or another judicial holiday to the next nonholiday. Both must be satisfied. The extension is not restricted to court filings: *Parsons v Estenson Logistics, LLC*, 86 Cal.App.5th 1260 (2022), published opinion pp. 7–13, applies CCP 12a to a private statutory wage obligation measured in calendar days and rejects the procedural-only and “calendar days means no extension” arguments. *Ystrom v Handel*, 205 Cal.App.3d 144 (1988), likewise rejects the nonjudicial-act distinction. *Gans v Smull*, 111 Cal.App.4th 985 (2003), concerns a contractual option, not a statutory period, and does not negate this conclusion. CCP 12b makes a public-office closure a holiday only for business to be transacted in that office; it does not extend a private collector's correspondence deadline whenever a courthouse closes.

Use the effective-date version of GOV 6700–6701 and CCP 135. They are not identical calendars. State general holidays include dates excluded from judicial holidays, including Columbus Day; judicial holidays include Saturdays and the Friday after Thanksgiving. CIV 11 still matters when CCP 135 excludes a particular general holiday. GOV 6700 currently includes the March 2026 amendment naming March 31 Farmworkers Day, as well as Lunar New Year, Diwali, April 24, Admission Day, and other listed dates. Do not use a generic federal holiday library or assume every holiday is observed on Friday when it falls on Saturday. GOV 6701 supplies the specific following-Monday and Veterans Day rules. A local employee day off is not automatically a statewide statutory holiday. Good Friday's statutory holiday is only noon to 3 p.m. It remains a business/working day for these whole-day collection periods and does not extend an end-of-day deadline. *Lancel v. Postlethwaite*, 172 Cal. 326, 330–331 (1916), holds that a partial holiday does not exclude the whole last day; *Purifoy*, footnote 9, applies that distinction when discussing business days. [Lancel's primary opinion](Lancel-web.json) was independently read. Its old Saturday outcome does not override today's express CCP 12a Saturday extension. Thus a 30-calendar-day deadline triggered March 4, 2026 remains Friday April 3, Good Friday, rather than Monday April 6. A fixed act specifically falling within holiday hours or a bank transaction requires its own applicable hour/method provision; the ordinary daily records-response deadlines here have neither feature.

## 2. Federal clocks stay separate

For an independently covered FDCPA collector, 12 CFR 1006.34(a) requires validation information in the initial communication or the required written/electronic notice within five **calendar** days afterward, subject to its payment and already-provided exceptions. The five days used to **assume receipt** under 1006.34(b)(5) are a different clock: exclude Saturdays, Sundays, and the legal public holidays listed in 5 USC 6103(a). The ensuing validation period runs 30 calendar days after actual or properly assumed receipt. California terminal extensions do not change this direct federal extrajudicial period. The CFPB's official small-entity guide expressly illustrates an August 8 Saturday validation end date (chapter on validation periods). A consumer's timely submission can arrive after the period: under 1006.38, assess whether it was submitted within the period, then cease upon receipt. Never use receipt-after-day-30 as an automatic rejection.

For California's separately incorporated historical 1692g duty, retain its actual-receipt trigger and California computation rules; a federal assumed-receipt compliance calculation does not extinguish a longer applicable state protection. Reg F 1006.104 preserves greater state protection. A combined notice must not affirmatively tell the consumer that the federal date extinguishes independent California record-request rights.

Reg F 1006.22(c)'s postdated-instrument notice has its own express weekday/federal-holiday exclusions: for an instrument postdated more than five days, send notice of intent to deposit between three and ten qualifying days before deposit; do not deposit or threaten deposit before its date. This payment-method rule is relevant when that method is proposed; the present investigations do not authorize a postdated-payment program simply by providing a validation packet.

## 3. Identify the actor and license before authorizing the act

FIN 100001–100002 impose a DCLA license gate on engaging in the business of collecting consumer debt in California. The statute expressly reaches a collector located in California or collecting from a California debtor. Its consumer-debt predicate uses the on-credit formulation; do not infer DCLA coverage merely from the broader federal definition or from the existence of unpaid rent. Its debt-collector definition reaches regular collection for oneself or others and qualifying collection-media businesses and debt buyers. Evaluate the actual legal person's role and activities, not whether the UI calls it software, an agent, or an owner assistant.

FIN 100001(b)(1) exempts the actually licensed DRE person and the other specifically listed financial entities/activities; (c) supplies its distinct student-loan and covered-commercial exclusions. A client's license is not the vendor's license. A person qualifying for a licensing exemption does not obtain a blanket exemption from Rosenthal collection conduct; 100001(b)(2) preserves the specified DFPI enforcement powers. The DFPI PRO 05/21 page currently describes third **prefiling** text and a December 2025 comment process, not an adopted categorical rent exemption. Do not implement draft definitions as current law.

Separately, BPC 10130 and 10131(b) ordinarily require the appropriate real-estate license to collect rents for another for compensation; one qualifying act can suffice. Owning one's own property is different from acting for another. BPC 10133's actual officer/general-partner, attorney-service, fiduciary and other exceptions require their stated facts, including no special compensation for the specified officer/partner exception. BPC 10131.01(a)(1) exempts resident managers and their employees in the listed properties. The separate employee-of-property-management-firm exception in (a)(3) is limited to its enumerated functions, including accepting rent, and requires actual employment and supervision; it does not authorize an independent vendor to negotiate debts or leases. Under 10 CCR 2724, a delegated supervising salesperson needs two years' full-time licensed experience within the preceding five years and a written delegation agreement; a supervising broker associate also needs the written agreement. Section 2725 preserves the responsible broker's oversight. Section 10131.01(c) additionally requires compliance with [10163](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=10163.) for the specified buildings: procure the additional branch-office license for each qualifying place of business; the Commissioner determines whether a location requires one. The March 17, 2026 DRE AI advisory confirms that using AI does not transfer the broker's responsibility to the tool.

Operational facts to obtain: actor's legal identity; debtor/property/actor location; original creditor versus collecting for another; agreement creating credit and default dates; regularity and compensation; actual license and employing broker; precise claimed statutory exception; activities being performed and who controls them. If the known facts establish a required license and none exists, do not undertake that licensed collection act. Missing facts require factual investigation, not a claim that licensing law is unresolved.

CIV 1788.11(b) requires the applicable California license number upon request during telephone collection; (f) requires it in at least 12-point type in written/digital collection communications. This is separate from a records packet's license field and separate from an exemption determination.

## 4. A further first-notice duty was necessary

CIV 1812.700–702 applies to third-party collectors subject to the FDCPA and requires its California rights disclosure with the first written notice initially addressed to a California address of a debtor (an address test, not a residence finding). Use the statutory disclosure, including its contact information, subject only to 1812.701(a)'s authority to change it as necessary for FDCPA changes making it inaccurate. It is not satisfied merely by the five federal validation statements or the assigned-debt/debt-buyer records notice. If the initial oral contact principally uses another language, deliver the translated notice within five working days. Section 1812.701(b) requires type at least as large as the debt text, but need not exceed 12 point: this is not a universal 12-point minimum. The separate 1788.14.5/1788.52 prominent records notices and 1788.11 license number have their own 12-point requirements. Violations are Rosenthal violations under 1812.702; *Timlick* does not license routine omission merely because some notice defects can later be cured.

Primary: [1812.700](CIV1812.700.txt), [1812.701](CIV1812.701.txt), [1812.702](CIV1812.702.txt). This dependency must be reflected in a combined-notice branch, not parked as future notice design.

## 5. Electronic delivery is a substantive condition

For a current FDCPA collector, 1006.42(a) requires a delivery method reasonably expected to give actual notice and a retainable, accessible form. A known bad address, returned message, unmonitored bounce, or address-specific opt-out defeats reliance on that delivery attempt. Postal delivery to the last known address has the stated safe harbor only if the collector does not know or have reason to know it is no longer valid. Required disclosure by a disappearing portal message or a link the consumer cannot access is insufficient.

Section 1006.42(b) specifically requires 15 USC 7001(c) consent for an electronic validation notice sent after the initial communication and for electronic original-creditor/verification responses under 1006.38(c)/(d)(2)(i). The exception for validation information actually contained in an initial electronic communication does not dispense with the other channel, privacy, content and retention requirements. A subsequent message does not retroactively turn that message into the initial communication.

E-SIGN consent is affirmative, unwithdrawn, and within the agreed scope, following the required clear disclosures about paper rights, withdrawal, procedures, costs, hardware/software and access. The consumer must consent electronically or confirm consent in a manner reasonably demonstrating access to the electronic form. Possession of an email address, one online payment, or a collector's unilateral announcement does not establish this. Changed access requirements engage 7001(c)(1)(D). Section 7001 preserves substantive content and timing obligations.

For California-only records responses, 1788.14.5(d)(2) and 1788.52(c) expressly permit the agreed email request/response channel. CIV 1633.5 and 1633.8 additionally require actual agreement to transact electronically and retainability, and preserve prescribed formatting. Scope of agreement is factual; one electronic transaction does not compel every later transaction. CIV 1633.15 determines electronic receipt by entry into the designated, retrievable processing system, not when an employee later reads it; do not restart a response clock at office opening without a legal basis.

For the incorporated historical federal verification duty that says “mailed,” ordinary UETA consent alone does not erase a specified delivery method: CIV 1633.8(b)/(d) preserves that requirement. A full E-SIGN-compliant electronic substitution is available for the ordinary consumer transaction affecting interstate commerce: 15 USC 7001 permits electronic records and 7002(c) prevents circumvention through UETA's nonelectronic-method provision. E-SIGN took effect October 1, 2000, before the January 2001 incorporation date; this conclusion does not import the later Reg F amendment into California. The CFPB's [2020 final-rule explanation](RegF-final2020.pdf), printed 85 FR 76851, expressly interprets E-SIGN's transaction coverage to include debt collection and its writing substitution to reach the verification/original-creditor disclosures that FDCPA 809(b) says must be mailed. That later agency explanation supports the reading of the preexisting statute; it is not itself incorporated California law. Record full 7001(c) consent and delivery evidence for that route. Without that consent and covered transaction, mail the verification; mere acceptance of an emailed records request is not consent for every distinct statutory notice.

**Do not extend this substitution to an eviction/default notice just because it is bundled with collection information.** 15 USC 7003(b)(2)(B) excludes the specified default, acceleration, repossession, foreclosure, eviction and right-to-cure notices under a rental agreement for the individual's primary residence. Court documents are separately excluded. CIV 1633.3(c) expressly excludes CCP 1162 notices from UETA substitution. Classify each notice by its actual function and follow that notice's required service; the ordinary validation letter is not automatically an eviction notice, and the combined eviction/default letter cannot rely on E-SIGN as its substitute service authority.

## 7. Closure implications and evidence limits

The final synthesis must incorporate the distinct calendar rules, additional notice, licensing gates, delivery conditions and federal action predicates above. It must also incorporate the parallel assigned-debt, pleading, COVID-assignment, tenancy-credit and identity-theft resolutions. None can be deferred solely because the initial work was called three investigations. Conversely, deciding an unknown lease balance, litigation damages or a particular entity's license requires actual facts; it does not make these legal meanings unresolved.

Response/cure remains bounded by the already captured *Afewerki*, *Timlick*, *Gonzales*, *Jerman* and *Rotkiske* authorities in federal/REMEDIES.md: a prospective right to proceed is different from a defense to damages for a completed violation. Federal liability is not generally cured by California's fifteen-day defense; legal misinterpretation is not the federal bona-fide-error defense; repair must undo the injury where the cure doctrine requires that result. No compliance packet automatically proves the underlying fee, rent, assignment, or identity-theft claim valid.

Collection-fee enforceability is assigned to the separate [FEE_RESOLUTION.md](FEE_RESOLUTION.md) determination, pending its author's completion; it is not supplied by a packet's required itemization. This dependency is not yet accepted by this draft.
