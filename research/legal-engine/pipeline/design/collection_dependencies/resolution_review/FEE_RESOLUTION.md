# California collection fees: resolved operating rule

2026-10-01. Scope: fees added while collecting the California residential outgoing account; not a general determination of the underlying rent, repair, or deposit claims.

## Conclusion

Apply §1692f(1)'s express **original-agreement authorization or legal permission** test through §1788.17, while examining §1788.14(b)'s collection-expense prohibition. The best-supported reconciliation is that an otherwise lawful fee expressly authorized in the agreement creating the debt is permitted under §1788.17 notwithstanding §1788.14(b); the latter is not a categorical veto on that original-agreement branch. California Attorney General Opinion 02-601 directly adopts this reading. Its precise facts concern a credit-card issuer's specified optional demand-draft fee, but its construction of the same statutory interaction is persuasive beyond that product. This is an adopted statutory interpretation, not a claimed binding appellate holding.

**Correction to the initial report:** legally recoverable actual expense is one positive branch, not the only contractual fee branch. An otherwise valid, specifically authorized payment-service fee need not be converted into breach damages or capped universally at documented actual collection cost. Conversely, a generic promise to pay reasonable collection expenses does not expressly authorize every fee or validate a percentage commission. For breach/default compensation, Bondanza/Garrett, Civil Code §§1671 and 3300–3301, and the actual clause's conditions determine enforceability.

An optional payment agreement newly made during collection is not the original agreement creating the debt. Without original express authorization or separate affirmative legal permission, exclude the collector's added payment-service fee. Consent and a free alternative alone do not satisfy that test. This preserves the independent state prohibition in the mortgage cases without misreading them as deciding that an original specified authorization can never suffice. Any separately applicable substantive prohibition still controls: federal collection permission does not validate an unlawful contract term.

## What the primary authorities actually establish

**Bondanza v. Peninsula Hospital & Medical Center, 23 Cal.3d 260, 266–269 (1979)**: a generic promise to pay reasonable collection expense did not authorize automatically adding one-third of the balance upon assignment. Calling the amount the hospital's actual commission did not cure the problem. The majority applied liquidated-damages law and prohibited the percentage assessment or other sums not representing actual collection costs under Garrett. It expressly distinguished the agency's freedom to set its price to its creditor customer from imposing that economic burden on the debtor. The dissent's endorsement of commission indemnification is **not** the court's rule. [Opinion](https://www.neochart.com/catalog/cases/cal-3d/volume_23/0260_01/index.html); captured fee-Bondanza.html/.txt.

**Garrett v. Coast & Southern Federal Savings & Loan Assn., 9 Cal.3d 731, 738–741 (1973)**: substance controls over a late-charge label. Default compensation must relate to the loss; a valid prospective estimate requires a reasonable endeavor to estimate fair compensation and the statutory impracticability predicate. Invalidating the penalty does not extinguish actual damages. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/9/731.html); captured fee-Garrett.html/.txt. Current **§1671(c)(2), (d)** expressly applies the stricter rule to dwelling leases. The ordinary commercial presumption in subdivision (b) is not the rule for these residential leases.

**Dey v. Continental Central Credit, 170 Cal.App.4th 721 (2009), slip pp.5–10**: dismissal followed defects in pleading the operative contract and illegality; the opinion also discussed HOA-specific collection statutes and management-company cases. It did not hold that every percentage collection charge is valid or decide the independent §1788.14(b) question. Its warning against detaching Bondanza from its contract and liquidated-damages facts is sound. HOA permission is not permission for a rental-account charge. [Court PDF](https://cases.justia.com/california/court-of-appeal-4th-appellate-district/D052254.PDF?ts=1396114173). Direct download returned HTTP403; actual opinion text recovered through the web tool is preserved in fee-web-retrieval.json, not represented as a locally downloaded PDF.

**Torliatt v. Ocwen Loan Servicing, No.19-cv-04303-WHO, Dkt118, July14,2021, pp.5–8**: denied a motion to stay the case pending resolution of the Carrington appeal; §1788.14(b) is independent of §1788.17. Distinguished Thomas-Lawson v. Carrington's optional-new-agreement reasoning because the parties there treated state/federal claims alike and the opinion did not independently analyze §1788.14(b). Torliatt cites DiFlauro, Corona, Lembeck and Langston for the state payment-service prohibition. This is persuasive district-court reasoning, not a California appellate holding. See existing Torliatt2021.pdf/.txt. **DeSimone v. Select Portfolio Servicing, EDNY Dkt101, Sept13,2024**, pp.35–37 treats original express contract authorization as a positive route, then finds that the deed of trust authorized specified default services but never mentioned pay-to-pay fees; its new-agreement argument also failed. At pp.48–49 the court separately permits the §1788.14(b) pleading to proceed because the challenged service charge lacked California-law authorization. Thus its independent-state-claim discussion cannot fairly be cited as rejecting the original-agreement route it had just recognized. Persuasive, not controlling; existing DeSimone2024.pdf/.txt. Neither case adjudicates an expressly specified original residential-lease payment fee or the lease's actual breach expenses.

**Reichert v. National Credit Systems, 531 F.3d 1002, 1004–1007 (9th Cir.2008)**: residential debt collector could not rely on the landlord's unexplained addition of a $225 attorney-letter fee. The lease limited attorney-fee entitlement to successful litigation. The collector did not argue that the fee was authorized by contract or law; illegality rested on the conceded lease/Arizona-law setting. The appellate holding concerned the bona-fide-error defense, not California substantive entitlement or a general ban on presuit attorney fees. [Official court opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2008/07/07/0615503.pdf); captured fee-Reichert.pdf/.txt.

**California Attorney General Opinion 02-601, Oct29,2002, pp.5–6** is the strongest contrary authority to the initial report's overly independent-gate framing. It expressly acknowledges §1788.14(b)'s omission of an agreement exception, then reasons that §1788.17's notwithstanding clause imports the original-agreement route despite that omission. The opinion concludes a specified demand-draft payment fee in the original credit-card agreement is permissible. We adopt that statutory reconciliation, while retaining substantive enforceability tests. Its reasoning does not authorize a fee first agreed during collection, a generic unstated markup, or an unlawful penalty. The opinion is official interpretive authority, not a binding judicial decision; its distinct discussion of when debt is due is not adopted here. The PDF itself prints “1788.7” once in the concluding discussion although its quotation and analysis unmistakably concern §1788.17. [Official opinion](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/opinions/pdfs/02-601.pdf); fee-AG02-601.pdf/.txt.

The competing reading treats §1788.17 as only an additional prohibition and §1788.14(b) as requiring wholly separate permission even when the original contract expressly authorizes the fee. We reject that categorical construction here: it does not give the notwithstanding clause the effect identified by the AG, and the payment-fee cases involve missing original authorization and do not compel it. Independent §1788.14(b) claims remain available where the original-agreement/other-law bases fail. This reconciliation does not turn every instance of federal compliance into immunity from all state protections.

## Mandatory residential payment-method gate: Civil Code §1947.3

The current section was amended by SB611, Stats.2024 ch.287 §3, effective **January1,2025**. Apply the following concrete rules to the current California workflow before allowing an original-agreement payment fee; do not backdate the new check-fee ban to an earlier charge without examining the then-effective law. [Official current text](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1947.3.); fee-CIV1947.3.html/.txt.

- **Rent/security-deposit payment by check to landlord or its agent: no payment fee.** Subdivision (b) forbids any such fee; original lease authorization cannot rescue it. The statutory waiver ban in (f) makes contrary consent void. This is a fee for paying by check, not an adjudication here of a separate dishonored-check remedy.
- **Keep at least one payment method that is neither cash nor electronic funds transfer** for rent and security deposits under (a)(1). Subdivision (e) allows mutually agreed cash/electronic rent payment only while another authorized method remains, subject to (a). An online-only collection design fails even if its fee appears in the original lease.
- **Limited exclusive-cash exception:** prior insufficient-funds check or tenant stop-payment instruction permits exclusive cash for no more than three months after the qualifying event. Give written notice identifying dishonor and the cash-payment period, attach the dishonored instrument, and comply with §827 where the demand changes lease terms. Without those facts and notice, preserve the normal noncash/non-EFT option; the exception does not authorize a check-processing fee. A stale dishonor does not support indefinite cash-only payment.
- **Classify the actual payment mechanism.** Subdivision (d) includes telephone transfers, ACH, recurring transfers, and electronically initiated transfers delivering a paper instrument within EFT. A truly check/draft/paper-originated transaction is excluded from that definition. Calling an electronically originated transfer a paper payment does not satisfy the non-EFT-option requirement.
- **Third-party rent tender:** (a)(3) requires acceptance subject to the payer's signed non-tenancy acknowledgment; the landlord may require it per payment or agree to one covering a period. The rule does not compel a housing-assistance-program contract or extend the due date/require acceptance beyond the specified §1161(2) notice period. Do not add a new payment-service fee simply because someone pays on the tenant's behalf.

This gate applies by its stated actor, obligation and payment predicates: landlord/agent receiving tenant rent or security deposit. Do not silently extend it to every repair-only claim or treat every debt purchaser as the landlord's agent. For a mixed outgoing account containing rent, preserve the lawful rent-payment route and do not impose a check fee on that rent portion through a bundled account-processing label. For an optional electronic fee expressly authorized in the original agreement, §1947.3 supplies **no affirmative fee authorization**; it supplies the mandatory alternative-payment and check-fee restrictions. The original-agreement route under the adopted §1788.17 construction remains distinct and cannot waive those restrictions.

## Mandatory notice-delivery expense exclusions

Exclude landlord/agent charges to the tenant for **serving, posting, or otherwise delivering notices described in Civil Code §§1946 and 1946.1 or CCP§1161**. The operative prohibitions are CIV§1946(b), CIV§1946.1(i), and CCP§1161(6). An original fee clause, actual incurred process-server invoice, or reasonable amount cannot override these prohibitions. Separate the notice-delivery component from any independently recoverable legal work; changing its name to collection expense or attorney disbursement does not make it chargeable. Do not equate these presuit tenancy notices with all later court-process service or extend this targeted prohibition to every unrelated document.

**Dates:** the prohibition begins **January1,2025**, including CCP§1161. The current CCP§1161 version states it became operative February1,2025, but that is not the first day of the fee ban: chaptered SB611 **§5** amended the predecessor §1161 with the identical subdivision6 prohibition during January2025; that predecessor repealed February1. SB611 **§6** amended the successor with the same prohibition, operative February1. There is no January gap. Current CIV§1946.1's footer cites SB1103 ch.1015 §3.5, the merged operative text retaining the fee prohibition; its current (i) is read directly rather than inferred from the older bill alone. Earlier charges require their actual historical law, not retroactive use of this amendment.

## Concrete decisions and necessary facts

| Proposed charge | Legal result and facts necessary to reach it |
|---|---|
| Ordinary rent/repair obligation already due to creditor | §1788.14(b) is not a ban on the original obligation merely because a collector now seeks payment. Apply the underlying obligation's own legal basis and §1692f(1) where applicable. A ledger label cannot disguise a collection expense as principal. |
| Creditor's reasonable actual collection expense under an express original undertaking | May be sought when the clause covers the activity, its trigger/notice/cure conditions have occurred, the expense was actually incurred for this account, and its reasonable amount is substantiated. Record work, dates, incurred liability, allocation to this account, and no duplication. Mere retainer, price list, future budget, or percent of balance does not prove this. An incurred but unpaid liability can be actual; payment alone does not prove reasonableness. §§3300–3301 and Bondanza provide the positive damages basis; §1788.14(b) remains checked rather than bypassed. Exclude the statutory notice-delivery fees described above even if actually incurred. |
| Original agreement specifically authorizes an optional payment-service fee | Under the adopted §1788.17/AG interpretation, §1788.14(b) does not categorically prohibit it. Verify the precise fee, amount/formula, service, contractual authorization and conditions; distinguish an optional service price from disguised default damages; enforce any separately applicable substantive limitation. Actual-cost proof is not a universal requirement for this branch. Apply the concrete §1947.3 gate above: no rent/security check fee and preserve the required payment alternative; no waiver. |
| Collector demands its own newly imposed convenience, processing, or collection-service fee | Do not add merely because the debtor could choose a free channel or assented to a new payment agreement. A generic new-contract theory would erase §1692f(1)'s distinction between the agreement creating the debt and later payment arrangements. In the examined California rental workflow no affirmative payment-service authorization has been identified; proposed discretionary pay-to-pay fee is excluded, not left awaiting speculative law. |
| Landlord/agency contract says agency receives 25%, 33%, etc. | That contract determines their compensation, not the debtor's liability. Do not pass it through automatically, even if the landlord has paid it. Evaluate actual lawful debtor obligation independently; assignment cannot enlarge the burden. |
| Original dwelling lease expressly sets a fixed/percentage default collection charge | Express wording satisfies only the contract-identification question. Under §1671(c)(2)/(d), require facts establishing impracticability/extreme difficulty of fixing actual loss and a reasonable contemporaneous compensation estimate. If absent, invalidate the liquidated charge; still evaluate separately proven actual damages without double recovery. An arbitrary percentage fails. A valid liquidated clause is not categorically forbidden just because it is a percentage. |
| Actual presuit attorney work covered by a fee clause reaching collection without suit | A court award is **not a universal prerequisite** to demanding this contractual obligation. Require the actual enforceable clause to cover presuit services, occurrence of its conditions, actual work and incurred liability, and reasonable amount. Exclude prohibited tenancy-notice delivery expense even when billed by counsel. Describe the remaining supported fee as a contractual claim, not an awarded judgment amount. CCP§1021 leaves attorney compensation to agreement except statutory rules; §1717 governs an action on the contract and does not itself create a prelitigation prevailing-party award. A generic costs clause must not silently be treated as an express attorney-fee undertaking. |
| Clause only permits fees to prevailing party in litigation | No current presuit attorney-letter addition; successful-litigation condition is not satisfied. Reichert's facts illustrate exactly this error. If proceedings occur, assess §1717 reciprocity, prevailing-party status, scope, reasonableness and procedural award requirements. |
| Requested litigation attorney fees or court costs | Preserve as requested relief while unresolved; do not describe as already awarded debt. Civil Code§1717 and CCP§1033.5(a)(10),(c)(5) govern fee determination; a private bill is not the court's determination. Valid existing judgment/order/stipulation supplies the relevant established amount, subject to its terms, credits and operative status. §1717(b)(2) ordinarily supplies no prevailing party after voluntary dismissal or settlement; do not invent an automatic fee award from the contract's existence. |

The distinction between creditor and collector concerns the **nature of the charge**, not an identity escape hatch: Rosenthal can cover a creditor collecting its own debt. Moving an agency charge to the landlord ledger does not legalize it. Conversely, a collector acting for the creditor can pursue the creditor's legally recoverable contractual expense; the state provision does not erase that underlying entitlement.

The factual predicates above are ordinary legal factfinding, not unresolved legal dependencies. If the actual contract or invoices are unavailable, the result is **not yet supported by the case evidence**, rather than an unanswered legal standard. Neither model confidence nor a landlord's asserted total substitutes for them.

## Why competing blanket rules are rejected

“Any contract authorization suffices” ignores the original-agreement limitation in §1692f(1), actual specificity and conditions in the clause, and California substantive limits. “Original contractual authorization never suffices without another express fee statute” conflicts with the AG opinion and DeSimone's original-agreement analysis. “Only an express fee statute can ever permit recovery” overreads mortgage convenience-fee decisions and disregards enforceable contract obligations recognized by California's damages and attorney-fee law. “Every fee first needs a court award” improperly turns prevailing-party litigation rules into conditions on every otherwise accrued presuit obligation. “Actual paid commission always equals actual recoverable loss” is precisely the position the Bondanza majority rejected.

This supplies the operational answer: collect justified underlying amounts and supported legally recoverable expenses; exclude the discretionary collector markup, invalid penalty and unearned litigation fee. Choosing to waive an otherwise recoverable expense is a commercial decision separate from this legal result.
