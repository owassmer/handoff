L18@P1: INTRODUCTION
L19@P1:  Plaintiffs Martin Gans and Stars Drive-In, Inc. appeal from a judgment dismissing
L20@P1: their complaint against defendants L.C. Smull, Robert H. Cowgill and S-C Development.
L21@P1: The dismissal followed the sustaining of defendants’ demurrer without leave to amend.
L22@P1: We affirm the judgment.
L23@P1: FACTUAL AND PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND
L24@P1:  On January 25, 1977, defendants entered into a 25-year lease with plaintiffs’
L25@P1: predecessors in interest for property defendants owned on Sherman Way in Canoga Park.1
L26@P1: 
L27@P1: The lease was assigned to plaintiffs on December 1, 1998.
L28@P1:  The lease contained an option to extend for two five-year periods. (Para. 36.1.) It
L29@P1: further provided: “To exercise Tenant’s options to extend, Tenant must notify Landlord
L30@P1: in writing on or before 60 days before the date of expiration of the within Lease . . . .”
L31@P1: (Para. 36.2.) “Notices by Tenant to Landlord shall be in writing and deposited in the
L32@P1: United States mail, postage prepaid, certified or registered, addressed to Landlord . . . .”
L33@P1: (Para. 19.)
L34@P1:  The lease term expired on July 31, 2002. The 60th day before the expiration date
L35@P1: was Saturday, June 1, 2002. On Monday, June 3, 2002, plaintiffs attempted to exercise
L36@P1: their option to extend the lease by letter sent to defendants by regular mail. Defendants
L37@P1: admitted receiving the letter the following day.
L38@P1:  On June 13, 2002, defendants wrote back to plaintiffs that their attempt to exercise
L39@P1: the option was untimely. Defendants added: “Please be advised that the Landlord has
L40@P1: elected to cancel your lease at the expiration on July 31, 2002, at which time you are to
L41@P1: vacate the premises pursuant to Article 17 of said lease.”
L42@P1: 
L43@P1: 1
L44@P1:  The 25-year period did not commence until the first of several conditions was
L45@P1-2: fulfilled. (Para. 2.1.) 3
L46@P2:  Plaintiffs filed the instant action on July 25, 2002, seeking damages for
L47@P2: anticipatory breach of contract, breach of a covenant to extend the term of the lease and
L48@P2: seeking declaratory relief. The gravamen of the complaint was that plaintiffs timely
L49@P2: exercised their option to extend the lease. By refusing to accept plaintiffs’ exercise of the
L50@P2: option, defendants breached the contract.
L51@P2:  Defendants filed an unlawful detainer action in the superior court on August 20,
L52@P2: 2002. (Smull v. Gans (Super. Ct. L.A. County, 2002, No. 02B05002).) Defendants filed
L53@P2: their demurrer in the instant action on September 6, 2002. They claimed Code of Civil
L54@P2: Procedure section 12a2
L55@P2:  did not apply to extend the time for notice from Saturday, June 1
L56@P2: to Monday, June 3, 2002.
L57@P2:  Shortly after defendants filed the demurrer, on September 16, 2002, plaintiffs filed
L58@P2: a motion for summary judgment in the unlawful detainer action. On September 23, 2002,
L59@P2: the superior court granted plaintiffs’ motion for summary judgment. It ruled that
L60@P2: pursuant to Lamanna v. Vognar (1993) 17 Cal.App.4th Supp. 4 and Ystrom v. Handel
L61@P2: (1988) 205 Cal.App.3d 144, plaintiffs’ June 3, 2002 letter exercising their option was
L62@P2: timely under Code of Civil Procedure section 12a. Language contrary to Lamanna and
L63@P2: Ystrom in Lynch v. Harrell (1941) 44 Cal.App.2d 863 was dicta and interpreted an earlier
L64@P2: version of Code of Civil Procedure section 12a. Additionally, plaintiffs’ mailing of the
L65@P2: notice by regular mail rather than certified or registered mail was immaterial, in that
L66@P2: defendants admitted receiving the notice the following day.
L67@P2:  The trial court in the instant case sustained defendants’ demurrer without leave to
L68@P2: amend on October 21, 2002. Judgment was entered on October 21, 2002. On
L69@P2: October 29, 2002, defendants filed a notice of appeal from the judgment in the unlawful

L81@P3: CONTENTION
L82@P3:  Plaintiffs contend that Code of Civil Procedure section 12a, along with other
L83@P3: statutory provisions, clearly establish that, where the last day on which to exercise a lease
L84@P3: option falls on a Saturday, the time in which to exercise the option is extended to the
L85@P3: following Monday as a matter of law. For the reasons set forth below, we disagree.
L86@P3: DISCUSSION
L87@P3:  Under Code of Civil Procedure section 12a, subdivision (a), “[i]f the last day for
L88@P3: the performance of any act provided or required by law to be performed within a
L89@P3: specified period of time is a holiday, then that period is hereby extended to and including
L90@P3: the next day which is not a holiday.” “Holidays” to include Saturdays. Plaintiffs contend
L91@P3: this section applied in the instant case to extend the time in which they could exercise
L92@P3: their option to extend their lease from Saturday, June 1 to Monday, June 3, 2002.
L93@P3:  Plaintiffs rely on Lamanna v. Vognar, supra, 17 Cal.App.4th Supp. 4 and Ystrom
L94@P3: v. Handel, supra, 205 Cal.App.3d 144. Ystrom involved the question whether Code of
L95@P3: Civil Procedure section 12a applied to extend the period of time in which a summons and
L96@P3: complaint must be served. (Ystrom, supra, at p. 147.) It was clear to the court that
L97@P3: “[s]ervice of process within three years of filing a complaint is an act required by law.”
L98@P3: (Id. at p. 148.) Therefore, section 12a applied. (Ibid.)
L99@P3: Lamanna was an unlawful detainer action. Defendant was served with a three-day
L100@P3: notice to pay rent or quit on Wednesday, May 20. The third day after service of the
L101@P3-4: notice was Saturday, May 23. Monday, May 25, was a legal holiday, Memorial Day. 5
L102@P4: Plaintiff filed the unlawful detainer action on Tuesday, May 26. (Lamanna, supra, 17
L103@P4: Cal.App.4th at p. Supp. 6.)3
L104@P4:  The question before the court was whether the time in which defendant could pay
L105@P4: overdue rent was extended by the three-day weekend, May 23 through May 25.
L106@P4: (Lamanna v. Vognar, supra, 17 Cal.App.4th at p. Supp. 7.) The court noted that under
L107@P4: Code of Civil Procedure section 12, “‘[t]he time in which any act provided by law is to be
L108@P4: done is computed by excluding the first day, and including the last, unless the last day is
L109@P4: a holiday, and then it is also excluded.’” (Lamanna, supra, at p. Supp. 7.) The court
L110@P4: concluded “that a tenant’s right to pay overdue rent without being subject to the
L111@P4: termination of the tenancy within the three-day period under section 1161 is an ‘act
L112@P4: provided by law,’ within the meaning of section 12.” (Ibid.) Therefore, under Code of
L113@P4: Civil Procedure section 12a, the three-day weekend was excluded from the computation
L114@P4: of the time in which defendant could pay the overdue rent. (Id. at p. Supp. 8.) The
L115@P4: unlawful detainer action could not properly be filed until Wednesday, May 27. (Ibid.)
L116@P4:  As defendants point out, the acts at issue in Ystrom and Lamanna were governed
L117@P4: by statute: service of process and payment of overdue rent in response to a three-day
L118@P4: notice to pay rent or quit. There is no statute governing the exercise of an option to
L119@P4: extend a lease.
L120@P4:  Plaintiffs attempt to analogize the instant case to Lamanna, arguing that “the act
L121@P4: that was to be performed by the tenant . . . was the performance of a term of the lease
L122@P4: which, if performed in an untimely manner, would trigger the landlord’s right to initiate
L123@P4: unlawful detainer proceedings.” The analogy fails. Lamanna involved a failure to pay
L124@P4: overdue rent after the issuance of a three-day notice to quit, an act governed by statute.
L125@P4: 
L126@P4: 3
L127@P4:  Pursuant to Code of Civil Procedure section 1161, subdivision 2, “‘[A tenant of
L128@P4: real property is guilty of unlawful detainer] [w]hen he continues in possession without
L129@P4: permission of his landlord . . . after default in the payment of rent, pursuant to the lease or
L130@P4: agreement . . . and three days’ notice, in writing, requiring its payment, stating the
L131@P4: amount which is due, or possession of the property, shall have been served upon
L132@P4-5: him. . . .’” (Lamanna v. Vognar, supra, 17 Cal.App.4th at p. Supp. 6.) 6
L133@P5: In the instant case, plaintiffs’ exercise of their option to extend the lease was not an act
L134@P5: governed by statue.
L135@P5:  Defendants take the position that this case is governed by Civil Code section 7.
L136@P5: This provides: “Holidays within the meaning of this code are every Sunday and such
L137@P5: other days as are specified or provided for as holidays in the Government Code of the
L138@P5: State of California.” If this section applies, Saturday, June 1 was not a holiday, the time
L139@P5: for plaintiffs’ performance was not extended, and performance was untimely.
L140@P5:  As defendants point out, the statutes governing contracts, including leases, are
L141@P5: contained within the Civil Code. (Civ. Code, §§ 1549 et seq., 1940 et seq.) The Code of
L142@P5: Civil Procedure, on the other hand, governs civil and special proceedings. This suggests
L143@P5: the time for performing a contract would be found in the Civil Code rather than in the
L144@P5: Code of Civil Procedure.
L145@P5:  Defendants claim that if the Legislature intended Code of Civil Procedure section
L146@P5: 12a to apply to contracts, it knew how to specify so. For example, Civil Code section 9
L147@P5: provides that days that are not holidays within the meaning of section 7 “are business
L148@P5: days for all purposes; provided” and contains further provisions “as to any act appointed
L149@P5: by law or contract.” (Italics added.) Civil Code section 11 provides that “[w]henever
L150@P5: any act of a secular nature, other than a work of necessity or mercy, is appointed by law
L151@P5: or contract to be performed upon a particular day, which day falls upon a holiday, it may
L152@P5: be performed upon the next business day, with the same effect as if it had been performed
L153@P5: upon the day appointed.” (Italics added.)
L154@P5:  Where the Legislature uses particular language in a statute, the omission of such
L155@P5: language in a similar statute tends to show a different legislative intent. (Holmes v. Jones
L156@P5: (2000) 83 Cal.App.4th 882, 890; Department of Industrial Relations v. Lee (1999) 73
L157@P5: Cal.App.4th 763, 768; see, e.g., Traverso v. People ex rel. Dept. of Transportation (1993)
L158@P5: 6 Cal.4th 1152, 1166.) Its omission of the “or contract” language from Code of Civil
L159@P5: Procedure section 12a, while using that language in Civil Code sections 9 and 11,
L160@P5-6: evidences an intent to exclude contracts from the purview of that section. 7
L161@P6:  For this reason, we hold that Code of Civil Procedure section 12a does not apply
L162@P6: to acts governed solely by contractual provisions. Accordingly, section 12a did not apply
L163@P6: to extend the time in which plaintiffs could exercise their option to extend their lease.
L164@P6: Their attempt to exercise the option was untimely. The trial court did not err in
L165@P6: sustaining defendants’ demurrer on that basis.
L166@P6:  The judgment is affirmed.
