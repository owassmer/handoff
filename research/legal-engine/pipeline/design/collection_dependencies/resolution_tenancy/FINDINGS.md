# Resolved tenancy transaction branches

Research resolution, 2026-10-01. Supersedes the open-ended payment-plan, utilities and damage rows in `../tenancy/FINDINGS.md` for synthesis purposes; preserves that historical report and all experiment files. This is reasoned legal research, not an active rule write. Parent review remains required.

## Governing conclusion

Use the acquisition/payment arrangement and the origin of each claimed obligation, rather than the words rent, utilities, reimbursement, damage or settlement. California consumer-credit coverage requires actual or alleged acquisition of property, services or money on agreed future-payment terms. Direct federal FDCPA debt requires a household consumer transaction but does not require credit. Then separately apply the relevant collector definition. A qualifying transaction does not establish that the amount is valid, nor does an invalid amount eliminate protections for its attempted collection.

The live official Civil Code 1788.2 was retrieved again in `sources/civ1788_2.html`: AB1521, Stats.2025 ch.200 sec.3, effective January 1, 2026. The consumer-credit language in (e),(f) remains. Prior report supplies amendments, actor analysis, source originals and the California-versus-direct-federal distinction. COVID rental-debt transfer restrictions under 1788.66 remain an independent branch even where ordinary advance rent lacks the credit predicate.

## New decisive California authority

**Hagey v. Solar Service Experts, LLC, 94 Cal.App.5th 1303, 1308–1309 (2023)** is published California appellate authority, not merely a federal analogy. The original opinion, reproduced as PDF by Justia, is captured in `sources/hagey_hodge_web.txt`, PDF p.7/source lines153–173. Electricity was supplied under an agreement, billed monthly for the prior month's generation, and needed no prepayment. The court holds that this arrangement embodies a consumer-credit transaction. Exact excerpt: “Pre-payment was not required, and each monthly payment was for past service”. The opinion also rejects the supposed debtor's lack of actual liability or participation in the original agreement as a coverage defense. It reversed a demurrer; the September26,2023 modification replaced footnote2 to clarify that the facts also account for the unchallenged judicial-notice ruling, rather than accepting every allegation alone. The modification denied rehearing without changing judgment or the credit rule (`sources/hagey_modification.txt`, source lines47–51). The opinion did not establish damages or resolve every utility allocation arrangement.

This is directly applicable reasoning for agreed postpaid household utilities. Calling the landlord's charge reimbursement rather than electricity does not undo receipt before agreed payment. Hagey's actual holding is solar electricity, not every public utility tariff or landlord allocation formula. The landlord extension below applies its rule rather than inventing a tenancy-specific holding.

**Hodge v. Travel + Leisure Co., No.5:24-cv-06116-EJD (N.D.Cal. Jan.29,2025)**, persuasive district order, expressly uses Hagey/Paredes/Davidson and still distinguishes postpaid acquisition from advance/concurrent services and unilateral late payment. `sources/hodge_end.txt`, source lines139–153. Dismissal with leave to amend for insufficient factual allegations is not a categorical exclusion of timeshares or monthly fees. It confirms that Hagey does not make every unpaid periodic obligation credit. Later proceedings were not relied on as final adjudication; this order's reasoning is used only for the distinction stated.

Prior published California authorities remain essential: **Gouskos**, 94 Cal.App.4th754,759–760, acquisition rather than vendor work alone; **Davidson**, 21 Cal.App.5th283,296, obtaining value on a promise of future payment; **Paredes**, 82 Cal.App.5th410,434–436, services received with contemplated insurance payment and patient fallback liability, without requiring a conventional loan. Original captures and precise locations are in `../tenancy/FINDINGS.md` and its sources. Federal district advance-rent decisions **Yatooma/Phillips/Leasure** support exclusion of ordinary prepaid rent that merely went unpaid. They are not controlling California appellate decisions; Phillips's later Ninth Circuit memorandum did not decide that issue.

**Whole-term leasehold counterargument resolved:** A fixed-term lease grants enforceable possession rights at execution, arguably value before all future monthly rent is paid. For an ordinary periodic advance-rent bargain, the selected reading follows Yatooma/Phillips and the later Hodge distinction: periodic rent is consideration for the corresponding occupancy period, not installments financing an already delivered entire leasehold merely because the term and future duties are fixed. An actual agreement financing an upfront leasehold price or permitting occupancy before agreed payment differs and is credit. Thus fixed duration alone does not establish credit; the consideration and payment structure do. This is the adopted statutory application supported by persuasive rental decisions, not a binding California categorical rent holding.

## 1. Payment plans and later agreements

**Resolved rule:** A later agreement merely postponing enforcement or dividing an existing noncredit balance into installments does not, by itself, turn the original acquisition into a consumer-credit transaction. Preserve the original transaction classification. This conclusion is a statutory application of the acquisition requirement, supported by the advance-rent distinction and federal debt-origin reasoning, not a claim that a California published tenancy case has categorically decided every settlement.

Apply these concrete alternatives:

| Established facts | California consumer-credit result | What the workflow does |
|---|---|---|
| Advance rent went unpaid; landlord later agrees to collect the same balance in installments, suspends suit, waives fees or reduces principal; no new property, service or money supplied | No new credit acquisition merely from the compromise/forbearance. Original prepaid-rent component stays outside the consumer-credit predicate | Honor the new schedule and release; recompute enforceable amount/default under the agreement. Still run direct FDCPA and COVID branches |
| Plan also prospectively permits occupancy or a distinct service to be acquired before its agreed payment date | Future deferred acquisition is credit; old prepaid arrears retain their origin | Separate old principal from new occupancy/service periods; apply Rosenthal to collection connected with the latter |
| Landlord or lender genuinely advances funds for the tenant, including paying a landlord/vendor on the tenant's behalf, repayable later | New household money/financed acquisition is credit | Follow the new loan obligation and payoff; prevent double collection of extinguished rent |
| Parties replace an obligation with a new note but supply no new funds, property or services; substantive change is repayment timing/compromise | Note title or contractual novation alone does not establish acquisition on credit | Classify from substantive exchange and original debt; do not set credit=true from the word loan or novation |
| Original postpaid utility/service debt is later compromised or put in installments | Its credit origin survives ordinary modification/forbearance | Apply Rosenthal to its collection under modified terms; a settlement label does not strip coverage |

A true funding transaction need not put paper cash into the tenant's hand; disbursement on the tenant's behalf can supply money. Conversely, a journal entry that merely renames existing rent principal does not itself prove an advance. Required facts are the actual exchanged performance, funds/payoff records, release language, effective dates and future service periods—not a free-form legal question left to the operator.

**Novation dependency resolved.** Official Civil Code1530 defines novation as substitution of a new obligation for an existing one;1531 specifies modes, including a new same-party obligation intended to extinguish the old one;1532 makes general contract rules applicable. Saved official HTML originals. **Alexander v. Angel, 37 Cal.2d856 (1951)**, California Supreme Court, `sources/alexander.txt` source lines43–46, makes intent controlling and places proof of novation on its proponent. Merely signing a schedule or note is not automatic extinguishment. Even established extinguishment answers what obligation survives; 1788.2(e) separately asks what the consumer acquired on credit. A compromise, release and new promise can be a valid contract without supplying the statutory acquisition.

**Strongest contrary argument addressed:** binding forbearance is valuable consideration, voluntarily acquired with a future-payment promise; Davidson's broad language might therefore be invoked to characterize the new arrangement as credit. The better application here reads that language with the statute's specified property/services/money acquisition, not every contractual concession. Merely delaying enforcement of an already completed noncredit exchange does not supply the tenant the underlying service on new deferred terms. Nor does characterizing the compromise release itself as intangible property change the selected result: a release reducing or replacing the same existing liability is the settlement of that obligation, not a newly supplied household acquisition merely by relabeling extinguishment as a purchase. A separate acquisition or genuine payoff advance is different; neither contractual consideration nor novation alone suffices. Actual prospective occupancy or new financing does. Accordingly the rule is no automatic conversion from forbearance, with affirmative coverage for a demonstrated new deferred acquisition. This is a resolved interpretation with disclosed litigation uncertainty, not an assertion that the narrower fact pattern has an on-point binding holding.

**Pollice v. National Tax Funding, 225 F.3d379 (3dCir.2000)**, persuasive federal authority, distinguishes original consumer utility obligations from nonconsumer property-tax obligations and preserves that origin after payment plans. `sources/pollice_paymentplan_footnote.txt` source lines495–498 (opinion n.40) explains why later plans did not bring taxes into FDCPA even though the plans could independently be TILA credit. That last point is material counterevidence: TILA/credit or usury classification is not interchangeable with Rosenthal's acquisition test or FDCPA's transaction test. Do not cite Pollice as a California holding or claim all refinancing preserves every old classification.

For direct FDCPA, **De Dios**, 641 F.3d1071,1074–1075 (9thCir.2011), also means a valid forbearance can change the default date used in a collector exclusion. Thus a plan may change default and performance obligations without changing whether the original balance is a consumer transaction. Prior official opinion saved under tenancy/sources.

## 2. Utilities and other services

**Resolved rule:** Agreed household services received before their contractual payment date are consumer credit. Hagey directly establishes that monthly billing for prior electricity is sufficient; no separate finance charge or formal installment instrument is required. A bill payable immediately upon receipt is still postpaid if the agreement permitted consumption before that bill. A due date after an invoice alone is insufficient where acquisition did not precede payment on agreed terms.

| Established arrangement | Operating result |
|---|---|
| Lease/addendum makes tenant liable after monthly metering/allocation for electricity/water already consumed | Treat claimed charge as consumer-credit debt; validate allocation and amount separately |
| Landlord agrees to advance a tenant's household utility payment with later reimbursement | Treat genuine advance as credit, whether framed as money advanced or financed service |
| Fixed utility charge is due in advance with rent, and only tenant's nonpayment creates delay | No credit merely from default; separate any genuinely postpaid reconciliation component |
| Ongoing service charge paid concurrently with the service, rather than acquisition before payment | No credit solely from recurring billing; use actual contract/service periods, consistent with Hodge's distinction |
| Claimed utility charge is unauthorized or billed to wrong tenant but collector alleges it arose under a postpaid household service agreement | Alleged-debt coverage still applies under Hagey; investigate/correct the false obligation, rather than exclude from protections |
| Charge is solely general property tax/assessment rather than an agreed personal utility purchase | Do not treat it as a consumer utility transaction merely because water/sewer appears in its label; Pollice distinguishes taxes from household service obligations |

**Counterargument:** utility trade associations claimed in a 2021 DFPI licensing comment that CPUC-regulated utility charges are outside the credit definition. That is advocacy, not an adopted regulation or judicial holding. Its cited dishonored-check/overdraft/towing cases do not decide agreed postpaid electricity. Published Hagey (2023) is stronger and later on the actual acquisition question. Public-utility licensing/tariff exclusions cannot be silently extended to a landlord's separate conduct. The comment is recorded in SOURCE_REGISTER.md as a considered contrary position, not supporting authority.

## 3. Damage, cleaning and lease charges

**California resolved rule:** An ordinary prepaid lease's covenant to reimburse damage, cleaning after breach, lost rent, reletting or termination liability does not itself supply a new acquisition on credit. Landlord restoration of its own unit after the tenant leaves is not automatically a service acquired by the tenant simply because the landlord invoices its cost later. A contingent promise to pay damages for breach does not convert every prepaid rental into a credit acquisition.

Two positive branches prevent over-exclusion. First, an actual tenant purchase of a repair, cleaning, parking, storage or other household service on agreed future payment is credit. A tenant can buy a service to fulfill a move-out obligation even after physically departing; the decisive distinction is a service actually supplied under that purchase arrangement, not occupancy status or the repair label. Second, sums alleged due **by reason of an underlying credit transaction** include associated contractual charges; they do not need to be an independently financed purchase. For a genuinely deferred-payment lease, an associated contractual damage/termination claim should therefore be treated as connected to that credit transaction. Validate the claimed entitlement separately. An unrelated tort does not become connected merely because the claimant also has a credit account.

**Direct federal resolved rule:** Contractual household rental liabilities, including alleged damage reimbursement, can be consumer-transaction debt without credit. Do not turn a contractual damage charge into an excluded tort by selecting that label. **Fleming v. Pickard,581 F.3d922,925–926 (9thCir.2009)**, official opinion saved `fleming.pdf/.txt` (text205–277), excludes an obligation originating in theft/conversion, without the necessary consensual transaction. **Hawthorne v. Mac Adjustment,140 F.3d1367,1371–1372 (11thCir.1998)**, official opinion saved `hawthorne.pdf/.txt` (text403–438), distinguishes **Brown v. Budget Rent-A-Car,119 F.3d922 (11thCir.1997)**: damage obligations connected to a consensual rental/loss-waiver transaction differ from unrelated automobile-accident tort liability. Hawthorne's careful account is not a blanket holding that every negligence claim by a landlord is contractual. Brown/Hawthorne are persuasive here; Fleming supplies binding Ninth Circuit origin reasoning. **Reichert**,531 F.3d1002 (9thCir.2008), independently applies FDCPA to an apartment collection account and an unauthorized fee; prior official original saved.

Accordingly: tenant damage demanded under a household lease covenant → federal consumer-debt predicate; non-tenant stranger's window-breaking liability with no consumer arrangement → no such predicate; actual new household loan to settle a tort → independently analyze the new credit advance, not merely the old tort label. Each result remains subject to federal collector status.

**Counterauthority read fully:** **DeNicolo v. Hertz Corp., No.19-cv-00210-YGR (N.D.Cal. Sept.30,2020)** denied a summary-judgment challenge to Fox's Rosenthal claim concerning rental-car damage. `sources/denicolo_end.txt` source lines262–300. Its discussion turns on due/owing, collector scope, default timing and evidentiary failures; it is not a reasoned holding that every rental damage obligation satisfies acquisition on credit. It defeats a claim of universal judicial exclusion but does not displace the statutory transaction analysis. This matters because an initial skim could have overstated it.

## 4. Mixed balances and action-level handling

Resolve two levels. At the obligation level, identify the substantive transaction before separating components. Section1788.2(f)'s 'by reason of' connection can encompass multiple charges under one acquisition bargain; ledger itemization alone does not establish separate transactions. A lease with an independently priced and performed postpaid utility addendum can supply separate acquisitions. In contrast, a single inseparable package of occupancy and services for an integrated price partly payable after delivery is a partly deferred acquisition: treat obligations arising from that package, including associated rent and contractual damage charges, as arising by reason of consumer credit. This follows the statutory relationship requirement and the published acquisition cases; it is a selected application, not a claim of a mixed-lease-specific holding.

For separability, examine what was purchased, separately agreed prices and payment terms, whether performance/entitlement can exist independently, and the contractual basis of each charge. Cross-default clauses or sharing one document do not alone merge acquisitions; separate line items do not alone divide them. A utility allocation reflecting a distinct metered service, independently computed price and independent post-service obligation supports separation from fixed advance occupancy rent. A single all-inclusive consideration with deferred true-up for the whole furnished occupancy package supports integration. When evidence cannot establish separation, do not authorize an exclusion for the disputed components by assuming it: obtain the actual contract/pricing/performance evidence, while treating any collection of the established covered obligation as covered conduct.

At the action level, one demand collecting a covered component is an act in connection with collection of that component under1788.2(b). Apply relevant collection-conduct duties to that communication. Do not falsely claim this converts every genuinely unrelated principal component into consumer credit, or use an uncovered rent component to strip protection from the covered part.

Example with established separate acquisitions: $8,000 unpaid fixed advance occupancy rent + $450 independently priced postpaid metered electricity under a separate service undertaking + $2,000 unit-restoration reimbursement solely under the prepaid occupancy bargain. California consumer-credit predicate: electricity yes; prepaid rent and restoration no on those facts. A combined demand still attempts collection of covered electricity and must follow applicable Rosenthal conduct requirements. Change the contract to an integrated occupancy/service package acquired partly on deferred payment: treat the package's associated amounts as connected to the credit transaction, rather than mechanically preserving the three ledger labels. Qualifying outside debt collector: all three claimed contractual household components meet direct federal debt predicate in either example. Regular self-collecting landlord: apply California actor analysis, without assuming direct federal collector status. COVID-period rent: run independent transfer ban. Deposit credits/payment allocation must be reconciled before stating the remaining amount, without discarding historical attempted-collection coverage for a paid component.

## Concrete evidence and remaining facts

For each claimed amount, retain the alleged agreement, natural-person/household purpose, actual or alleged thing acquired, acquisition/service period, original payment terms, subsequent modifications, payoff/credit records, claim basis and sender's collection role. The agent resolves document meaning and identifies these facts; focused Jev tasks can identify the supporting clauses/periods without receiving a compound legal verdict question; code maintains amount lineage, dates, exact arithmetic and selected rule outputs. Neither Jev nor a missing document should be asked to invent a legal category.

Missing lease/addendum, substantive integration of the bargain, or ambiguous service dates are missing case facts: obtain them before an exclusion-based action. The legal branches above supply a selected result once those facts are established. No assigned legal branch remains undecided: ordinary later forbearance alone preserves origin; genuine new deferred acquisition is credit; agreed postpaid utilities are credit; contractual damage follows its underlying acquisition and differs from a separate purchased service or independent tort; integrated bargains and separate acquisitions receive the different treatment above. The stated limits concern authority weight and future judicial disagreement, not unfinished research or a direction to defer the legal decision. No categorical tenancy appellate holding is fabricated. Coverage conclusions authorize no charge by themselves: enforceability, deductions and collection-action prerequisites remain the separate substantive rules in the parent dependency analysis.
