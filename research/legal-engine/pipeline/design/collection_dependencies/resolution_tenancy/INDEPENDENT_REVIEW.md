## Defects, challenges, and rechecks

**1. Material mixed-transaction omission — corrected.** The original example classified rent, utilities and restoration separately without establishing that these were separate acquisitions. Under Civil Code 1788.2(f), sums arising by reason of one credit transaction need not each finance a separate purchase. I required the author to address an integrated occupancy/services bargain partly paid after delivery. The corrected section 4 distinguishes substantive acquisitions using agreed consideration, payment terms, independent performance/entitlement and the actual claim basis. It covers the integrated package and associated contractual charges; line-item labels alone cannot create exclusions. The separate-acquisition example now expressly supplies separate service and pricing predicates. Rechecked against the [current official definitions](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1788.2.), particularly (b), (e) and (f). This is a reasoned statutory application, not an invented mixed-lease holding.

**2. Hagey version/procedural precision — corrected.** I located and read the [September 26, 2023 modification order](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2023/g061836m.html). It changes footnote 2 to account for the unchallenged judicial-notice ruling. The author captured the modification and corrected the description of the factual posture. The order leaves the judgment unchanged. The solar-agreement credit reasoning supports postpaid household electricity, while the opinion's actual holding and the broader landlord-utility application remain distinct. Verified the new capture and amended source register.

**3. Advance rent versus full-term leasehold — counterargument resolved.** I challenged the assumption that monthly prepayment defeats credit when a fixed-term lease grants a possessory interest before all installments are paid. The revised findings expressly choose periodic occupancy consideration as the relevant ordinary-rent exchange, supported by persuasive Yatooma/Phillips reasoning and Hodge's advance/concurrent distinction. Actual financing of an upfront leasehold price or deferred occupancy is separately covered. This is defensible; its weight is expressly weaker than a binding California appellate rent holding. Neither fixed lease duration alone nor later default decides the acquisition question.

**4. Forbearance, releases and new notes — counterargument resolved.** I specifically challenged whether binding forbearance or a release is valuable intangible property acquired with a future-payment promise. The revision distinguishes settlement/extinguishment of the same liability from a new household acquisition or genuine payoff advance. Novation changes the surviving obligation; it does not independently prove the Rosenthal acquisition predicate. The report adopts and defends that reading rather than stopping at an unsuccessful exact-case search. California Civil Code [1530](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1530.), [1531](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1531.), and [1532](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1532.) and the saved Alexander opinion support the separate novation inquiry; they are not misrepresented as Rosenthal decisions.

## Adversarial branch rechecks

1. A departed tenant signs a schedule for old prepaid rent, with principal reduction and a release but no new money or performance: the adopted no-new-acquisition result is explicit; the revised agreement still controls enforceable amount and payment timing.
2. The same document also finances next month's occupancy: that future acquisition is covered; it does not retroactively change separately established old transactions.
3. A lender actually pays the landlord on the tenant's behalf: the funding branch applies even without cash handed to the tenant; payoff prevents double collection.
4. Electricity is consumed under agreed postpaid terms, but the invoice says payable immediately: the credit result survives; invoice wording cannot erase prior acquisition.
5. The landlord restores its own unit under a prepaid lease's breach clause, versus a tenant buying move-out cleaning for later payment: the findings produce distinct acquisition outcomes and preserve direct federal analysis for the contractual claim.
6. A separately priced utility undertaking shares one demand with prepaid occupancy debt: the established covered component triggers applicable communication rules. An integrated package partly on deferred terms receives the broader underlying-transaction treatment.
7. A paid utility component disappears from the valid balance: past attempted collection is not erased. Future demand content and surviving transaction predicates must be evaluated anew.

All seven rechecks have an explicit legal result plus identifiable factual predicates. Missing contract, price, performance or payment evidence is an acquisition task, not a license to presume an exclusion. The findings retain separate collector-status tests for landlord/manager, outside collector and purchaser; this reopening review does not re-certify every unchanged actor authority in the older report.
