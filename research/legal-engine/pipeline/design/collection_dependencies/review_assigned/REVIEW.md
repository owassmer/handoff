## Consequential corrections and additions

1. **COVID rental-debt transfer bar is mandatory coverage, not antiwaiver boilerplate.** Parent independently caught this while this review was underway. CIV1788.64 is antiwaiver;1788.66 prohibits a person from selling/assigning the specified unpaid COVID rental debt, with March1,2020–September30,2021, rental-assistance eligibility and income predicates. Its participant language is broader than debt buyers. Being placed in the debt-buying title does not justify narrowing its express 'a person' language. Parent is following CCP1179.02 and federal assistance predicates. Recheck resulting branch before closure; I do not claim this dependency fully resolved by this report.

2. **Other evidence of agreement can suffice even where a signed agreement exists.** Published *Velocity Investments, LLC v. Nguyen*, C102846 (Aug28,2026), official PDF [source](https://courts.ca.gov/opinions/documents/C102846.PDF), pp6–8. A loan summary identifying borrower/account/date/amount, Truth in Lending disclosure, and transaction history of20payments together satisfied1788.52(b)'s access requirement. The court rejected the argument that only an executed agreement works when one exists. This is not a holding that any ledger or a generic blank agreement suffices: pp9–10 rejected a generic borrower agreement unlinked to the specific debtor/debt. Handoff should seek the executed agreement where available but evaluate adequate alternative agreement evidence, rather than unnecessarily surrendering recovery because one original document is missing. The holding interprets52(b), not directly14.5(b), whose drafting differs. Shared terminology makes it relevant reasoning, not a binding14.5 holding.

3. **Documentation obtained later does not erase prior violations.** *Nguyen* pp10–14: an amended complaint with missing attachments did not eliminate potential liability for the original filing; reasonable error-prevention procedures were factually disputed despite automation and attorney checklist. Access and filing attachment are separate duties. Agent/document automation must check the actual outgoing packet, not assume generating it or referring counsel completes it. This supports the initial memo's separation of communication-ready and litigation-ready packets.

4. **Final settlement statement also has30calendar-day deadline and required contents.** Initial summary made the exception sound broader:1788.54(c) expressly retains30days and incorporates(b)statement content. Settled balance must not be offered for onward sale; apply all credits and record finality before closing.

## 1788.52(c): late written record requests

The enacted text includes a request-consistency cross-reference to1692g. Its first two sentences set the15calendar-day supply/stop mechanism; third sentence imports consistency with federal validation requirements except where the title otherwise provides. The strongest text-based argument for a timing restriction is that1692g(a)(4)–(5),(b)condition written verification/original-creditor requests on the30-day window; treating the reference as meaningless is unsound. Conversely52(c)expressly covers requests for debt information/proof, the mandatory52(d)(1)notice offers records without a stated time cutoff, and the state title independently requires greater substantive records. These support caution about treating every postwindow request as a legal nullity. Distinguish record production under52 from immediate federal1692g(b)stop/verification mailing.

Independent retrieval of **all seven SB233 analyses** on official legislative bill-analysis page (analysis IDs256698,256209,256007,254200,253654,253235,251378) did not locate an explanation resolving the consistency clause's late-request scope. SenateJudiciary analysis253235 pp4,11 and AssemblyFloor256698 pp1,3 summarize15days on written request without a cutoff, but such summaries do not amend or negate the enacted cross-reference. Even a broad summary is not authoritative resolution of that particular question. Retrieval log records POST IDs and hashes.

Focused searches: combinations of `1788.52(c)`/`1788.52` with `untimely`, `late`, `thirty-day period`, `30-day validation`, `consistent`, `request`, `court`. Industry assertions of a categorical30day limit were found, but they are not adopted as authority. *Aragon v. DZR Group Inc.*,25-cv-0264-AGS-BLM, Apr10,2025 screening order pp5,7, is a primary district-court order mirrored [here](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/casdce/3:2025cv00264/804194/4/0.pdf?ts=1744358508). It dismissed verification claims where alleged letters asked only for cessation of contact and failed to allege a timely qualifying dispute/request. It cites52(c), but does not analyze its consistency clause, identify an otherwise proper late records request, or decide that state's standalone records right expires. Moreover its federal timing paraphrase refers to initial communication; operative1692g text measures the window from receipt of required notice. Do not import that paraphrase or elevate a screening disposition to controlling late-request authority.

**Conclusion:** no located controlling published decision resolves the precise scope after focused searches and full available SB233 legislative-analysis set. That is a documented research limit, not proof of settled law either way. The cross-reference materially supports the narrow interpretation, but does not warrant an unattended 'late: ignore' product branch. Recommended operating treatment: produce the already-required records free within15days for a written records request even when late, with a clearly labeled voluntarily applied response standard if timing-based legal coverage is uncertain. If incomplete, pause collection pending evidence/interpretation review; record whether the hold is legally compelled by a timely federal dispute, definitely applicable state duty, or this operational precaution. Do not describe the precaution as a judicially settled universal15day rule. If the operator seeks to continue despite a late unfulfilled request, that exact legal interpretation needs case-specific resolution; this review has not established an exemption.

Missing case facts remain separately ascertainable: exact request contents, writing, sender authority, actual receipt date, validation-notice receipt evidence, dates and identity of each collecting entity, previous sufficient responses, and concurrent holds. No notice-receipt evidence means the30day window cannot be declared expired merely from a send date.

## 1788.14.5: assignment and90day aging

Current(a),(b),(c),(e) repeatedly describe a collector to which delinquent debt has been assigned; (g)defines delinquent debt as covered nonmortgage debt at least90days past due and not charged off; (h)/(i) tie scope to sale/assignment dates. This text supports treating transfer of already-qualifying delinquent debt as the clear positive case. It does not expressly say how to handle assignment at day60 followed by continued collection at day100. No decisive published opinion or DFPI interpretation of that exact aging sequence was located through focused statute/90days/assignment/before-default/court searches.

The final SenateFloor analysis343329 (Aug31,2021, pp2–5) confirms Assembly amendment from30to90days, deletion of transfer-notice requirement, and purpose of addressing assigned accounts where creditor retained ownership. It neither expressly grandfathers every pre90day assignment nor expressly makes later aging trigger coverage. Earlier SenateJudiciary analysis, p10, describes assignment where original creditor retains ownership and collector controls collection decisions. It is evidence of placement scope, but its proposed30day threshold and separate creditor notice were not enacted. FinalFloor pp4–5 retains relevant placement rationale. Thus 'assignment' is not safely reduced to outright sale or title transfer; inspect the actual account placement/authority and subsequent transfers. A standing management contract alone neither establishes nor conclusively rules out the assignment of a particular delinquent account.

DFPI's *Saturn Systems, Inc.* consent order, signedFeb7,2025, pp3–6 [official](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Consent-Order-Saturn-Systems-Inc.pdf), addresses missing14.5(e)(1)first-written notices to two consumers afterJuly2022. It confirms real enforcement of notices; it does not supply account-aging or assignment dates sufficient to settle the disputed later-aging rule. A consent order is enforcement evidence, not a generally binding appellate interpretation.

**Operating recommendation:** clear positive afterdated qualifying assignment→apply14.5. Clear original-creditor collection with no assignment→do not fabricate14.5 coverage, but independently check other Rosenthal/federal duties. Earlier assignment later reaching90days→retain legal uncertainty and obtain placement/transfer records; satisfy document access, records request, and notice requirements as precaution while determining exact legal classification. Do not automatically treat the account as excluded or pretend its assignment date changed. If first communication preceded qualifying aging, do not falsely mark the original notice duty satisfied by later notice; record later notice as additional disclosure. Choosing to decline an otherwise feasible information request solely on earlier assignment requires a resolved legal position, not the absence of a located case.
