# Tenancy balances and collection-law applicability

Research finding for Goal 3, 2026-10-01. This is an investigated applicability analysis, not a deployed legal rule or a conclusion about an actual tenant account.

## Decision and boundary

Handoff must classify the underlying transaction, the particular balance component, the collection action, and the participant's actual conduct before choosing collection obligations. A residential lease is neither an automatic Rosenthal inclusion nor an automatic exclusion from all collection law. An outside collector is not a prerequisite for California coverage. Conversely, hiring an outside collector does not cure a missing California consumer-credit predicate.

Covered here: Civil Code 1788.2 consumer-credit/collector definitions; ordinary advance rent versus agreed credit; disputed debt and non-rent components; originator, property manager, separate collector, debt purchaser and creditor participation; temporal changes and contrary authorities. Separate parent investigations supply 1788.14.5/1788.50/1788.52 documents, 1788.17 federal incorporation and 1788.66 COVID rental transfer prohibitions. Licensing is distinguished from conduct coverage, not exhaustively compiled here. Rent validity, deposit deductions, local rules, bankruptcy, eviction and credit-reporting merits still require their own applicable law; absence of Rosenthal coverage does not approve those actions.

Acceptance evidence: operative statute captured; materially different California and federal predicates traced; actual holdings and authority weight checked; ordinary-rent contrary cases considered; source failures recorded rather than hidden; concrete branches below distinguish missing facts from interpretation. Independent review by parent remains required before Goal 3 closure.

## Current statute and temporal context

The live official [1788.2](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1788.2.) capture identifies Stats. 2025, ch. 200, sec. 3 (AB1521), effective January 1, 2026. See `sources/civ1788_2.txt`, operative text at lines 218–250. Subdivision (e) still requires property, services or money acquired **on credit**, by a natural person primarily for personal/family/household purposes; (f) covers sums owed **or alleged** owed by reason of that transaction. Subdivisions (b),(c),(l) connect covered debts and ordinary-course regular collection, for oneself or others. Subdivision (c) also expressly includes businesses composing/selling collection media. There is no general landlord exclusion or requirement to use an outside agency.

[SB187](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=201920200SB187), Stats. 2019 ch.545, removed the attorney exclusion and added express mortgage-debt inclusion; its express declaratory-of-existing-law clause concerns the mortgage amendment. Do not carry older attorney exclusions into post-January-2020 conduct or infer that the attorney change was also declaratory. Mortgage inclusion does not convert rent into mortgage debt. SB1286 expanded covered commercial debts for specified transactions from July 1, 2025; AB1521 subsequently changed the commercial definition/exclusions. These changes do not remove the consumer-credit requirement for household tenancy debt. A corporation leasing housing for business purposes requires a separate commercial analysis; do not classify it from the building's residential use alone.

[SB1324 (2022) official status](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billStatusClient.xhtml?bill_id=202120220SB1324) records it held in committee and later returned without further action. Its proposed express rental-debt inclusion is **not enacted law**. The official Senate Judiciary [analysis](https://sjud.senate.ca.gov/sites/sjud.senate.ca.gov/files/sb_1324_durazo_sjud_analysis.pdf), pp.4,8–10, describes the unresolved rent issue and contrary court reasoning; that is legislative analysis of a failed proposal, not an operative definition or judicial determination. Saved original PDF/text.

## What the cases establish

**Gouskos v. Aptos Village Garage, Inc., 94 Cal.App.4th 754, 759–760 (2001)** is published California appellate authority construing the Rosenthal credit predicate. It distinguished a consumer's acquisition on credit from a vendor merely performing work before receiving payment. The repaired car was never returned to the customer; no acquisition occurred. Do not invert this holding into either 'every unpaid bill is credit' or 'any later invoice is excluded.' Primary opinion reproduced at [FindLaw](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/ca-court-of-appeal/1072143.html), captured `gouskos_web.txt`, source lines L75–85. Official archive attempt returned 404; mirror is identified as such.

**Davidson v. Seterus, Inc., 21 Cal.App.5th 283, 296–297, 302–304 (2018)** is published California appellate authority. It interprets credit as acquisition without immediate payment with a promise of future payment, and rejects importing the federal default-at-acquisition limitation into California's broader collector definition. It concerns a mortgage servicer, not a rental holding. Primary opinion mirror [here](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2018/d071502.html); saved `davidson_web.txt` and `davidson_end.txt`. This supplies the rule for actual agreed tenant credit; applying it to a documented arrears-paid lease or separate financed service is a reasoned statutory application, not a claim of a tenancy-specific appellate holding.

**Paredes v. Credit Consulting Services, Inc., 82 Cal.App.5th 410 (2022)**, published Court of Appeal, confirms that ordinary services can be acquired on credit without a formal loan agreement. The patient received treatment on agreed deferred insurance payment terms, remaining responsible if insurance wholly denied payment. The court found sufficient evidence of the credit predicate at anti-SLAPP's prima-facie stage. It did not decide final debt validity or make all bills credit. Source: court opinion in the [Supreme Court petition's Appendix B, pp.34a–38a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/22/22-988/263020/20230407094343706_CREDIT%20CONSULTING%20SERVICES%20INC_Petition.pdf), saved original `paredes_petition_appendix.pdf`; use the appended **opinion**, not petitioner's arguments. This is consequential contrary evidence to any rule requiring interest, financing fees, special consideration, or a formal credit instrument before Rosenthal can apply.

**Ordinary advance-rent line:** *Yatooma v. OP Property Management LP*, 2017 WL 3120259, pp.5–6 of July 20, 2017 order, follows *Phillips v. Archstone Simi Valley LLC*, 2016 WL 7444550, and *Leasure v. Willmark Communities*, 2013 WL 6097944. Monthly advance-rent transactions did not confer credit; later nonpayment did not retroactively create it. *Yatooma* also rejected a unilateral few-day grace period on its facts. Primary [Yatooma order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/cacdce/2%3A2017cv02645/674922/27/), captured `yatooma_web.txt`, L66–74. These federal district decisions are persuasive, not controlling California appellate holdings. *Picazo v. Kimball, Tirey & St. John*, 2018 order pp.12–13, applies the same distinction even to a subsidized tenancy with renewal rights; such rights are not themselves an extension of credit. Its source was reviewed online; raw download failed (403).

Two citation traps matter. First, the [official 2018 Phillips Ninth Circuit memorandum](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2018/10/25/17-55854.pdf), saved original, is expressly nonprecedential and resolves FDCPA limitations and FCRA issues. It does **not** adopt a Rosenthal rental-debt holding; describing the rent rule as Ninth Circuit law because the judgment was affirmed is inaccurate. Second, *Ortiz v. Lyon Management Group*, 157 Cal.App.4th 604, 618–619 (2007), discusses apartment credit in an ICRAA/CCRAA tenant-screening case. *Connor v. First Student*, 5 Cal.5th 1026 (2018), disapproved its incompatible-statute/vagueness result. [Connor opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/2018/s229428.html), saved `connor_end.txt`, L112. That disapproval is not a new holding that rent is consumer credit, but Ortiz should not be presented as controlling Rosenthal rent authority. Nor is unpublished *Sanai* a precedential state-law answer.

**Collector participation:** *Olson v. La Jolla Neurological Associates*, 85 Cal.App.5th 723, 734–739 (2022), is published California appellate authority. It distinguishes personal regular participation from a creditor exclusively retaining an unaffiliated billing company. It preserves single-economic-enterprise analysis, and leaves agency liability possible where the right to control collection manner and means is shown; mere basic billing information was insufficient on that record. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/court-of-appeal/2022/d079265.html); `olson_web.txt` and `olson_end.txt`, L102–137. Neither creditor status alone nor outsourcing alone settles all liability. This matters to Handoff: actual automated communications, instructions, control and corporate relationships must be examined rather than assigning obligations by job title.

## California and direct federal coverage must be separate

The current official [15 USC1692a](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title15-section1692a&num=0&edition=prelim), saved `fdcpa_current.html/.txt`, does not add California's 'on credit' words to household transaction debt. It separately defines collectors, exclusions and creditor false-name conduct. A disputed amount remains an alleged obligation; proving it valid is not a prerequisite to regulation of collection.

**De Dios v. International Realty & Investments, 641 F.3d 1071, 1074–1075 (9th Cir.2011)** is published binding federal circuit authority in this circuit. The property manager obtained the right to collect before contractual default, and qualified for 1692a(6)(F)(iii). The court expressly did not decide whether the manager satisfied the initial collector definition. Default turns on contracts and governing law; it is not automatically a number of days past invoice. The stipulated forbearance mattered. Saved [official opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2011/04/11/08-56288.pdf). Do not extrapolate to a new agency retained only after default, or import this exclusion into California's collector definition.

**Reichert v. National Credit Systems, 531 F.3d 1002, 1005–1007 (9th Cir.2008)** applies FDCPA to an apartment collection account and an unauthorized attorney-fee addition. Prior accurate creditor submissions and a conclusory procedure affidavit did not establish the bona-fide-error defense. Saved [official opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2008/07/07/0615503.pdf). Operationally, validate each ledger component and its legal/contract basis before forwarding it; outsourcing does not make an unsupported fee recoverable.

**Henson v. Santander, 582 U.S.79 (2017)** holds that buying defaulted debts for one's own account does not satisfy the 'owed another' limb merely because they were previously owed to another; it expressly does not resolve the principal-purpose limb. [Official opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/16pdf/16-349_c07d.pdf). **McAdory v. M.N.S. & Associates, 952 F.3d1089 (9th Cir.2020)** holds that a purchaser whose principal business purpose is debt collection can qualify even if it hires others for debtor contact. [Official opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2020/03/09/18-35923.pdf). Both saved. Thus neither 'owns the debt' nor 'outsources contact' is a complete federal exclusion. California debt-buyer documentation is a separate statutory test handled by the parent.

Licensing is a separate issue. Official [Financial Code100001](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=FIN&sectionNum=100001.) includes exclusions for specified licensees, including real-estate licensees; that does not repeal Rosenthal conduct duties. DFPI's indexed official rulemaking page currently describes PRO05/21 third text as a **prefiling draft** with comments due December12,2025. Direct page/PDF downloads returned 403. Do not use its proposed residential-rent exclusions as adopted regulations. Licensing compilation/adoption certification is excluded from this substantive applicability deliverable, and cannot be claimed complete from search-index text.

The remaining case facts are conditional inputs, not missing legal research. Genuine legal uncertainty is narrower: no located controlling California rental-specific opinion decides every later payment-plan or mixed damage/reimbursement arrangement. The general statute and published credit-acquisition decisions still supply the governing test. Handoff should investigate and explain the actual arrangement, preserve legitimate collection, and avoid claiming either a blanket landlord exemption or automatic statutory coverage.

## Adversarial checks and search limits

1. $8,000 advance-rent arrears, in-house manager: no automatic credit because large/unpaid; no automatic Rosenthal because regular manager. Test both predicates.
2. Same balance sent to qualifying outside collector after default: direct FDCPA may apply although Rosenthal credit analysis did not change.
3. Same collector engaged before default: De Dios exclusion may apply federally; it does not exclude a separate California credit transaction.
4. Owner funds a tenant-requested service repayable later: evaluate genuine credit even if ordinary monthly rent remains outside the persuasive rental line.
5. $0 valid balance but $3,000 alleged demand: alleged debt language prevents an invalidity-based escape from applicable collection law.
6. No interest, insurance/third-party expected payment: Paredes defeats an automatic no-credit result.
7. Debt buyer outsources everything: do not miss McAdory principal-purpose coverage or potential Olson/Cavalry enterprise facts.
8. Ordinary advance rent accrued during protected COVID period: independent 1788.66 transfer prohibition must still be evaluated.
