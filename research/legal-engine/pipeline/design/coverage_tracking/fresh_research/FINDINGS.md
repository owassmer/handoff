# Fresh investigation: two-day recovery of left-behind property

Investigated October 1, 2026. Independent fresh official-source reads for a bounded Handoff decision: an ordinary California residential tenancy has ended, the tenant has vacated, and the former tenant reclaims personal belongings promptly. This is a storage-fee decision, not authorization to dispose of property or a complete abandoned-property workflow. No prior research captures or tracker state were reused or edited.

## Resolved fee decision

**Two-day recovery does not create a universal storage-fee waiver. Select the actual statutory return route first.** Chapter 5 provides the early-recovery exceptions below, but Civil Code 1965 supplies a separate residential return-on-written-request route with its own actual-cost rules. CCP1174(h) addresses the alternatives after enforcement of possession. The first version missed these necessary sources; this revision incorporates independent review and preserves the first version under `history/before_independent_review/`.

**Under Chapter 5, do not charge storage when the former tenant reclaims the property within the statutory two-day period and it remained in the dwelling.** Civil Code [1987(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1987.) overrides subdivision (a)'s ordinary storage-payment condition and requires release to the former tenant. The text also recognizes reclamation by a person the landlord reasonably believes owns the property as a trigger; it does not simply substitute any claimant for the former tenant named as the recipient of mandatory release and fee protection.

**Moving belongings out of the dwelling but keeping them on the premises does not necessarily permit a charge.** Independently, [1990(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1990.) prohibits assessment when the former tenant reclaims property stored on the premises within two days of vacating. Under [1980(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1980.), premises include associated common areas. Thus moving boxes from the apartment to an associated common storage room does not defeat this former-tenant fee bar. This conclusion applies the enacted language together; it is not a claim that a located court decision adjudicated that precise example.

For legitimately off-premises storage, these location-dependent exceptions do not supply a categorical two-day waiver. Release and reasonable costs remain governed by 1987(a) and 1990(a)–(b); safekeeping and reasonable care by [1986](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1986.). Failure of an exception is not itself proof that a particular expense is reasonable or recoverable. There is no general two-day free-storage allowance for a tenant who waits beyond the qualifying period: a later otherwise proper claim can involve the reasonable storage cost for the whole storage term.

## Separate residential surrender route: Civil Code 1965

[Current official Civil Code 1965](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1965.) supplies a mandatory residential surrender route when its conditions are met; it is not an unspecified possible alternative. It does not require that legal termination already be established, as distinct from the tenant having vacated. The tenant or authorized representative must submit a written request within 18 days of vacancy identifying the property and providing the tenant's mailing address; the landlord must then possess or control the property. If payment is demanded, the landlord must mail or personally present a written itemized cost demand within five days after actual receipt of the request, unless the property is returned first. Where costs are properly demanded, the tenant tenders those costs and agrees to reasonable mutually arranged pickup no later than72hours after tender. No demand or no permissible cost means no invented payment prerequisite;1965(e) separately supplies a three-day presumption of reasonable time for its liability analysis, subject to contrary evidence and the stated tender/pickup conditions. A landlord may not condition surrender on an untimely or unitemized demand under this route.

The fee definition in 1965(b) permits reasonable removal expense actually incurred and/or the reasonable value of actual removal labor, and actual reasonable storage costs capped at the fair rental value of the space reasonably needed. **A qualifying 1965-only surrender can therefore involve permissible actual costs even within two days; that section contains no two-day exemption.** This does not establish that any particular in-unit storage cost was incurred. No reasonable storage cost actually incurred means no storage charge under this provision. Do not substitute a full-apartment daily rate, a fabricated expense, or opportunity-cost assertion for evidence of a cost permitted by this section. Returning property before making the demand does not preserve a statutory payment-before-surrender condition.

Why not import 1990(c) universally? Section1990(a) frames cost assessment under its own chapter, while 1965(b) separately defines costs and 1965(c) expressly makes its chapter unavailable once Chapter5 disposition has been initiated or completed. AB2521 amended 1987/1990 but did not amend1965. Reading those provisions together preserves the separate statutory methods rather than exporting one exception into the other method. A contrary policy argument favors free quick recovery under every method; the route-specific structure and enacted scope are stronger than that generalization. This is a reasoned statutory interpretation, not an asserted appellate holding.

**Route identity must be established from actual actions and dates.** A documented Chapter5 notice under1983 is evidence that its procedure was initiated; mere custody of left-behind property does not alone establish that, because1965 itself presupposes custody. Once the1965(c) exclusion applies, use the applicable Chapter5 rules rather than retroactively asserting1965 to charge a fee. Likewise, a landlord cannot defeat an already-operative1965 surrender obligation by later relabeling its actions or withholding release:1965(e) supplies liability for violating that obligation, including actual-damages/bad-faith/fee provisions. This limited interpretation follows the timing and structure of1965 and the no-own-wrong maxim; it is not permission to choose whichever fee is larger. The section also excludes1940(b) occupancies and preserves other remedies in1965(f).

For a two-day **request** without completed pickup,1965 can require arranging surrender under its stated timing and cost conditions. That written request is not automatically the same factual event as timely reclamation for the Chapter5 fee exception. Establish request, tender, agreed pickup and access separately.

## Post-writ property: CCP1174 interface

[CCP1174(f)–(m)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=1174.) must not be omitted merely because the main question names a Civil Code chapter. Subdivision(h) provides release pursuant to1965 **or** release to the tenant/eligible owner with storage costs as provided in1990 and claim by the applicable writ/notice date. Accordingly the1990 route carries its on-premises two-day fee bar, while a valid1965 route has the requirements just described. The word “or” does not erase1965(c)'s route exclusion. Establish the writ's actual terms, possession/vacancy date, notice history and which statutory procedure was initiated. The no-charge calculation still turns on the tenant vacating; the writ's final claim date is a different limit.

Subdivision(m) expressly incorporates1980's owner/premises/reasonable-belief definitions, so the common-area analysis is not lost in this post-writ route. Subdivisions(g),(j) separately govern safekeeping and release immunity. Only this property-release/fee interface is resolved here; eviction judgment validity and the full enforcement process are not compiled.

## Exact predicates to establish

| Predicate | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Actual route and vacancy/termination | Notice/procedure initiation, written tenant request and writ records. Termination plus vacancy matter under1981(a)/1983(a);1965 requires vacancy and its request predicates. Discovery of belongings is not the vacancy event. |
| Property/tenancy falls within this statutory route | Ordinary household belongings in an ordinary residential dwelling, with no applicable special-property exclusion. 1980.5, 1954.26(d)(1) and 1940 complete the residential/commercial distinction. For Chapter5,1981 excludes specified special regimes, utility-owned equipment, manufactured/mobile homes and their contents, and certain animals. Those chapter-specific exclusions are not silently imported into1965, which has its own1965(c) scope conditions. |
| Claimant and recipient identified | Former-tenant identity; if another owner is involved, facts supporting 1980(b),(d) ownership/reasonable belief. Do not infer all other-owner rights are identical: 1990(c)'s early on-premises fee bar expressly names the former tenant. |
| Where belongings remained or were stored | Dated photographs, inventory and movement/storage record. Distinguish dwelling, associated premises/common areas and genuinely off-site storage. |
| Timely reclamation | Vacancy date, actual recovery or timely attempted recovery, pickup/access records and communications. The clock starts from vacating, not from notice service, invoice, discovery or termination if that occurred at a different time. |
| Notice/property identification | Chapter notice and described property under 1983/1987. The ordinary notice deadline of at least 15 days after personal delivery or 18 after mailing is distinct from the early fee exception. The sample notices in 1984/1985 use an early date and a later disposal date. |

Read “reclaims” as taking back the property in the ordinary unobstructed case, rather than merely declaring an intention to pick it up at some indefinite later date. This follows the physical recovery structure of 1987(a), the distinction between claim and taking possession in the notices, and the early-return purpose of AB2521. A prompt inquiry alone does not establish that recovery happened. Conversely, a landlord cannot wrongfully refuse a timely ready-and-able pickup requested for a reasonable time and then rely on the resulting delay to manufacture a fee; applying [Civil Code 3517](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3517.) supports that result. Record whether the landlord wrongfully prevented reasonable recovery instead of treating missing handover evidence as tenant delay; refusing an unreasonable pickup time, such as an unagreed2a.m. demand, is not automatically wrongful obstruction. These are statutory applications, not located reclamation-specific holdings.

No notice-omission loophole is adopted. A landlord cannot establish a Chapter5 storage charge merely by withholding its required notice. Section1990(c) supplies that route’s fee-assessment restriction;1987/1983 supply its notice/release structure. If1965 instead applies, its actual-cost, written-demand and surrender conditions govern. Chapter5 is optional and1981(e) preserves other rights/liabilities, but that savings clause itself authorizes no fee. A contractual/common-law theory outside both resolved statutory routes is not approved by this investigation.

## Time computation

“Two days” is not a hard-coded 48-hour stopwatch or automatically two business days. [Civil Code 10](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=10.) excludes the first day and includes the last, subject to holidays. [CCP 12a(a)–(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=12a.) expressly extends statutory periods across codes when the last day is a holiday, including Saturday. Apply the Civil Code 7/Government Code 6700–6701 holiday rules and the additional 12a/135 rules; intermediate weekends are not simply deleted. CCP12b's public-office closure rule concerns business of that office and does not let a private landlord's closure policy redefine this deadline.

Example selected under these general rules: vacancy on an ordinary Tuesday gives Wednesday day one and Thursday day two. If the second day falls on Saturday, the final day extends to the next nonholiday, ordinarily Monday. An actual date calculation must check statutory/officially appointed holidays and applicable observance, including the current versions of GOV6700 and CCP135. No actual tenancy date was supplied here. The application of general statutory timing rules is explicit legal reasoning, not an asserted two-day-property appellate holding.

## Strongest contrary reading and resolution

The argument for charging after any move out of the dwelling relies on 1987(c)'s narrow wording and the [May 2012 Assembly Judiciary analysis](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/asm/ab_2501-2550/ab_2521_cfa_20120430_124215_asm_comm.html), which describes moved-out property as potentially chargeable. The later Senate summary also emphasizes dwelling retention. Neither summary overrides the broader fee-assessment text enacted in 1990(c), together with the preexisting common-area definition. Give both operative sections effect: 1987(c) specifies mandatory release and former-tenant payment protection; 1990(c) independently bars the stated on-premises cost assessment. Off-site storage remains distinguishable.

A second argument treats the chapter's exclusions as removing every consequence. That is demonstrably too broad: [1989(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1989.) expressly overrides 1981(c) for the landlord's release-to-former-tenant immunity. This exception preserves that particular consequence; it does not import 1987's fee waiver into every mobilehome matter. Handoff must retain the provision-level override even when the general chapter route is excluded. The ordinary residential belongings assumed here do not trigger that exclusion.
