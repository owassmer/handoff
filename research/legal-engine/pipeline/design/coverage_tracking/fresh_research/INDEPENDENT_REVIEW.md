## Material defect found and closed

The original chapter-centered enumeration omitted Civil Code 1965 and CCP1174(h). The question did not stipulate that Chapter5 disposition had already begun. Deferring unspecified alternative rights was therefore not enough: directly available statutory authority could change the answer. I independently recovered and read both official sections, reported the omission, and rejected the original unconditional two-day fee-waiver conclusion. The author preserved the original artifacts and revised the legal conclusion, not the question to evade the defect.

The correction is accepted. [Civil Code 1965](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1965.&lawCode=CIV) requires surrender on its own predicates: timely written request with description/address, landlord possession/control when received, timely itemized written cost demand if payment is required, permissible tender, and reasonable agreed pickup within 72 hours after tender. Subdivision(b) addresses actual reasonable removal/storage expense, including actual removal labor, and caps storage by fair rental value of needed space. It contains no categorical two-day waiver. No demand or no permissible actual cost does not create a fictional payment prerequisite. Subdivision(e)'s three-day presumption concerns reasonable surrender time for liability; it is neither Chapter5's two-day fee clock nor a universal 72-hour rule independent of tender.

The strongest competing reading would export 1990(c)'s fee prohibition to every residential surrender. I reject that reading for this bounded decision: 1990(a) situates cost assessment in its chapter, 1965 supplies a separate cost definition, and 1965(c) excludes an initiated/completed Chapter5 disposition. AB2521 did not amend1965. This is statutory synthesis, not a represented appellate holding. Actual initiation history controls; custody alone cannot establish Chapter5 initiation because1965 itself presupposes custody. A landlord cannot use a retrospective label to evade an already operative surrender duty. The corrected report properly requires facts and does not authorize choosing the larger charge.

[CCP1174(h)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1174.&lawCode=CCP) expressly supplies surrender under1965 or the release alternative using storage costs under1990. The latter incorporates1990(c)'s limitation; the former remains subject to1965(c). Subdivision(f)'s notice exception independently confirms the relevance of the1965 route after possession enforcement. Subdivision(m) preserves the1980 definitions for the specified property provisions. No conclusion about eviction judgment validity is needed to resolve this fee interface.

## Other substantive challenges resolved

**Location and competing legislative history.** Independently read1987(a)–(c),1990(a)–(c),1980 definitions, and the full enclosing chapter. Section1987(c)'s dwelling condition does not exhaust the fee question:1990(c) separately reaches on-premises storage;1980(c) includes associated common areas. Moving boxes to an associated common room therefore does not necessarily remove the former tenant's early fee protection. The May2012 Assembly discussion and August2012 Senate summary emphasize the dwelling and supply the strongest contrary narrowing argument. The enacted1990(c) text controls that conflict. Genuine off-site storage differs, but expense reasonableness and1986 safekeeping remain independent requirements. No general deduction of two free days follows when reclamation occurs late.

**People and notice.** Section1987(c) recognizes reclamation by a reasonably believed owner in its trigger but names the former tenant as mandatory recipient and protected fee debtor. Section1990(c) expressly names the former tenant. The analysis does not erase that asymmetry or promise identical rights to every owner. Sections1983–1985 distinguish notice deadlines and identified property from the vacancy-based two-day period. Omitted notice does not itself establish a right to charge; neither1981(e) nor1965(f) independently authorizes a fee.

**Timing and access.** Read CIV10/7, GOV6700/6701, CCP12a/135/12b and CIV11. CCP12a(b) expressly reaches other codes; its Saturday/final-holiday extension is not confined to court filings. Excluding the vacancy day and extending a final Saturday is supported; discarding intervening weekends or treating two days as exactly48 hours is not. No actual tenancy date was supplied. Current holiday versions and appointed holidays still require case-date application. A written request, readiness for reasonable pickup and physical recovery are different facts. CIV3517 supports rejecting manufactured delay from wrongful landlord obstruction; it does not make refusal of an unreasonable, unagreed2a.m. pickup wrongful. The report accurately labels these applications as reasoning rather than located case holdings.

**Scope and time versions.** Read1981's optional procedure and exclusions,1980.5,1954.26(d),1940, and1989(a)'s express override of1981(c) for release immunity. An exclusion cannot erase that particular override or export all Chapter5 benefits to excluded mobilehome matters. Conversely, Chapter5 exclusions cannot silently become1965 exclusions. Checked AB2521 chaptered §§7/9 and double-jointing §§10/11: the latter address1946.1/1950.5, not these fee rules. The2013 fee effective date,2025 scope amendment,2026 holiday amendments,1988 addition of1965 and1994 effective version of1174 are correctly distinguished from retrieval dates.
