# Fresh investigation: two-day recovery of left-behind property

Investigated October 1, 2026. Independent fresh official-source reads for a bounded Handoff decision: an ordinary California residential tenancy has ended, the tenant has vacated, and the former tenant reclaims personal belongings promptly. This is a storage-fee decision, not authorization to dispose of property or a complete abandoned-property workflow. No prior research captures or tracker state were reused or edited.

## Resolved fee decision

**Do not charge storage when the former tenant reclaims the property within the statutory two-day period and it remained in the dwelling.** Civil Code [1987(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1987.) overrides subdivision (a)'s ordinary storage-payment condition and requires release to the former tenant. The text also recognizes reclamation by a person the landlord reasonably believes owns the property as a trigger; it does not simply substitute any claimant for the former tenant named as the recipient of mandatory release and fee protection.

**Moving belongings out of the dwelling but keeping them on the premises does not necessarily permit a charge.** Independently, [1990(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1990.) prohibits assessment when the former tenant reclaims property stored on the premises within two days of vacating. Under [1980(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1980.), premises include associated common areas. Thus moving boxes from the apartment to an associated common storage room does not defeat this former-tenant fee bar. This conclusion applies the enacted language together; it is not a claim that a located court decision adjudicated that precise example.

For legitimately off-premises storage, these location-dependent exceptions do not supply a categorical two-day waiver. Release and reasonable costs remain governed by 1987(a) and 1990(a)–(b); safekeeping and reasonable care by [1986](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1986.). Failure of an exception is not itself proof that a particular expense is reasonable or recoverable. There is no general two-day free-storage allowance for a tenant who waits beyond the qualifying period: a later otherwise proper claim can involve the reasonable storage cost for the whole storage term.

## Exact predicates to establish

| Predicate | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Residential tenancy actually terminated and tenant actually vacated | Lease/end-of-tenancy and surrender records. Both conditions matter under 1981(a)/1983(a); discovery of belongings is not the vacancy event. |
| Property/tenancy falls within this statutory route | Ordinary household belongings in an ordinary residential dwelling, with no applicable special-property exclusion. 1980.5, 1954.26(d)(1) and 1940 complete the residential/commercial distinction. 1981 excludes specified special regimes, utility-owned equipment, manufactured/mobile homes and their contents, and certain animals. |
| Claimant and recipient identified | Former-tenant identity; if another owner is involved, facts supporting 1980(b),(d) ownership/reasonable belief. Do not infer all other-owner rights are identical: 1990(c)'s early on-premises fee bar expressly names the former tenant. |
| Where belongings remained or were stored | Dated photographs, inventory and movement/storage record. Distinguish dwelling, associated premises/common areas and genuinely off-site storage. |
| Timely reclamation | Vacancy date, actual recovery or timely attempted recovery, pickup/access records and communications. The clock starts from vacating, not from notice service, invoice, discovery or termination if that occurred at a different time. |
| Notice/property identification | Chapter notice and described property under 1983/1987. The ordinary notice deadline of at least 15 days after personal delivery or 18 after mailing is distinct from the early fee exception. The sample notices in 1984/1985 use an early date and a later disposal date. |

Read “reclaims” as taking back the property in the ordinary unobstructed case, rather than merely declaring an intention to pick it up at some indefinite later date. This follows the physical recovery structure of 1987(a), the distinction between claim and taking possession in the notices, and the early-return purpose of AB2521. A prompt inquiry alone does not establish that recovery happened. Conversely, a landlord cannot refuse a timely ready-and-able pickup and then rely on the resulting delay to manufacture a fee; applying [Civil Code 3517](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=3517.) supports that result. Record whether the landlord prevented recovery instead of treating missing handover evidence as tenant delay. These are statutory applications, not located reclamation-specific holdings.

No notice-omission loophole is adopted. A landlord cannot establish a chapter storage charge merely by withholding its required notice. Section 1990(c) supplies the fee-assessment restriction; 1987 and 1983 supply the ordinary notice/release structure. Chapter 5 is an optional procedure and 1981(e) preserves rights/liabilities outside unsatisfied chapter requirements. Consequently this investigation does not approve an alternative contractual or common-law fee if the landlord says it did not use the chapter: that asserted alternative requires its own authority and facts.

## Time computation

“Two days” is not a hard-coded 48-hour stopwatch or automatically two business days. [Civil Code 10](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=10.) excludes the first day and includes the last, subject to holidays. [CCP 12a(a)–(b)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CCP&sectionNum=12a.) expressly extends statutory periods across codes when the last day is a holiday, including Saturday. Apply the Civil Code 7/Government Code 6700–6701 holiday rules and the additional 12a/135 rules; intermediate weekends are not simply deleted. CCP12b's public-office closure rule concerns business of that office and does not let a private landlord's closure policy redefine this deadline.

Example selected under these general rules: vacancy on an ordinary Tuesday gives Wednesday day one and Thursday day two. If the second day falls on Saturday, the final day extends to the next nonholiday, ordinarily Monday. An actual date calculation must check statutory/officially appointed holidays and applicable observance, including the current versions of GOV6700 and CCP135. No actual tenancy date was supplied here. The application of general statutory timing rules is explicit legal reasoning, not an asserted two-day-property appellate holding.

## Strongest contrary reading and resolution

The argument for charging after any move out of the dwelling relies on 1987(c)'s narrow wording and the [May 2012 Assembly Judiciary analysis](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/asm/ab_2501-2550/ab_2521_cfa_20120430_124215_asm_comm.html), which describes moved-out property as potentially chargeable. The later Senate summary also emphasizes dwelling retention. Neither summary overrides the broader fee-assessment text enacted in 1990(c), together with the preexisting common-area definition. Give both operative sections effect: 1987(c) specifies mandatory release and former-tenant payment protection; 1990(c) independently bars the stated on-premises cost assessment. Off-site storage remains distinguishable.

A second argument treats the chapter's exclusions as removing every consequence. That is demonstrably too broad: [1989(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1989.) expressly overrides 1981(c) for the landlord's release-to-former-tenant immunity. This exception preserves that particular consequence; it does not import 1987's fee waiver into every mobilehome matter. Handoff must retain the provision-level override even when the general chapter route is excluded. The ordinary residential belongings assumed here do not trigger that exclusion.
