### Legal Assistance

**Finding Assistance**

- NYC Tenant Resource Portal offers an [Eviction Prevention Tool](https://www.nyc.gov/content/tenantresourceportal/pages/) to help renters navigate public and private resources that can stabilize their housing situation. A tenant seeking help will be taken through a series of questions and directed to the most useful resources based on their responses.
- The [Right to Counsel NYC Coalition](https://www.evictionfreenyc.org/en-us/) offers resources on how to respond to an eviction notice and connect with available resources.
- NYC Dept. of Homeless Services (DHS) has information for tenants at-risk of being evicted from their apartment in both their [Rent Issues](https://www1.nyc.gov/site/dhs/prevention/rent-issues.page) and [Rental Assistance](https://www1.nyc.gov/site/dhs/permanency/rental-assistance.page) sections of their website.
- DHS also offers information for adults and families [seeking shelter](http://www1.nyc.gov/site/dhs/shelter/shelter.page), as well as information for people needing housing due to issues such as [domestic violence](https://www1.nyc.gov/site/dhs/prevention/domestic-violence.page). DHS also runs the [Homebase program](http://www1.nyc.gov/site/dhs/prevention/homebase.page), which offers an extensive network of neighborhood-based services.
- [LawHelp NY](https://www.lawhelpny.org/) is a web site with links to free and affordable legal aid and information about your legal rights, courts, and more in New York and elsewhere.
- NYC’s Human Resources Administration (HRA) offers a “[One Shot Deal](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01104)” emergency assistance program that helps people who cannot meet an expense due to an unexpected situation or event. Additional information available from [Housing Court Answers](http://housingcourtanswers.org/answers/for-tenants/housing-court-tenants/rent-arrears-tenants/one-shot-deals/) and [Mobilization for Justice](http://mobilizationforjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-get-an-emergency-grant-Housing-March-2016-2.pdf).
- HRA’s [Office of Civil Justice provides free legal representation, advice and other legal assistance](https://www1.nyc.gov/site/hra/help/legal-services-for-tenants.page) to NYC tenants, regardless of immigration status, facing eviction, harassment, disrepair and other housing-related issues.
- HRA also offers [rental assistance resources](https://www1.nyc.gov/site/hra/help/rental-assistance.page).
- Mobilization for Justice offers [anti-eviction programs](http://mobilizationforjustice.org/projects/housing/) for low-income tenants, seniors and SRO residents. They also offer an array of information, including [Fact Sheets](http://mobilizationforjustice.org/get-help/fact-sheets-and-self-help-guides/) in English, Spanish and Chinese.
- Tenant organizations such as [Tenants and Neighbors](https://tandn.org/contact-us/) (212-608-4320), the [Met Council on Housing](https://www.metcouncilonhousing.org/help-answers/) (212-979-0611) and [JustFix.nyc](https://www.justfix.nyc/en/) offer information and housing resources.
- [Coalition for the Homeless](http://www.coalitionforthehomeless.org/get-help/) offers assistance for those being evicted; fleeing domestic violence; needing shelter (or dealing with problems in a shelter); and related issues.
- Tenants facing harassment by their landlords can obtain information about free or low-cost legal assistance on NYC’s [Tenant Harassment Assistance page](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01042).
- The [NYC Commission on Human Rights](https://www.nyc.gov/site/cchr/index.page) handles issues and complaints related to discrimination in housing. The NYC Human Rights Law prohibits discrimination on the basis of several protected categories, listed on their website.
- The NYS Attorney General offers an [Immigrant Tenants Rights FAQ](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/immigration_tenants_rights_web.pdf?utm_source=Open+Newsroom+Rent+Updates&utm_campaign=4550b1589f-Rent+update+issue+3_COPY_01&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_bde3f24ce8-4550b1589f-24027292) on their website.
- The New York Public Library offers links to a wide array of housing resources on such topics as affordable housing, homelessness, senior living, legal advice and community organization support, on their [Housing & Tenants Rights Resources webpage](https://www.nypl.org/about/remote-resources/community-resources/housing-tenants-resources?mc_cid=40b49d248b&mc_eid=275bc4fe8d).
- The website [JustFix.nyc](https://www.justfix.nyc/en/learn/) (also in [Spanish](https://www.justfix.nyc/es/learn)) answers questions about tenants rights in NYC on such topics as eviction, repairs, charges and discrimination.

  - [New York Legal Assistance Group](https://www.nylag.org/tenants-rights/) 212-613-5000
  - [Urban Justice Center](https://snp.urbanjustice.org/housing) 646-602-5600
  - [Mobilization for Justice](https://mobilizationforjustice.org/get-help/how-to-get-help/tenants-housing/) 212-417-3700
  - [Housing Court Answers](https://housingcourtanswers.org/contact-us/) 212-962-4795
- If you need a referral to a lawyer and you are not eligible for free legal services, contact the New York City Bar Legal Referral Service at 212-626-7373. The [NYC Bar Legal Referral Service](http://www.nycbar.org/get-legal-help/) offers a lawyer or paralegal who will answer the phone and help you find an appropriate way to handle your legal problem.

They also offer [free legal advice](http://www.nycbar.org/get-legal-help/our-services/monday-night-law/) one evening a week on specific issues, including landlord-tenant matters, as well as a [free advice hotline](http://www.nycbar.org/for-the-public/free-legal-services) for low-income NYC residents. The number is 212-626-7383.

### Security Deposits

Detailed information can be found in our [Security Deposits FAQ](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/security-deposits/).

According to the [Housing Stability And Tenant Protections Act of 2019](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/resources/rent-regulation-laws/rent-laws-of-2019/):

> Within fourteen days after the tenant has vacated the premises, the landlord shall provide the tenant with an itemized statement indicating the basis for the amount of the deposit retained, if any, and shall return any remaining portion of the deposit to the tenant. If a landlord fails to provide the tenant with the statement and deposit within fourteen days, the landlord shall forfeit any right to retain any portion of the deposit.

However, the aforementioned 14-day requirement does not apply to rent regulated tenants.

Ultimately, the return of your deposit would be decided by the [NYS Attorney General](https://ag.ny.gov/consumer-frauds/housing-issues) or a Small Claims Court judge. If you feel you have exhausted attempts to get your deposit returned through your former landlord, you may wish to file a complaint, available in 3 versions:

- [Online Form](https://formsnym.ag.ny.gov/OAGOnlineSubmissionForm/faces/OAGRSHome)
- [Printable pdf in English](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/nyc-rent-security-complaint-english.pdf)
- [Printable pdf in Spanish](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/nyc-cfb-rent-security-complaint-form-spanish.pdf)
You may also choose to file a claim in Small Claims Court. For more information on Small Claims Court in New York City, see the Small Claims Court section below.

### Small Claims Court

The Civil Court of the City of New York has jurisdiction over civil cases involving amounts up to $25,000 and other civil matters referred to it by the Supreme Court. It includes a small claims part for informal dispositions of matters not exceeding $10,000 in NYC and $5,000 elsewhere in NYS and a landlord and tenant/housing part for landlord-tenant matters of unlimited amounts and housing code violations. Information on Small Claims Court can be found on their website and in their [Guide to Small Claims](https://www.nycourts.gov/new-york-city-small-claims-court/starting-case-nyc-small-claims-court).

Assistance and advice navigating Small Claims Court is also available from the [NYPIRG Small Claims Court Action Center](https://www.nypirg.org/sccac/) and their Consumer Helpline at 800-566-5020.

### Mediation

The Rent Guidelines Board receives many emails and calls about tenant-landlord disputes. Sometimes, tenants and landlords are too quick to file a complaint with a government agency or sue in court before trying to work with the other party. This can be unproductive for both sides. One solution to [tenant-landlord disputes is mediation](http://www.nycbar.org/for-the-public/alternative-dispute-resolution-adr).

If you have a problem with your apartment, keep the following things in mind:

- Understand the law – Don’t automatically assume that the other party is at fault. As a tenant, you need to be aware of your responsibilities under the law; also keep in mind that landlords are only required to provide the services explicitly stated in your lease and under the housing laws. If your apartment is rent stabilized, you have [additional rights and responsibilities](https://hcr.ny.gov/tenants).
- Communication is important – Make sure that your landlord knows about the problem and its level of severity. Remember, owners of rental buildings may have employees who fail to deliver messages and superintendents who may shirk their responsibilities. The landlord (or his/her managing agent) must know exactly what the problem is, how serious it is, and how it should be addressed.
- Proceed slowly – If your landlord or managing agent is not responsive, the best policy is to slowly escalate the level of your complaints. Don’t rush to file a complaint or a lawsuit. For a good example of this policy, see [Broker Info](https://rentguidelinesboard.cityofnewyork.us/resources/apartment-hunting/broker-info/).
- Try to solve the problem before filing a formal complaint – Before you file a complaint with a government agency or go to court, try to work things out with your landlord, or if this is impossible, try to find another route such as mediation. Legal solutions can be quite complicated, time-consuming, and potentially very expensive. One way of getting what you want and avoiding the hassles of Housing Court is mediation, where a neutral third party hears both sides of a disagreement and helps develop solutions that meet everybody’s needs. The following Community Mediation Centers provide free mediation services:
  1. Manhattan and Brooklyn: [New York Peace Institute](https://nypeace.org/)
  2. Bronx: [Institute for Mediation & Conflict Resolution](https://www.imcr.org/)
  3. Queens: [Community Mediation Services](http://mediatenyc.org/)
  4. Staten Island: [New York Center for Interpersonal Development](https://nycid.org/what-we-do/conflict-management/community-based-mediation/)
Remember, government agencies are often understaffed and overwhelmed by their caseloads; courts and lawyers can also be very expensive and time-consuming. Filing a complaint may not be the quickest or most satisfactory way to resolve your problem. Of course, if you have made reasonable efforts to resolve your problem and no progress has been made, complaints to an administrative agency or court proceedings may be necessary. Although inconvenient and time consuming, the law does ultimately resolve landlord/tenant disputes.
