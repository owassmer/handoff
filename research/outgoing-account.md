# Outgoing account workflow and product design

Handoff should prepare a complete outgoing account, carry the accepted treatment into communications and financial actions, and resolve later changes through actual completion. This worked case specifies that behavior, the operator experience and the capabilities needed beneath it.

The operator value combines full workflow automation with protection of the operator's money through better legal and commercial decisions. The engine should prevent unnecessary work and vendor expense, preserve supported recovery, meet obligations and resolve disputes before avoidable escalation. These decisions begin with inspection and repair scope, not just the final account. The refund below exercises one changed-fact behavior; it is not the intended destination of the main demonstration. That demonstration must show the engine determining what work and charges are justified, and whether correction, recovery or settlement best serves the operator within the applicable obligations.

This is a proposed product design with prepared case facts, not a recorded Holland tenancy or a change to the live demonstration. Amounts are USD. The numerical example below remains useful for checking behavior, but is not the selected demonstration case. The demonstration should be adapted from a real California case with a substantial outgoing-account record; see the [California case recommendation](california-case/selection.md).

## Starting from the existing product

The current demonstration has completed physical work and provider payments. Its New York setting is the reason to replace it: the immediate prospect work calls for California. It was a useful selection because the real case supplied settlement and outgoing-account evidence. Preserve that strength in the California replacement.

The demonstration is an adaptation of real events for realism. We can change prepared owner instructions, dates and authority and fill gaps to create a coherent operating world. The existing instruction reserving deposit settlement is not a reason to reject the underlying case. Keep research records faithful to their sources while authoring the demonstration to exercise the intended product.

Reuse Handoff's inspections, invoices, correspondence, documents and coordinator. Begin the adapted case when the ending tenancy becomes known, with financial work running alongside physical work. Use a California case unrelated to Holland as the main narrative; Holland research informs relevance without making the demonstration a reenactment of that prospect's complaints.

## The prepared case

A conventional California apartment tenancy ends and possession returns on September 1, 2026. One adult tenant, Maya Chen, is the contracting tenant and deposit payee. Monthly rent is $2,400; the held deposit is $2,000. The prepared agreement and reconciled ledger establish $1,350 unpaid rent and a $150 final utility bill that Maya has already paid. There are no other opening charges or credits in this example.

Handoff has the agreement, payment history, move-in condition record and photographs, the pre-move-out inspection material, move-out observations and photographs, and itemized contractor bills and completion photographs. The operator has authorized Handoff to investigate and prepare the account; acceptance will authorize the specified statement, postings, deposit application and payment request. A later refund recommendation requires its own acceptance. No collection referral is authorized by this initial decision.

The physical plan includes $350 cabinet repair, $500 vanity repair, $200 cleaning and $600 routine repainting. The repaint is owner expense. Initially, the condition record and available photographs support treating the cabinet and vanity damage as occurring during the tenancy. The cleaning restores the documented arrival standard. Relevant claimed work is complete and invoiced by September 9. Separate owner improvement work keeps the unit unavailable until September 25.

These are stipulated example facts, not legal conclusions inferred from an invoice. Before using a property in a live or jurisdiction-specific demonstration, establish its actual location, regime, agreement and dated requirements, including local protections, inspection compliance, documentation, recipients and permitted delivery/payment methods. The engine must not select a rule from a postal city or assume a photograph alone establishes responsibility.

California's statewide framework supplies the timing pressure: an itemized accounting and remaining deposit are generally due within 21 days after vacancy, and pre-existing damage and ordinary wear cannot be charged against the deposit. September 22 is the ordinary 21-day date for this example. It is not an instruction to wait until then. The [California Attorney General's guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/media/Know-Your-Rights-Security-Deposits-English.pdf) supports those general principles; the current operative text of [Civil Code 1950.5](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1950.5.&lawCode=CIV) and applicable local requirements must supply the full implementation. This worked case does not depend on an estimated-deduction exception.

## The account the operator decides on

By September 10, Handoff has reconciled the account and recommends issuing it before the unit is physically ready.

| Account item | Amount | Treatment and basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Unpaid rent | $1,350 | Reconciled against agreement, credits and actual receipts |
| Final utilities | $150 | Supported final bill; already paid below |
| Cabinet repair | $350 | Supported tenant damage and reasonable repair expense |
| Vanity repair | $500 | Initially supported by available condition evidence; later changes |
| Cleaning | $200 | Necessary to restore the documented arrival standard |
| Total charges | $2,550 | |
| Utility payment already received | −$150 | Matched to the utility bill, not a new deposit deduction |
| Deposit applied | −$2,000 | $1,350 to rent, $350 to cabinet repair, $300 to vanity repair |
| Remaining balance | **$400 owed** | $200 remaining vanity charge and $200 cleaning |

The $600 repaint appears in the account's related work as an owner cost, with the reason it was excluded. It remains an expense in Money; it is not added to the tenant balance. The paid utility bill remains visible so the tenant can reconcile the whole account without being charged twice.

The recommendation says: “Apply the $2,000 deposit and send the itemized account showing $400 remaining. The utility bill is already paid. Routine repainting stays with the owner.”

The operator can inspect each material reason, edit a proposed treatment or discuss the recommendation. A numerical concession immediately changes the totals; a factual objection prompts investigation. Both routes update the same recommendation. The acceptance control states its effects: **Accept and send account**. It authorizes the reviewed account and its specified follow-through, including the listed recipients and methods, rather than an unspecified future collection strategy.

## What Handoff does after acceptance

Handoff retains the accepted account and issues the statement with the required supporting material through the appropriate delivery method. It applies the deposit in the operating ledger and updates the tenant payment balance. It records actual responses from those systems and follows incomplete operations without asking the operator to perform them.

If the ledger update succeeds but the payment portal update fails, Handoff knows the two systems disagree. It retries or investigates the affected operation and reports the actual next step. It does not mark the account complete because the statement exists, or replay successful money operations because another operation failed.

On September 17, Maya pays the $400. Handoff matches a confirmed settled receipt to the account. The account then has no remaining balance, while unit readiness still awaits the owner work. The product can show **Account settled · Unit work continues**. A later material event can reopen financial work without changing completed repair history.

## A dispute changes the result

On September 18, Maya writes: “The vanity was already broken. I found the repair acknowledgement from my move-in week. I paid the balance yesterday, but please correct it.” She attaches the original dated maintenance reply and photographs.

Handoff reads the message, matches it to the vanity charge and retrieves the corresponding maintenance record. The recovered material shows that the move-in checklist was incomplete and confirms the defect predated Maya's tenancy. The paid contractor invoice remains valid; the tenant's responsibility for that $500 changes. Handoff investigates the substance rather than dismissing the dispute because the account was accepted or paid.

Handoff marks the disputed item under review and prevents new automated collection activity for that item while it investigates, within its authorized controls. It reconciles the latest payment state before recommending financial follow-through. Unrelated work continues.

| Revised account | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total charges | $2,550 | $2,050 |
| Utility payment received | $150 | $150 |
| Deposit available and previously applied | $2,000 | $2,000 |
| Balance before the additional payment | $400 owed | $100 refundable |
| Additional payment received | $400 | $400 |
| Result after all receipts | $0 | **$500 refund due** |

Handoff recommends: “Remove the $500 vanity charge. The maintenance record confirms it was present at move-in. Maya has already paid the previous $400 balance, so refund $500 and send the corrected account.” This is a factual correction, not a discretionary concession or a new company-wide policy.

The operator sees the $500 change and the decisive evidence together. **Accept correction and refund $500** accepts the revised treatment and the listed payment instructions. Existing supported charges remain intact; Handoff does not seek approval again for the repairs or every unchanged account line.

## Carry the correction through

After acceptance, Handoff posts the $500 correction, sends a corrected statement, updates the portal and initiates the authorized refund to the verified payee. Internally, the corrected allocation releases $100 of the original deposit and returns the $400 subsequent payment. Preserve both origins while presenting one understandable $500 refund when the payment arrangements allow it.

If a refund request times out, Handoff checks whether it was accepted or settled before attempting another payment. While that outcome is uncertain, show **Refund confirmation pending** with the useful next action. A submitted instruction is not a completed refund. If the transfer fails or is returned, obtain valid payment instructions and continue the same obligation without losing the correction.

On September 21, the prepared scenario supplies a settled $500 refund, the corrected ledger and portal balances, and confirmation that the corrected statement was sent through the required method. Handoff sends a concise completion message with the statement and payment reference. Do not require the tenant to reply merely to complete a performed obligation; a continuing substantive dispute would remain active.

On September 25, the separate owner work passes its completion check. Both outcomes are now complete. The earlier financial resolution was valid independently of readiness.

## If another provider already holds the balance

The same change must work when a collector already has an account, including a referral that predates Handoff. Recognize the actual referral and authority; do not generate a new referral or invent an earlier Handoff decision.

For the disputed item, send the authorized hold or correction promptly and follow the provider's response. After account correction, confirm its collection balance and payment destination reflect the change. Reconcile money received by the provider as well as money received directly, including the timing of settlements, fees and remittances. A provider's account credit and an actual tenant refund are different events.

Do not declare recovery resolved while the provider still requests the old amount. If there is no supported API, Handoff can carry out the authorized correspondence and follow-up itself. Additional reporting or legal processes, if present in the case, also need their own completion evidence. They are not assumed to disappear when a local ledger is corrected.

This variation follows the concrete failure described in the [Holland resident research](holland/residents_and_risk.md): a revised balance did not initially reach the collection/payment system. It does not assume that the prepared case is that resident's case or that its jurisdiction is known.

## Operator experience

Give **Account** a full case tab beside the existing work and money views. Money continues to answer what has been funded, committed, billed and paid across the case. Account explains what the departing tenancy owes or is owed and why; both views read the same financial records.

The case header shows **Unit** and **Account** progress independently. The primary recommendation area presents whichever meaningful decision needs attention, including an account decision while physical work continues. The coordinator's progress message describes the current work across both outcomes.

| Moment | Main account view | Operator interaction |
|---|---|---|
| Preparing | Reconciled amounts, missing material and next required action/date | Supply genuinely unavailable context if asked; Handoff obtains accessible records itself |
| Ready for decision | Full account, prominent net result, concise reasons and planned follow-through | Inspect, edit, discuss, accept |
| Issued | Issued account and actual statement, ledger and payment progress | Inspect activity or give new instructions |
| Disputed | Affected line, tenant's actual message, investigation progress | View the issue without having to conduct the investigation |
| Correction ready | Before/after treatment, payment already received, refund due and decisive evidence | Accept or change the coherent correction |
| Refunding | Corrected account and actual refund progress | See what Handoff is following up |
| Resolved | Final charges, money received/returned and resulting balance | Open statement, payment record and meaningful history |

Each charge opens its amount, basis, relevant source and associated work without forcing all evidence onto the opening screen. Keep issued statements accessible unchanged, with a clear link to the later correction. The main view shows current treatment. Long accounts support scanning, grouped totals and expandable details; scrolling one document must not displace the account being compared.

## Engine capabilities required

| Capability | Required behavior in this case | Existing foundation |
|---|---|---|
| Account reconciliation | Match tenancy, charges, credits, deposit and receipts; avoid duplicate utility or tenant payments | Existing identities, agreements and document access |
| Applicable requirements | Determine allowed treatment, dated duties, required material, recipients and methods; act before physical completion when necessary | Legal research supplies inputs; coverage and case applicability still require completion |
| Account reasoning | Assess necessity, responsibility and reasonable amount; distinguish evidence correction from concession | Existing coordinator and contextual reasoning |
| Checked financial calculation | Calculate and allocate in integer cents; distinguish held deposit, applied deposit, receipts and refunds | Reusable calculation patterns; current provider-payment contracts are job-specific |
| Account recommendation and decision | Bind acceptance to reviewed lines, treatment and intended effects; reject stale acceptance | Existing work-plan pattern, extended to a distinct account recommendation |
| Financial execution | Issue statement, post treatment, reconcile portal, receive payment, refund and follow confirmation | Existing messages/activity and adapter approach; account actions must be added |
| Revision after performance | Correct by the necessary differences, preserve issued records and account for payment in flight | Existing durable records and command handling |
| Whole-case coordination | Wake for deadlines, messages and payment outcomes; continue independently progressing work | Existing coordinator; completion and wake conditions need both outcomes |

Extend recognizable records for the tenancy account, its lines, accepted recommendation, issued statements and financial transactions. Reuse parties, documents, messages and relevant obligations. Inspect existing reusable finance code before choosing exact types. Do not force a refund into a provider payment requiring a repair job, or build a competing hidden ledger behind the operator's account.

Jev is optional for a focused question such as whether an attachment concerns the disputed item. It should earn its place through comparison. It cannot determine the refund amount, authorize it, or replace the agent's assessment of contradictory evidence. This first workflow does not require a new model or a universal legal language.

## First implementation and demonstration

Build one connected path: reconcile the prepared account, recommend it, accept and issue it, recognize payment, investigate the new evidence, recommend the correction, refund and reconcile completion. Use the real coordinator and normal business actions against prepared external responses. The document describes the intended behavior; it is not evidence that the implementation already performs it.

The first operator exercise should prove that the operator can understand and change both recommendations without acting as a relay. Its engine checks should cover a duplicate payment event, a new receipt arriving between recommendation and acceptance, a refund timeout after submission, and an unchanged repair plan during account correction. The provider-held-balance variation should confirm that local correction alone does not finish the work.

The accompanying [prepared account data](outgoing-account-example.json) fixes only the financial facts and expected arithmetic for this example. Generality requires changed amounts, evidence and outcomes through the same code. The jurisdiction research and routing shortcomings remain work to resolve; the example does not establish legal coverage across Holland's portfolio.
